# How to ACTUALLY play your Role in Deadlock
channel: moe9times
date: 2026-07-21
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5bj_EYm06EM

## Covers
Dota-style positions 1 to 6 in Deadlock, defined by farm priority: hard carry, off carry, front line tank, roaming carry, flex support playmaker, hard support. Lanes, farming, fight jobs, itemization and archetypes for each, with Wraith, Mina, Abrams, Calico, Paige and Sinclair as examples. For players who win lane but feel lost after the Guardian falls.

## Claims
- Deadlock does not make you pick a role, but aligning your play style with your hero's role gives more impact and more wins. [0:30]
- The team that extracts the most value from its farm usually wins; souls dictate how hard you hit and how long you survive. [0:30]
- Not all souls are equal: a Seven with 60K souls is a force multiplier that team wipes, while a Rem with 60K is strong but his value comes from base spells and cheap utility. [1:00]
- Rem's value is in base spells: a big burst heal can bait enemies into overextending, and his sleep (nap time) sets up Rejuvenator steals. [1:30]
- An under-farmed Seven does minuscule damage, gets his Storm Cloud canceled and becomes a soul bag for the enemies. [2:01]
- Souls on the map are limited, so the biggest team mistake is not failing to farm but farming the wrong things on the wrong people. [2:33]
- Positions are defined by who should have farm priority on the map at any moment: position one gets the most farm, position six the least. [2:33]
- These are not hard rules; every hero can take over a game, so do not flame teammates for off-role items or play. [3:03]
- Position one (hard carry) farms most effectively to build a soul advantage and then deals a big chunk of the team's damage. [3:33]
- If your skill is higher than your lobby, hard carry is the easiest role to climb with. [4:05]
- A position one hero often has a very weak early game but a game-defining late game, and commonly excels in 1v1s and farms very fast. [4:05]
- Position one play style: get rich and stay rich. [4:37]
- The hard carry usually plays the safe lane (left lane), which has a tier 1 camp in lane the enemy lacks, the air vent to the bridge buff, and the double Sinner's Sacrifice building in the left jungle. [4:37]
- A poverty position one turns the game into a 5v6, since carries lack utility and do pitiful damage without their power spikes. [5:09]
- As position one, never miss a trooper wave when it is safe. [5:39]
- Waves spawn 30 seconds apart and give around 500 souls, scaling through the game. [5:39]
- Pushing waves pushes your presence up the zipline and forces the enemy to respond or risk losing objectives to troopers. [5:39]
- Think of farming on a 30 second timer: each wave clear buys 30 seconds to farm jungle, hit Sinner's Sacrifice or grab boxes. [6:09]
- Sinner's Sacrifice is probably the highest soul value and also gives four permanent buffs, which the hard carry is best placed to use. [6:09]
- Dying is terrible on position one: every 30 seconds dead misses a wave plus the farm between waves. [6:09]
- The enemy always targets the carry; keep a mental note of the heroes who can kill you and their items, and dodge them around the map while farming. [6:40]
- If your counter shows on the opposite side of the map, you have space to farm. [7:10]
- Do not be too greedy with item purchases; you cannot do damage dead. [7:10]
- The three carry types are gun carries, tank carries and spirit carries. [7:10]
- Gun carries are the most common; gun items scale better on average but cost much more than spirit items. [7:10]
- Gun carries have the highest single target damage in the game, and late items like Ricochet can shred the whole enemy team. [7:41]
- Sliding gives you infinite ammo, so keep moving to minimize reload downtime. [7:41]
- Gun carries need strong tracking aim to secure late game kills. [7:41]
- Tank carries like Victor start weak but become the strongest front line in the game once scaled, dealing big damage and healing more than can be dealt. [7:41]
- Tank carries need refined positioning because they get kited by people abusing their lack of range. [8:12]
- Spirit carries like Seven specialize in high AoE damage; cooldown management is their biggest concern since Deadlock has no mana, only cooldowns. [8:12]
- Wasting your strongest spell leaves you doing effectively nothing for a long time; be deliberate and play your effective range. [8:43]
- Kills are a large soul source; Walkers, the Urn, the Rift and in a way Mid Boss grant souls and open up the game. [9:15]
- The carry holds much of the team's damage, so they must show up to important fights; do not farm aimlessly. [9:15]
- As the game reaches its peak the carry's goal is maximum damage, with sustain items keeping them alive while they deal consistent damage. [9:45]
- Late game the carry's top priority is killing the enemy frontliners, since only they can output enough damage or on-hit debuffs to shut them down. [9:45]
- As carry, do not waste time chasing hypermobile heroes; wait for your team's utility to lock them down. [10:16]
- Never be at the very front of a fight as the carry; let frontliners soak the CC, because one bad Mo & Krill combo, Bebop hook or Paradox swap kills you instantly. [10:16]
- Wraith embodies position one: insane farm accelerant, weak early game, fast snowball. [10:47]
- Wraith is criminally weak in lane; her base gun damage is underwhelming and her cards do nothing to strong laners like Abrams or Shiv, so play safe and farm instead of fiending for early kills. [11:18]
- Wraith's full auto plus Tesla Bullets clears jungle camps faster than almost anyone. [11:18]
- Wraith's teleport gives freedom in the side lane to escape danger and keep pressure on the enemy's camps. [11:49]
- Wraith's Walker push is very strong thanks to fast fire rate and the ability to teleport out. [11:49]
- Wraith's ultimate disarms and silences one target for almost 4 seconds, nearly guaranteeing a side lane 1v1 and killing most enemy carries. [12:20]
- If Wraith is shoved under tower and has her farm stolen, she lacks souls for items and the enemy carry can take her Walker, making her lane unsafe. [12:54]
- Position two (off carry) plays the off lane (right side lane), which lacks the air vent to the bridge buff. [13:26]
- The off lane has no 2 minute tier 1 camp; instead it has tier 2 camps on the garage roof and in the church that spawn at 6 minutes. [13:26]
- Playing position two like a hard carry works but is not optimal, since you lack easy access to the double Sinner's Sacrifice and have a weaker side of camps. [13:56]
- Position two's goal: make the opposing carry's life a living hell, because a position one that falls behind cannot play the game. [13:56]
- A good off carry has a better start to the mid game, a strong duel, self-peel, survivability and high burst. [14:26]
- Higher tier camps take longer to respawn, and the off lane side has almost no tier 1s, so it has more downtime. [14:26]
- Position two's number one goal is to win lane: use burst or sustained poke to push the weaker carry off the wave and stop them ramping. [14:26]
- The enemy position one is happy with an even lane; the position two should not be. [14:56]
- Finish lane quickly so you can go bully other places. [14:56]
- Do not feed the enemy position one. [15:27]
- Watch ult economy: an advantage of even a few hundred souls creates a point where you have ultimates and they do not; abuse that spike to snowball. [15:27]
- In the early game it is always worth using ultimates to secure kills; if the enemy laners use theirs, use yours to counter or you give up your natural advantage. [15:27]
- Expect the pressured enemy hard carry to call for help; watch the map for early ganks rather than relying only on a missing call from mid. [15:58]
- After lane, position two roams to mid when needed and ganks if mid is stalled, to activate your two roaming mid laners. [15:58]
- Resolving lanes quickly gives your team map presence to absorb farm and play for objectives. [16:29]
- The off carry is a stabilizer: with a strong mid game, go where you are needed to shut down problems across the map. [16:29]
- If things are stable, the off carry's second and more important goal is destroying the enemy Walker in their lane. [16:59]
- Destroying a Walker gives a huge amount of souls, a chunk split to those who sieged it and an additional amount to everyone on the team. [16:59]
- No Walker makes the lane dangerous for the enemy carry: Walkers give a 15% defense boost to anyone nearby and do big damage to attackers. [16:59]
- With the Walker gone the lane can be shoved to their base, wrecking their map presence. [17:31]
- If your hard carry throws their lead, the off carry must take over their position. [17:31]
- Most off carries fall off late as better scalers keep farming, so AFK farming without purpose on position two is detrimental. [18:02]
- As the game goes on, position two should pick up survivability or utility instead of pure damage, since enemies buy resistances that cut burst and stop instant pick-offs. [18:02]
- The two off carry archetypes are spirit nukers and bruisers, both built to punish squishy carries. [18:32]
- Spirit damage is cheaper to build than late game gun damage, so spirit nukers hit power spikes much faster. [18:32]
- Pocket can insta-kill carries early if he lands his full combo. [19:03]
- Spirit nukers use their disengage to deal with supports and frontliners trying to peel, and usually have very fast early camp and wave clear. [19:03]
- Spirit nukers fall off fast once enemies build heavy spirit resist, including tier 4s like Witchmail or Spellbreaker; abuse the strong timing while it lasts. [19:33]
- Bruisers like Yamato have high base damage, built-in sustain and survivability, and catch tools to force fights, letting them stat check opposing carries. [19:33]
- Mina is perhaps the definitive off carry: Love Bites gives an oppressive lane and her sustain wins almost every trade. [20:04]
- Squishy carries get disintegrated by a Mina passive proc plus Rake; with any competent partner she wins lane fast and roams freely. [20:04]
- Mina's ultimate silence shuts off common carry escapes like Wraith's teleport and Infernus's dash; her blink escapes most lockdown. [20:34]
- Mina's tower damage is incredible because her passive procs on structures and triggers her spirit items; even after nerfs, use her ultimate on Walkers to claim them when the moment calls for it. [20:34]
- Position three is the front line tank who soaks damage while carries deal damage from behind. [21:04]
- Space making is core to Deadlock: squishy teammates cannot play while taking every stun, slow and silence. [21:34]
- Position three should never sit in the back; their kit is built to be in the enemy's face, and less breathing room forces bad enemy decisions. [21:34]
- Attention is a resource: be a big flashing target, since every second spent shooting you is time for your carries and supports. [22:05]
- Just standing in the right places body blocks for teammates and messes up enemy pathing. [22:05]
- Do not hold W into the enemy team; with six people shooting you will die as anti-heal and shred items get bought. Use natural cover and choke points and coordinate engagements. [22:36]
- Want to take CC on position three: you likely have debuff resistance and survive what kills other roles. [22:36]
- Position three's job is locking fights down, not getting kills; good trigger discipline matters, as one perfect interrupt beats a cosmetic stun on the enemy tank. [23:06]
- Always ask whether to engage or peel; engaging can be as simple as walking forward to gain ground and pin enemies or corner them. [23:36]
- Peeling means turning to help your back line when divers jump over you; do not keep engaging while your back line is dove. [23:36]
- If the fight looks good keep pushing; if it is bad, fix the leaky pipes. [24:06]
- Frontliners have sluggish camp clear, so their main activity is fighting. [24:37]
- Position three is best in the mid lane for easy air vent access to both side lanes; catch the mid wave whenever you can and never drop a wave. [24:37]
- Trooper souls are split between everyone in the lane; lingering in a carry's lane for multiple waves without doing anything delays their power spikes and lets the enemy carry solo farm. [25:07]
- On position three, think about how to get kills and then get out of the way. [25:37]
- Before a gank ask: can we beat this person? Early, your value is peel; Abrams Shoulder Charge or Billy's Rising Ram is worth much more with your carry there to follow up. [26:07]
- Estimate whether your damage plus nearby allies kills the target before they escape, especially against slippery heroes. [26:07]
- Ask who is missing: always assume a support or another initiator is lurking nearby until proven wrong, and track where every enemy is. [26:39]
- Your disengage is usually poor, so an unexpected Bebop hooking you under tower makes you a soul gift. [26:39]
- Pay close attention to enemy ultimates, several of which can flip a gank. [27:10]
- Position three's number one item job is staying alive in fights: buy resistances, movement, purges and lockdown as needed; when you die the back line collapses. [27:41]
- Position three usually lacks solo pushing power; its job is enabling carries to push objectives. [27:41]
- After a gank, convert it into extra value: stealing their jungle, a tower, the Urn or Mid Boss. [27:41]
- Communication, even default pings, lets carries position for your ganks and pushes; it is the most underrated skill. [28:12]
- The two position three styles: the brawl tank, a beefy front liner who will not die, and the wall breaker, a map-wide nuisance threatening Walkers and other structures to divert enemy attention. [28:44]
- Wall breaker requires a lot of game understanding; played badly it is just throwing. [29:14]
- Abrams is the best brawl tank example: Infernal Resilience regenerates part of all damage he takes, so sustained damage barely kills him. [29:45]
- Abrams' Siphon Life makes him nearly immortal while active, stealing health from everyone nearby. [30:15]
- Abrams' Shoulder Charge lets you choose where to charge someone: push back enemies pressing too hard or catch people not watching you. [30:15]
- Abrams' Seismic Impact is point-and-click engage that shuts down scary ultimates and pulls airborne heroes down. [30:46]
- Both Shoulder Charge and Seismic Impact double as disengage, letting Abrams commit deeper than other tanks if he manages cooldowns. [30:46]
- Position four (roaming carry) plays mid like a League jungler: setting up ganks to kill targets fast, the aggressive roamer to position three's defensive one. [31:16]
- Position four heroes have strong movement and very early power spikes; position three plays for the late game while position four plays the early game and falls off. [31:47]
- Position four makes space by out-positioning with movement instead of tanking; long combat is bad and spells are your key resource. [32:18]
- Position four should not spend much time on camps on their own side; they specialize in invading the enemy jungle and killing enemies in their camps, often diving over the front line and escaping. [32:18]
- Winning lane matters for position four because their farm should mainly come from kills. [32:51]
- Deadlock has no wards; you are revealed only when an enemy sees you or near troopers, so arriving at a lane not via the zipline lets you surprise enemies. [33:21]
- On position four avoid showing on waves; if your whereabouts are unknown, gankable enemies farm less confidently, slowing their souls. [33:51]
- In fights position four's goal is bursting someone down; do not overcommit, trust teammates to finish the low target after your combo. [33:51]
- Taking 1,500 damage out of nowhere makes it very hard for an enemy to walk into you and your team; push up together and finish them. [34:23]
- Position four's 1v1 is weaker than most of the roster; do not take side lane 1v1s, set up kills instead. [34:23]
- Keep timers in your head and set up map pressure every 5 minutes with buffs, Urns and the Rift. [34:54]
- Position four is the main damage source for early Rift fights since carries are not online and lack sustain items, so damage dealt to them is basically permanent. [34:54]
- After every gank, convert into forward progress or an objective. [34:54]
- Late game, position four builds defensive items and acts as front line (Calico or Silver) or builds utility as a disrupter (Apollo); Calico and Silver's healing keeps them in fights, Apollo's riposte and ultimate disable strong targets. [35:25]
- More damage late on position four rarely helps; spirit resist items are more flexible than bullet resist and often more effective since most spirit damage is cooldown limited. [35:25]
- Consider skipping Escalating Exposure for team utility items such as Knockdown or a silence item. [35:55]
- Position four archetypes are spirit nukers and brawler tanks; brawlers like Silver like fighting and skip high investment defensive items early to focus on damage. [36:25]
- Calico is the prime position four: Ava scouts the map without showing on vision so she can pick the moment to leap in and dump her spells. [36:55]
- Calico lacks utility beyond damage, so missing leaps or bombs makes her useless; she is an incredible box runner and gains extra souls on kill, so she prefers not to take wave farm. [37:25]
- Position six (hard support) should have impact with the fewest souls possible; impact comes from lockdown, healing and barriers, not damage. [38:25]
- A perfectly timed Divine Barrier can turn an enemy burst gank into a disaster for them by baiting an overextend while allies arrive. [38:25]
- A hard support building nuker items (Kelvin with Boundless Spirit and Spirit Burn) is not how the role should be played. [38:25]
- The gap between a mediocre and skilled hard support is spell timing and positioning. [38:56]
- Paige landing Captivating Read in the mid to late game usually gets you killed, but if she casts Bookworm and Captivating Read together and you dodge, she has nothing for about 30 seconds; be patient and cast for high impact. [38:56]
- Hard support heroes generally have strong base abilities and game-changing ultimates with long cooldowns. [39:28]
- The hard support is the backbone of the hard carry's survival: hover near them, close enough to save them but not visible on the map or taking souls. [39:59]
- If the support dies but the carry lives, the carry keeps farming and the gank's momentum creates space elsewhere. [40:31]
- If your hard carry is a lost cause, it is fine to support whoever will pave the path to victory. [40:31]
- The hard support lacks souls for items like Spellbreaker or Resilience, so stay alive by using line of sight, corners and fog walls. [40:31]
- Hard support farm should come from boxes, assist gold, the Urn and comeback souls given to the lowest two net worths on the team, not from waves; a rich hard support has probably taken too much lane farm. [41:01]
- If you are at a wave and your carry is nearby in jungle, hold the wave for your carry and leave soul range quickly. [41:32]
- The hard support is generally the best person to run the Urn, especially when their high impact ultimate is on cooldown. [41:32]
- The Urn should no longer be used as a distraction play because the bounty decays and the carrier is no longer a flashing indicator on the map. [42:02]
- Hard support itemization fills the team's utility holes: against a channel that must be stopped, buy Knockdown. [42:02]

- If a hard support is eyeing Tankbuster or Boundless Spirit, ask whether the team actually needs that damage. [42:33]
- Paige is the clearest hard support: nearly unable to kill alone, but with one ally she brings huge lockdown, shields and heals, and can play very far back. [43:04]

- Position five (flex support, playmaker) exists because survivability has diminishing returns; two Paiges are bad since if no one dies, the higher damage team wins over time. [43:36]
- Position five's job is to make equal fights unfair. [44:37]
- The most common position five type is displacers; landing one Bebop hook on a badly positioned Paige turns an even fight into a 5v6. [44:37]
- Displacement works both ways: pull an overextended ally (a diving Seven) back to safety. Bring allies where it is safe and enemies where it is unsafe. [45:07]
- Area denial shapes fights without hooks, like a molly in Counter-Strike; a Paradox bomb flushes enemies out of a spot. [46:09]

- When roaming, balance time across all three lanes so you do not sap one teammate's souls too much. [48:41]
- Position five is the preemptive playmaker: keep buffs set up and safe and be one of the first to rotate for the Rift; the team will look to you to clean up fights and unstick stalled lanes. [48:41]

- Position five has the most build freedom: strong base damage and survivability plus more spare souls than a hard support; ask what the team needs (gun or spirit damage, anti-heal, utility, survivability, shred). [49:43]
- Sinclair must adapt the build every game; playing Bolt Sinclair every game is a disservice to the team. [50:54]

- Position five heroes are often more mechanically demanding, needing strong aim, movement or both. [54:27]
