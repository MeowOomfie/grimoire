# How to Lane like an ETERNUS in 2026 | Comprehensive Deadlock Lane Guide
channel: HERESY  date: 2026-01-27  url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X9yhG5vKca4

## Covers
Lane phase: soul advantage, the big four checks (health, items, souls, ults), wave priority, cover and veils, slide to save ammo, poking securers, lane timers (2:00, 3:30, 4:30, 5:00, 6:00, vaults), healing trooper, guardians, low vs high cooldown fighters, sustainers, strong laners, roamers, wave clearers, map asymmetry (strong left lane, weak right lane, blue lane), lane items and 4.8k investment breakpoints; heroes Dynamo, Calico, Viscous, Lady Geist, Billy, Abrams, Infernus, Ivy, The Doorman, Wraith, Vyper, Pocket, Vindicta, Mo & Krill, Victor, Paradox, Holliday, Mina, Yamato, Grey Talon, Mirage, Bebop, Haze, Shiv, Warden, McGinnis

## Claims
- The primary goal of every player in the lane phase is to secure a soul advantage, because it lets you consistently win fights over the map's objectives later (mid boss, walkers that are easier to destroy after 15 minutes). [0:31]
- Given the choice between a strong soul advantage and taking the guardian early, err on the side of the soul advantage, since it lets you take that guardian and other objectives later. [1:01]
- You are preparing for the mid game; do not make lane decisions that help short term but hurt your mid game. [1:33]
- Before fighting, constantly check the big four: health, items, souls and ults. Fighting smarter means taking fights you know you have the advantage in when the opponents don't know it. [2:03]
- Being healthier is not the same as being stronger: if the enemies spent their souls in the shop while you sit on 2,000 unspent souls, they may be more effective in the fight. [2:33]
- If you and your lane partner both deal mostly gun damage and the enemies bought Battle Vest, a fight that looks winnable on health can be badly lost. [3:03]
- Being down a thousand souls per player matters mid lane because many heroes get a big jump from putting two points into an ability; you may be fighting without your key upgrade while they have theirs. [3:33]
- Ult economy is critical: many heroes are balanced around their ultimate. Dynamo's ult is around a 4 minute cooldown, so he is strong with it and comparatively weak without it. [4:05]
- If you and your partner both have ultimates and the enemies don't, you have a significant chance to win even after losing lane, being down souls and pressured under tower, if you use the ults decisively. [4:36]
- The reverse also holds: a winning lane pushing the enemy guardian while up items and souls can both die if they miss that the enemies have ultimates and they don't. [5:06]
- Prioritize clearing the wave before fighting players; otherwise you fight the enemy heroes and their minion wave at the same time. [5:37]
- If the enemy wave is constantly pushed to your guardian you are anchored to it and only control your tower. Pushing the wave first gives you the center line, high ground, the bridge, and first move on camps and boxes. [6:07]
- Your default every single wave should be to clear the wave first; if you don't, have a very good specific reason. [7:08]
- Play around cover and the veil. Close range heroes like Billy and Abrams are very good at contesting the middle ground veil. [7:08]
- Most heroes hold their gun on the right side of the model, so peeking cover from the right lets you shoot minions and players while nearly fully covered, compounding hundreds of chip damage over the lane. [7:38]
- High level players use heavy melee lunges (often to evade around corners), slide after dashing to keep momentum, and wall bounces for erratic movement that costs no stamina. [8:08]
- Firing while sliding expends no ammo for any hero, so slide as much as possible in all-ins, even dashing just to slide afterwards. [8:38]
- The securer of a soul always has the advantage, so denying souls the enemy is watching is hard. Instead, use otherwise dodgeable poke (for example Lady Geist's Essence Bomb) on the enemy when they go to secure. [9:08]
- Fight in windows where the big four say you have the advantage; don't habitually fight without a reason to think you'll win. Example: souls spent, ult up, enemy Viscous without ult, so a fight while lower health than both enemies was a good fight. [10:09]
- For the first three waves there are essentially no objectives. Guardians start with 75% resistances decaying to -50% over 12 minutes, so at 2 minutes you do basically zero tower damage. [11:10]
- Early on, unless your matchup makes you much weaker, err on the side of aggression: take space, have brawlers rush the middle veils, clear minions fast for wave priority. [11:42]
- After the first wave you can buy an 800 soul item; unless you are shoving them under tower and can keep poking, go back to shop immediately, or they fight you on wave two with an item advantage. [12:12]
- In the first three waves, if the enemies are stronger, there is no downside to playing extremely defensive: wait for the healing minions, buy a healing item, "lose gracefully" until objectives spawn at 2 minutes. [13:14]
- At 2:00 camps and boxes spawn. Boxes spawn nearly everywhere, but tier one camps spawn only in the blue lane and your team's left lane. [13:44]
- The point of lane phase is souls, which matter much more than killing your opponents or even their tower. Two blue laners who farm diligently can each be a thousand souls richer by 8 minutes than green laners who only fight. [14:15]
- Farm when fighting is disadvantageous and fight when it is advantageous; the skill beyond mechanics is judging which you are. [15:18]
- A camp taken about 5 seconds after it spawns at 2:00 respawns at 3:30 (1 minute 25 seconds after the kill), so a stolen enemy camp can be reliably stolen again. [15:49]
- The bridge buffs spawn at 5:00, but contesting them without giving up resources starts with clearing the 4:30 wave as fast as possible. [16:19]
- If you reach the bridge early you would normally run boxes until the buffs spawn, but when your blue lane has lost, expect their blue laners to rotate to your buff and play cautiously. [16:50]
- At 6:00 tier two camps spawn, and with jungle camp health reduced 30% most heroes can clear them that early; after clearing the wave, contest your camps or the enemy's if stronger. Monster Rounds speeds this up. [17:50]
- Center sacrifices (vaults) take 300 melee damage to kill and when they spawn are worth by far the most souls for the time, more with the golden statue buffs from a well timed heavy melee. They are the quickest way to snowball outside of kills. [18:50]
- In low elo vaults often stay unsecured for minutes; be the one who takes them. [19:21]
- With a decisive lead and the enemy guardian down, steal the enemy vault (about 500 souls) before chasing a kill, because the vault is guaranteed value and chasing is not. [19:52]
- A healing trooper's health pack heals you, your team and your troopers in a large radius for 16% of missing health plus 150 HP, plus 4 flat HP per minute. [20:53]
- The community kills the healing trooper last to heal more from damage taken, but the heal applies over 2 seconds to players and over 8 seconds to troopers, making your troopers effectively unkillable by minion attacks. [21:23]
- Killing the healing trooper first can guarantee the shove and wave priority; the extra self-healing from saving it last is marginal, maybe 50 HP unless you took heavy damage. Each wave is a choice between the two. [22:23]
- Guardians aggro the nearest target with single target damage, but switch to you immediately if you damage an enemy hero and hold aggro until you fully leave range. [23:56]
- Parrying a guardian stuns it for 2 seconds. Standing on it and heavy meleeing continuously is fastest but it re-aggros and hurts, so only do it when it is low or you must kill it quickly. [24:28]
- The most efficient low-damage way to hit a guardian is dash in, parry, heavy melee, dash away and slide to stretch your magazine; use it after winning a quick lane fight and crashing a wave. [25:02]
- Most heroes can be classed as low cooldown fighters (always some fight threat from short cooldowns, gun or passive) or high cooldown fighters (long cooldowns, or one key ability on a long cooldown). [26:03]
- Infernus is much stronger with Flame Dash, which is about a 40 second cooldown, so fight him when he doesn't have it. [27:03]
- As a high cooldown fighter, don't take constant trades, walk up too far or take gun duels. Ivy, with a low damage close range gun, should not keep gun dueling The Doorman, who has the advantage. [27:33]
- Calico's Gloom Bombs are on a sub 10 second cooldown in lane, so poking her every ~10 seconds gives her constant chances to hit you; Wraith, who is strong only all-inning with full auto and cards, should not keep fighting her. [28:03]
- Low cooldown examples: "Barash" (caption, hero unclear) constantly stacks his mark and wins long engagements; Abrams has very high health regen and wants to gun fight constantly; Vyper has a very strong weapon and wants to fight when no one has cooldowns. [28:33]
- High cooldown examples: Pocket only wants quick engagements with cloak and satchel up; Billy is strong with Blasted and weak without; Dynamo's only real combat ability is his stomp, so he is exposed when it's down; Vindicta is weak without flight, which is on a long cooldown. [29:04]
- High or low cooldown fighters are not inherently strong or weak; the concept holds regardless of patch and meta. [29:35]
- Sustainers (strong self-healing, not ally healers like Dynamo or McGinnis) have more effective HP; 200 poke damage on Abrams, Mo & Krill or Victor does much less than on Paradox or Holliday. Put your finite poke on non-sustainers; the sustainers want you to attack them. [30:06]
- Strong laners Valve wants powerful in lane: Mina (survivability, sustain, poke), Lady Geist (wave clear, sustain, poke), Yamato (CC immune ult, strong poke from right clicks and Power Slash, and one point in Crimson Slash halves enemy fire rate, so she is rarely vulnerable). [31:08]
- Roamers have strong ganking tools; consider roaming especially from the blue lane. Holliday and Grey Talon may need their ultimates to roam well, others have the tools in their first three abilities. [31:39]
- If you are winning with constant wave priority, instead of (or after) taking the enemy camp or boxes, bounce pad and roam to another lane; towers are easy to dive if you are healthy or have ult, and it does psychological damage in solo queue. [32:10]
- Playing with a wave clearer lets you run more boxes between waves or gank; wave clearers can hold the tower 1v2, buying roamers time, so they synergize, especially in a duo. [32:40]
- The map is asymmetrical: your left side lane is the strong lane and your right side lane is the weak lane. [33:11]
- The strong side has a tier one camp, a one-way veil for safely defending its tower, and safer zipline access that is hard to hyperline. [33:41]
- Offensively, the strong side has an angle on the enemy secret shop without guardian aggro and an open sightline on the enemy zipline, so weak side defenders struggle to recall and must run most of the way to their walker. The weak side defenders also lack a veil. [34:42]
- Blue lane is symmetrical: both sides have a tier one camp behind a veil, so you choose between invading the enemy tier one camp or clearing your own. [35:12]
- In blue lane you can fight people at the enemy secret shop without guardian aggro, and both sides have high ground, so blue lane is very punishing: many attack angles if winning, the reverse if losing. [35:42]
- Blue lane is the shortest distance back to base, so after a quick shove you can zip dash home to heal within a wave and hardly lose souls. [36:44]
- The weak side is poorer, under more pressure when shoved, and exerts less pressure when it shoves out (guardian blocks the shop angle, and the angle exposes you to the one-way veil): easier to lose and harder to win. [37:15]
- The strong side reaches the bridge buffs faster via the bounce pad and wall jump straight to the buff; the weak side has safer but slower access through the room it controls. [38:17]
- The jungle is mirrored: the weak (right) side has less important camps, only two center sacrifices, fewer tier two camps, larger tier three camps in the back (less relevant at 8-12 minutes) and its secret shop, so losing that tower early hurts less. [39:18]
- The strong (left) side has four center sacrifices and the double room, the most souls per clear time on the map at 8 minutes; both teams should fight to secure theirs and the enemy's. [40:19]
- The strong side has no secret shop, so losing the strong side (yellow) tower costs your jungle defense and shop access and can be game-deciding; do not lose it. [40:49]
- Follow builds by known top streamers or pro players to avoid most itemization pitfalls. [41:19]
- Headshot Booster gives a flat 45 headshot damage on a timer, better early when people have 600-800 HP than later at 3,000. Monster Rounds helps when consistently shoved in, keeping the wave off you so you can avoid poke and run boxes. [41:49]
- Reactive Barrier is extremely good in lanes where both enemies can proc it. Mystic Burst is flat damage, weak late but a big early boost. [42:20]
- Spirit scaling is flat while gun scaling is multiplicative, so even gun heroes can buy Extra Spirit early; the first 800 often still gives +7 spirit even without the full breakpoint. [42:51]
- Melee Lifesteal is good on heroes that can proc it with an ability (Abrams off Shoulder Charge, Billy and Calico off their melee skills); otherwise it telegraphs your melee and invites parries. [43:22]
- Mystic Regeneration suits constant spirit damage; Mirage can proc it from every gunshot with a zero cooldown spirit proc, healing off multiple targets, so it is uniquely good on him. [43:52]
- Restorative Shot is best against big hitboxes (Mo & Krill, Dynamo, Bebop) since it heals far more off heroes than NPCs; skip it if you can't hit shots or face thin targets like Mina or Rem. [44:22]
- Extra Regen is always on (3 regen, works in combat); Healing Rite used off cooldown gives at least 5 regen per second but doesn't work in combat and can be canceled. Used intentionally (for example while running boxes) Healing Rite can beat Extra Regen. [44:52]
- Buy Extra Regen anyway if your hero builds Healing Booster later. [45:23]
- Percent-based items (Bullet Lifesteal, Tankbuster) get better as the game goes on and are usually not optimal in lane; buy a couple of lane items, not your whole build, and don't sacrifice mid game power. [46:25]
- Always buy at least one healing item, even when winning; if you win your HP trades, more HP lets you keep funding them without releasing pressure. [47:27]
- Slowing Hex is good for roaming and joining fights elsewhere but is a mid game item; generally don't buy it early lane. [47:57]
- Rebuttal drastically lowers the parry cooldown; melee heroes facing it must be careful throwing melees after the parry resets, since it often kills people in a single trade even in Eternus 6. [48:27]
- An enemy buying Healing Rite says they don't want to fight while channeling; invade their jungle or shop side to cancel it, or 2v1 the teammate they left behind. [48:59]
- Going from 3.2k to 4.8k invested in a category gives a big stat jump, so it is mathematically optimal in most cases to reach a 4.8k investment spike as soon as reasonably possible. [49:30]
- Top player builds put 4.8k into gun or spirit early besides optional healing items: Mirage goes 1600, 1600, 1600; a Shiv build rushes 4.8k spirit; Pocket does the same. [50:31]
- After hitting the 4.8k spike, get very aggressive on the map to overwhelm opponents with a stat advantage. [51:33]
- Builds don't always hit 4.8k at 5k souls because hero-specific items come first (Warden likes Quicksilver Reload on his grenade), but nearly all have 4.8k in one stat by 10k souls spent. [52:03]
- Mina may want 4.8k spirit, but into two heroes that proc Reactive Barrier (for example Bebop plus someone) Reactive Barrier can be better; situationally strong items give abnormal value per soul. [53:03]
- Efficiency is what Deadlock is all about, for farming and for spending souls. [53:33]
- Cooldown items bought in lane (Restorative Shot, Melee Lifesteal, the shielding items, Active Reload, Headshot Booster) must be used as often as possible to get their value. [53:33]
- Headshot Booster has a 9 second cooldown and procs at any range; pop the enemy in the head before body shots or minions. [54:04]
- Restorative Shot goes on a 6 second cooldown on the next bullet; aim it at an enemy hero roughly every 6 seconds, using it on a minion only if no hero will be near for about that long. [55:04]
- Weapon Shielding and Spirit Shielding proc on heavy damage of their type, Reactive Barrier on CC. A Haze with about 730 HP early who gets hooked by Bebop and procs a 325 barrier should trade, not run; fight until the barrier is used up or it is wasted. [55:34]
- To improve fastest, mentally apply yourself: each game, learn one enemy cooldown (for example Viscous's cube and splatter), and assess wave to wave whether you are strong. [56:35]
- Don't fight all the time just to get better at fighting, and don't AFK farm boxes; find a rational middle ground. [57:37]
