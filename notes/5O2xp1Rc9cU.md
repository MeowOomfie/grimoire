# Dominate The Early Game | Everything I've Learned in 4,000+ Hours
channel: Deathy  date: 2026-02-02  url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5O2xp1Rc9cU

## Covers
Early game (first 10 minutes) fundamentals: aggression, pushing the wave and killing the healing trooper last, trooper traffic light for trades, MOBA and shooter positioning, effective range, stamina management, forming a lane theory, playing to your items, sustain/burst/poke hero types, advantage and disadvantage states, denies by rank, resetting, guardian resistances, timings (2, 5, 6, 8, 10 minutes), ability point and ult spikes, Sinner's Sacrifice, Abrams, Mo & Krill, Pocket, Lash, Haze, Mirage, Mina, Vindicta, Shiv, Kelvin, Warden, McGinnis, Extra Regen, Healing Rite, Monster Rounds, Headshot Booster, Restorative Shot, Extra Stamina

## Claims
- The early game, also called the laning stage, is the first 10 minutes and sets you up for success; improving it is one of the most important ways to win more games. [0:00]
- In lane you should be aggressive; aggression is generally rewarded in Deadlock and is a catalyst for improvement: someone who plays 100 lanes aggressively improves faster than someone who plays 100 lanes passively. [0:32]
- Use your abilities; there is no mana cost, so not using them is missed damage. [0:32]
- As a rule of thumb, push the wave into the enemy tower, but kill the healing trooper last: it heals for a percentage of missing health, so keeping it alive longer rewards aggression. [0:32]
- Pushing the wave lets you poke the enemy under their tower with gun or abilities, try to deny their souls, steal their resources (their small camp or boxes on their side), and gives you the option to gank; it gives you agency. [1:03]
- A good trade means you come out of the engagement in better relative condition than your opponents: more souls, more health, more cooldowns, whatever benefits you. [1:33]
- Use troopers as a traffic light (green, yellow, red) to decide trades. [1:33]
- Green light: you have more troopers than the enemy (say four vs two); take a fight and they must retreat or take large amounts of trooper damage, so look for fights. [2:03]
- In the people he coaches, troopers are very often the number one source of damage taken during the laning stage. [2:03]
- Yellow light: equal troopers (four vs four, how the lane starts); the troopers favour no one, so only fight if you believe you have another advantage, such as higher base gun DPS. [2:03]
- Still try to take yellow light fights so you learn the limits of different heroes and what is possible. [2:35]
- Red light: you have fewer troopers (two vs four); a fight most likely costs significant trooper damage, so only fight with an overwhelming advantage, like an enemy low enough to kill instantly. [2:35]
- Once you understand trooper interactions, the system can be abused: an enemy in a green or yellow light can feel falsely secure, so a quick surprise attack can catch them. [3:05]
- Do not overthink it; Deadlock games are usually won by good fundamentals, not flashy unexpected plays. [3:05]
- Positioning is often forgotten; both MOBA and shooter positioning apply. [3:05]
- MOBA side: do not stack up so one ability hits you and your teammate or you and your troopers (for example a Kelvin grenade); make the enemy choose between hitting one of you or the troopers, which makes it harder for them to push the wave to your tower. [3:35]
- Shooter side: use cover and play to your weapon's strengths; Abrams is a shotgun hero and should play around tight corners and abuse the veils in the lanes. [3:35]
- Heroes like Haze or Vindicta heavily benefit from open sidelines and will often look to secure the bridge to pressure their opponents. [4:05]
- Entering a lane, think of your weapon's effective range and your opponent's; as Haze into Pocket, Abrams, Shiv or any shotgun hero, avoid contesting at close range, abuse the bridge and stay away from the veils. [4:05]
- Like a pistol not swinging an AWP angle in Counter-Strike, or Widowmaker versus Pharah in Overwatch, you need to know matchups and how effective your gun is at each range. [4:37]
- Stamina management matters, especially in a lane where you may need a quick escape from certain abilities. [4:37]
- Against Warden, avoid getting low on stamina when he has cage and flask; buying Extra Stamina is a great tip against those heroes. [5:07]
- To analyse a lane, look at the enemy heroes' main ability threats and base stats, mainly HP, regen and gun DPS. [5:07]
- The most important thing is to form a theory of how you want to approach the lane, then apply it. [5:37]
- Heroes with long cooldowns, like Abrams, have large openings where an offensive trade may be beneficial for you. [5:37]
- Pocket starts with negative spirit resist, so he is naturally vulnerable to spirit damage and you should focus your abilities on him. [5:37]
- Think about which abilities an enemy needs to use before you can fight them; in a good matchup you might be able to fight them all the time. [6:08]
- The goal is to train your subconscious to automate the criteria for a positive trade; mindless thousands of games build the skill too, but focused practice is much faster. [6:08]
- If you do not know what you are doing in itemization, following a build is your best bet. [7:09]
- Play to your items' strengths: if you buy Extra Regen but are constantly full health, fight more; if you bought Monster Rounds but only shoot heroes, you should have bought something else; with Headshot Booster or Restorative Shot, use the effect the moment it is off cooldown. [7:09]
- Ask yourself what an item is good at, then do more of that. [7:39]
- Hero types are a rough guide, not a list, since heroes get rebalanced; some heroes fit none or several. [7:39]
- Sustain heroes (most clearly Abrams and Mo & Krill) have natural sustain in their kit, so they can take what looks like a negative trade, such as dealing 100 and taking 150, because they heal more; they benefit from wars of attrition and want to trade constantly unless the engagement is very unfavourable. [8:09]
- Burst heroes (such as Pocket or Lash) output large burst through abilities but do not want prolonged engagements; they are usually strong in lane because most engagements are short, and want short fights when abilities are up and slightly more passive play on cooldown. [8:39]
- Poke heroes (most iconic: Haze or Mirage) have a build-up mechanic that increases damage as they keep shooting you; they benefit from long engagements and want to constantly shoot the enemy heroes. [8:39]
- Mina could be considered both a burst and a sustain hero. [9:09]
- Think about what type of hero you are against and what your own hero benefits from, and adapt; the types are a guardrail, not a Bible. [9:09]
- In an advantage state the wave is usually pushed at their tower, and you have four options: kill them by poking or diving, deny their souls, steal their resources (small camp, boxes), or gank another lane. [9:39]
- At lower ranks with good aim, denies are great; feel out how much the enemy lets you deny, but as you rank up denies get very difficult, so shift to the other options. [10:10]
- If you can ever kill your opponent, that should be your default option. [10:10]
- A very common mistake is both sides trying to kill each other for 10 minutes without significant progress. [10:10]
- If you cannot kill your opponent by poking or diving, steal some of their resources after clearing the wave, or gank another lane if your hero is good at it. [10:40]
- Stealing boxes or a camp can give a few hundred soul lead, which gets your timings quicker; getting your ultimate one wave before your opponent can give a huge chance to kill them and grow the lead. [10:40]
- Any action is better than no action; if unsure in an advantage state, try any of the options and see what happens. [10:40]
- In a disadvantage state (wave constantly at your tower, under pressure), the priority is to avoid feeding; one or two deaths in lane is not a big deal, but three or four makes it hard to come back because of the soul disparity. [11:11]
- When behind, focus on preventing the enemy's options: secure your own boxes and small camp the moment they spawn so they cannot steal them, and if your opponent goes to gank, one of you should match the rotation so the nearby lane does not lose instantly. [11:41]
- To avoid dying a lot, consider defensive items that let you regen or kill the wave quickly, such as Monster Rounds, Extra Regen or Healing Rite. [11:41]
- Do not be afraid to go back to base, especially right after the wave died; losing one to two troopers worth of souls is fine as long as you come back with full health. [11:41]
- Resetting is especially cheap past six minutes because you can use the zipline speed boost to come back very quickly. [12:11]
- Towers (guardians) have resistances that are reduced as the game progresses; on the current patch your tower will pretty much never die before four minutes, unless you are against very specific heroes like McGinnis and losing lane really hard. [12:11]
- So before four minutes you can do boxes, gank or whatever you like and let the wave crash into your tower if you are in a neutral or losing lane. [12:41]
- Around eight to 10 minutes the tower dies very quickly, and you can kill one even without a wave there. [12:41]
- At two minutes the boxes and small camps spawn, one of the first chances to build a lead. [12:41]
- At objective timings, steal the enemy's resources if you are winning and secure yours if you are losing. [13:13]
- Your biggest lane spikes are usually ability related, which are more universal than item spikes. [13:13]
- Most heroes rush two points in one ability, which takes three ability points (AP), acquired at 2,800 souls currently; this is one of the main lane spikes. [13:13]
- If you hit the 2,800 spike before your opponents you can usually take advantage of it for one wave; if they hit it first, play more passively until you get it; the same goes for the ultimate unlock at 3,600 souls. [13:43]
- At five minutes the buffs spawn and it is an opportunity to fight; if you are strong, go to the buffs right away, and at low rank just going as they spawn almost guarantees you get them, a significant advantage. [13:43]
- At six minutes the medium camps spawn; do them between waves, and if you cannot kill your opponents or take their tower, take one of their camps instead, or failing that your own. [14:14]
- Eight minutes is arguably one of the most important early timings: the Sinner's Sacrifices (slot machines, vaults) spawn, worth around 350 souls or about one wave by rough math, plus bonus stats for the last hit at the correct timing; secure them as fast as possible, stealing the enemy's if ahead and securing your own if not. [14:14]
- At 10 minutes the buffs spawn again, the Urn appears along with mid boss, and the laning stage officially ends. [15:15]
