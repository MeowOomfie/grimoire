# Jungling is LOSING you games - A response to @Your_Deadlock
channel: Deathy
date: 2024-10-08
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=07y2WnAJKNY

## Covers
A 2024 replay review (the game has changed a lot since) responding to a jungle-pathing video, using that creator's Seven game. Argues lane waves beat jungle camps, steal the enemy jungle when ahead, take the bridge buffs, and use a lead to pressure objectives instead of farming more. For players who over-jungle after winning lane.

## Claims
- Seven up 600-700 souls with the enemy Lash's tower down at 7 minutes is right to start prioritizing the jungle, since with a maxed ball build he has little ganking potential. [1:30]
- Before going to a jungle camp, push the wave one more time if you do not need to fear your laner. [1:30] [2:01]
- When jungling, avoid missing creeps as much as possible; in the example he missed about 8 or 9 creeps, roughly a whole wave or two. [2:01] [2:33]
- At that point of the game a creep is worth around 85 souls, so missing 8 or 9 creeps costs about 700 to 800 souls, likely as much as he got from the jungle, leaving the net worth gap unchanged. [2:33] [3:03]
- Push the lanes properly before jungling to get both the lane value and the jungle value. [3:03]
- Try to come back to your Guardian, but more importantly almost never prioritize jungling over farming creep waves, because waves are worth more than camps on average. [3:34]
- Farming one camp while your laner has pushed the wave, then returning to tower without missing creeps, is a fine play. [3:34] [4:04]
- There is no dedicated jungle role in Deadlock; everyone should farm the camps near them when convenient. [4:34]
- At 10 minutes the most important thing is the bridge buffs, and the second most important when you are ahead is stealing the enemy jungle. [5:34]
- If a teammate is closer to the bridge buff, let them take it and steal two enemy camps yourself (for example the church camp and the one next to it). [5:34]
- Stealing camps from the enemy side gets you further ahead: when ahead, always prioritize farming their jungle over yours if you can. [6:05]
- If you are near the bridge buff when it spawns, there is no reason not to take it; the buffs are about as strong as a 3,000 soul item. [6:05] [6:36]
- The movement bridge buff gives a lot of stamina and is about as good as Superior Stamina, huge for farming. [6:36] [7:06]
- Better players take the buffs as soon as they spawn and take your jungle if you leave it. [7:06]
- Know how much stronger you are: a 13k Seven should turn and kill a 0/3 Abrams with 6k net worth and no items instead of running. [8:06] [8:36]
- Holding 6k unspent souls is a big deal because dying drops a big bag; the Abrams picked it up plus comeback souls and went from 6k to 10k. [9:07]
- When 3 to 5k ahead, buy items and use the lead to pressure the map instead of just farming more. [9:07] [9:37]
- Saving 6,200 souls this early is greedy and limits your impact on the map. [10:08]
- On Seven, items like Tesla Bullets or Mystic Vulnerability would have helped him farm camps much faster than saving. [10:08]
- Magic Carpet gives longer ball and Static Charge duration and is a get-out-of-jail item for split pushing Walkers with an escape, but it does not help you win the game. [10:39]
- Winning means getting more souls than your opponent; if both of you just jungle neither wins, so pressure waves to make space for your jungle and limit theirs, and steal their jungle. [11:09]
- Farm your own jungle when there is nothing better to do and you cannot have impact on the map right now. [11:09] [11:40]
- Heroes like Seven farm the jungle much better than others, so they should be a little more willing to go there. [11:40]
- In the example game the team led 82k to 69k, mostly on him, but the souls were unspent and no pressure was put on the map, so the enemy came back. [12:10]
- Over 30 to 40 viewer reviews, the most common mistake he sees is jungling and not applying pressure afterwards. [12:10] [12:40]
- Objectives beat jungling: taking a tower, defending a tower you can actually defend, or taking the bridge buffs is usually better than jungling. [12:40]
- Jungle when the waves are decently pushed and there is no pressure for you to apply on the map. [12:40] [13:10]
- Stealing the enemy jungle is the right mindset; it is safer with an escape like Magic Carpet. [13:40]
- Jungle rotations ignore that teammates also want those camps; a slow jungler like Mirage should not run the same rotations as Seven, one of the fastest junglers, because he gives up too much map. [14:12] [14:42]
- You win by applying pressure on objectives, not by taking the jungle; heroes with 30 to 50k souls who keep farming are useless to the team. [15:12]
- Take part of the jungle and use your slight lead to apply more pressure, rather than absorbing the entire jungle. [15:43]
