# The Most Misunderstood Mechanic in Deadlock
channel: Metro Deadlock
date: 2025-11-16
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=foN-aAbeO6E

## Covers
Ziplines as the map-control tool: drawing a virtual control line at the end of your pushed zipline, why pushed lanes decide Urn fights, safe jungle invades, Walkers as a natural wave barrier, the Mid Walker as the hardest objective, spawn-room rotation choices, Rejuvenator follow-up and the zipline jump-dash escape.
Told through a replay of a game the coach (playing Wraith) came back from while far behind. For players who want better macro and map awareness.

## Claims
- Ziplines dictate the map state in Deadlock; use where your zipline ends to decide what is safe to farm, where it is safe to fight and how to fight, and win games by pushing lanes to extend your zipline. [1:00]
- At the end of an extended zipline you can escape by jumping on the zipline and doing a dash off it, and your teammates can reach that point of the map fast too. [2:31]
- Draw a virtual map-control line across the end of your team's pushed zipline: the space up to it is yours, the rest belongs to the enemy, and it changes every second as zipline lanes get pushed. [3:02]
- Pushing out lanes should be your number one priority whether you are a carry or a support, before looking for team fights, farming or stealing enemy jungle, because it gives you ways in and out and makes you more likely to survive and win team fights. [3:33]
- The longer the Urn is held, the more players on both teams rotate toward it, since it gives a lot of money. [4:03]
- If your lane near the Urn drop is pushed deep into the enemy side, the enemy carrier cannot simply walk the Urn in; your teammates can zipline from their Walker to the drop much faster than enemies on the far side of the map. [4:34]
- An enemy using the forward teleport at the secret shop to reach a fight lands on your side of the map, which is very risky for them and easy to catch. [5:34]
- With your zipline pushed far into enemy territory, it is safe for your team to occupy the Urn drop space even with no information on where the enemies are. [6:39]
- In the early game expect the enemy to try to deposit the Urn; teams usually only hold it to deny a steal in an even Urn fight when one team is far behind. [7:10]
- A team behind in souls can still have good map control if the richer enemy fails to push the creep waves; creep waves provide map control. [7:40]
- With map control you can farm enemy jungle camps with less risk of being punished, which takes resources away from the enemy; if they come, teleport or get to the zipline and escape, or let your team rotate in over the zipline if the fight is winnable. [8:10]
- When the lane is pushed far into enemy territory you can put aggression on the enemy fairly safely and end up up in numbers, because enemies elsewhere cannot rotate in fast. [9:40]
- When the enemy Urn carrier rotates to deposit, your top priority is pushing out the wave nearest the drop and forcing defenders off it. [10:41]
- Trading one for one while forcing three enemies to rotate can still win the exchange: the Urn despawned undelivered and the soul gap went to about even. [11:12]
- Every pushed creep wave gives souls, and in the early game normal creep waves are worth more than the jungle. [11:43]
- The further your creep wave is toward the enemy side, the more map control your team has to play the game. [11:43]
- When you see enemies ganking you at the end of your zipline, teleport or zipline out; enemies are reluctant to chase you inside the protection of your zipline. [12:14]
- Every meter an enemy steps past their own zipline risks your team arriving and turning the fight around. [12:44]
- After the lane phase most Guardians die around the 14 to 15 minute mark, though in very one-sided games some stay up a long time. [12:44]
- A standing Walker is a natural line of defense, like a dam: an enemy creep wave, even eight or more creeps, has to stop and shoot it, and if your team plays the macro correctly someone comes and shoves the wave back. [13:16]
- Once a team's Walker dies there is no natural barrier, so an uncontrolled creep wave walks all the way to the base Guardians; the wave equilibrium shifts toward the side that lost its Walker. [14:19]
- If your lane is lost and the enemy Guardian is still up, a wave you push out comes back fast because the enemy creeps overwhelm yours at their Guardian. [14:50]
- Every minute spent up a Walker or tower gives more chances to steal enemy jungle and get kills on the enemy side, while the enemy has little free farm on your side. [15:20]
- The Mid Walker is your team's bastion: it gives a big area of map control so you can safely secure your own jungle, your own double Sinner's Sacrifice and the middle of the map; losing it leaves a big dent. [15:51]
- When the enemy is ahead and controlling the map, the first instinct should be to push the wave back to regain map control rather than farm your available jungle. [16:52]
- With wave control, making an aggressive Sinner's Sacrifice steal is fine even with an enemy nearby: if they contest you, your creeps get more time to push and damage the Walker. [16:52]
- Pulling an enemy into a fight away from his wave lets your double creep wave crash into his tower unanswered, killing creeps before he is in range and buying your team map time. [17:54]
- Pulling two enemies to one side of the map leaves them stuck pushing their wave back or farming their own jungle, which does not put them further ahead, since they cannot steal your jungle. [18:54]
- The Mid Walker is the hardest objective in the game to take, harder than base Guardians and the Patron, because it gives resistances to everyone around it and has only one narrow, risky angle to shoot it (an off angle exists past a veil). [19:57]
- In many high Eternus games the Mid Walker stays up almost until the very late game, while side Walkers have less HP, are easier to shoot and fall eventually. [20:28]
- Pushing the Mid Walker while the enemy team is free to defend their territory is a bad play; sending four or five enemies to kill one player there is also poor time investment for them. [20:58]
- Every time you leave spawn, ask where you should go and why; choosing which side of the map and which zipline to take is an essential decision. [21:59]
- Walkers get more resists the more people push them, so three players shooting it make it take less damage than two. [22:30]
- The more players stacked together, the less money each gets from kills, lane creeps and jungle, so death-balling as six is inefficient; in high-level mid games (18 minutes counts as mid game) players move in smaller packs and only group as six for the final late-game fight. [23:02]
- The Mid Walker is the easiest place on the map to reach from anywhere, so expect at least one or two enemies to defend it; pushing it as three into two defenders is likely to get you killed. [23:32]
- On spawn, do not rotate to a Walker that is already lost (low HP, enemy wave in range with no backdoor protection before you arrive); go to the lane where the situation is still salvageable. [24:35]
- As soon as there is nothing else to do, shove the creep wave and pressure the enemy Walker; extending your map control is the number one essential thing. [25:37]
- If the nearby options are not worth it (no good camp, another lane already decided), stay and support the teammates you can actually help in their fight. [26:08]
- When the enemy pushes into your base around 19 minutes, they usually back out after taking the base Guardians; there is no way they kill both Shrines and the Patron then, because your dead teammates respawn and others rotate back. [26:38]
- After an enemy deposits the Urn they usually back out; do not teleport in first and chase alone. [28:12]
- When you are behind in money, even trading three for two in a fight is good for the team that is behind. [28:42]
- Good rule of thumb: from the 18 to 20 minute mark, depending on skill level, you have enough damage to kill the Mid Boss quickly, and the enemy respawn time is long. [29:43]
- Enemy respawn times get longer the longer the game goes. [29:43]
- An enemy overcommitting to kill your Mid Walker can cost them a big net worth swing if you win the fight elsewhere. [30:14]
- Rejuvenator gives massive map control: a 6v6 becomes effectively 6v9 with three extra lives, so the enemy is scared to fight and you can take the enemy side jungle and play aggressively. [30:14]
- A common big mistake after Rejuvenator is rushing in to force a fight in the enemy base or on their Walker, where the defensive advantage can offset the Rejuvenator. [32:16]
- Almost always the correct play after Rejuvenator is taking map control, taking farm, and making sure the enemy does not get as much farm as you; the team with it just gets further ahead the longer they have it. [32:16]
- When the enemy is ahead in money, avoid bigger team fights since they are hard to win; play more aggressive once you have Rejuvenator lives. [31:15]
- Coming late to a fight to farm Sinner's Sacrifices for your next power spike is acceptable when your team is healthy and still has a Rejuvenator life. [32:47]
- With map control, a low teammate in a base push can go back to base, heal and return, so you can keep pushing or staying around the enemy base while enemies are down. [33:50]
- Think of ziplines as lifelines to your team and, more often, as map control: the further your ziplines extend into enemy territory, the safer you can invade enemy jungle for kills and farm. [34:20]
- Number one rule: never walk past your zipline, even for a kill; where your zipline ends the enemy zipline starts, so the enemy can retreat, send reinforcements, or zipline home to heal and return, turning an even fight into a 4v2. [34:20]
- Play inside the radius of your ziplines and make full use of them. [35:20]
- Zipline escape tech: at the end of your zipline when enemies come and you are not being shot, hold the attach button (space bar), then immediately crouch and dash off, ideally to the side for more speed; it costs one stamina and sends you very far. [35:20]
- If you have no dashes left you can still attach to the zipline and crouch off to get around a corner. [35:52]
- Done correctly you dash off the zipline almost before connecting, so there is very little chance of being shot off it. [36:24]
- Always push out lanes, because a pushed lane lets you go into the enemy jungle and steal enemy Sinner's Sacrifices for more value and map control. [36:24]
- An all-vitality (green) tanky Wraith build that ignores fighting, shoves waves and farms Sinner's Sacrifices is working very well in Eternus games; constantly shoved lanes give your team more map control and comebacks in the long run. [36:55]
- Creep waves are the best way to get money other than Sinner's Sacrifices, and cleared fast they give even more; think of killing lane creeps as map control that leads to enemy jungle camps, enemy Sinner's Sacrifices and kills. [37:25]
- If your Walkers are still standing, defending them is top priority, because from the moment one dies the enemy has more map control on that side for the rest of the game until you kill their Walker. [37:56]
- Enemies invading your jungle far from their ziplines take longer to arrive, which gives you the advantage. [38:26]
- The zipline is the best movement tech in the game; only vents come close, and vents are more for escaping. [38:26]
- Passive teams lose: shoving a wave, damaging a Walker and forcing four enemies to rotate is more valuable than sitting on your own Walker safely farming your own jungle. [39:26]
- Loser camps are the camps right next to your base; if you are behind and your first instinct is to farm them, the enemy farms the rest of the map and gets further ahead. [39:56]
- When behind, push out the wave first to get map control, then farm the jungle. [40:27]
