## Covers
An Eternus 6 aiming guide in three parts: sensitivity and settings (DPI, polling rate, cm per 360, crosshair, graphics), in-game aim tips (warm-up, aiming with movement, fixed-distance enemy movement, reading enemies), and aim training (Aim Lab, KovaaK's, Voltaic).
For players who feel held back by mouse control.

## Claims
- You don't need crazy good aim to reach a high rank; solid aim is enough unless you want to reach the top only playing M1 carries like Haze, Wraith, Infernus or Mirage. [0:00]
- In-game sensitivity depends on the Deadlock sensitivity slider and your mouse DPI; find your DPI in the mouse's manufacturer details or its software. [0:30]
- The most common DPIs pro players use are 400, 800 and 1,600; 1,600 or higher is technically better, but 800 or 1,600 is totally fine and it realistically doesn't matter. [1:01]
- DPI also changes your Windows and in-game shop/menu cursor speed; if that is uncomfortable, change the pointer speed in Windows mouse settings. [1:01]
- Set your mouse polling rate as high as possible, usually 500 Hz or 1 kHz, for responsiveness; 2K, 4K or 8 kHz are unlikely to feel different and could even cause lag in games. [1:31]
- Use a mouse sensitivity converter (convert from Deadlock, show advanced, input sensitivity and DPI) to get your cm per 360, the universal measure of sensitivity across games (the coach uses 0.32 at 1,600 DPI). [2:02]
- Shooter pros usually use somewhere between 20 and 50 or 60 cm per 360; it is the sweet spot for speed and precision and has you aim with arm, wrist and fingers. [2:33]
- If you currently use a higher sensitivity (lower cm per 360), moving into that range takes time to learn arm aiming and may need a bigger mouse pad, but likely improves your aim long term and lowers wrist injury risk. [3:03]
- Short-range shotgun heroes or movement heroes that dash around enemies may favor a higher sensitivity (lower cm per 360); longer range heroes may benefit from lower sensitivity (higher cm per 360). You can use a different sensitivity per hero. [3:03]
- It is a myth that you must find one perfect sensitivity forever; anyone with solid mouse control can adapt to a change within a reasonable range in under 30 minutes. [3:33]
- If you are struggling, constantly changing sensitivity, especially mid-game, is not the fix; the underlying issue is a lack of mouse control. [4:03]
- Use a small, static crosshair with high contrast colors; there are Deadlock preset crosshair sites, and Crosshair X (a Steam app) overlays a custom crosshair on any game. [4:03]
- Set graphics settings to low: less visual clutter, more FPS and less mouse input lag. [4:33]
- Do a short warm-up before or during queue: in the firing range shoot orbs, track the bots, dash and slide while keeping your crosshair on the dummy's head, duel a friend, or use an aim trainer. [5:04]
- Use your movement to aim, not just your mouse: strafe to bring the crosshair onto the target, which helps prevent overflicking at longer range. [5:04]
- If you are not at risk of taking much damage, limit your movement or stand still to increase accuracy; sliding around when no one is shooting you only makes hitting harder. [5:04]
- Know enemy movement: dashes, jumps and wall jumps lock players into set animations and trajectories. [5:34]
- Shiv's Slice and Dice is a fixed distance, so anticipate it and adjust; Mirage's tornado and Calico's Leaping Slash are also fixed distances. [6:05]
- Vindicta's Flight launch straight up is hard to track and may not be worth bullets, but once she is in the air her movement is limited and slow and easy to track. [6:05]
- What goes up must come down: jumping and wall-jumping players, Lash using his grapple and Holliday using her Bounce Pad follow a predictable trajectory when falling. [6:05]
- Read your enemies: people tend to run to cover in a straight line, which makes them easy to track. [6:36]
- Look at your enemy, not your crosshair, so you can read their movement; some people switch to watching the crosshair at long range, so try that too. [6:36]
- Aim training quickly improves mouse control with diminishing returns: the first 50 to 100 hours give the most value; once you are decent, playing the game may be a better use of time. [7:06]
- Aim Lab is free, has a ranked system, lots of content and is more gamified; KovaaK's costs about 8 dollars on Steam, has no built-in rank, and is more customizable and responsive (the coach's preference). Either works. [7:37]
- Find a good routine and run it as often as your schedule allows; even a few hours a week or 15 minutes every other day likely brings improvement, but repeating the same easy scenario won't. [8:08]
- Use the Voltaic benchmarks and routines (playlists for Aim Lab and KovaaK's) to get a rank per aiming category, and their weakness-oriented playlists to fix specific weaknesses like tracking. [8:08]
