# I'll Fix Your Deadlock Macro in 8 Minutes
channel: Natan  date: 2026-10-17  url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xwclv0agS3A

## Covers
Macro: push the nearest wave, objective timers cheat sheet (sinners, bridge boss, Urn, Rift, Mid Boss), cross map plays, side laners vs mid wave roamers, farm vs fight around power spikes, minimap awareness, not over-grouping, staying level with teammates, late game Mid Boss; heroes Wraith, Mirage, Paige, Mina, Holliday, Venator

## Claims
- The first step is always to push the nearest wave; if all teammates are in blue and green, there is a wave you should push in yellow. [0:00]
- Troopers are always the highest source of souls, and pushing a wave creates map pressure: enemies must respond or lose structure health and souls. [0:00]
- If unsure what to do, look at the map and push a nearby wave; don't just passively clear waves crashing into your walkers, actively push them past the midpoint of the map. [0:31]
- The game is played around timers and objectives; there is almost always another objective coming up. [0:31]
- Timers: sinners (captioned "sentinels") at 8 minutes; bridge boss and the first Urn 2 minutes later; the first Rift a minute or two after that; then sinners respawn depending on how fast they were cleared; next bridge boss and Urn at 15 minutes; second Rift at around 18-20 minutes. [0:31]
- Around 18-20 minutes death timers ramp past 30-40 seconds, so taking Mid Boss becomes a very viable option. [1:02]
- Prepare for each objective spawn: farm up to your next power spikes if needed so you're as strong as possible, then push your waves and move to the objective. [1:32]
- When an objective is in play, get active on the map. If the enemy is delivering the Urn, contest them or use the knowledge that one or two or more enemies are at the deposit to make a cross map play. [1:32]
- A cross map play is using the information that the enemy is taking an objective on one side to make a play elsewhere; if you won't contest the Rift, get a walker, a kill, or at worst a bunch of enemy jungle camps. Don't sit AFK in the jungle. [2:02]
- Current meta: two players sit in the side lanes taking solo waves and jungle between waves, while the other four play for the mid wave, roam for kills and invade or secure jungle. It is not a hard rule; sometimes it is one side laner and five managing the other two lanes. [2:32]
- Decide your game plan by asking whether you are a side laner. Side laner qualities: scales well and is soul hungry, good duelist, objective damage, takes jungle well, good escape, fast rotations. [3:02]
- Wraith is the ultimate side laner: scales, soul hungry, good duelist, one shots walkers if left alone with an objective, escapes with her TP, and rotates fast with passive sprint speed and TP. [3:02]
- Mirage doesn't take jungle well and isn't soul hungry, but is a very oppressive duelist who can push a side lane far; objective damage isn't great, but his tornado survives ganks and his ult gives unmatched rotations. [3:33]
- Paige is a horrible side laner: she doesn't need souls, spikes very early, isn't a strong duelist, lacks big objective damage, and has no good escape or fast rotation. Still, if a side wave is unpushed, even Paige can be responsible for pushing it. [3:33]
- Side laner 30 second loop: shove the side wave as far as you safely can, then take sinners, camps, boxes, anything giving souls between waves; steal from their side if possible, securing your own is fine. [4:04]
- As a side laner still scaling, you often don't care about being at the first Rift; use the information that many enemies are there to look for a kill and/or a walker on the other side. [4:34]
- Side laners usually only rotate to fights already near them; how often depends on the hero. Wraith likes to scale and may only join fights with ult up and cards ready, while Mina, also a side laner, relies less on long cooldowns and spikes earlier, so she can be much more aggressive mid game. [4:34]
- Non side laners play for the mid wave, push it if they can, and sometimes fight over priority of who pushes it. [5:04]
- Recognize opportunities by gathering information: the map, health bars, ultimates, items. Example: as Holliday with mid wave pushed and ult almost ready, go kill an enemy Venator overextending in a side lane. [5:34]
- Whenever you aren't directly in a fight, keep your eyes on the minimap and the top bar. [5:34]
- Avoid grouping four people on every mid wave, because troopers and kills give much less souls when shared; rotate who pushes the mid wave while others take sinners, invade or rotate to a side lane. [6:05]
- When an objective is in play, don't AFK farm in the jungle. Between objectives, if you're close to a power spike (item or ability upgrade), farm safely on your side of the map where there is a lot of farm. [6:35]
- After reaching the spike, be aggressive: push up on the enemy side to steal jungle or kill players farming; then farm again toward the next spike and repeat the cycle. [7:06]
- Always actively push waves as far as you can; that is genuinely the number one tip, and you are almost certainly not pushing waves actively enough. [7:06]
- Try to be on roughly the same horizontal line on the map as your teammates, which creates a lot more pressure. If teammates draw attention somewhere, move up with them and use that pressure. [7:37]
- If your whole team is in base, don't go make noise; you'll get collapsed on and teammates can't punish elsewhere. [7:37]
- Late game, once some walkers are down, Mid Boss is by far the most important objective: whoever can safely do so pushes out side waves, then the team groups up and gets kills to go Mid Boss. [8:07]
- If Mid Boss isn't up, keep pushing waves, play for power spikes, look for kills when strong, and play for the next objective: bridge boss, Urn, Rift, Mid Boss. [8:07]
