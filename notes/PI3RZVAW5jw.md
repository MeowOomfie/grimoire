# The Ultimate Guide To Deadlock Laning - Always Win Your Lane
channel: Metro Deadlock  date: 2025-09-08  url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PI3RZVAW5jw

## Covers
Laning basics: push the wave first, cover, guardian aggro, securing souls vs denying, HP as a resource and base regen (Warden, Abrams, Seven), guardian parry dives, healing creep, staying in lane, Extra Regen vs Healing Rite vs Restorative Shot, dying to the guardian on purpose, strong and weak laners (Wraith, Yamato, Sinclair, Holiday, Mina, Mo & Krill, Viscous), Monster Rounds, Headshot Booster, Backstabber, Infernus builds (Rapid Rounds), Kelvin (Close Quarters, Extra Charge), aggressive over defensive items

## Claims
- In Deadlock you want to push the creep wave first; many players focus too much on harassing the enemy and on denying. [0:00]
- The first thing to do at the start of laning is walk up to the enemy creeps and shoot them, and secure the soul, which is your main income source in lane. [0:32]
- Shooting the enemy players first is a mistake if you play correctly; keeping the wave pushed toward the enemy while securing your souls gives you a creep wave advantage. [1:03]
- A creep wave advantage means you have more creeps toward the enemy tower than they have on you, and it lets you start damaging the enemy tower. [1:33]
- Unlike in other MOBAs, Deadlock guardians only aggro what is right in front of them and pick the closest target, so if you hide behind your pushing creeps you can shoot enemy players without the guardian aggroing you. [1:33]
- In 99% of cases pushing the creep wave is most important. [2:04]
- Cover is essential: stand where you can hit enemy troopers and their souls while the enemy players cannot hit you; every lane (left, right and middle) has enough cover for this. [2:04]
- Once your wave ends up at the enemy tower you can start harassing the enemy players, and they cannot trade favorably because they still have to deal with the creeps. [2:35]
- On a hero that is great at harassing, such as Mirage or Haze with a buildup effect, you can leave the pushing to your teammate and sit on high ground in the side lanes harassing early, but only if your teammate follows up. [3:06]
- Figure out early whether your lane partner is competent; if not, focus on your own job of pushing the wave first. [3:06]
- The more you push waves without taking too much damage, the lower the enemy guardian gets and the more likely you win the lane. [3:37]
- Kills on enemy heroes are not that essential early; a kill at level one is worth less than an entire creep wave, so killing four creeps earns more than a kill without the creeps. [3:37]
- A big beginner mistake is not last hitting your own souls; 50% of the money from creeps comes from the soul secure, the rest from the last hit on the creep. [4:07]
- The player who shot the creep has an advantage on its soul, so securing your own (teal or green) souls should make them very hard for the enemy to deny. [4:38]
- Do not spend much time trying to deny enemy souls: if the enemy plays correctly they have the first-hit advantage on the red souls from your creeps, so you will almost never deny them. [4:38]
- Players who deny like in Dota end up neglecting the rest of the wave; waiting mid-wave for a creep to go low and then failing the deny leaves you with nothing while the enemy pushes your wave. [5:09]
- Focus on securing your own souls and on killing creeps, not on denying. [5:39]
- You can start denying when the enemy is under pressure, meaning your wave is crashing into their tower: they hide behind the tower trying to stay alive and secure last hits, so they miss deny timings. [6:09]
- Getting damage on enemy players is easier while you are pushing their walker or guardian, because creeps in front of you mean you take less damage, unless the enemy completely oversteps. [6:39]
- Trading HP is essential; every hero has base regen and it differs by hero, with Warden and Abrams very high (Warden about 3 HP a second, Abrams even higher with Infernal Resilience leveled). [6:39]
- Abrams has one of the highest base regens in the game, especially with extra regen items. [7:09]
- Treat your HP bar as a resource meter; being full HP and scared to lose health while your lanemate is low is the wrong way to play. [7:40]
- Trading even poke fights at high HP is good when you out-regen the enemy, for example full HP Abrams against a 600 HP Seven, whose base regen is only about 1 a second. [8:10]
- At around 75% HP or more you can trade easily without being in danger of dying immediately; dying is bad because it gives the enemy gold. [8:40]
- You can also spend HP when pushing a guardian: tank the tower for a second and get a parry off so your creeps stay alive longer and damage the tower or a player. [8:40]
- To dive the tower once your wave is on it, jump in and get a parry on the guardian; it stops shooting for a second, giving you time to damage the tower, clear the next wave or dive and kill an enemy player. [9:12]
- Two ways to negate guardian damage: parry its melee so it does nothing for a bit, or stay behind your creeps, since it only shoots you when you are ahead of them. [9:42]
- Every second in lane at full HP is wasted regen; pressuring the lane is the number one thing to think about. [10:13]
- Taking damage from both enemies is better than your lane partner dying or leaving lane to heal, because going back to base loses multiple creep waves of money and lets the enemy pressure your guardian. [10:13]
- From around five to six minutes guardians die very easily, and enemies with a full wave of four creeps can kill your tower very fast. [10:43]
- The healing creep, marked by a flag on its head, heals you and your teammate for 40 HP every 6 seconds while you are next to it, which in the early game is more healing than the entire Extra Regen item. [11:13]
- Keep your own healing creep alive as long as possible and kill the enemy healing trooper first, even when you are low, since it heals them every 6 seconds it lives; he got three heals (120 HP) from one wave in the example. [11:43]
- With your healing creep alive and theirs dead you can trade easily and even parry the guardian and take some damage. [12:14]
- The zipline speed boost does not come into effect until about 6 minutes, so going back to base before then loses at least a few creeps if not a full wave, more than 400 souls or about half an early item; you almost never want to go back. [12:44]
- The three meta items for staying in lane when low are Healing Rite, Extra Regen and Restorative Shot. [12:44]
- Extra Regen is great on tanks that already have high base regen, because it heals over time while you keep pushing and fighting. [13:15]
- Healing Rite heals more than Extra Regen but must be activated, and one point of damage from an enemy player cancels it, so stay in cover and play passive for about a wave and a half while it runs. [13:15]
- Restorative Shot heals 50 when your bullet hits an enemy player, usable every 6 seconds, and does nothing if you miss; hitting it every 6 seconds is the highest early healing, so take it only if you trust your aim and watch its cooldown. [14:17]
- Restorative Shot's 15 healing from NPCs is weaker than both Extra Regen and Healing Rite even if you shoot a creep every 6 seconds. [14:48]
- If you win a fight but are very low, you can die to the enemy guardian on purpose: you respawn quickly at full HP and the enemy gets no souls. [15:50]
- If an enemy player damaged you in the last 3 seconds or so before the guardian kills you, it counts as their kill, so wait a few seconds after the fight before doing it. [16:20]
- The guardian-death respawn was nerfed in a recent patch, but at around seven seconds early on it is still faster than going back by zipline, healing and coming back. [16:20]
- Lane matchups are near infinite with two heroes per side, so meeting the same matchup is rare, and the lane may call for more passive or more aggressive play. [16:50]
- Wraith is one of the weakest laners, if not the weakest, doing very little damage before her cards reach level two, traded off for a strong late game as a hard carry. [17:21]
- Yamato is insane in lane in almost all matchups: her right click deals big AoE damage from long range, Crimson Slash regens HP if enemies come close, and she has a nuke. [17:51]
- Strong laners include Yamato and Sinclair (high burst and damage over time, Yamato with sustain), Holiday (pressure with Crackshot and barrels), Mina (recently buffed, big damage with her 3), Mo & Krill (use Scorn on creeps to regen) and Viscous. [18:21]
- Weak laners can still win; your own skill matters most, and learning the matchups is easier if you only play one to three heroes. [19:23]
- In a disadvantaged lane you kill enemy creeps slower than they kill yours, so you deal with one or two creeps reaching your tower at the end of each wave. [19:53]
- Monster Rounds is great if you struggle to push: more damage to creeps and less damage taken from them, including 25% less from the guardian, which helps push and kill it; it suits almost any hero. [20:23]
- Headshot Booster is the highest-DPS early item: bonus damage on a headshot every 8 seconds, and a body shot does not put it on cooldown, so it is easy to use and far more forgiving than Restorative Shot. [21:26]
- Skip Headshot Booster on heroes who struggle to land headshots, such as shotgun heroes like Abrams or Pocket; most of the cast can use it to harass. [21:57]
- Backstabber is one of the most broken, overstated items right now and is bought early on many heroes: health, damage on back hits, lower enemy bullet resist and movement speed. [22:27]
- Backstabber procs when an enemy dashes away from you, even while facing you, and works on every hero; it is especially good when your wave is crashing their tower and they dash or turn to run. [22:57]
- Most heroes' builds focus on either pushing the wave faster or damaging the enemy; weak-damage heroes like Wraith often buy Monster Rounds. [23:58]
- On Infernus, Buds takes the passive route (staying alive and pushing), while Zergy buys Rapid Rounds second because faster fire applies the burn faster, the best damage option over Headhunter; it is personal preference. [24:29]
- Spend your money whenever you have it in lane; early every item, even an 800, is a major power spike, and the enemy who spends first will out-damage you. [24:59]
- Kelvin, a tanky hero with strong early gun damage, goes Close Quarters then Extra Charge for more grenade damage; early items are ones that help immediately in lane. [25:29]
- He disagrees with buying Battle Vest and Enchanter's Emblem as a first or second lane item: it lowers your damage, and Close Quarters or Headshot Booster is a better trade-off. [25:59]
- An aggressive approach is always better than a defensive one, in lane and everywhere in Deadlock; pushing and damaging beats playing passive and trying to deny. [26:30]
- As long as you take cover while shooting creeps you will end up on the enemy tower more and win the lane. [27:00]
