# Aim is Op in Deadlock, Here's How to Master It
channel: Shotty
date: 2026-09-18
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DVMHtOcOSWc

## Covers
Aim fundamentals for Deadlock: tracking versus clicking, common aim mistakes (waiting for aim to improve by playing, aim tension, overflicking, holding the fire button), matching target speed and movement, wrist versus arm aiming, sensitivity ranges in cm per 360, leading targets and waiting for direction changes.
Examples use Venator, Vindicta, Drifter, Mina, Celeste and Holliday. For players who want to hit more shots, especially on precision heroes.

## Claims
- For Deadlock, aim breaks into two categories that matter: tracking (like Venator's primary gun) and clicking (like Venator's ultimate). [0:30]
- Common mistake: assuming your aim will improve just by playing; aim takes technique and conscious effort, and without practice the time to reach even above average aim is disproportionate unless you have about 5,000 hours to put in. [0:30]
- Aim tension (overgripping the mouse, wrist pain from tracking too hard) is fixed mostly by conscious effort: notice it, exhale, grip the mouse lighter, until it becomes second nature; some practice playlists or maps help. [1:30]
- A real match gives few quality gunfights: maybe three good fights in 10 minutes of lane, about six by 20 minutes and about 10 in a whole 30 to 40 minute game, so matches are poor aim practice. [2:00]
- Common mistake: panic flicking or overflicking past a target; always try to under aim and smoothly catch up to the target. [3:02]
- Aiming past a target forces you to correct for the overshoot plus any movement they make, so you end up aiming double or triple the distance you would have under aiming. [3:33]
- Common mistake: holding the shoot button; on Drifter, Mina and sometimes Vindicta, especially heroes with small magazines, it turns the fight into a reload simulator. [3:33]
- Value your shots: aim deliberately before each click, as if every shot were Vindicta's ultimate where a miss lets the target escape. [4:03]
- Wait for an opportunity to shoot, such as the enemy going into an animation you can read, unless you must rush the shot because they are about to escape or jump on someone. [4:03]
- Tracking is the fundamental of all aim, and smoothness comes first: if you cannot draw a smooth straight line with your mouse, you cannot aim well on Vindicta or Venator's ultimate. [4:34]
- Most heroes have a set dash and movement speed, so you should be able to stay on them smoothly with no jitter by matching their speed, like gluing the crosshair to a slow Drifter. [5:04]
- First tracking skill to learn: match the target's speed. Do not track every hero as if they could teleport; follow them as if they will keep moving in one direction, then react when they change direction. [6:04]
- Do not try to predict movement too much; predicting is when players start missing. [6:04]
- Use passive aim: mirror the enemy's movement (strafe the same way they strafe) so your movement keeps the crosshair on them without moving the mouse, which is how experienced players out aim players with better raw aim. [6:34]
- Use both wrist and arm: wrist and fingertips for precision and small motions, arm for large, fast motions such as 180s and 360s. [7:04]
- For a 180 or 360, lift and swipe the mouse across the pad, put it back down in the center and then aim, instead of raising sensitivity to do it in one motion. [7:35]
- Do not go faster than about 25 cm per 360; if you are faster, slow down to the 25 to 30 range, because faster has diminishing returns on aim. [8:05]
- If you are slower than about 50 cm per 360, speed up to 50 or even 40. [8:35]
- Shotty uses about 40 cm per 360 in almost every game; it is partly personal preference. [8:35]
- Fast sensitivity suits players whose wrist and fingertips aim better; slower sensitivity suits players whose arm is quick and precise. [8:35]
- Slow sensitivity hides smoothness problems: at 60 cm per 360 your shake is less visible than at 30 even if it is the same. [9:05]
- cm per 360 means how many centimeters you move the mouse in real life to turn 360 degrees in game. [9:05]
- For clicking a moving target, stay slightly ahead of them instead of directly on them; aiming directly on them makes you react to every move they make. [9:36]
- When someone changes direction they stand still for a moment, so a crosshair just ahead of them still hits; if they do not change direction, the crosshair is already in place. [10:37]
- Aim at the peak of a jump, because at the peak the target cannot change direction or strafe unless they jump again. [10:37]
- Against fast heroes like Celeste, Holliday or anyone with Magic Carpet, do not aim at them raw; always wait for the direction change, or you burn your magazine. [11:07]
- Slower, more predictable heroes such as Drifter or Venator can be aimed at directly or just barely ahead. [11:07]
- Air strafing Celeste players create moments where they barely move on your screen because they are moving toward or away from you, so you can keep the crosshair still to hit them. [11:37]
- Do not flick to shoot; 99.9% of players should use a slow, controlled aim style because it gives fewer chances to overflick or underflick. [12:07]
- Use a flick only as a gap closer (target far away, or they just hit a bounce pad), then micro adjust onto them and shoot. [12:37]
- With Venator's ricochet split shot ultimate, slowing down to hit one ricochet on the enemy team can hit all three squishies for a team wipe. [13:07]
