# The BIGGEST Mistake In Deadlock (Why You Can't Win)
channel: Metro Deadlock
date: 2025-02-01
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nsrlmZU8Lek

## Covers
Five macro mistakes seen at every rank, easiest to hardest: farming the jungle too much instead of lane creeps, playing reactively (on the map and with counter items), playing too safe when behind, playing too risky when ahead, and over-committing to kill one split pusher, plus a bonus on why K/D doesn't matter.
For low to high rank players who lose games despite decent mechanics.

## Claims
- Macro (when to fight, farm, push or play safe) is what wins games; with mediocre aim and ability usage but correct macro you can reach Eternus. [0:30]
- The number one mistake, from Initiate to Eternus, is farming too much jungle, especially your own side; farming lane creeps is always more valuable than farming the jungle. [1:02]
- On average one lane creep is worth about 100 souls and a creep wave about 400, while a tier 2 camp early is worth around 80 x 3 = 240, so taking a tier 2 camp instead of a wave loses you and your team around 160 souls. [1:32]
- The longer the game goes, the more souls you get from everything. [1:32]
- Pushing waves also forces the enemy to react and defend lanes, and if they don't you get more objectives and map space. [2:03]
- After lanes, a solo yellow laner whose Guardians are both gone should go defend a creep wave pushing his Walker: lots of souls plus a protected Walker. [2:03]
- Don't farm a wave a teammate is already on: after 8 minutes creeps are shared and each of you gets only 60% of the souls (current patch). [2:34]
- With a teammate on the wave, go take a camp instead: 240 from a tier 2 camp plus the ally's full 400 from the wave beats both of you splitting it for around 220 each. [3:05]
- TLDR: stop farming your jungle, you will not get ahead doing it. [3:35]
- Someone is expected to farm the jungle: with all four lanes occupied, the two extra players roam, help teammates or farm jungle, which is fine; going camp to camp all game is not. [3:35]
- Pushing out lanes and winning early teamfights nets far more souls and advantage than jungling. [4:05]
- Killing an enemy Walker opens up that whole side of the map (its camps and Sinner's Sacrifices) for you to farm safely, and taking a second Walker grows the area you control even more. [4:05]
- A Walker gives 500 souls plus extra, about 750 in total, to everyone on the team, around 2,500 souls for the team, and forces the enemy to play back and push out those lanes. [4:37]
- Mistake two (mostly low to mid rank): playing reactively instead of actively; think ahead like chess rather than reacting after the enemy acts. [5:08]
- If the enemy is pushing your yellow Walker and reaches the pink area near it, rotating then is usually too late and they take the Walker for free; you have to anticipate earlier. [5:38]
- Letting the enemy push deep on your side gives them more options: push the wave back, then rotate to push Green Lane, gank for a kill or rotate mid. [6:09]
- Reactive counter items are usually wrong: rushing Debuff Remover against a scary Bebop (for example a double bomb Bebop) doesn't help if you have no damage to kill him afterwards. [6:39]
- In the mid game without many damage items, get a few damage items first so you can kill the enemy, rather than rushing a defensive item early. [7:39]
- Example: at about 10k souls (around 10 minutes, maybe 12 to 14 depending on rank), dying to a farmed Haze ult does not mean you should buy Metal Skin yet; keep building damage until you can kill her, then worry about defense. [8:09]
- Most builds in the in-game list are fine within the top five options, as long as they are not tagged all time, which usually means the build hasn't been updated in a while. [8:09]
- Metro's builds list utility items as needed, after the mid game section, because he buys them later to get his core up first. [9:11]
- Exception: if everyone on the enemy team deals massive spirit damage, buying spirit armor early, before finishing the mid game build, is worth it. [9:42]
- Against a single Bebop, rushing Debuff Remover before your core snowball item (for example Burst Fire) throws the game, because there are five other enemies too. [9:42]
- Pick defensive items that work against multiple enemies: against a Haze and a Pocket, Ethereal Shift beats Debuff Remover (for Pocket's Affliction) or Metal Skin (for Haze's ult), since it lets you live through the Haze ult and, timed right, dodge the Pocket ult. [10:13]
- Mistake three (every rank): playing too safe when behind; sitting in your safe jungle and on towers out of fear of dying makes you fall further behind with 0% chance to win. [10:45]
- The more behind you are, the riskier you have to play: kill enemies, take objectives and farm enemy creep camps to make up the deficit. [11:15]
- Playing safe while behind lets the enemy farm more creeps, your jungle and more Walkers, and leads to 5 or 15 minute stomps. [11:46]
- When behind, look for favorable trades: group up with a teammate and push a tower while another teammate pushes a tower on the other side, forcing the enemy to choose which to stop. [12:17]
- The more pressure you put on a leading enemy, the more choices they must make and the more likely they are to mess up; if you never push or force fights, they farm the whole map until they can't lose fights. [12:48]
- When the enemy is so far ahead they group in threes or fours to push your blue Walker, Metro aggressively pushes a side lane alone for an enemy Walker, and often gets it. [12:48]
- Even if three enemies rotate back to kill you on their Walker, it is a good trade: you got tower and Walker damage, forced them back to their side and gave your team space to push other lanes. [13:18]
- Up to Phantom, the main mistake is not taking risks when behind; look for favorable trades, two or three people pushing, pickoffs and comeback gold. [14:18]
- Mistake four: playing too risky when ahead; forcing more objectives and risky 1v2s is the only way the winning team can lose. [14:18]
- The more you are ahead the safer you should play, because killing you once gives the enemy roughly double the souls from comeback gold. [14:49]
- When ahead, push lanes out while it is safe and then farm, pick safe options and pushes, don't overcommit and give no openings for a comeback. [14:49]
- Mistake five (high rank): committing too many people to one fight, for example four players collapsing on a split pushing McGinnis or Wraith under your Walker. [15:51]
- Killing one split pusher with four people is a bad trade: one kill's souls plus near-nothing assists for four players' half a minute to a minute, while the other enemies push your lanes and farm your jungle for far more. [16:21]
- Against one enemy pusher of normal net worth, respond with one to two people, three at the absolute maximum, even if he is ahead of you in net worth. [17:55]
- Defending under your Walker gives you a resist bonus, so even a 1v1 there is very hard for the pusher to win. [18:26]
- Players over-respond because they are scared of fair 1v1s and want guaranteed teamfight wins; with one or two defenders you should easily push him away or kill him, and with three you almost always need the kill. [18:57]
- Scale the response: two enemy pushers, respond with two to four; three, respond with three to five; with four (or even three if you can guarantee the teamfight win) you can consider sending the whole team, because three dead enemies can mean a free Mid Boss. [19:28]
- Metro, as the split pushing Wraith, often dies at the Walker to four or five defenders while his team takes the rest of the map and wins easily. [19:58]
- Bonus: kills and deaths don't matter; a 0/5 player who killed two Walkers with those lives is worth more than a 2/0 player who farmed your jungle all game. [20:28]
- You can easily rack up kills or assists from enemies over-committing without achieving anything of value. [21:32]
- If you know you can kill a low Walker before four incoming enemies kill you, go for it every time; one death for a Walker that opens the map, can give a Flex Slot and extra souls, and makes the enemy commit several people, is always worth it. [22:02]
- Don't be scared of small skirmishes, 1v1s and 2v1s; even losing many 1v1s with bad micro, you will get better at fighting and climb in the long run. [23:04]
- The player who sits in their own jungle for 20 minutes with the highest net worth may be why the team loses: the team plays down one, can't defend Walkers or farm, and misses free Walkers and Flex Slots. [23:34]
