# This Hero Teaches You 99% of Deadlock
channel: Shotty  date: 2026-09-12  url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kntQWV24SHw

## Covers
Venator as a teaching hero: movement, right-hand peeking, positioning, forward thinking, cooldown management, fight hygiene, carry farming efficiency, playing around the ultimate, aim (tracking, clicking, projectile); also Vyper, Holliday, Celeste, Seven, Apollo, Warden, Lash; items Mercurial Magnum, Metal Skin, Spiritual Overflow

## Claims
- Venator teaches 99% of what you need to be a great player because his kit forces the fundamentals while you do nothing extra. [0:00]
- Some heroes let you ignore mechanics; on Vyper he did not need to know movement since he could dash, slide or buy stamina and air dash everywhere with lots of stamina by minute 10, and heroes like that can hide weaknesses you never learn. [0:30]
- Venator has almost no leniency for mistakes: he is big, slow and clunky, which forces you to learn movement. [1:01]
- Learning to move on Venator (wall bouncing, navigating the map) transfers to every hero; going from Venator to Holliday or any faster hero is night and day. [1:32]
- He kept saying every gun build should have Mercurial Magnum, calling it free and almost a Metal Skin counter; a Warden build with Mercurial Magnum and Spiritual Overflow later proved it, which shows how much of the game is still untapped. [3:02]
- Almost every player below Ascendant, many Ascendants and sometimes pros position badly, as if bullets do not hurt. [3:33]
- Right-hand peeking matters, especially on slower heroes: the camera sits over your right shoulder, so you see more to your right; test it behind a wall in lane. [3:33]
- Position so you are always right-hand peeking: line your crosshair on a target before you swing, then swing and already be shooting or landing a grenade. [4:04]
- If you position or take fights badly on Venator, you take far more damage than on most heroes, because he is a big, slow target and cannot escape with stamina like agile heroes. [4:35]
- Many players play "farm simulator" or "team fight simulator" with no forward thinking; that can carry you to around old Oracle or a bit higher, but thinking ahead wins much more. [5:06]
- Venator forward thinking: position next to walls, bait enemies where you can land the stun, back up behind a wall so they think you are farther away then dash jump for a wall stun, and place traps so a chasing enemy traps themselves next to a wall for a free wall stun and kill. [5:36]
- His roughly 30 second cooldowns force cooldown management, his slowness forces positioning, and his kit only pays off when you trap, bait or wall someone, which forces forward thinking. [6:07]
- Because the lessons are forced, he is especially good for newer players: you learn by playing him. [6:38]
- Fight hygiene is how you fight, position and go for kills; most players just run in and shoot. [7:08]
- With proper positioning it is very hard to die or be out-zoned: on Venator keep moving back, trapping, slowing and shooting, and play near walls so an enemy who gets close is at a disadvantage; few heroes (for example Lash) can still get to you. [7:39]
- As a carry Venator must farm very efficiently because he does not kill camps fastest; grenade plus melee is fast but needs some items. [8:10]
- Farming choice on Venator: spend grenades on camps and have none for lane or fights, or save grenades and punch camps down, since his punches do good damage. [8:10]
- His slow movement forces efficient rotations and box routes, teaching you to get the most souls in the least time. [8:40]
- He thinks Venator is good at all stages: one stun in lane, mid game or late game and the target is pretty much dead. [8:40]
- Venator can and often should perma farm, but better players perma farm until they get their ultimate, especially maxed with five points, then roam for a pick; if it opens the map, take Mid Boss or whatever the opportunity is, otherwise go back to farming. [9:10]
- That teaches more map activity than a "farm bot" Seven, or than Apollo, who maxes his 3 and fights the enemy team for the rest of the game until he falls off. [9:40]
- In the mid game, around 20k souls Venator does not really need to farm and can leave lane to get picks. [10:10]
- One ult pop plus a good wall slam is about two picks: wall slam, heavy melee and grenade at once, then pop ultimate and shoot; the target is probably dead, and the other two ultimate shots can kill a second enemy if you have the aim. [10:10]
- Aim is extremely important in Deadlock, even for strafing; Celeste players need good aim and smooth mouse control to air strafe, shoot and use abilities at speed. [11:11]
- Smoothness is the fundamental of aim; Venator trains smoothness because his gun is a tracking gun, precision because his ultimate is a clicking ultimate, and projectile aim because his grenade (his 1) can be bounced and arced. [12:12]
- Venator is like "anime ankle weights": not bad, but most heroes do some things better, so after mastering him other heroes feel easy. [12:44]
