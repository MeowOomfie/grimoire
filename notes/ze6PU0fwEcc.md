# Aim is Really This Simple
channel: Shotty
date: 2026-07-28
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ze6PU0fwEcc

## Covers
A general aim talk from an aim coach, framed like gym training: technique, varied practice, progressive overload, and changing sensitivity on purpose. Not Deadlock specific, but uses Vindicta as the example. For normal players with inconsistent aim, not aim trainer leaderboard chasers.

## Claims
- Aim is just hand-eye coordination on mouse and keyboard: you see, you move, you click; most people focus on the wrong part. [0:31]
- Good aim gives you a higher baseline in every game, but games are more than aim; a new player with great aim can crush on Vindicta and still die 40 times without knowing movement, positioning and farming. [1:03]
- Improving aim is: learn technique, practice that technique in a few different ways, and keep progressively overloading, like the gym. [2:03]
- Take a few maps, learn to do them properly, and keep training them. [2:34]
- Getting rank one in an aim trainer is not the same as having good aim in games; normal players should not use the playlists built for people chasing aim trainer ranks. [3:05]
- Do not set one sensitivity and never touch it; different games want different sensitivities because the kind of aiming differs (for example Counter-Strike versus Deadlock or Overwatch). [4:06]
- A fast sensitivity is better for quick movements and speed; a slow sensitivity is better for precision. [4:06]
- On Vindicta you usually want a slightly slower sensitivity so you hit your shots and your stabs. [4:38]
- Break up the monotony ("shock the muscle"): change the sensitivity, play different games, anything that makes your body adapt; repeating the exact same thing forever stalls progress. [5:08]
- Age is not a barrier: players aged 40, 50 and 60 got great aim by following a routine and practicing. [5:39]
- Do not throw every routine at yourself at once; add harder routines only after doing the earlier ones for a while. [6:09]
- 90% of aim is technique, and 90% of the purpose of aim training is learning technique. [6:40]
- Train what you actually do in your game: a normal player improving at Valorant should focus on clicking, precision and strafing maps rather than lots of reactivity tracking maps. [7:11]
- Focus on smoothness and speed matching: most characters have a set strafe speed, so you must be able to move your crosshair at one steady speed. [7:11]
- Most players cannot draw a straight left-right line without jittering or staggering; fix that before attempting hard maps. [7:41]
- Shaky aim and death gripping go away as your body adapts, like trembling on a new bench press weight that stops after a year or two; it is a skill issue that time fixes. [8:12]
- Even a skilled aimer still shakes on maps he is bad at; the shaking stops once he gets good at them. [8:42]
- Fast sensitivity trains the fingertips and wrist to make tiny, precise adjustments. [11:46]
- Slow sensitivity trains the arm to move quickly, adapt fast and do 180s. [12:16]
- Pick sensitivity to target a weakness: weak wrist and fingertips, go faster; weak at resetting or moving quickly with the arm, go slower. [12:16]
- Routine plan: a few technique playlists at one set sensitivity, practice for one to three weeks, then change sensitivity to work on weak points, or move to harder versions of the maps if you notice none. [12:46]
- A routine can stay under 30 minutes and still improve you. [13:17]
