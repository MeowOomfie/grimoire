# 9 Things Deadlock Doesn't Teach You
channel: Deathy
date: 2025-08-25
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tsq0e24Cfi0

## Covers
Nine mechanics the game does not explain (August 2025, after teleporters and some jump pads were removed): parrying the guardian, denying yourself, the center sacrifice jackpot and golden statues, the under-mid boxes, unsecured souls, mantle sliding, build direction and the sandbox DPS meter, checking enemy items, and tracking who you can 1v1.
For newer and returning players.

## Claims
- If you keep damaging lane opponents but cannot finish them, walk up to the guardian when they are low and parry it as soon as it melees you (it usually does right away, still a little buggy); the parry stuns the guardian for a few seconds so you can kill a passive enemy under it. [0:00]
- Parrying the guardian has two uses: diving enemy heroes when you already have an advantage, and getting more damage on the guardian. [0:31]
- When pushing a guardian with both enemies hurt or dead, dash in, parry, then back up; the guardian stops shooting for a few seconds so your troopers deal more damage to it. [0:31]
- Parrying the guardian gives no bonus damage against it; it only helps you push it or dive. [1:01]
- Denying yourself is often better than buying healing when you are low (say 200-300 HP) after winning a lane fight. [1:01]
- Three ways to deny yourself: let the enemy guardian kill you, stand on the rooftop spikes (a few seconds at 200 HP), or let a neutral camp kill you (for example at 50 HP). [1:33]
- Denying yourself respawns you in base with full health and cooldowns and gives the enemy no souls for the kill; clear the wave first so you lose no souls. [2:04]
- Self-denying is most useful before 5 or 6 minutes and stops working by about 10 minutes, because it relies on the very short early respawn timers. [2:04] [2:34]
- If you get both kills in lane, you can deny yourself to their guardian; wait about 10 seconds first so they do not get an assist. [2:34]
- Center sacrifice jackpot: heavy melee when the lights reach the end of the center sacrifice to get four golden statue buffs. [3:05]
- Early golden statue buffs give around 15 to 20 health each; about 100 health is worth roughly 300 to 400 souls by his valuation, so the jackpot is very valuable. [3:05]
- When stealing an enemy center sacrifice from a risky spot, do not stick around for the jackpot, since denying it to them matters more; when farming your own safely, always heavy melee for the four buffs. [3:36]
- Pick up golden statues around the map when they are on your way, without min-maxing every one; some areas have more statues. [3:36] [4:06]
- The boxes underneath mid, along with a few golden statues there, spawn at 10 minutes. [4:06]
- The under-mid boxes give about 600 to 800 souls when they spawn at 10 minutes depending on luck (it is RNG), more later in the game; they are usually worth more than a center sacrifice and sometimes more than a tier three camp, and any hero can break them. [4:37]
- In low ranks the under-mid boxes often go almost the whole game untouched; take them at 10 minutes if you have nothing better to do. [5:07]
- Do not fight while holding a large amount of unsecured souls; that is how you go from 2,000 souls up on a Wraith to 3,000 down after one lost fight. [5:07] [5:37]
- Unsecured souls show bottom left next to your soul count; troopers give only secured souls, while the jungle and boxes give unsecured souls, which secure over time or when spent. [5:37]
- Unsecured souls are spent last, so spend your whole bank (for example all 1,600 when 500 are unsecured) to use them up. [6:08]
- Dying with a big bag gives the enemy kill souls plus your dropped souls, which can swing net worth by thousands from one kill. [6:08]
- Teleporters and certain jump pads were removed, which hurt rotations; the fix is mantle sliding: hold crouch (Ctrl) while you mantle so you slide when it ends, which adds momentum. [6:08] [6:38]
- Mantle slide every single time you mantle, on anything, even a random sign with no ramp. [7:09]
- Since the item rework, a build needs a direction; with 12 item slots it cannot do everything. For example, decide whether Lash plays around Ground Strike, Flog or his ultimate. [7:39] [8:10]
- There are no bad items in Deadlock, only items that are better under certain conditions or on certain heroes. [8:10]
- To test item DPS, go into the sandbox and type the console command he gives as "HUD damage meter 1" to show a DPS meter. [8:10] [8:41]
- Testing method: pick a hero (for example Billy), buy items, enable the meter, then swap one item for another (for example Glass Cannon versus Berserker at max stacks) and compare damage; he believes most build makers do this. [8:41]
- Check enemy items often, especially before a 1v1; for example a Wraith in a side lane may have Silencer. [9:12] [9:42]
- If you play Mina, check whether your target has Slowing Hex before fighting, since that can be really bad for you. [9:42]
- Hover over enemy items while farming jungle or hitting troopers and note who counters you and who has no spirit resistance, then pick targets accordingly. [9:42] [10:12]
- Keep a mental note of who you can and cannot 1v1. Infernus is generally good against Mo & Krill, a big target easy to keep afterburn on, while Infernus struggles against Mirage, whose Tornado cancels Flame Dash and leaves him defenseless. [10:12] [10:43]
- Net worth decides threats too: if the enemy Wraith has 30,000 while her team and you have 20,000, you will not beat her in a 1v1. [11:15]
- Seeing the scariest enemy on the opposite side of the map is a green light to be aggressive; if you do not know where they are, play more passively until you have info. [11:15]
- If your hero is vulnerable to pick heroes (for example Mina against a Mo & Krill with Phantom Strike or a Paradox who can swap you), be careful, and seeing them on the other side of the map means you can push the walker. [11:45]
