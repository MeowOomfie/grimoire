## Covers
A 2024 replay review of a high-rank Wraith game (1v1 lane vs Infernus): how to win the lane with creep pressure, how to keep a lead after the lane, mid-game farming routes, the 10 minute bridge buff, and how to deviate from a standard build (Glass Cannon, Ethereal Shift, Inhibitor, Leech, Titanic Magazine, Soul Rebirth). For Wraith players and anyone trying to convert a lane lead. This is a 2024 video: the game, items and map have changed a lot since.

## Claims
- The lessons here (winning lane, keeping the lead, deviating from a set build) apply to every hero, not only Wraith; following a build one to one is why many players don't get the most success. [0:30]
- Start the lane by going in for melee last hits on the creeps; with all your creeps up you can sit behind them and get free damage on the enemy. [0:30]
- Wraith's Card Trick cards sometimes fail to lock on to a target, so they are a bit unreliable for trick shots. [1:01]
- The most important part of the lane is keeping pressure on the enemy, and you do that by focusing the creeps early, with lots of melee last hits and early damage on the creeps; if you have your creeps and the enemy doesn't, you can do whatever you want. [1:32]
- After an early kill, don't stay too long: the enemy respawns almost instantly early on and comes back on the speed zipline for a free kill on you. Stay at most about 5 more seconds for a few creep kills and some tower chip damage, then back out and buy. [2:02]
- After a kill you can also use the zipline to heal and buy in base without losing any creeps. [2:32]
- Securing creeps with melee kills gives the enemy no chance to deny them; then you can sit back and harass freely while the enemy has to go for last hits, and maybe get denies or a kill. [3:02]
- In a 2v2 lane, ideally one player focuses on harassing and one on last hitting; if you only last hit over and over and ignore the enemies, they can collapse on you easily. [3:32]
- In a 1v1 lane creep pressure is everything; it wins most lanes and works at any level, without needing great aim or last hit reaction time. [4:02]
- As long as you stay behind your creeps you get free harass; as soon as the enemy creeps are back, focus on hitting creeps again. [4:33]
- It is fine to sometimes let the enemy last hit or deny your creeps as long as you trade it for pressure, for example taking their tower earlier or getting a kill. [5:03]
- When the enemy guardian is about to die, have your gun reloaded so you can secure the last hit on the tower, because tower souls are shared across your whole team (a 200 or 250 bonus each), which is a big deal early on. [6:06]
- Once you have pushed to the enemy Walker, trading is hard because the Walker does a lot of damage; options are playing far back and focusing on denying his last hits, or going back to farm your tier one creep camp. [6:36]
- When an enemy is low and you are confident he dies, don't melee early: a melee is the one way to throw, since he can parry it and turn the fight. Let your reload finish and shoot, and only melee when he is turned away. [7:07]
- Shooting an enemy who is channeling Healing Rite cancels his healing. [7:38]
- Staying to hit the enemy Walker deep in enemy territory is risky because you can get ganked easily; he calls dying there an overcommitment, even though the Walker went down and gave the team a big gold lead. [8:11]
- Seeing the enemy behind your creep wave tells you he is too deep, which is when to zip around for a kill instead of taking creep kills. [8:42]
- When your own wave is too far into enemy territory to farm, farm the jungle, and look for the most efficient path to get as much money as possible. [9:12]
- Options after the lane include going back to a wave pushing into your tower, or looking for a gank; check for a potential kill nearby or take a double creep wave pushing in. [9:45]
- The camps far back on your own side he tends to farm later or ignore, since they take long to reach and are far from every other objective; on Wraith he prefers to be more proactive so he can help elsewhere. [10:15]
- The most efficient way to farm in the early game is creep waves, which give more money per time than any neutral camps; later, when you do huge damage, there are faster ways. [11:16]
- Golden statues and boxes are underrated; farmed on specific paths they give big bonuses that are worth it. [11:16]
- Pushing into enemy territory is acceptable when you know nobody can gank you, for example the most dangerous enemy just died and the others are visible in their lanes. [11:47]
- The bridge buff that spawns at 10 minutes is huge: at 10:00 be on the bridge waiting for it. [11:47]
- The gun buff on a DPS hero like Haze, Wraith or Vindicta lets you take almost any fight safely and win; it lasts three minutes and also speeds up creep farming. [12:20]
- Even a non-gun bridge buff speeds up farming: the survival buff gives more HP and regen, the movement buff gives five stamina bars and more move speed. Always look for the bridge buff at 10 minutes. [12:20]
- After the bridge buff, go to the center sacrifice punch boxes that spawn at 10 minutes; they give the most money on the map at 10 minutes for how fast you can farm them. [12:50]
- With the survival buff (about 1.4k HP) plus Fleetfoot, Tesla Bullets and Bullet Lifesteal, he can safely farm the mid creep camps in neutral territory. [13:54]
- That is how Wraith works: if you are not behind anyone and are rich, anyone contesting you 1v1 gets lifted with Telekinesis and dies to the cards. [14:25]
- He should not have used his ult that early while reloading. [14:56]
- Stay forward in your jungle so you can help teammates; Telekinesis means nobody can touch the lifted target, so helping a teammate under attack gets a free kill. [15:58]
- Early in the game, unless your team groups up, doing anything alone like pushing objectives is very risky; after a won fight, don't overcommit, make sure teammates don't die. [16:28]
- As the most powerful player on your team, stay around the middle of the map so you can help any teammate who needs it; if nobody needs help, go back to farming. [16:59]
- When three teammates are grouped pushing a tower and two enemies are with them, it is safe to farm some of the jungle elsewhere. [17:30]
- If the rest of the enemy team is fighting on the other side, you can push a Walker for free for money for you and your team. [18:00]
- If you want to carry, the most important thing is not dying, especially with the highest net worth on the team, because the more net worth you have, the more money the enemy gets for killing you. [18:30]
- Wraith is not a ganking hero; play her defensively in the mid game. She starts initiating fights or ganking in the late game; in the mid game you farm and make sure nobody can touch you. [19:33]
- Playing Wraith aggressively like a Haze leads to fights where you teleport in, lift and get a kill, but then three or four enemies come in and trade you out. [20:03]
- In the early game Wraith is not great in full five-man fights or any fight with more than two players per team. [20:34]
- Farming passively does not put more pressure on your team; his teammates died from overcommitting deep in the jungle alone. If the enemy pushes Walkers aggressively as three or four, then you come and team fight with your team. [20:34]
- If you want to carry, focus on your own game; that is not selfish, it is how you play Wraith and Deadlock in general. [21:36]
- His usual next item at this point is Intensifying Magazine (3k), but with 7k saved he deviated and bought Glass Cannon. [22:06]
- Glass Cannon is risky: it reduces your max HP and by itself is not that strong; each hero kill grants permanent weapon damage up to seven stacks and dying removes a stack, so it is a snowball item. [22:36]
- Only buy Glass Cannon if you think you can get kills afterwards without dying; if you expect to trade evenly or not get many kills, buy something else. [22:36]
- Alternatives to Glass Cannon: Crippling Headshot (more solid in general, a debuff on the enemy for your whole team), Lucky Shot (more damage no matter what), Ricochet (farms faster, though he dislikes it on Wraith), or Inhibitor (weapon damage, survivability and a slow that makes 1v1s and chases easier). [23:06]
- He bought Glass Cannon because he was very far ahead and could burst anyone on the enemy team, and was confident he would not die in the next few minutes. [23:37]
- Before buying it, check what on the enemy team can kill you fast: here Bebop's hook, Grey Talon's burst and Kelvin's wall; Haze could not contest him 1v1 and the Infernus was far behind. A well farmed Shiv would have been scary. [24:07]
- Glass Cannon before Intensifying Magazine was justified because he had enough money for a 6.3k item and believed he could snowball and carry. [24:38]

- Ethereal Shift is excellent, and the more he buys it the more he likes it; he bought it to regain the survivability he gave up with Glass Cannon and to escape situations he otherwise could not. [26:42]
- Once your damage is enough to kill anything, a defensive item like Ethereal Shift is worth more for 3k than a bit more health or a Spirit Shielding, Spirit Armor or Bullet Armor. [27:14]
- Grey Talon is very immobile, so when caught out he can be chased down. [28:14]
- When it is safe, farm the offensive options: taking a middle camp or a camp on the enemy side gets you money and takes money from the enemy; farming your own camps is the safe option when under pressure or when there is nothing else to farm. [28:44]
- With strong map control and presence in the middle, enemies who cross to your side or skirmish in groups of two or three can be contested and killed. [29:46]
