# How Deadlock Pros Win Games In The First 10 Minutes
channel: Deathy
date: 2025-10-22
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kifJcY4e9OE

## Covers
Breakdown of the lane phase in a North American tournament grand final (Melee Creeps vs Sneaky Golems), mostly from a Mina POV: wave priority and high ground, killing the healing trooper, using downtime on small camps and boxes, playing around ability point power spikes, resetting after pushing, using ultimates early, stealing enemy jungle, and matching rotations.
For players who want to win their lane phase and turn it into a lead by 10 minutes.

## Claims
- Winning the lane phase is not mainly a hero thing; the winning team's Bebop, traditionally a very strong laner, had zero lane kills and only 3,400 damage in the first nine minutes. [0:30]
- From the start both laners play for wave priority, because both teams know pushing the wave is really important; one player also plays for early bridge control. [1:00]
- Wave priority means pushing the wave forward; with it you keep high ground control and secure the positioning. [1:30]
- Holding the high ground and positioning makes advantageous trades easier, and lets you kill the healing trooper right away, which reduces the healing the enemy side gets and maximizes your trades. [1:30]
- On Mina, after losing a trade and taking heavy damage, buy Extra Spirit. [2:00]
- When one enemy laner is not supported by their partner on the high ground, your side can regain control of the bridge faster. [2:30]
- If the HP situation favors the enemy in your lane, expect them to strike back and take control of the lane. [2:30]
- High-level players are on top of the small camps and boxes the moment they spawn, especially when there is nothing better to do. [2:30]
- Mina should heal a lot off the small camp with her rake; missing it costs her the heal. [3:01]
- If you see no productive or advantageous trade, go hit boxes or do the small camp; there should be no wasted time, and these small efficiencies keep you in the game. [3:01]
- A lead of about 200 souls in lane is not much, but it is really important. [3:01]
- Once you have wave priority, decide what to do with it; if you are not yet confident you are strong, wait. [3:32]
- A power spike is a big increase in your relative strength; for Mina, two points in Love Bites is one. [3:32]
- Mina buys Mystic Shot and gets two points in Love Bites about 200 souls later, and the winning team gets aggressive as soon as that power spike unlocks. [4:02]
- Abuse small timings where the enemy has not yet reached their own second ability point (here the enemy Paradox). [4:02]
- High-level players are not afraid to reset, but reset at the correct time: push the wave first so you do not miss creeps, then go back and return to lane right after. [4:02]
- If you are very unhealthy you cannot apply pressure, and without pressure even a lead does not matter. [4:32]
- Try to take advantage of a short window where you have your ultimate and the enemy does not, but check first: if the enemy also has their ultimate, the window does not matter. [4:32]
- At high level people take a lot of trades and go for plays. [5:02]
- A common mistake seen in coaching is holding ultimates for a long time; use them right away. In pro lanes, Bebop, Infernus and Mo & Krill ults were all used early to take advantage of early game spikes. [5:02]
- Lane totals can look roughly even (four kills to three, no tower down) and still turn into a big lead at 10 minutes through what you do next. [5:32]
- The first step of snowballing is stealing enemy jungle: any downtime between waves after the wave is cleared, go straight to the enemy jungle and take a camp; it adds up. [6:03]
- Mina uses her ultimate off cooldown in skirmishes, and the hero does a huge amount of damage. [6:33]
- After getting a kill, convert it into the tower. [6:33]
- After taking a tower, keep a huge focus on efficiency: always be doing something, such as stealing enemy resources or farming toward your next item. [6:33]
- When you lose your lane badly, rotate to another lane to try to have impact and salvage it, especially on a very active hero like Paradox. [7:04]
- If you won your lane and an enemy who lost their lane rotates, match the rotation. [7:34]
- If you notice enemy laners missing from your lane, expect a gank elsewhere and go to the other lanes to impact them, reacting to or guessing the lane being ganked. [7:34]
- Winning a fight off a matched rotation leads into a further snowball; keep sending resources to extend your lead and take more of the enemy's resources. [7:34]
- Sending numbers to the enemy's last remaining tower can win the trade and huge map pressure even if you lose a tower on the other side; just avoid two teammates dying at the same time. [8:04]
- Stay as active as possible; being a step ahead with clean rotations gave the winning team priority in most lanes and roughly a 3 to 4k lead at 10 minutes. [9:05]
- When winning, do three and four person invades on the enemy jungle, stealing everything, cleaning up the Sinner's Sacrifices and camps, then position for the bridge buffs. [9:05]
- If the enemy commits to a play on your last remaining tower, you can take both bridge buffs. [9:35]
- Lesson one: grab positioning in the lane phase; posturing, taking the high ground and making yourself feared makes it much easier to apply pressure and keep wave priority. [9:35]
- Lesson two: play around your power spikes, be aggressive at the correct times, use your abilities and ultimates when you get them, and play around the 2.6k soul power spike when you get two ability points in one ability. [10:05]
- Lesson three: always be doing something between waves, before and after you win lane: boxes, aggression or stealing enemy camps. [10:05]
- If you win your lane, look to rotate to another lane, and rotate to the lane that is getting ganked if your laners are missing. [10:36]
- Wave priority in most lanes from winning the lane phase is what let the winning team take the first Urn fight, which produced the 10k lead and the snowball. [10:36]
- At a high level the lane phase is incredibly important. [11:07]
