# 8 Things I Wish I Knew Sooner in Deadlock
channel: Metro Deadlock
date: 2026-10-25
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VDE-EmbWbBQ

## Covers
Eight improvement tips from a high-level player: a small hero pool, knowing your power spikes, taking fights to learn your limits, a reliable laning phase, not baiting teammates, playing actively with a plan, practicing weaknesses and reviewing one mistake per game. For players who have many hours but are not improving.

## Claims
- Another 100 hours of farming jungle camps will not make you better if you are missing these fundamentals. [0:00]
- Focus on a small hero pool of up to three heroes; there are too many interactions to learn every hero quickly, and a small pool teaches which fights you can get away with, which to avoid and when to stop farming. [0:00]
- Violet plays completely differently in lane depending on the matchup: against Mirage you must bait out his Tornado before using your 3 to stun, and depending on your lane partner that can be very hard, so Mirage is a hard lane for Violet. [0:31]
- No pro plays every hero really well; even flex players like Dima off and ABL have a preferred pool. Trying all heroes is fine, but focus on a small pool to improve. [1:32]
- Know your power spikes: specific points that sharply raise your hero's power. Even up to Eternus people misunderstand them. [1:32]
- Wraith has one massive power spike: the first 6k item, which in his build is Capacitor. Wraith needs a lot of time to get online. [2:03]
- Big item buys usually line up with ability level ups in good builds. [2:33]
- Wraith ability order in his build: level Card Trick first to max, a point in the ultimate and a point in Teleport, then max Full Auto second. [2:33]
- Apollo gets online much earlier; in ABL's popular build his first big spike is maxing Flawless Advance, his main damage tool, which raises the scaling on everything, healing and max damage. Before it is maxed he is quite weak. [2:33]
- In ABL's Apollo build the Flawless Advance max lines up with the first 3k buy, Spirit Strike. [3:03]
- Power spikes differ by hero and by build; find them by buying to the item or level up, taking a fight and noticing you feel very strong. Watching a high-level player of your hero also shows them. [3:33]
- Knowing when your hero is strong or weak tells you when to take fights and when to farm and play passively. [4:04]
- The number one tip for all skill levels: take more fights to learn your limits. [4:04]
- The common low and mid rank pattern (passive lane, everyone runs boxes, AFK farm 20 minutes, one fight, Mid Boss, one Walker push) wastes your learning time. [4:34]
- With about three 30 minute games a day, spending most of it farming means you never learn what matters: which fights you can win, when to back out, who to focus, whether to drop your ult early, whether to arrive early or late, and how to position. [5:04]
- The best players are best mainly because they know when they can take a fight and are confident taking it, not because of flashy movement or aim. [5:35]
- ABL plays very aggressively but almost never dies, and when he dies he almost always gets a trade, because he spent thousands of hours learning 1v1, 1v2, lane and mid game matchups. [6:06]
- Know whether your hero is better at isolated fights or team fights. [6:36]
- Farming efficiently and being in the right spot come naturally; spending 80% of your time farming is wasted time. [6:36]
- Take uncertain 1v1s you are not sure you can win, so you learn whether you can; if you lost to a misplay (a missed ability), take the same fight again next time and win it. [7:08]
- Limit testing is not feeding: do not run 1v6, but be more aggressive. [7:38]
- Rather go 0-6 or 6-0 in lane than 0-0; playing just not to go negative is the wrong attitude. [7:38]
- Your goal in lane is to get ahead, and for 80% of the roster getting ahead means winning fights; if both teams farm only their own side the souls split 50-50 and no one gets ahead. [8:08]
- Winning fights creates openings: after a team fight win or a side-lane pick you can split push a Walker or take Mid Boss. The player who creates these openings carries and climbs. [8:38]
- Do not care about your KD; limit test and figure out which fights you can take. [9:08]
- In high-level ranked games, top players are almost always aggressive and, unless on a carry role, almost always fight; even lower Eternus players play too passive. [9:08]
- Build a reliable laning phase; laning is the most important part of the game because it comes first and dictates everything after. [9:39]
- Being a macro genius does not matter if you always lose lane and the enemy is 20k up after laning. [10:09]
- Learn laning before movement, item builds or macro fights: practice last hitting, avoid getting denied, and know which matchups you win and lose and adjust. [10:09]
- If you win your lane 60 to 70% of the time, you build repeatable advantages that make mid and late game much easier. [10:39]
- Stop baiting your teammates: if everyone waits for someone else to start the fight and comes in late to clean up, nobody initiates, the enemy initiates on you and you most likely lose. [11:10]
- Starting a fight, especially in the late game, is almost always game winning, all the way up to Eternus 6. [11:40]
- Play according to your role: tanks and initiators must actively look for the fight. [12:10]
- If teammates will not follow, communicate: as Paradox or Holliday looking for a lasso or swap, call that Mid Boss is coming up in 30 seconds, say what you have, and ask the team to group with you. [12:10]
- Joining a fight early beats showing up last for three kills; arriving early would have let the team clean up and push base, take Mid Boss, kill the Shrine or win. [12:40]
- Supports can wait for the team to engage, and some heroes are bad at going in first, but arriving too late to help is the real problem. [13:11]
- You are statistically the best player on your team only about one game in six unless you are high Eternus; the "I am most important" mindset makes your team wait while the enemy plays aggressive. [13:11]
- Play actively, not reactively: always have a plan (push out side waves, need 2,000 more for the next big item, start a fight because Mid Boss is up in 30 seconds). [13:42]
- Waiting on a side lane until the map shows the enemy pushing as six and two teammates dead, too far to help, is the wrong play; the enemy had a plan and you did not. [14:12]
- Call plans like "we are strong, let's go into their jungle as four"; even with poor execution, a shared plan usually works, even in Eternus, because the other team often has no plan. [14:42]
- Walking into the enemy jungle often finds someone to kill, then a free Mid Boss, then the win; Mid Boss is really strong right now. [15:13]
- Supports like Solomon or Dynamo, who need the team to engage or a stationary fight for their big ult, can still play reactively in the back but should still call a plan first. [15:13]
- Practice what you keep failing: find what you are worst at and work on that one thing. [15:44]
- The most important things to learn are macro (where to be on the map and how to get souls), proficiency on your hero, and team fighting. [15:44]
- Do not over-practice one thing you enjoy; he spent half a year AFK farming on Wraith and popping off with aim, and his movement is still bad for his rank. [16:14]
- Find your weakness in your replays: dropping a lot of bags means play safer or spend money earlier; never being in team fights means your positioning or planning ahead is bad. [16:46]
- Review one mistake every game: rewatch the game, find what went wrong (positioning, cooldown usage, fighting before ults were up, wrong counter items). [17:16]
- Set one specific goal for the next game, for example farm without dropping a 1,000 soul bag, join team fights earlier, or push out waves first so the enemy cannot take Walkers for free while you are in the jungle. [17:46]
