## Covers
Hidden item interactions: cooldown and duration items on passives, Tesla Bullets fire rate, diminishing resistances, anti-heal stacking, Crippling Headshot, Golden Goose Egg, Headhunter lifesteal, Vyper bola tech, Plated Armor, shred, Fortitude, Unstoppable, Spiritual Overflow, Rescue Beam slingshot.
For players who know the items but not how they actually interact.

## Claims
- Transcendent Cooldown lowers the cooldown of every passive item in the game, not only active items. [0:31]
- It now also works with Siphon Bullets, which is why late game Wraith players buy Siphon Bullets and then rush Transcendent Cooldown: the cooldown reduction applies to Siphon Bullets' passive lifesteal effect. [0:31]
- On a normal late game Wraith build, Transcendent Cooldown makes Surge of Power basically always active and lets Tesla Bullets and Capacitor proc more often, about 30% more damage if used properly. [1:02]
- Consider Transcendent Cooldown in late game builds with passive cooldown items even with only two actives; on his Wraith it beats items that give more health because Siphon Bullets procs more often. [1:33]
- Superior Duration works the same way on passive durations: a common Infernus build buys Toxic Bullets then Superior Duration, which extends Toxic Bullets. [2:03]
- Superior Duration also extends Burst Fire (to about 6 seconds) and lifesteal 6k items like Vampiric Burst. [2:34]
- With many passive cooldown or duration items, put Superior Duration or Transcendent Cooldown in your late game build if it makes sense. [2:34]
- Tesla Bullets is one of the most misunderstood items; it has a 50% proc chance on every bullet, so the higher your fire rate, the more reliably it procs. [3:04]
- Tesla Bullets is usually bought on Ivy, McGinnis and Wraith, heroes that proc it consistently over time. [3:04]
- Never buy Tesla Bullets on burst fire heroes like Seven or Paradox: it can only proc per burst and bursts have downtime, so it procs far less than on full auto heroes. [3:34]
- Do not buy it on slow fire rate heroes either, which includes Mina and Warden; testing shows it procs too little. [4:04]
- Unless you have a strong combo with Capacitor and really want its slow, there are always better items than Tesla Bullets on those heroes. [4:04]
- Buy Tesla Bullets and Capacitor on high fire rate heroes: Billy, Graves, Haze, Infernus, Ivy, McGinnis (captioned "Minus"), Venator, Vindicta and Wraith. [4:04]
- The common flat resist items are Spirit Resilience and Bullet Resilience, and early Battle Vest and Enchanter's Emblem; resistances of one type stack diminishingly, so a second spirit resist source does not add its full value. [5:08]
- Example: Enchanter's Emblem's 18% plus another 8% should be 26% on paper, but the tab screen shows less; the more you stack, the more diminishing the returns. [5:39]
- Resistances work on your HP, so the more HP you have the more they are worth: 25% reduction on 4k+ HP blocks about 1,000 damage. [6:09]
- Resistances get stronger the longer the game goes, because boons add health. [6:09]
- Resistances are better on tanky heroes than on squishy ones like Mina, which is why most Mina builds buy little resistance and rarely Spirit Resilience or Bullet Resilience. [6:39]
- Spirit resist is insane right now because there is so much early spirit damage; on heroes like Apollo, items that give spirit resist alongside other effects are worth stacking despite diminishing returns. [6:39]
- A normal Apollo build reaches about 38% spirit resist from Spirit Resist Locket, Enchanter's Emblem, Greater Expansion and Cold Front without buying Spirit Resilience; people always guess too low. [7:39]
- With 38% spirit resist in the early or mid game you feel unkillable without having invested much in green items. [8:11]
- Lifesteal also has diminishing returns, for example Vampiric Burst plus Fury Trance. [8:11]
- Don't overstack lifesteal; better to have one big lifesteal item (Vampiric Burst, Infuser for spirit damage, Leech for hybrid) and then invest in HP, survivability and resistances. [8:41]
- Battle Vest amplifies weapon damage and Enchanter's Emblem amplifies spirit abilities, and many heroes buy them more for that bonus than for the resist. [8:41]
- Buy Enchanter's Emblem on heroes that benefit from extra spirit power and Battle Vest early on heroes that shoot their gun a lot. [9:11]
- On a raw gun hero like Haze, Enchanter's Emblem is not usually best; get Battle Vest early for survivability and get spirit resist from Spirit Resilience or Fury Trance. [9:11]
- On a hero that uses both like Warden, buy whichever you need more; in the current meta it is almost always Enchanter's Emblem because there is more early spirit damage, but you can swap it for gun damage if you don't care about the resist. [9:41]
- Anti-heal items are among the most neglected, especially at low rank, and Healbane is strong on a lot of heroes. [10:41]
- Healing reduction stacks only from different sources: two teammates applying Healbane to the same enemy still give only 35% healing reduction. [10:41]
- Healbane and Toxic Bullets stack additively, as do Healbane and Decay, so a hero that can use both gets a very big anti-heal effect. [11:11]
- This is especially great against Victor: a Shiv with Decay and Healbane stops him healing almost entirely. [11:11]
- Crippling Headshot is the only item that stacks with itself: two teammates applying it to the same target give double the bullet resist and double the spirit resist reduction. [11:41]
- In the late game, if you need shred and two heroes can apply Crippling Headshot easily, stack it; high level games often have multiple on one team. [11:41]
- Golden Goose Egg's 10% damage penalty does not apply to percent health damage items: Toxic Bullets, Scourge and Siphon Bullets deal the same with or without the egg. [12:11]
- So a support like Rem can keep the egg longer and still run Scourge late, cast on allies, with no damage downside. [12:42]
- Golden Goose Egg is really good on supports that don't need much damage and get value from healing or buffing the team, like Rem or Paige. [13:12]
- Headhunter gives flat bonus headshot damage, 75 base scaling with boons to 215 late game. [13:43]
- Bullet Lifesteal works on Headhunter's bonus headshot damage, so headshots heal you from Headhunter's own healing and from Bullet Lifesteal. [13:43]
- With Vampiric Burst active, the first Headhunter shot gives a lot of healing, because the bonus damage counts toward the healing. [14:13]
- Headshot heroes like Lash, and Warden, can run Bullet Lifesteal and maybe Vampiric Burst late; a meta is developing around gun Lash who buys Siphon Bullets late. [14:43]
- Vyper's bola makes the enemy untargetable by you and your teammates, but Armor Piercing Rounds lets you shoot through the bola, which is why it is one of Vyper's most common late game items. [14:43]
- Late game Vyper can one-shot combo someone by throwing the bola and unloading her gun; they can't do anything and take bonus damage. [15:14]
- Plated Armor is a late game item against on-hit effects and bullet damage, and it works multiplicatively with your resistances, so with Bullet Resilience you are nearly unkillable by bullets. [15:44]
- Armor Piercing Rounds is a decent counter to Plated Armor: 50% of the time your on-hit effects go through it. [16:15]
- On-hit heroes like Mirage (Djinn's Mark) or Infernus (burn) can buy Armor Piercing Rounds late against Plated Armor to reapply their procs more easily. [16:15]
- Shred is anything that reduces enemy bullet or spirit resist, and it applies on the enemy's overall resist, so the more resist they have the more value it gives. [16:46]
- Focus on resist reduction more the later the game goes; against about 50% spirit resist with no reduction you literally do half damage, and an item like the one captioned "spirit vent" adds a lot. [17:18]
- Without any shred on your team you will struggle to win late game fights if the enemy has it; make sure a few people on your team have resist reduction. [17:50]
- Crippling Headshot is the best shred against both damage types because it works for your whole team as long as they focus the right target. [17:50]
- Shred items stack: on Warden he buys Hollow Point early and later Crippling Headshot if he needs more shred, taking a 0% resist target to -24% bullet resist. [18:20]
- Negative bullet resist exists, so an enemy with no resist takes more than 100% damage, but resist reduction is diminishing with the same formula as resists, so you can't overstack it. [18:51]
- The bonus damage from shred is much higher when the enemy has bullet resist than when they don't. [19:22]
- Fortitude was recently buffed and was the hard meta a few months ago; its heal kicks in after 10 seconds and heals 2% of max HP until full or until you take damage. [19:22]
- Only enemy hero damage or jungle creeps cancel the Fortitude heal; lane creep damage does not, which is why it is getting back into the meta. [19:53]
- With Fortitude you can regen without going to base as long as you don't take damage from enemy players or farm jungle camps. [20:23]
- Unstoppable is one of the most misunderstood and underbought items in the game. [20:23]
- Unstoppable makes you immune to stun, silence, sleep, root and disarm, but it also temporarily suppresses negative status effects, which covers most effects in the game. [20:54]
- Fire rate reduction (Suppressor, Dynamo's stomp, Yamato's slash) stacks down to -50%, so you deal half damage; Unstoppable removes it. [21:24]
- Unstoppable also removes spirit damage reduction such as Silencer or Inhibitor. [21:55]
- Think of Unstoppable as "deal double damage for 6 seconds" in the late game against many comps. [21:55]
- In the very late game consider Unstoppable instead of another damage item (Ricochet, Shadow Weave, Spiritual Overflow, Spellslinger), especially as a gun hero against lots of fire rate reduction. [22:25]
- Unstoppable also stops movement slows and lockouts. [22:25]
- Spiritual Overflow now gives 20% ability duration, useful on heroes like Seven who want a longer Power Surge. [22:55]
- Spiritual Overflow's duration does not apply retroactively: proc it first (shoot a few bullets) and only then cast the ability you want extended. [23:25]
- Spirit power usually applies retroactively and fire rate applies instantly, so only the duration needs that order. [24:25]
- Rescue Beam slingshot tech: activate it, pull, then immediately cancel; the teammate is flung much faster and further, which matters when they are close to you and would only be pulled a short way. [24:25]
- The slingshot works best with buffed teammates (for example as Kelvin) or from high ground, but also works next to them. [25:26]
- The downside of canceling Rescue Beam is less healing; it is a hail mary to get a focused teammate out instantly, which is usually better than the slow pull while they die anyway. [25:26]
