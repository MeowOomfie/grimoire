## Covers
Short ergonomics guide for shooters including Deadlock: why wrist-only aiming caps your aim and hurts your wrist and back, how arm and wrist split the work, and how to set chair and desk height, forearm position and mouse pad for arm aiming.
For players switching from wrist aiming or getting wrist or back pain.

## Claims
- Wrist-only aiming caps your aim unless you play on very high sensitivity, because pivoting from the wrist limits range of motion and forces you to lift and reset the mouse while tracking or switching targets, which hurts smoothness and consistency. [0:00]
- Wrist aiming puts pressure on the wrist from the slanted position, which over time can lead to nerve damage and carpal tunnel, especially without an armrest. [0:31]
- Wrist aimers tend to sit further from the desk and lean forward, causing poor posture and back problems. [0:31]
- Use arm aiming together with the wrist: swipe the arm to bring the crosshair close to a far target, then correct with the wrist. [0:31]
- Use the arm for large movements (wide flicks, tracking in close-range fights) and the wrist for small flicks, micro adjustments and long-range fights against small targets. [1:01]
- Proper arm aiming lets you do 180s without resetting the mouse, and steadies vertical aim by guiding it with shoulder and forearm. [1:01]
- Arm aiming helps prevent carpal tunnel by moving strain from the wrist tendons to the arm and shoulder. [1:01]
- To set chair height: sit up straight, drop your shoulders, lift your forearm parallel to the table, make a fist with a neutral wrist, and line your index finger up with the table edge. [1:31]
- If the table is above your index finger, raise the chair; if it is below, lower the chair; with a standing desk, adjust the desk instead. [1:31]
- To test the form: shoulders relaxed, scoot in until your stomach touches the desk edge and rest your forearms on the desk; slight friction from the edge pushing into the forearm is normal. [2:01]
- If your shoulders shrug in that position the table is too high (raise the chair or lower the table); if a big gap forms under the start of your forearm and you hunch to keep it on the table, the table is too low (lower the chair or raise the table). [2:01]
- Many pro players leave the elbow and up to one third of the forearm hanging off the table, which allows faster flicks while keeping mouse control. [2:31]
- Keep knees bent at about 90 degrees with thighs parallel to the floor; if your feet do not reach the ground after adjusting, get a footrest. [2:31]
- If the mouse pad edge digs into your forearm on upward movements, hang a large mouse pad slightly off the table edge. [3:02]
- If a sweaty arm sticks to the mouse pad and drags, wear compression or arm sleeves. [3:02]
- Switching from wrist aiming takes a few days to get used to. [3:02]
