# An Eternus' Guide to "Optional" Items
channel: HERESY
date: 2026-09-12
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YinpOpNjpLg

## Covers
How to decide the "optional" 3.2k and 6k slots in an E6 build: the anti-CC trio (Dispel Magic, Counterspell, Warp Stone), Stamina Mastery, Spirit Resilience vs Bullet Resilience and Metal Skin, Unstoppable, Ethereal Shift, Knockdown, Rescue Beam, Fortitude and Veil Walker.
For players who copy high-rank builds and want the reasoning behind the situational items, framed to stay useful after balance changes.

## Claims
- Itemization is always situational; good builds amplify a hero's strengths and cover its weaknesses, so many heroes favor particular "situational" items. [0:30]
- Top Wraiths buy Dispel Magic practically every game because Wraith is so vulnerable during her teleport that even one enemy Slowing Hex, Silence Wave or similar effect forces it. [0:30]
- The main situational trio is Dispel Magic, Counterspell and Warp Stone; almost every hero builds at least one, and because they overlap and take core slots it is rare to buy all three in one game. [1:02]
- Dispel Magic's identity is cleansing DoTs and soft CC; it also grants 10% spirit resist. [1:32]
- Buy Dispel Magic when enemies have several DoT sources to cleanse, such as afterburn, Shiv knives, Mirage scarabs, a mark DoT (caption "Jin's mark") or maybe decay. [2:02]
- Also buy Dispel Magic when your hero needs a movement ability for positioning, such as Wraith, Mina, Apollo, maybe Calico; with enemy Slowing Hexes or other disables you are practically forced to. [2:02]
- If the enemy has only one or two DoT sources (for example only an Infernus) and your hero is not badly affected by Slowing Hex, prioritize other counter items. [2:02]
- Dispel Magic is common at the top level largely because of its 10% spirit resist; spirit resist is one of the strongest stats, so the item is rarely non-viable while it keeps that stat. [2:32]
- He suspects Dispel Magic may someday be reworked to have more anti-CC at the cost of its spirit resist. [2:32]
- Counterspell specializes in blocking ultimates, though it can block non-ults; you can often decide on it in the loading screen. [3:02]
- Look for fight-warping abilities to Counterspell: Death Slam, Affliction, lasso, Chain Gang, Drifter's ult and similar. [3:02]
- Counterspell blocks every incoming ability during the parry window, not just one, so it also lets you contest the following Rejuvenator into enemy CC. [3:02]
- Counterspell fortifies Rejuvenator contests on frontliners and prevents instant kills like a Lash or Holliday ult on backliners, so it is viable in every game; the only question is whether it beats your core or other situational items. [3:32]
- Counterspell also works on item effects and has half the cooldown of Dispel Magic, which is why it is core on snipers like Vindicta who want to block Knockdown. [3:32]
- Warp Stone lets you dodge many important ultimates outright and gives bullet resist after warping, similar to how Dispel Magic is a pseudo spirit resist item. [4:02]
- Warp Stone does each job less well than the other two but is also an offensive tool, since the teleport can gap close as well as dodge or retreat. [4:32]
- Warp Stone synergizes with Dynamo and Mo & Krill (better ults) and perhaps Abrams (better Shoulder Charges), and with heroes lacking strong movement like Victor or Venator because it gives verticality. [4:32]
- Lean to Warp Stone when the enemy comp does not push you strongly toward Dispel Magic or Counterspell, and when enemies have enough gun damage to use its passive. [4:32]
- Stamina Mastery is a purely movement item; it suits heroes too squishy to use armors, like Paige, and heroes that use stamina for damage, like Lash or Grey Talon. It competes with Dispel Magic, Warp Stone and Counterspell. [5:03]
- Pocket often prefers Warp Stone to Stamina Mastery because he must instantly reposition out of satchel and cannot afford to dash; heroes that fly all game and use the air for damage can prefer Stamina Mastery. [5:03]
- Against many direct movement disables like Slowing Hex or Knockdown you are often forced to buy Dispel Magic or Counterspell before Stamina Mastery. [5:33]
- Stamina Mastery is the most offensive 3.2k optional; buy it when you can get away with it, but be careful against people who can take you out of the sky without other counters. [5:33]
- Spirit Resilience appears in most builds and Bullet Resilience rarely; the first reason is that most damage is spirit. [6:03]
- Statlocker data from the summer: after the 10 minute mark, 60% of total PvP damage is spirit, and the share favors spirit more as the game goes on. [6:03]
- Second reason: every hero except Venator and Haze does high spirit damage, while gun damage comes in bursts from specific heroes such as a Vyper, Haze or Silver jumping on you. [6:34]
- Because gun damage comes in bursts from one target, Metal Skin (another 3.2k anti-gun item) outperforms Bullet Resilience by blocking all of that gun damage. [6:34]
- Gun carries like Wraith or Warden do close to half their damage as spirit on-hit effects, so Spirit Resilience counters a fed Wraith better than Bullet Resilience or Metal Skin. [7:05]
- Spirit Resilience is in almost every build because it is one of the best and least situational items in the game. [7:05]
- Resists get better the more HP you have; on squishy heroes like Pocket, Mina and Vindicta, items that let you avoid damage (Warp Stone, Counterspell, Dispel Magic, maybe Ethereal Shift) are often better. [7:35]
- Full resilience is viable in rare cases: a high effective HP hero like Warden or Shiv with extra money before an important fight like mid boss might afford Plated Armor. [7:35]
- Metal Skin is best bought specifically to counter a fed enemy gun carry; do not prioritize it over Spirit Resilience and wait until an enemy gun carry actually becomes a problem. [8:06]
- Unstoppable is bought for three reasons; first, to avoid being stunned out of a high-impact ultimate on Seven or Dynamo, where it is practically core (buying the alternative he calls "Adrenaline" instead is generally ill-advised to him). [8:06]
- Second, to contest the Rejuvenator as a frontliner without built-in CC immunity, like Abrams; games are won and lost punching crystals in the mid pit, so a tanky hero can buy Unstoppable solely for that fight. [8:36]
- Third, to cap off a carry's build and avoid game-losing stuns or hooks, for example on Wraith. [8:36]
- Except on Dynamo, do not consider Unstoppable until after 25K souls; other situational items are generally higher priority. [9:06]
- Ethereal Shift is the more expensive but better version of Counterspell, bought for the same reasons; its only non-cost drawback is needing an item slot. [9:06]
- Combine Ethereal Shift and Counterspell to fortify Rejuvenator contests: dodge most hard CC, then parry enemies punching the Rejuvenator after you pop out. [9:06]
- If you reach the late game without Counterspell and want to survive dives or ultimates, consider saving for Ethereal Shift; its move speed and spirit resist after the shift give 5+ seconds of safety vs Counterspell's less than one. [9:36]
- Knockdown is an anti-flyer and anti-ult item; buy it only on supports, preferably with direct CC follow-up like Paige, not Rem, against Vindicta, McGinnis, Seven, Dynamo and similar. [9:36]
- Knockdown has a pretty terrible win rate in most situations because it is far more useful when it sets up your own skill shots; as a pure counter it is out-competed for its cost by Slowing Hex or Curse. [10:06]
- Rescue Beam is the counterpart to Healing Nova and should be thought of as an anti-hard-CC item; on heroes that would consider Healing Nova, buy Rescue Beam against Dynamo, Holliday or Mo & Krill because it pulls teammates even while fully stunned. [10:37]
- Fortitude and Veil Walker are counterparts: both give sustain and map presence, letting you farm boxes or rotate to objectives instead of going back to base or risking a solo wave to heal. [10:37]
- Fortitude gives great health and healing, especially on high HP heroes, since its healing is a percent of max HP. [11:07]
- Veil Walker gives less healing in most cases but grants invisibility for better ganks and rotations. [11:07]
- Unless overtuned, Fortitude and Veil Walker are less broadly useful than the anti-CC trio or the classic armor set. [11:07]
- Veil Walker is currently overtuned and bought on most heroes: too much healing, too much move speed, and an invisibility detection range that is too close while lasting too long; it will 100% be nerfed soon. [11:37]
- After the nerf Veil Walker should still have a place on heroes with hard CC they want to land on the right target, since stealthing up close is good; consider it on Mo & Krill or Holliday. [11:37]
