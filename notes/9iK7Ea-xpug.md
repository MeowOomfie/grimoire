# YOU Don't Understand These Deadlock Items
channel: HERESY
date: 2026-06-09
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9iK7Ea-xpug

## Covers
Misunderstood or undervalued items: Close Quarters vs Monster Rounds, Heroic Aura, Tankbuster, Fleetfoot, Long Range and Sharpshooter, Silencer, Return Fire in lane, and why most players should stop buying Transcendent Cooldown.
For players who follow left-to-right builds and want the hidden math behind item choices.

## Claims
- Close Quarters gives 20% weapon damage within 15 m of the target, which in lane is most of the time you trade or kill the wave. [0:30]
- Monster Rounds gives 25% weapon damage only against NPCs, a standard flat weapon damage increase rather than a percent amp, so it gives practically the same NPC damage as Close Quarters when you stand close. [0:30]
- Unless you are a frontliner who wants Monster Rounds' bullet resist passive for diving the tower, almost always err toward Close Quarters, since it also adds damage against players. [1:01]
- If your build buys only one or two gun items in lane to survive and shove the wave, consider Close Quarters instead of Monster Rounds. [1:01]
- Heroic Aura is perhaps the most under-purchased item: buffed twice recently, it does not feel good on you alone, but it gives the equivalent of a Battle Vest of bullet resist to practically your whole team in every team fight for the rest of the game. [1:32]
- The most important fights are team fights (sieging walkers, mid boss, the extended King of the Hill urn fights), and there Heroic Aura is worth far more than its 3,200 souls if you face any appreciable gun damage. [2:03]
- Heroic Aura will be nerfed one day; use it now, but its effect does not stack, so you want only one per team. [2:35]
- Tankbuster is over-purchased; it appears in many left-to-right builds for spirit nukers like Grey Talon, Mina and Pocket. [2:35]
- Tankbuster does the same damage on Mina as on Kelvin or Rem, because unlike almost every other damaging spirit item it does not scale with spirit. [2:35]
- Tankbuster's cooldown is one of the only ones not affected by Transcendent Cooldown, so on a fed spirit carry buying Boundless Spirit and Transcendent Cooldown it has no synergy and is probably less efficient than other 3,200 spirit items. [3:05]
- Tankbuster applies its damage in an AoE, so hitting several targets with the triggering spell does far more damage; Mina procing it with Love Bites on one target does far less than Paige hitting Captivating Read on three people in the mid boss pit or Rem throwing a pillow at three people. [3:35]
- You can still buy Tankbuster on pure damage heroes, especially into tanks where its true damage and current health damage are more effective, but it is generally better on supports as an extra damage item than on full spirit nuker carries. [4:06]
- Fleetfoot's real power is near infinite sliding, which means nearly infinite ammo in team fights; it is bought to solve ammo problems, not movement speed problems. [4:37]
- Combine Fleetfoot with another movement speed item like Enduring Speed or Blood Tribute for effectively infinite slides and ammo during the active. [5:08]
- The name Long Range is misleading: 15 m is a common range, especially for backline carries, so you do not need to be a sniper to buy it, and it is likely underused in modern gun builds. [5:38]
- Venator, The Doorman and Seven can all use Long Range very effectively, and its upgrade Sharpshooter was recently buffed. [5:38]
- The Doorman by default hits 29 to the body and 43 to the head; with Opening Rounds and Sharpshooter as the opening 4.8k he quotes 57 to the body at range, then 110 to the head and 75 to the body. [5:38]
- Counting the boons you would have by 4.8k, The Doorman hits 140 to the head and 94 to the body as early as 5 minutes into the game. [6:10]
- If you buy High Velocity Rounds and Long Range, Sharpshooter costs only 800 more since both are components, so you hit the 800, 1.6k, 2.4k and 3.2k breakpoints and skip 4k straight to 4.8k for maximum lane efficiency. [6:40]
- Silencer is a counterpart to Heroic Aura; its real power is arguably the spirit damage reduction on your bullets, not the silence. [7:11]
- Silencer's spirit damage reduction does not need build-up like Inhibitor or Toxic Bullets: any single bullet that hits applies a 6 second -25% spirit damage debuff. [7:43]
- Silencer is extremely valuable on heroes with high fire rate or magazine size in late game team fights, even if they are not gun carries wanting 6.4k gun investment. [7:43]
- On a gun carry into spirit damage in the late game, Silencer doubles as your spirit resist item and a damage item that helps your whole team. [8:13]
- You do not need to be Wraith or Venator to buy Silencer; any high fire rate hero can buy it to help the team, and in the deep late game (30 to 35 minutes plus) someone on your team should have it practically every game. [8:44]
- Return Fire is known to be situationally good into heavy single-source gun damage like Vyper, Venator, perhaps Wraith (a hybrid). [9:14]
- Return Fire might be broken in lane: its cooldown is only 23 seconds, so it is up every wave and lets you out-trade opponents regardless of their items if you only fight during its 6.5 second window. [9:44]
- Late game fights last 30 to 50+ seconds every few minutes, so you use Return Fire once or twice per fight; in lane you keep trades to 6 to 8 seconds and can get 70 to 90% uptime instead of about 30%. [10:15]
- Do not buy Return Fire into spirit heroes or on every hero; it fits lane frontliners with high healing like Abrams or Mo & Krill, or heroes laning with a healer like Rem, Kelvin, maybe Dynamo or McGinnis, who fund constant aggressive trades. It also scales well into the late game. [10:46]
- Return Fire damage is calculated before resistances, so even with near 100% resists (for example Mo & Krill in Burrow) it still does full damage. [11:16]
- PSA: stop buying Transcendent Cooldown; it is purchased incorrectly often even in Eternus 6 lobbies. [11:48]
- Treat Transcendent Cooldown not as a 3,200 ability cooldown upgrade but as roughly a 2,400 soul item CDR upgrade, since that is where most of its value goes. [12:18]
- Transcendent Cooldown's item CDR also applies to non-active item cooldowns, for example it makes Torment Pulse tick faster. [12:18]
- Buying Transcendent Cooldown with zero or one affected item effectively spends 3,200 souls for about 5% ability cooldown reduction, a waste of souls. [12:48]
- Only buy Transcendent Cooldown to reduce the cooldowns of a large number of important actives; practically never buy it unless you have full actives. [13:18]
