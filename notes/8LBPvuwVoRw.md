# Why you aren't Improving in Deadlock
channel: Midknight
date: 2025-11-13
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8LBPvuwVoRw

## Covers
An off-the-cuff talk on limit testing as the way to improve: farming at low HP and greeding camps against the wave, calling objectives, experimenting with builds, and PvP limit testing when learning a new hero.
For players who play too safe to learn.

## Claims
- Eternus players do die to jungle camps; even an E6 player executes to camps very often. [0:31]
- You should be limit testing as much as possible; you are not playing correctly if you are not. [0:31]
- Farm limit test 1: if you don't know whether your HP can survive a camp (for example 200 HP on a medium camp), try it, and learn from dying (for example Warden at 10k souls and 200 HP can't full farm a camp without his shield up). [1:02]
- Farm limit test 2: test whether you can greed a camp before the wave reaches your tower or walker or crashes into you. [1:32]
- You will lose towers and die to camps doing this, but that is the correct way to play; good players constantly limit test. [2:02]
- At very high elo the mistakes get smaller (seconds late rather than losing a wave to a tower) because they know how efficiently their hero farms, but they still execute. [2:02]
- Limit test hardest while learning the game; it is the most efficient way to improve. [2:33]
- Low elo calls mid boss and Urn far less than high elo; if unsure whether a mid boss works (respawn timers, wave state, zip back), try it, and if it fails analyze why, in the VOD if needed. [2:33]
- A play that fails isn't necessarily wrong: a good mid boss call can fail because a teammate showed up late without their ult or wasn't there to melee it; an Urn run with a player advantage is still good if the fight is misplayed. [3:03]
- Ults factor in: you won't always win a 5v6 against Lash, Dynamo and Warden ults when you are out of ults. [3:33]
- Asking "why did this go wrong?" and trying again lets you learn in 200 hours what takes most players a thousand. [3:33]
- Build limit testing: if far ahead in lane, test whether you can afford two greens early, or go pure purple with no green; if you die quickly you need a green. [4:04]
- Don't buy two pure defensive items like resistance items before your major spikes, since you still need damage. [4:04]
- Generally itemize offensively when ahead and defensively when behind (he says "behind" for both, a slip). [4:04]
- Into a scary Shiv and Kelvin lane, test whether Spirit Shielding, Extra Regen, Healing Rite and Monster Rounds let you slow play the lane, or whether you need offensive items. [4:34]
- Once players reach Oracle or Phantom, they should start making their own builds or at least modifying builds they like. [4:34]
- Test items you are unsure of (for example a regen item on Warden), then check the statistics screen or your VOD. [5:04]
- When learning a hero, "run it down" in the sense of limit testing, especially in lane; that is the only way to improve micro. [5:35]
- Ido lost his first 18 of 20 Shiv games limit testing, and now looks like a monster on Shiv; he learned in 20 games what would have taken a hundred playing safe. [6:05]
- Limit test constantly for the first 10 to 20 games on a hero; keep limit testing hard in lane for about the first 25 to 30 games, but tone down mid and late game limit testing after the first 10 or so, then play at a reasonable pace. [6:36]
- Warden is good right now in solo queue below Eternus; at Eternus players know how to handle him. [7:06]
- Going 1 and 8 on Warden while running at enemies taught him the limits of the build and why Shiv and Kelvin is a broken combo, more than sitting under tower throwing flasks would have. [7:38]
