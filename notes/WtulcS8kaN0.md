## Covers
A review of game one of Night Shift 51's NA Finals, Poppers Peoples vs Bird with Clock (BWC), mostly from Lowmain's (Billy) view with his team's unmuted comms. The thesis is that top NA players are mechanically elite but weak at deep strategy, team coordination and communication: cross-map pressure, setting up deathballs off side lane priority, closing out bases, and calling power spikes.
For competitive and aspiring pro players, and anyone who wants to play macro as a team rather than as a solo queue stack.

## Claims
- The best NA players' main gaps are deep strategy, team coordination and communication, and these three are closely linked; the points apply to all NA teams including the best. [0:00]
- After winning a fight decisively enough while already holding blue, forcing an early mid boss was correct. [1:02]
- Up two kills after the Rejuvenator fight, using immediate yellow wave priority to push the walker is fine; you have to take risks to win, and with a respawn advantage it can be less risky than farming the jungle first and taking a 6v6 walker siege with Rejuvenator later. [1:32]
- If the enemy has no green walker to defend and holds blue priority, expect all of them to collapse on your yellow push, so the pushers need teammates to make something happen with them or across the map. [2:02]
- Rule: when your teammates make a play and you are not joining, you must make a play on the other side of the map; teams should create pressure simultaneously. Hiding in base abandons them to a 6v3. [2:33]
- The remedy is to join immediately or play the other side of the map so that if the enemy over-rotates onto the sieging teammates, you extract high value elsewhere; this should be understood intuitively and also communicated as a direct strategy. [3:03]
- Poppers using his Yamato ultimate to guarantee the Rejuvenators was probably correct. [3:33]
- Holding wave priority on both side waves at once is rare and valuable. [3:33]
- During the mid boss fight a BWC player (Arabus) ran the wave past the mid boss and took blue walker with it, a smart trade play. [4:04]
- Mistake: Poppers (Yamato) chased a Paradox who had gone off the map, which is basically impossible to chase, while his team was already crashing into the walker. [4:04]
- When three teammates siege a walker and every enemy has respawned, the other three have two options: reinforce as soon as possible or attack something else; with no walker across the map and no blue priority, they had to reinforce, and not being there is being out of position. [4:35]
- Mistake: instead of rotating, pushing base guardians for cross-map pressure, or reinforcing a blue split, Poppers hit jungle camps and boxes while not even farming toward a specific item spike. [5:36]
- Yamato is a tempo fighter; even without his ultimate, with his team holding Rejuvenator and up souls he is useful in combat. He could have teleported and ziplined to the fight in about 12 to 15 seconds and went to hit boxes instead; meanwhile the team lost its double Rejuvenator and failed the siege. [6:06]

- The Lash and Calico in the losing fight could likely have escaped and preserved Rejuvenator if anyone had called that they could not rotate in time. [7:38]
- If anyone on the team simply reads the minimap and calls it, the team can retreat, survive and keep its Rejuvenators, which is good for them even if the fight could not be won. [8:08]
- Lowmain pushing the blue wave after respawning was exactly right, and calling for help on blue was good. [8:38]
- Zeno (Viscous) had two options, join the siege (where he arguably should already have been) or siege blue; rotating late to the fight was the weaker but acceptable choice. [8:38]
