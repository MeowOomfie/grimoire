# The Deadlock Mental Guide
channel: Midknight
date: 2026-03-27
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3LE-uzeI-Bc

## Covers
A lecture on mental and tilt: why you tilt, owning your rank, the 30-30-30 rule, the three big tilt mistakes (running it down, tilt queuing, flaming), reactive and preemptive ways to stop tilt, framing feedback as "next time", and using mental against enemies (conditioning, rage baiting the already tilted).
For any player who tilts or blames teammates.

## Claims
- 90% of the time you tilt because you can't control your teammates; you notice their mistakes more than your own because everyone in an elo has different strengths and weaknesses. [0:30]
- First fix: accept you can't control your teammates; say "I can't control my teammates" out loud to yourself, not on mic, and move on. [1:00]
- You are the rank you deserve, not held back by teammates; in very low elo the MMR system adds slight variance, but your rank is at least extremely close to what you deserve. [1:30]
- The improvement mentality is not blaming teammates and analyzing your own mistakes, for example VOD reviewing yourself; once you drop the idea that teammates keep you at your rank, you climb quickly. [2:00]
- Even with the worst teammates, focus on your own mistakes; even Eternus 6 players make mistakes every game, and blaming the team means you learned nothing from that game. [2:31]
- 30-30-30 rule (from Overwatch, probably more skewed in Deadlock because of snowballing and solo carry potential): 30% of games you can't win, 30% are in your control, 30% you can't lose; the last 10% are disconnects and the like. [3:01]
- For every game you can't win you will play one you can't lose; play the bad ones, focus on your own mistakes, go next. [3:32]
- Every game is winnable; never run it down. In lower elo, even down 60k, the enemies will take stupid fights, so look for a pick on a side lane or play around a key team fight to get back in. [4:32]
- Don't tilt queue, the gambler's mentality of queuing more to win back MMR; if you are losing, get off and take a break. [5:02]
- His hard rule: after two losses in a row, get off; rage queuing makes you play far below your ability (he once fell from Eternus to Oracle in one night). [5:02]
- Being upset at your team is fine, but flaming them on mic or in chat is trolling; it drops your chance to win that game by about 10%, since nobody plays better after being flamed. [5:32]
- Complain off comms instead (to friends in a Discord call or to stream), never to their face. [6:02]
- Reactive fix: when tilting, between games or when you die, get up and walk around your room; anything physical gets you out of your head. [7:04]
- Splash your face with cold water; other physical actions work too, like pen spinning, ring rolling, fidgets or grip strength trainers. [7:34]
- Preemptive fix: do the same small ritual before every game (like Hiko shooting teammates' heads or knifing the barrier in Valorant), or every time you die if you want to sweat; it tells your brain it is a new round and drops held tilt. [8:05]
- The mute key is the most OP button in the game: always mute anyone toxic, since 90% of the time flamers say nothing productive and will drag your mental down with theirs. [9:36]
- If you have a weak mental, mute a teammate even if they just annoy you; with a strong mental you can instead try to talk a tilting teammate down. [9:36]
- If a teammate is very toxic you can ask others to mute them too, but they will see it, so don't push a mildly tilted player over the edge. [10:06]
- Mute enemies who tea bag or type question marks; with a fragile mental you can mute all enemies at the start of the game, you lose nothing. [10:36]
- Be a positive teammate and get people talking on mic early; people are less likely to flame someone they have built a talking relationship with. [11:06]
- The best things for your mental by far: exercise regularly, eat and sleep well, and don't play 20 hours straight. [11:36]
- Don't call out a teammate's mistake as what they should have done ("Dynamo, why weren't you at mid boss"); that counts as flaming. [12:06]
- Frame feedback as what to do next time ("next time let's all be at mid boss", "next time let's Mo & Krill ult the carry", "let's focus Wraith next time"), since there will be more mid bosses and Urn fights. [12:36]
- Conditioning: enemies react to what you did earlier in the game, so on the first use of an ability people usually melee after (Billy's 2, Calico's slash, Yamato's grapple), don't melee after. [13:06]
- Then do the same thing consistently, for example light melee after the first two Calico slashes, so they start parrying by habit; then deviate, and they get caught without a parry for a lot of damage. [13:36]
- As Bebop, if the enemy always parries after your hook, don't melee until they stop parrying; as the hooked player, if you skipped a parry once, parry the next hook since he may start light meleeing. [14:07]
- Always be conscious of what the enemy thinks you will do. [14:37]
- Don't be toxic to enemies unprovoked; it is equally likely to make them lock in. [15:07]
- If an enemy question marks or tea bags you, their mental is already straining: mute them first, then tea bag or question mark them every time you kill them (for example type "ABRAMS" in all chat each kill after they mock Abrams). [15:37]
