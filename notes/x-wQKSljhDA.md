## Covers
Billy, analysis by Midknight of a high-level game by the player Together, played on a new breakable-box focused build (it turns out to be a stacked custom game, not solo queue).
Replay commentary with corrections, plus general macro: box routes, itemizing when ahead, melee mind games, urn and walker trades, mid boss value.
Recorded right before an orange-team change; patch of 2026-06-09.

## Claims
- The build is very focused on breakable boxes, and still has merit after the latest changes. [0:00]
- Grit is rushed first because Grit is strong. [0:31]
- Melee Lifesteal comes next, a slight surprise, but it lets him force good trades. [0:31]
- Mistake: after buying the item he locked into forcing a trade with Grit instead of syncing up with his Wraith first; Grit still turned a minus 600 HP trade into minus 400. [1:08]
- A Warden and Rem lane wants to play slow and wait for the breakables to spawn. [2:09]
- A smaller box route is good; start at the far end of the route and work back toward lane, which would have netted two more boxes. [2:39]
- Common mistake below the top ranks: doing a big box route when boxes spawn at 3:00 and giving up priority on the first wave. [2:39]
- They pushed hard because the enemy Rem was roaming; pushing puts the threat of a dive on the remaining laner (Warden). [3:09]
- Call: if the enemy laner is still in lane, do the box route; if he is gone, take priority and threaten a dive, then box and buy. [3:09]
- Monster Rounds make it easier to take priority and open up better dives. [3:40]
- He would not have used that many melees if he were not low; low on HP, he had to go for Melee Lifesteal value. [4:10]
- Heal creep changes: the heal now ticks up much slower, so you may not get the HP before you die. [4:41]
- After taking the tower and getting kills he bought extra health instead of Point Blank. [5:12]
- Itemizing rule: when ahead, your boons give you the damage, so itemize more defensively; when behind, itemize more offensively. [5:44]
- After killing the enemy they should have invaded the enemy tier two camp. [5:44]
- Mistake: farming defensive boxes when you have priority; take the offensive boxes. [6:14]
- Do not headshot a guardian; melees do the most damage to objectives, and heavy melees plus shooting destroy objectives even on non-melee heroes. [6:14]
- Cultist is now one of the first items in his build, and his first tier three comes early because he is very ahead. [6:44]
- When sitting just short of gun spikes he does not force anything; with the spike and Cultist up he becomes huge. [7:46]
- When very ahead, use the pressure of being fed to invade and steal enemy camps with Cultist. [8:17]
- Go to the secret shop as soon as you can. [8:17]
- Melee mind game: most players light melee twice and stop, because the enemy usually parries the third; very high level players go for a third on purpose to bait the parry. [8:17]
- People normally stop at two or four light melees, so stopping at three is rare. [8:47]
- Arctic Blast is built fairly soon in this build. [11:24]
- Giving up an urn heist to Silver was a major mistake by the losing team. [11:54]
- Kite enemies further away from their door so they cannot reach it; not stepping back and letting Silver get to the door was a mistake. [13:30]
- With no teammates nearby and on a 600 bag, do not engage; just farm toward the next item. [14:32]
- People force fights less close to urn time because they need their ultimate for the urn fight; burning it too close means it will not be up. [14:32]
- Not backing off is a way to bluff that teammates are nearby; Silver did this because she can 1v2 decently and a single nearby teammate would swing the fight. [15:34]
- Cultist on enemy camps: Cultist the creep captioned "script", because it ruins the camp for them. [16:04]
- Cultist on your own camp: never the "script" creep; Cultist one of the side creeps so whoever farms it later can melee both creeps at once. [16:04]
- A team that has stolen many camps likely has an invisible lead (he guesses about 10K) even if the game looks neutral. [17:06]
- If a team keeps its walkers alive about 5 more minutes, the other team starts to feel the lack of flex slots. [18:08]
- Taking an urn creates farm out of nothing, while the enemy's walkers stay on the map ready to take later; that is why neutral souls are generally considered better than a walker, though flex slots still matter. [18:38]
- Outside pro play, when the enemy shows three or four on one side of the map for no reason, punish it by splitting another walker. [19:38]
- If absolutely desperate for flex slots, a Hail Mary walker play makes sense around 32 minutes. [20:09]
- A one for one walker trade is worth it, but if you would lose two and mid boss is available, mid boss is worth more than a walker. [20:42]
- A good Rem ultimate is worth a one for one; after it they should have backed off instead of waking the enemy up. [20:42]
- Fighting at your own base with Rejuvenator and losing is a strange spot; punish enemies sitting on 1 HP after mid boss. [21:12]
- Kills at mid boss help you take walkers and push the lead. [21:42]
- Late, trading an urn for a walker can be worth it if your team absolutely needs a flex slot. [22:12]
- Ask the team to run the urn first; if no one will, run it yourself even on a slow or gun hero, it is better than nobody. [23:45]
- The expected value of mid boss is about two walkers. [24:16]
- Run the urn even if your team has a worse urn fight, or the enemy will run it; it is better to control the fight and the urn. [25:17]
- Whoever gets the urn gets everything. [25:49]
- Down a player against a team with Rejuvenator, a Hail Mary on mid boss beats waiting. [26:50]
- Late item choice: he went an item captioned "sub" over Indomitable, because the enemy had many silences and stuns (Paradox wall, Doorman ultimate, Rem sleep, Warden cage, Silver) and pro teams coordinate around Indomitable. [27:20]
