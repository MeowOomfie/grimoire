# The REAL Way To Get Ahead In Deadlock (Macro Guide)
channel: Metro Deadlock  date: 2026-07-02  url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Cta_ITcDlY

## Covers
Rift timing and scouting, new Urn rules and value, jungle vs fighting, split pushing, rift mega creeps, comeback resistances, Mid Boss and Rejuvenator, Viscous, Holiday, Celeste, Rem

## Claims
- This patch is no longer one big Urn fight; there is more room to split push, jungle efficiently, fight the Rift or out-macro people, and a single player's impact is much higher for carry, support or tank. [0:00]
- You can see the glow of a spawning Rift in the side lanes before the warning timer appears, up to a minute before the enemy, so being near the lane lets you guide your team there early. [1:01]
- The Rift activates whether or not anyone is around, so if you are there and the enemy is not, you get a free Rift and a big gold boost. [1:01]
- The Rift spawns on a random timer: first between minute 11 and 13, then every 6 to 8 minutes after; the randomness is intended so it does not become one predictable team fight. [1:33]
- You can hear the Rift spawning from far away, even closer to mid than to the spawn, so listen for it to know where and when it spawns. [2:03]
- The Rift is now the most valuable objective and the most money on the map, so securing it is still the number one priority, but joining the Rift fight is not always the best choice. [2:33]
- Jungle is stronger because bounties were removed from many kills, so fighting nets less money; farming priority is still lane creeps first, jungle second. [2:33]
- A carry on the side lane while the Rift spawns on the other side can skip the Rift and split push or farm jungle instead if the situation allows. [3:04]
- In one game the enemy ignored the Rift and split pushed two Walkers at once, and after the speaker's team won the Rift, the enemy was ahead in money. [3:04]
- The new Urn can still be picked up by one player and gives sprint speed; you can now use movement abilities while holding it, but you cannot drop it manually; it only resolves when deposited or when an enemy punches it off you. [3:34]
- The Urn now gives much less money and only to the carrying player, so it is viable on any character; treat it as a side quest when there is nothing else to do or when chaos elsewhere guarantees a free drop off. [4:05]
- The first Urn is roughly 950 souls and the value grows the longer the game goes. [4:05]
- Comeback mechanics apply to the Urn: if your team is behind, running it gives a huge amount; the speaker expected about 2,000 and got over 4,000 souls for a roughly 10 second run. [4:36]
- High-movement heroes like Viscous, Holiday and Celeste run the Urn very fast; the Viscous record is around 10 seconds with Majestic Leap. [4:36]
- The Urn is not necessarily a support's priority; a strong team fighter with a big ult up should join the Rift fight over running an Urn on the other side of the map. [5:07]
- Since the Rift side is random, the Urn drop off can be right at the Rift fight: bring the Urn into the fight, or fight the Rift with all your cooldowns and then pick up the Urn and run it. [5:37]
- While holding the Urn you can do anything: bait the enemy team or even attack a Walker; just do not walk up to the drop off and hand it to the enemy. [6:08]
- If the enemy is waiting at the drop off, keep holding the Urn; there is no penalty, it disappears after 45 seconds, it denies the enemy, and you deposit as soon as you see an opening. If the Urn is on your side there is basically never a downside to picking it up. [6:38]
- Do not take a team fight just to secure an Urn; it is not worth losing a fight after, and getting it in while three teammates die is always a net negative. [7:09]
- The Urn is great for heroes who want farm but are not efficient junglers, ideally with high movement like Viscous or Holiday; run it whenever it is up. [7:40]
- The number one mistake is going out of your way with the whole team to stop an Urn deposit. [7:40]
- If the enemy has the Urn and you control the drop-off lane, be there to intercept, get a melee in and deposit it yourself (instant deposit); but leaving another lane to stop it is not efficient. [8:10]
- Going from the left lane back to base and over to the right lane just to pick up the Urn is not a good play; it is a bit of extra money for one person that matters more the further behind you are. [8:41]
- A deposited Urn spawns souls that hold all its money, so secure them; the enemy team can deny and take nearly all of it if the depositor is not paying attention. [8:41]
- The Urn is the lesser objective; unless you can guarantee a free Urn, joining the Rift or split pushing is more important. [9:11]
- You can bait with the Urn by running it in circles so the enemy follows you or camps the drop off, while your team last hits and soaks money as you hold it hostage. [9:11]
- If the Rift is spawning on your side and you are already on that side lane, immediately push the wave toward the enemy side and harass them out of lane to gain map control early, without diving their Walker. [9:41]
- With map control on the Rift side it is much harder for the enemy to arrive in time, and even an even team fight is easier because you hold the ground. [10:11]
- If you are a carry, a single-target pick-off hero strong in 1v1s, or very farmed, and you are on the far side and cannot reach the Rift reasonably, split pushing or other map pressure is very strong. [10:42]
- Players are now more spread out (one runs the Urn, one jungles, one is at the Rift), which creates more openings for aggressive plays; taking map space and playing other objectives is essential this patch. [10:42]
- Winning the Rift spawns mega creeps for your team: five at base, up to 12 the further behind you are. [11:12]
- These creeps have infinite HP and push the lane very fast, especially with Rem; after a decisive Rift win, immediately push that lane and at least try to take the Walker. [11:43]
- One Rift win took the speaker's team from about a 10k deficit to a 10k lead, from the comeback Rift souls plus the free fast push from the creeps. [12:13]
- Use the Rift creeps to finish a Walker or even push the enemy base to the Shrines; they are weaker without Rem, and with a Rem on your team it is a crazy strategy. [12:45]
- The Rift still grants comeback resistance up to 35%; it used to be all or nothing (35% spirit, bullet and debuff resist when more than 10% behind), and now it scales linearly from 0% at even net worth to the full 35% at 15% behind. [13:15]
- If your team is 15% or more behind in net worth, still try to fight the Rift since it is your best fighting ground, unless the enemy is in your base; if only a bit behind or even, respect that it is an even fight with no resists. [13:45]
- More objectives on the map make it easier to come back; if your ults are on cooldown, you are not farmed enough, or you are falling behind, going jungle is a strong play, stealing enemy farm first and always pushing out waves first. [14:15]
- The speaker came back in multiple games by jungling instead of chasing the main objectives; do not underestimate efficient farming. [14:45]
- Mid Boss works the same: after a big team fight win or multiple pick offs, run it. [14:45]
- If you can take Mid Boss for free, prioritize it over the Rift; Rejuvenator is still the best buff in the game, so when up in the mid game with two or three enemies dead, take Mid Boss and let the enemy have the Rift in most cases. [15:16]
- Being proactive is better than ever: pushing lanes to the enemy side keeps them there and lets you take more Urns and Rifts, so pushing out lanes matters more than ever this patch. [15:48]
- A weak carry who needs money should tell the team they will skip the Rift fight and split push and farm instead. [15:48]
- Do not force random fights without objectives; there is always an Urn, a Rift or a Walker to split push, so play for one of them. [16:18]
- Stealing enemy jungle is great because jungle is worth a lot, but if the enemy contests you on their side with multiple heroes, it is fine to back out; be aggressive without over-forcing team fights. [16:50]
- Because the Rift spawn time is hard to read even up to tournament level, knowing when it spawns and creating the opening early is your number one way to get ahead; even a carry on the far side can call for the team to push out that wave. [17:20]
- Many Rift fights are not full 6v6s because of other things going on; ask whether you can kill a Walker in time while the Rift fight is still going. [17:20]
- Watch the map more than ever, test limits and try new choices even if they backfire; the worst thing is playing passive and scared. [17:50]
