# HOW TO FIND YOUR MMR IN DEADLOCK - And analyzing your stats
channel: Deathy
date: 2024-09-01
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YGrFz00_Gio

## Covers
A 2024 walkthrough of finding your rating on a third-party tracker and reading stats, both on the tracker and in the in-game post-match graphs (lane stats at 9 minutes, souls by source, damage breakdown). For players who want to review their own games. This is from September 2024, before ranked mode existed; the profile screens and the sites have changed since.

## Claims
- There is no ranked mode in Deadlock right now; websites estimate your rating and roughly what percentage of players you are in. [0:00]
- Don't obsess over the rating: it is an imaginary number, but a good way to know roughly which percentage of the community you are in. [0:32]
- The best rating site he found is deadlocktracker.gg: log in with Steam, open your profile, and it shows matches, overall win rate, its DLT rating and your percentile (his says top 0.01%). [1:02]
- Most of the tracker stats you shouldn't pay much attention to; use "view all heroes" for per-hero net worth per minute, damage per minute and so on. [2:05]
- KDA doesn't matter much: the game is about objectives, killing towers and the Patron, not kills. [2:05]
- Win rate is the stat that matters most. [2:35]
- Net worth per minute (souls farmed per minute) is fairly important: on most characters you should be at least 1,000 souls per minute. [2:35]
- Below 1,000 souls per minute you are probably not farming efficiently: not farming enough lanes, or not farming jungle camps when you have the chance. [3:05]
- Damage per minute and damage taken per minute don't really matter; creeps per minute can be interesting but don't pay too much attention to it. [3:05]
- In game, clicking your icon at the top right opens your profile (recently reworked) with per-hero stats; don't pay much attention to them beyond win rate for fun. [3:37]
- In a post-game screen, souls and damage are the interesting basics (who did the most damage, who had the most impact, who pushed objectives), but the first thing he opens is "view graphs". [4:08]
- The souls collected graph shows the game's situation, for example a team 6k souls ahead at 10 minutes that kept and grew the lead, an early lead converted into a win. [4:38]
- The souls per minute graph by player shows who was most farmed and who farmed most efficiently. [4:38]
- He doesn't really look at the kills and deaths graph. [4:38]
- The healing graph is interesting if you play a healer like Kelvin or Viscous. [5:08]
- The graph to look at most closely is lane stats, which compares the laning stage at 9 minutes, when lane is usually ending: kills and total souls of your lane against the enemy lane. [5:08]
- Example: his lane had 19,400 souls to their 13,800 at 9 minutes, about a 6,000 soul lead; hovering a portrait highlights that player's share (he had 10,000 souls, five kills, 59 last hits, one deny, 5,600 player damage). [5:39]
- The lane stats graph also shows when each objective fell, for example the enemy tier one tower at 3:32, tier two at 7:24 and base Guardians at 23:42. [6:11]
- Use the lane stats graph to see quickly which lane was lost, for example a teammate down 2,000 souls at 9 minutes with far fewer last hits. [6:42]
- Lanes in the graph are predetermined by the game, so if players swap lanes the graph gets messed up. [7:13]
- Look at why a lane was won or lost: an even-souls lane can come from the enemy getting more denies (17 against 15) even while your side got more kills and player damage. [7:13]
- Compare denies, player damage and matchup per lane, for example having more than double the enemy's player damage. [7:43]
- Souls by source shows where your souls came from; look at it in games where you farmed well to learn how you got rich. [8:13]
- Example: 35k souls at 26 minutes, almost 1,400 per minute, with almost 15,000 from troopers, 5,800 from enemy kills, 5,400 from neutrals and 5,300 from objectives, so troopers were the main source. [8:13]
- Heroes differ: Seven, who jungles a lot, gets a bit less from troopers and a bit more from neutrals and kills; Vindicta gets a lot more from enemy kills than average. [8:43]
- For almost every hero troopers are the main source of souls, which tells you what to prioritize. [9:13]
- The damage breakdown splits damage into troopers, enemy players, neutrals (jungle) and objectives; use it to see which teammates are not hitting creeps enough (his lane did 47,000 and 55,000 trooper damage against 20,000 to 27,000 for others). [9:13]
- Damage dealt by type breaks damage down per ability, useful when testing a new build or heavy investment in one ability or spirit (his Pocket game: top damage from one ability, then Barrage, then gun, then Enchanter's Satchel). [10:13]
- Healing by type shows what healed you (his Pocket: mostly Spirit Lifesteal, some Diviner's Kevlar). [10:13]
- Damage to players and damage from players graphs are not really useful. [10:43]
- Don't judge teammates by KD alone; check objective damage, split pushing and impact, and don't flame teammates with a negative KD. [10:43]
- Example: a Viscous with a bad KD had a fantastic game itemizing around saving people with his Cube and going in with his ultimate. [11:14]
