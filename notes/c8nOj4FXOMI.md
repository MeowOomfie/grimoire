# The MOST IMPORTANT Objective in Deadlock
channel: Natan
date: 2025-09-21
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c8nOj4FXOMI

## Covers
Map control explained by an Eternus 6 player: a definition, the imaginary line through waves and heroes, why trooper waves are the most important objective (hero positions, zipline length, vision, kill chances, time), what lets you push waves further, how minimap vision works and how to manipulate it, and the snowball loop that extends a lead.
For players who find map control intangible and want to extend leads and end games faster.

## Claims
- Map control is which part of the map each team controls, decided by the location of trooper waves and heroes, and wave location influences where heroes are; it is constantly changing. [0:30]
- You win by taking the enemy Patron, which requires controlling most of the map; controlling only your side never reaches it. [1:00]
- More map control means the enemy has less space, you have farm they can't take, and they can only be in a smaller area, which is information. [1:00]
- Map control tells you where you are allowed to be; being in an area you don't control is risky and you might die. [1:30]
- Visualize map control by drawing an imaginary horizontal line through your waves and heroes; at the start of the game it is a flat neutral line. [1:30]
- Taking a Guardian and pushing the wave up bends the line so you control a larger part of the map. [2:02]
- Each hero pulls on the line, and stronger heroes and more heroes in one location warp it further. [2:02]
- If your whole team is in base, the line is warped back even where a wave is pushed, so a lone hero there (the example Wraith) is beyond your line in enemy territory and gets punished. [2:33]
- Structures have a safe aura: a Walker gives resists nearby and deals damage, so your wave pushed into the enemy Walker does not mean you control the Walker area. [3:03]
- An enemy team squeezed into a small area probably has many heroes there, which warps the line back past your pushed wave and makes pushing that wave risky. [3:34]
- In the example most of the farm on the map falls inside your map control territory, so you have access to more farm and more information. [4:05]
- The longer the game goes, the less waves matter and the more heroes matter for map control. [4:05]
- The mid wave gives the most map control, because a side wave only has stuff to do on one side of it. [4:35]
- A far-ahead enemy team with mid control stacked in one place warps the line far onto your side even if you have pushed two side waves. [4:35]
- Rule of thumb: stay on the same line as your waves and teammates. [5:06]
- Running past your wave is dangerous because you have no information about where enemies are on that part of the map; you are running in blind. [5:06]
- Getting kills past the wave is often pointless; low rank games last longer because players ignore waves, run past them to fight, then have to go back and push the wave to take an objective. [5:36]
- Staying on the same line lets teammates help or trade on the other side of the map and creates more pressure. [5:36]
- The out-of-position Wraith should back off, take some camps or buy an item while teammates return, then push up together with the Mo & Krill and Calico while Pocket and Drifter push elsewhere. [6:07]
- On the same line, if the enemy jumps one of you, your team has a numbers advantage elsewhere and can take an objective as a trade. [6:07]
- If the enemy also stays on their line, you get vision cheats: even unseen, they are probably behind their line, and finding one on your side would be unusual. [6:39]
- Trooper waves are a time-limited, dynamic objective: they spawn every 30 seconds, eventually die, constantly move, and taking them gives an advantage. [7:09]
- Troopers are the highest soul income source, so most of your souls in a game come from them; they also remove backdoor protection and damage structures. [7:09]
- For map control, waves decide hero positions, decide the length of your ziplines, give vision (people must show on them), give chances to kill (people must show up), and clearing or pushing them gives you time. [7:39]
- Clearing a wave gives you time for things that are not time-limited: a jungle camp, another Guardian, or looking for a kill. [7:39]
- Pushing a wave further lengthens your zipline, improves vision by shrinking the area the enemy can be in, and gives more time before it returns to your structure. [8:10]
- Pushing further doesn't improve your chance to kill, but a kill further up the map is worth more: killing an Abrams at a forward wave lets you damage the Walker or take camps, unlike killing him while he hits your objective. [8:10]
- When a wave meets another wave or hits a structure, at least one hero should show on it, or the troopers kill each other and both teams lose the souls. [8:40]
- If one team clears its wave, it pushes into the other team's structure, so someone from that team must show up too; every wave decides the location of at least two heroes. [9:10]
- Ideally you have wave priority: get the push into the enemy Guardian so enemies must respond and are busy, giving you vision of them and time to act elsewhere, such as stealing boxes or jungle camps. [9:40]
- Be back when the wave returns to your lane to keep the push, or at worst collect it at your Guardian. [10:10]
- The game becomes turn based: after pushing into the enemy Walker you have until the wave returns to your Guardian, then you must show there and the enemy gets their turn. [10:10]
- Trade-off: returning to clear every wave keeps the push but leaves less time to roam far (for example to help another Guardian); waiting for the wave to reach your Guardian gives more time but hands the enemy map control. [10:41]
- Having the first turn is generally better: take a camp and keep pressure, then when a chance appears, commit to staying away longer for a Guardian while still getting back in time to defend yours. [11:11]
- Going two players to kill someone past the wave while the other lane pushes means you take Guardian damage even though you got a kill; push waves first, then act, stay parallel to your waves and be back for the next one. [11:41]
- Destroying structures pushes waves further: it gives extra slots (Flex Slots) and souls that make you stronger, and more time and space on the map. [12:11]
- With the Guardian dead and the wave pushed to the Walker, you can walk deep because you know the enemies are collecting the wave, and you have more time before it returns. [12:11]
- The easiest way to be stronger is a numbers advantage in one area: show up two against the one clearing the wave, push it, trap him under the structure, and take the first move there. [12:42]
- Being 10k souls up on someone at a wave, or having just hit a strong power spike, gives you wave priority and lets you force them off. [13:12]
- An inherently stronger hero wins priority: a side lane Mirage against a lone Dynamo pushes him off and clears first. [13:12]
- Some heroes cheat this: Seven can clear the wave despite a numbers disadvantage, and Ivy can clear from long range even when weaker. [13:12]
- Knowing enemy locations lets you push waves further even when weaker: if the whole enemy team is on the other side of the map, nobody stops you. [13:43]
- Especially in lower ranks people stack up too much, so not stacking with your team and pushing elsewhere cheats you extra map control. [13:43]
- Vision comes from map awareness and game sense, more than communication; from the end of the laning phase, constantly check the minimap. [14:14]
- Stare at the minimap while walking, clearing waves, shooting camps or taking a Sinner's Sacrifice, to track teammates' plans and spot enemies. [14:14]
- You show on the minimap if you hit a trooper, get hit by a trooper, or are seen by an enemy. [14:44]
- Ping enemies when you see them: a ping gives you map hacks on them, so pinging a Holliday seen on the mid wave lets you watch where she walks and react. [14:44]
- You can also hear enemies, so call out things like hearing Lady Geist taking a Sinner's Sacrifice or a jungle camp. [15:14]
- Read and predict enemy movement: a Wraith who just cleared a wave will probably go back and clear jungle camps. [15:14]
- Doing this for all six enemies is daunting and comes with experience; people are likely on the same line as their waves and teammates, and pushing past their waves is generally a mistake. [15:45]
- Manipulate vision: after clearing a wave, don't walk or run off to a side lane in front of the enemy hero clearing their wave, because they will call it out. [15:45]
- Don't always show on waves: let one person clear and sit hidden nearby, waiting to jump an enemy who shows up. [16:15]
- Four players all shooting a wave to take a Guardian tells the enemy the plan, they send everyone and you get a 4v4 stalemate (a deadlock). [16:45]
- Instead show one player on the wave; when the enemy responds with one, appear with four and take the Guardian while they are taking jungle camps. [16:45]
- You don't want enemies to know where your entire team is. [17:15]
- An enemy squeezed into little map control has less farm and tends to show with more people on one wave, giving you more information. [17:15]
- Extending a lead is a feedback loop (snowballing): being stronger gives wave priority and kills, which take objectives, which push waves further, giving map control, farm and vision, which make you stronger again. [17:46]
- Higher rank games end faster than lower rank games because players understand map control and use it to snowball. [17:46]
- Holliday is a menace in high ranks and has historically been high priority in competitive play because she is already stronger at the start and jump starts the snowball. [18:48]
- Use the space you create: play on the edge of your map control to choke the enemy; taking a Guardian, pushing to the Walker and then farming your own camps is useless; steal their stuff and keep pressure. [18:48]
- Role changes how you use map control: a farming hero pushes the wave and takes the forward camps; a strong roaming hero with kill setup pushes and goes to help at another Guardian. [19:19]
- High rank snowball example: mid wins hard, the Guardian falls, the wave is pushed to the Walker so an enemy is stuck clearing it, the winners take camps, enemies fear face checking, and two players appear to take another Guardian. [19:49]
- A team that is stronger with better vision can defend a Guardian with three against a four-player commit, freeing others to take the last Guardian, then take all the farm and structures, the Urn and the Mid Boss, and end the game. [20:20]
