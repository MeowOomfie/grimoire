# When should you actually fight Rift? | With Eternus Example
channel: Deathy
date: 2026-10-14
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fat6LTvoJMk

## Covers
When to fight the Unstable Rift and when to give it up: Rift soul values, comeback resistances, options for hard winning, slightly winning, neutral and losing teams, power spikes, ult economy, best Rift items, and how Mid Boss changes the Rift after 25 minutes.
Second half walks through six Rift fights from the coach's own high rank games (Baba, Celeste). For mid game macro decisions at any rank.

## Claims
- The Unstable Rift usually spawns between 11 and 13 minutes, then every 6 to 8 minutes after; it is one of the most important mid game objectives. [0:00]
- The first Rift gives 1,482 souls; it does not scale before 13 minutes, so any Rift before 13 minutes is 1,482 souls. [0:30]
- After 13 minutes the Rift's value grows by 222 souls per minute; a Rift at 26 minutes gives 4,368 souls. [1:02]
- The first and second Rifts are always worth fewer souls than a Walker; losing either Rift but taking a Walker in exchange is a souls win. [1:02]
- The Rift has comeback bonuses for the team that is behind: bonus spirit resist, bullet resist and debuff resist, faster capture, and more souls. [1:33]
- Max comeback resistances start at 10% at minute zero; at the first Rift they are usually 21 to 23%, and late game up to 40% if significantly behind. [2:03]
- To get the max resistances the team net worth gap must be 15%. [2:03]
- Example: first Rift at 13 minutes, 50,000 vs 60,000 souls (roughly a 20% gap), the behind team gets 23% bullet and spirit resist (the coach notes his math was not double-checked). [2:33]
- Example: at 19 minutes, 90,000 vs 95,000 (5% gap), the behind team gets only 10% bullet and spirit resist. [3:04]
- In game, just remember: if you are behind you get resistances at the Rift, and if you are winning the team fighting you gets them; weigh that when deciding to fight. [3:04]
- Hard winning means up 15% net worth or more; normal winning is around 5 to 10%. [3:35]
- When winning, the enemy's Rift resistances can often nullify your soul advantage. [4:05]
- Winning option A: fight the enemy before the Rift spawns or zone them off it. There is a spawn warning and they must walk to the Rift, and the resistances only apply around the Rift. [4:35]
- Getting one or two kills before the Rift spawns usually makes the fight good for you even with their resistances, for example fighting 6v4. [5:05]
- Winning option B: give up the Rift and split push; best when you lack the map control to zone them, and only when split push objectives exist. [5:05]
- These winning options apply mostly to the first two Rifts; after that the Rift is a different objective. [5:35]
- Winning option C (hard winning only): let them capture the Rift, which removes their resistances, then fight right after while still far ahead on net worth. [5:35]
- Option C is especially good when Mid Boss is available right after: give the Rift, win the fight, take Mid Boss. [6:05]
- Winning option D (hard winning only, not recommended for most players): fight and stall out the Rift so it times out and the souls pop up on the bridge, denying their main comeback mechanic. [6:35]
- Danger of option D: if you lose that fight the enemy gets the Rift plus a ton of souls for killing you, and the game flips. [7:05]
- With only a normal lead, option D is not possible because the resistances are too much, and option C is unlikely because their Rift souls may close the gap. [7:35]
- Another winning play: force Mid Boss and give up the Rift, for example on the second Rift when up 10 to 15% and they have something like Rat King ult. [8:06]
- Mid Boss gives 3,000 souls plus three Rejuvenators, about the same souls as the second Rift. [8:36]
- Downside of trading Mid Boss for Rift: you will probably lose your Walker in the Rift troopers' lane, since even one enemy pushing with them takes it. [8:36]
- In a neutral game (within about 5% net worth) either fight the Rift or split push. [9:06]
- Never give up the Rift and do nothing in return; that is a misplay. Take Mid Boss, push a Walker, something. [9:06]
- Factor one for neutral Rifts: did you or several teammates just hit a power spike? [9:36]
- A power spike is maxing an ability, putting the last point in an ability, or buying an item of 3,200 souls or more. [9:36]
- Factor two: ult economy; anyone can count that they have six ults and you have two. [9:36]
- Factor three: team fight composition and ult quality; team fight heavy ults like Seven and Rat King help much more in a Rift fight than in a random skirmish. [10:06]
- If you are unsure whether to fight, go for it and then analyze why it worked or failed. [10:36]
- If you know a Rift fight is coming and have 1,600 souls, Restorative Locket and Healbane are among the best Rift fight items for heroes that like them. [11:06]
- Restorative Locket gets many charges from abilities used in the Rift; the coach has used two full Lockets, or one and a half, in one Rift fight, giving a lot of healing and stamina. [11:06]
- Healbane is good for its anti-heal and its healing: with up to six deaths in a Rift fight, being the only one with Healbane gives you the heal from every kill, and the proc can bait enemies who think you are low. [11:36]
- Radiant Regeneration is also good for Rift fights but more situational; far fewer heroes can buy it. [12:06]
- When losing, whether by 5% or 20% net worth, just fight the Rift; it is your best chance to come back about 99% of the time. [12:06]
- There are exceptions when losing, especially later in the game, and other plays usually mean you are in a dire situation anyway. [12:36]
- Past about 25 minutes the Rift itself matters less than winning the fight, because a won fight leads into Mid Boss. [13:06]
- If Mid Boss is dead, traditional Rift logic applies; if it is alive at 20 to 25 minutes, respawn timers are long enough to go Mid Boss after the Rift. [13:06]
- The first two Rifts usually do not lead into Mid Boss in matchmaking, especially at lower ranks, but the third Rift easily can. [13:36]
- Winning Mid Boss is a much bigger advantage than the roughly 4,000 souls a Rift gives around 25 minutes. [13:36]
- Example 1: kills 24 to 25, slightly ahead on souls (under 10%), two picks just taken (Apollo and Deadman Danny as captioned), even ult count with Yamato, Haze and a strong team fight ult coming: they fight the Rift. [14:06]
- Rift troopers are very strong, especially early; you can often push the Walker with them. [15:07]
- In example 1 the enemy did not contest and was on the other side of the map, so they took the Rift, pushed the Walker with the troopers, lost Yamato but took the Walker and left: get your thing, get out. [15:38]
- Example 2: up 6 to 7,000 souls with map control, their Wraith starts the Rift, but they have zero ults and the enemy has comeback resistances, so they give it up. [15:38]
- In example 2 they got nothing in return, which is the mistake to avoid, but cutting losses beat everyone dying in an unwinnable Rift fight. [16:40]
- Example 3: Mid Boss spawning in 1 minute and up 12 to 13%, they try to catch the enemy before the Rift; with Lash ult they chase Venator. [17:10]
- In example 3 they pushed aggressively because if the enemy reached the Rift and won the fight, it would be Mid Boss for them; zoning them off let Baba capture the Rift and they later converted Mid Boss. [17:40]
- Example 4: every ult up, enemy missing Lash and Deadman Danny ults, up 2,000 souls, but nearly all their net worth is on their Wraith. [18:40]
- When most of your net worth is on one player, fighting the Rift is iffy, because one carry is easy to shut down in a big team fight. [18:40]
- Still, generally fight the Rift in that spot unless you really think you lose the team fight; they had strong team fight ults (Celeste, Rat King, Infernus, Baba). [19:10]
- While waiting for teammates in a Rift fight, play to stall time: take aggro and get enemies chasing you instead of focusing the Rift. [19:40]
- Radiant Regeneration paid off: Baba's ult hitting four people gave a lot of Radiant Regeneration healing. [20:11]
- Example 4 was lost; sometimes the correct call loses because a heavily funded carry or a poor player lacks impact. [20:11]
- Example 5: behind 6,000 souls (about 10%), they fight the Rift; the enemy knew not to fight it and split pushed instead. [20:42]
- In your elo enemies may fight you on a comeback Rift anyway; 100% take that fight. [21:13]
- When the enemy split pushes during your Rift, use the Rift troopers to pressure a Walker, or you fall too far behind on souls. [21:13]
- Do not send all six to siege a Walker: more heroes near a Walker give it more resistance, so six can be slower than two, and you lose map farm. [21:43]
- In example 5 two players took the Walker; the lead stayed at 6,000 souls after a minute had passed, which the behind team is happy with. [22:13]
- Example 6 at 19 minutes: behind 14,000 souls (a bit over 10%), a comeback Rift, so they fight it. [22:43]
- Before fighting a Rift, get wave priority so waves push elsewhere; the coach cast his 3 on an even mid wave just enough to push it forward instead of clearing it fully, then went straight to the Rift. [22:43]
- In example 6 the enemy gave up the Rift because of the resistances; the team got a kill and tried to snowball. [23:44]
- After a comeback Rift even one or two kills makes a big economic difference, and with Mid Boss available they might enable Mid Boss. [23:44]
- Chasing with map momentum after the Rift got kills (Sinclair), map pressure, the Urn and farm, cutting the gap from about 12% to under 10%. [24:15]
