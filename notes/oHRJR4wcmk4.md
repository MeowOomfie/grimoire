# Why you are LOSING fights that you should NEVER lose
channel: Deathy
date: 2025-08-13
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oHRJR4wcmk4

## Covers
Knowing your role in a teamfight: hero roles (brawler, anti-carry, backline damage, counter-initiator, initiator/pick, frontliner), who to target, how your play changes once ult or key abilities are spent, and how items change the way you fight.
Examples from Deathy's games on Yamato, Lash, Paradox and Lady Geist. For players who lose fights they go into because they press buttons on whoever is closest.

## Claims
- Fights usually go badly because people fail to understand their role in the fight; learn your hero's general role first. [0:00]
- Hero roles are a broad guideline; many heroes fill several roles. Yamato is a brawler but could be an initiator since she can start fights, though she doesn't really generate picks; Mo & Krill is a frontline brawler and also an initiator because he can start picks. [0:32]
- Yamato has two identities, brawler and anti-carry. All her abilities (Crimson Slash, grapple, Power Slash) are short range without upgrades, so she naturally wants to fight close range. [1:02]
- Yamato's ultimate gives resistance and cleanses all debuffs, making her an ideal brawler who can get in, tank, ult and tank more. [1:33]
- Yamato's anti-carry part comes from Power Slash (first upgrade gives bullet resist while channeling) and Crimson Slash (first upgrade gives a fire rate slow, 20 to 50%), which make her good against backline damage heroes like Wraith and Infernus. [2:04]
- As Yamato facing Shiv, Abrams and Infernus, don't go on the first hero in front of you just because he's close; ignore Shiv and Abrams, go straight on Infernus and use all your abilities on him. [2:34]
- Yamato is a tunnel vision hero: she is very good at putting all her abilities on one hero, even while getting shot by others. [3:05]
- Wraith is a backline damage hero, not a burst hero, and doesn't need to focus a specific target; in the same fight she should damage the Shiv and Abrams in front of her and kite them back if they go on her, not reach for the Infernus. [3:35]
- A backline damage dealer's goal is to deal damage for the entire fight to whoever is easiest to hit, dealing first with the heroes trying to stop her doing damage. [4:06]
- Lash is a counter-initiator: he benefits when a fight is already happening, for example going in for his ult while an allied Shiv distracts the enemies. Contrary to popular belief Lash does not like to be first in; he likes to be the second or third hero. [4:06]
- Yamato example: initiate (zipline tech into Power Slash), then immediately identify their damage dealer and grapple onto her. Look at souls: the McGinnis had the highest souls, so focus her; Infernus would have been another great target. [4:36]
- Lash is an in-and-out hero: don't fully commit most of the time, keep some stamina and resources in the bank to disengage. [5:07]
- As Lash, hide between ability cycles (for example under the bridge when Seven pops his ult), go in every time Flog and slam are off cooldown, and back up to reset when they are down. [5:37]
- Lash fights are often poke fights with no ult setup: keep going in whenever abilities are up without tunnel visioning anyone; emulate this play style. [6:07]
- Pocket has a very similar play style: go in when you have cloak and suitcase, don't go in without your cooldowns. [6:07]
- Paradox is an initiator/pick hero, but initiate on people you can actually kill. Around the Urn, don't ult the Abrams in front of you; when the Infernus runs, swap him because he is one of their damage dealers. Wraith or Haze would also be excellent targets. [6:37]
- Swap people you can kill, not just anyone. [7:07]
- Paradox: if an enemy Wraith is caught but your team may lack the damage to clean her up, swap her in. [7:07]
- Once Paradox has used swap, change to an annoying play style: shoot, kite backwards and create space and problems for the enemy. [7:07]
- Your hero's play style in a fight changes depending on whether you have your ult or certain abilities; think about that during fights. [7:37]
- Lady Geist is a frontliner who can tank a lot of damage: with swap, decent health and low mobility, don't sit too far back; stay in the front line so enemies have to go through you to damage your team, ready to take damage and create space. [7:37]
- Mistake (Lady Geist): down two players and 30k while the enemy sieged, Deathy went for an Infernus his Yamato was already on, ended up isolated in the backline with no mobility and died immediately. He should have played the front line, not committed to the kill, fought the many targets on the left side of the minimap and bought time for teammates to respawn; the Yamato was fine diving deep because she had ult. [8:39]
- Up 12,000 going for the Urn, identify the biggest threat (Dynamo with Black Hole), catch him isolated and kill him before he can use Black Hole; the fight looks good once he goes down. [9:10]
- After getting the key pick as Lady Geist, put your body in front of the enemy damage dealers so they can't do whatever they want, playing around swap, Life Drain and a tanky build. [9:40]
- Play around your itemization: offensive items like Burst Fire, Toxic Bullets, Tesla Bullets, Berserker, Crippling Headshot and Glass Cannon should change how you play fights, if only slightly. [10:10]
- Toxic Bullets is an anti-tank and anti-healing item: shoot the tanky heroes who heal. Against Warden, shoot him before or during his ult to apply it and reduce his healing. [10:41]
- Tesla Bullets is really good at farming, so if it is one of your first items, farm a decent amount. [10:41]
- With Glass Cannon you deal a lot of damage but are squishier: play further back, shoot whoever you can and kite. [10:41]
- With Colossus you want to be tanky and get in: create space for your team and use Colossus at the right times to tank as much damage as possible. [11:12]
- Before moving on in a game, ask whether an item changes how you want to play or what your hero can do; some items are farming spikes, some let you dive the backline, some let you win 1v1s you couldn't before. [11:12]
- Counterspell example: as Infernus versus Wraith, the 1v1 is hard because she ults you; with Counterspell, run at her, bait her ult, Counterspell it and win the 1v1. [11:43]
- With Warp Stone against Yamato, hold it until the very end of her Power Slash and you can usually dodge it. [12:15]
- Fury Trance gives 40% spirit resist and fire rate, letting you stand up to heroes that do a lot of spirit damage to you. [12:15]
- With over 150 items, ask of each one: what does it give me that I did not have before, and what does it let me do against the enemy heroes that I could not do before. [12:45]
