# How to COME BACK from LOSING lanes - A guide
channel: Deathy  date: 2024-09-05  url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z-N8WE2Y2fk

## Covers
Coming back from a lost lane: measuring a lost lane (Lane stats graph, soul difference at 9 minutes), spend souls on 500 and 1200 items (Headshot Booster, High Velocity Mag over Heroic Aura; Wraith Tesla Bullets exception), ganking other lanes at a numbers advantage, farming when far behind (push lanes then jungle), objectives as comeback souls, denying souls at a lost Walker; examples against Infernus, Yamato, Haze, Paradox, Abrams

## Claims
- To check if you lost lane, click View Graphs, Lane Stats, then your lane, and look at the soul difference at 9 minutes; generally losing lane means about a 1,000 soul difference or more, and the bigger it is the harder the comeback. [0:31]
- In his example he was already 1,000 behind the Infernus at 4:45, an extremely significant early advantage because the Infernus had his ultimate and he did not, and the Infernus then ganked a nearby lane to give it an advantage too. [1:01]
- When behind, you cannot afford to be greedy with items; spend your souls, buying as many 500 items as you need to be somewhat useful in lane. [1:32]
- If your opponent has a 500 soul lead but has not spent it because they are saving for greedy items, while you have spent all yours on 500 items, they probably will not feel much stronger than you. [1:32]
- He bought Headshot Booster and High Velocity Mag, applied as much pressure as he could and got a kill that was part luck and part taking the opportunity; saving for Heroic Aura would have meant never getting that kill. [2:02]
- Lesson one: always spend your souls if behind and do not save for greedy items; buy a bunch of smaller items, 500s and 1200s. [2:02]
- Exceptions exist: as Wraith, a lot of the time you would still rather save for Tesla Bullets. [2:35]
- If your own lane is lost, do not hesitate to look for opportunities in other lanes where enemies might be overextended; he ganked a lane pushing his Walker and killed the Haze and then the Paradox. [2:35]
- Always take fights at some advantage: in another lane that means a numbers advantage, in your own lane a creep or money advantage; when behind you will not have the latter, so fight somewhere else to force an advantage. [3:06]
- At 1K behind you can usually still find opportunities; at more than 2K (he was 2.5K behind a Yamato) you cannot really fight your lane opponent, because even spending your souls wisely is not enough. [3:38]
- That far behind, push lanes as much as possible and only farm the jungle when your lanes are pushed; the main focus is farm, not plays. [3:38]
- He harassed the Yamato a little but knew he could not kill her, so he tried to get as much farm as possible and avoided fights, being too far behind to realistically impact them. [4:09]
- It does not hurt to show up to fights near your farm or objectives, even if you are mostly there for the wave. [4:41]
- Farm waves as fast as possible, shooting creeps and using abilities; many people do not shoot creeps fast enough or do not use abilities, and pushing waves fast lets you do other things. [4:41]
- After pushing the wave while behind, go jungle; if you were ahead, instead keep going and push your advantage onto a tower. [5:11]
- Repeat that pattern until the enemy is on an objective on one side of the map, then play the other side and pressure an objective there. [5:11]
- Objectives give quite a significant amount of souls and are one of the best comeback mechanics if you are allowed to take a lot of them. [5:11]
- Take any chance to push Towers, even if it risks being a little aggressive and dying; with many enemies on the right side of the map fighting his Mo & Krill, he judged taking a Tower likely safe even with a Yamato there. [5:41]
- Keep looking for any objective you can pressure for souls, for example a hurt Tower guarded only by a weak Haze; despite losing lane quite hard he was only 1K behind the Abrams. [6:12]
- Understand when your objectives are gone: when the Yamato and Abrams pushed his badly hurt Walker and there was likely no way to defend it, he still showed up to deny as many souls as he could. [6:12]
- Denying those souls got him about 400 souls, about one wave's worth; denying 400 to 500 souls also takes them from the enemy, effectively about a 1,000 gold swing. [6:42]
- This matters most when you are significantly behind, because many people do not secure the souls after they take objectives; it is one of the few comeback mechanics you have. [6:42]
- In that game they came out of the laning stage over 4,600 souls behind (captioned "46 600") with seven kills to zero; if he can come back to only a 1 to 2K deficit in the mid game, you can make comebacks in your games too. [7:14]
- Once in the mid game, standard mid game principles apply (he points to his how to gain a lead in the mid game video). [7:14]
