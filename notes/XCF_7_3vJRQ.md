# Learning How Deadlock Works: The Laning Phase | 1-on-1 Coaching #1/4
channel: PeerContent  date: 2026-10-13  url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XCF_7_3vJRQ

## Covers
One-on-one coaching of a one-month position one (M1) player on Mirage and Wraith (with Haze, Rat King, Bebop teammates; Seven, Paige, Shiv, Drifter, Infernus opponents): anchor vs roamer, early lane timers (1:30, 2:30, 3:00, 4:30, 7:30, 9:30, 9:47), push first wave and buy, wave three ability spike, denies, orb secures and ammo, minion damage, T1 camp respawn, tower damage resist by minute, Sinner's Sacrifice, Urn spawn sides and who takes it, side lane 1v1s, counting numbers, solo kill and walker bounties, never two plays at once, valuing towers, following named builds

## Claims
- The coach starts every new student at the laning phase, then decision-making early, because many players throw their game to help someone else when they should not. [2:02]
- Each lane has an anchor and a roamer; at the 3 minute wave when the boxes spawn, the position one normally sits in the wave while the other player roams. [5:05]
- The favored box invade spots are the theater on one side and the haunted lot on the other. [5:35]
- If your lane partner will not leave and you are permanently two-manning the lane, it is better for you to go do the box runs instead; otherwise you both fall behind and miss your ult spikes because the enemy got boxes and you did not. [5:35]
- Deadlock is a timer-based game: the timers basically tell you where to be most of the time. [7:06]
- Default rule: push the first wave, insta buy, then get aggressive; you should already be bought by the 1:30 wave. [7:06]
- If you push into the enemy aggressively first, they buy their T1 item because they are pushed under their shop, and they end up an item above you. [7:37]
- The 1:30 wave is about getting wave prio, because you need it to take your camp; the student's camp had been up 40 seconds untouched. [7:37]
- You will get baited into fights a lot, even in high ranks, so deciding whether to fight matters more in this game than most. [8:08]
- VOD review checklist item one: did you buy after the first wave. [8:40]
- Checklist item two: secures; do you actually aim and shoot the orb after the last hit, or walk off to clear. [8:40]
- Ammo management: shooting an orb gives the ammo back, so with one bullet left you can shoot one orb and still shoot the next; miss it and you miss both. [9:11]
- Most lower-level players, and players in general, take too much damage from minions, and minions do more damage than the players do; this is on the checklist too. [10:14]
- The student's lane did not take its camp or neutral boxes until around 4:30 and was not set up for the buff; the fix is knowing which wave to shove when. [12:46]
- The first wave matters to shove so you can buy and come back; the second wave matters less because clearing it gives no advantage beyond poke. [12:46]
- The third wave is super important: clearing it (one or two minions, depending on denies) gives your second ability, which is a spike, so push it quickly, get level two, and force the fight before they get level two and force it on you. [13:16]
- Lane spikes to track are item spikes, ability spikes and soul spikes. [13:16]
- If your partner has not left lane, just ask "are you running boxes or am I?"; most times it should be them. [13:46]
- The student's own lane build: Extra Regen first for sustain, then Headshot Booster because the enemy had better clear and Seven has a big head. [14:16]
- Coach correction: as a new player, follow a named build, learn the other fundamentals first, and come back to builds later; with an analytical mind, items will come quickly. [15:18]
- During your learning period, always be aggressive in lane, even in matchups you supposedly should not win. [15:48]
- Haze and Mirage actually win very hard against Paige and Seven. [15:48]
- The more aggressive you are, the more you learn about your spikes, and that always comes with wave prio. [16:19]
- In laning phase Mirage most times wants mid lane, so he anchors mid while the team roams; outside laning it depends on team comp. [16:19]
- Mirage falls off later in the game; Wraith is the closer and the better pick than Mirage for a ranked grinder who wants to carry. [17:51]
- Wraith's early game is in a fine state now; enemies only snowball her if you misplay. [17:51]
- Wraith has only a few lane spikes: when you have all your cards at once, when you have two points in cards, plus item and level spikes; play around them and do not get over aggressive afterwards, and you reach late game to close it out. [18:21]
- In the first two waves, even a single deny delays the enemy item spike and level spike by quite a bit, so you can fight wave three even harder; do not be afraid to aim for denies, especially when you have space. [19:22]
- Lane timer guideline: buy after the first wave by 1:30; 1:30 wave push into box; 2:30 wave push into deciding anchor or roamer. [19:52]
- T1 camps have a 1 minute 25 second respawn; if you go to clear at 2 minutes you usually finish around 2:05 because you take time to melee it; he notes the T1 respawn at 2:25 to 2:30. [19:52]
- 4:30 wave: take wave prio into the buff. [20:22]
- Favor pushing the wave first and then getting aggressive and poking. [20:22]
- At every stage of the game, pushing the wave is the most valuable thing you can do: it gives the most souls and map space. [20:52]
- There are only two forms of vision in Deadlock, minion waves and height; a pushed wave shows where the enemy will go, since someone has to pick it up, so you can account for one enemy on the map. [20:52]
- Waves are about 25 seconds apart (it used to be 30); once you clear, you have 25 seconds to clear camps, rotate to a fight or impact a different lane, so push the wave and then rotate. [21:23]
- As position one you are often weak-sided, defending a walker alone while the enemy stacks the other side; your whole job then is not to die, safely push out waves, absorb farm and become the scaler, which Wraith loves. [21:23]
- Sinner's Sacrifice (captioned "senders"): at 7:30 push the wave and you can secure the 8 minute one, but you cannot do doubles or go straight to bell tower, because each one takes 320 HP and forces a reset; do a single one and go back to the wave. [21:53]
- Tower damage resist: base 50% at start, 25% at 3 minutes, no bonus resist at 6 minutes, then 25% bonus at 9 and 50% at 12 (as he stated it). [22:23]
- Most lanes start damaging towers around 6 minutes when the shred is gone and towers drop quickly; one death then has a lot of impact, though a better player can beat it earlier. [22:53]
- Around 5 minutes any kill equals a tower, because what is captioned "fast ships" does not come online until 6 minutes, so players reset slowly; getting someone to 1 HP and forcing a reset is just as valuable as the kill. [23:54]
- When the Urn is in play, no camps are to be taken. [24:26]
- The Urn is almost always taken by the roamers. [24:56]
- The M1 takes the Urn only in a rare case, such as having killed the tower fast and pushed the wave to the walker with nothing more to pressure; otherwise the M1 stays in the side lane keeping pressure up. [24:56]
- If they take the Urn across the map and you see a full five-on-five fight while you are one-on-one, take that 1v1, assuming some advantage (ult, income, item spike); this is the most impactful thing an M1 can do to win games. [25:26]
- 9:30: push the wave, buy and set up, so that any fight after that you have an item spike on enemies who likely have not bought yet. [25:26]
- The student said he prefers taking winning trades, farming and outscaling; the coach replies that solo kills are worth so much money in this game. [26:27]
- The 4,800 investment spike is a power spike to be mindful of for a gun build. [26:27]
- Many M1s just farm while ahead rather than pushing anything, so their income is never converted into anything useful. [27:28]
- A non-M1 hero who does not use a lead for proactive plays stops mattering; know the time in the game when you are most oppressive. Silver is trash late game, so on Silver you are on a timer to win the game early, and if you just farm you get outscaled. [27:59]
- M1s like Wraith are strong all game and stronger late, so positioning matters most: dying is super bad, and roamers will pick on a Wraith, buy Slowing Hex and camp her lane because Wraith is not allowed to reach late game. [28:29]
- When copying builds from Tracklock, pick builds from players with a single name, like the pro M1s Zergi and Osis; builds with long titles like "Mirage hybrid gun spirit" are mostly bad. [30:31]
- Counting numbers ("tracking"): if you see all five enemies elsewhere, take the 1v1; if you only see three, there is a good chance they are playing on you. [31:01]
- Wraith's first spike is having all her cards: one nuke window. [32:03]
- Rat King is pretty weak in lane, so that lane will not get kills off the Wraith spike alone. [32:03]
- Sliding (captioned "mental slide"), just holding crouch, is the most basic movement and improves your gameplay instantly. [33:04]
- After the first or second wave you can tell whether you are simply the better player, even in the highest lobbies. [33:04]
- When you have them poked out at the 2 minute wave, you have pressure and can invade; once they group up, shoot them and heavy melee. [33:04]
- 1:30: push the wave, and if they are under their tower at very low HP, go to their camp. [33:35]
- Correct style: by 2:30 their camp should already be down and you should be back on the bridge after the invade, then go to your own camp. [34:06]
- You will be anchoring almost every single game; Wraith especially should not be walking around the jungle. [34:06]
- As the anchor at 2:30: push out the wave together, the roamer goes to theater, you take your other camp and do a very short box route, then come back; your goal is to not let them do tower damage while your partner is gone, even if that sacrifices some income. [34:36]
- As the anchor, do not walk up on the bridge and die. [35:08]
- Around 7 to 8 minutes, get into a side lane and do not get stuck in mid; ask "is any side lane free?", for example "Bebop (captioned Baba), come take mid, I need to go to outer lane". [35:08]
- Be in your side lane before the Urn. Rather than picking green for the most farm, play on the Urn spawn side. [35:38]
- The Urn spawns yellow at 10, 20 and 30, and green at 15, 25 and 35: ends in zero, yellow; ends in five, green (assuming it is turned in within 5 minutes). [36:08]
- Play yellow at 10 because after the quick fight someone takes the Urn and runs away from you, leaving you free to force a 1v1, fight or invade; on the green side they would be bringing the Urn to you and you would be stuck in a fight that could get you killed. [36:39]
- Be careful swinging melees. [37:10]
- Use comms with less experienced teammates: "You go do boxes, I got this." [37:10]
- The M1 plays the most selfish in the game: do not die for anyone or for an objective; clear, get pressure and be as aggressive as possible without dying. [38:11]
- Enemies will actively hunt the M1, so read numbers on the map constantly and watch the minimap all the time; if you see no one on the map, do not push the tower. [38:41]
- If they want you dead you are dead; you have to rely on your support, and Bebop is as close as you get, though most Bebops do not know they are supposed to do that. [39:11]
- If teammates die around you, try to trade out the kill; if you cannot, do not be around. [39:11]
- The student missed the buff timer; lane timers are the biggest thing to work on. [40:13]
- There is a way to still dash parry. [41:15]
- If your partner is not leaving, go run boxes instead, but first ask him to go run them; with two M1s in lane, either can leave. [42:16]
- Value the tower more: twice the student dove under a tower for a kill, got poked and could do no tower damage, when the tower could have been down at 5 minutes. [43:22]
- Kill the tower so you or your partner can move around the map; once you kill it, ask someone to trade lanes ("Bebop, come trade me, I'll win your lane next") to move your pressure. [43:22]
- The game is structured by spawn timers, but anyone can disrupt your timing, so knowing when to change your game plan is important. [44:54]
- 9:47: push this wave into the buff; if you are not already in an outer lane, you will have to be at the Urn fight. [44:54]
- Always go to the yellow side: if stuck mid at 9:47, clear the wave, go to yellow (it spawns at 10), secure the buff, then move into the side lane and ask someone else to take the Urn. [45:26]
- Infernus can also be an M1. [45:26]
- The higher you go, the shorter lanes get, often ending around 7 minutes because of roamers. [45:57]
- Towers get hard to break later, especially once you are forced to TP a lot, so taking them early gives you space. [45:57]
- At 3,200 souls you need to buy; buy before ever going into a fight like that while sitting on a ton of souls. [46:27]
- Take the initiative on comms: "Bebop, come mid, I got left" or "I'm ganking left, can you cover this?" [46:57]
- When the Rift fight is lost and you see a teammate die and HP bars dropping, stop; you cannot fight the Rift anymore, so go straight mid for the walker. [47:33]
- Count numbers: with only Paige respawned and everyone else shown on the map, you 1v1 Paige with your ult up and kill her even under her own walker. [48:03]
- Killing the walker solo plus a kill gets close to 3K early: the walker is 3,500 total with about 1,800 to you, and a solo kill at that stage gives around 800 to 900. [48:33]
- Aggression is favored for learning: aggressive players who must be disciplined and know when to back off learn Deadlock, while passive teams that play for late game never really learn it. [49:35]
- Recognize when the play is over: if teammates are dead and HP bars are dropping, even rotating cannot win it, so do something else, like ganking the left walker. [49:35]
- Do not go to save a walker that is already half dead; you arrive to an empty wave instead of impacting. Always look for trades. [50:07]
- Missing an Urn timer because Drifter traded his life to poke you out is fine and out of your control. [50:44]
- With the Urn moving away from you and three enemies shown on the mid wave, that is a free tower. [51:17]
- Never do two plays at once: clear the camp and come back to clear the wave; clearing a camp and then going to bell tower is two plays, the wave pushes in, and against another M1 you lose your walker. [51:17]
- Losing a walker gives them 1,800 souls, and dying on top gives them 3K; at 16 minutes that is about 4K, since solo kill value goes up with time. [51:17]
- Clear a camp or Sinner's Sacrifice and come back, go on a box run and come back; only after pushing the next wave go to the next thing. Sinner's Sacrifice especially forces an HP reset, and bell tower has three. [52:18]
- If you rotate out of your outer lane to mid, you are willing to sack that walker; after getting a kill or two you need to call "pull mid" because you committed your rotation to the mid objective. [52:53]

- Do not fall for a call to stack and finish a walker: five- or six-man grouping this early for a walker alone is bad and throws games. [53:24]
- Around 16 minutes, when respawn timers go up, five-man grouping is for pulling mid after a kill. [53:24]
- Otherwise always play side lane looking for 1v1s: at 17 minutes against a fed Shiv with max knives and no dispel, clear and play defensive; with dispel and more income than him, play aggressive. That push and pull applies to both side lanes. [53:54]
- Winning a side lane 1v1 while the fight is elsewhere forces someone to leave the fight to defend, winning your team the fight by proxy, or gives you the walker free; most pro teams lose when one side-laner dies and the other M1 becomes Thanos. [54:24]
- No matter how good you are, you must support yourself with income; that gets fixed before nuances like missed kill opportunities. [55:55]
- Watching top NA players is still useful because NA does not punish as hard, so you learn how to exploit not being punished while keeping good habits. [61:28]
