# Niche Lane Techniques.
channel: HERESY  date: 2026-07-12  url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xq1hjjEE1K4

## Covers
Advanced laning for players past the fundamentals: slow pushing the third wave to crash on the tier one camp spawn, bridge buff value, launch pad and vent techniques per lane (blue, yellow, green), killing the healing trooper first, going back to base when too low, solo heal and the 108% soul split, freezing and denying despite the healing trooper, Calico, Shiv, Vindicta, Billy, Rem, Viscous, Victor, Silver, Haze, Yamato, Mirage

## Claims
- These techniques are for players who have practically mastered the laning phase fundamentals; they will not make you Eternus 6 on their own. [0:00]
- Slow push the third wave, the one that touches down at the 1:30 mark and is the last wave before the tier one camp spawns at 2 minutes. [0:00]
- Shoving that third wave in too quickly to contest the tier one camp is a mistake: the weaker side (Vindicta and Billy in his clip, against Calico and Shiv) free farms the whole wave under their tower before 2 minutes, making the camp fight practically neutral. [0:30]
- Done right (his Rem duo as lane bullies against Viscous and Billy), take trades with the enemies directly to force them off the wave so they cannot clear yours fast, then shove their wave in when you choose, crashing it as the camp spawns, not before. [1:01]
- Making the enemies deal with both the camp and the wave at once gives more outplay chances and divides their attention while you focus solely on bullying them at the camp. [1:31]
- This does not require a stronger 2v2 matchup, only being momentarily stronger, such as a health advantage after the enemy (Shiv and Silver against his Victor) took a poor trade the wave before and hides under tower. [1:31]
- In that case delay pushing so your whole wave crashes just as the camp spawns, forcing them to deal with the wave while you take their camp behind them. [2:02]
- Bridge buffs spawn at 5 minutes and are incredibly important; whatever the buff, each is worth at least the equivalent of a thousand souls in item value. [2:02]
- Every lane's fastest access to the bridge buffs is its air vent or launch pad, but if you are shot on it you take 15% of your max health as damage, get stunned and lose your trajectory. [2:32]
- In the blue lane, wall bounce off the edge by the launch pad, dash to the side, double jump and use a melee lunge to reach the bridge comfortably. [2:32]
- That blue lane technique works at any point in the game to chase kills or challenge players on the bridge who would shoot you off the bounce pad, and it is especially useful in lane because blue lane sight lines are very open. [3:02]
- In the yellow lane, wall jump just before you touch the bounce pad; it sends you quickly sideways with less height and breaks the normal trajectory so you are harder to shoot, then wall jump straight up onto the bridge. [3:02]
- From the green lane it is harder because the nearby building is in the way: when you collide with the building, dash sideways along it to start a wall jump, then a standard double jump and a heavy melee; done together this reaches the bridge buff without being cancelable. [3:33]
- Common wisdom is to kill the healing trooper last because it partly heals for missing HP, so saving it heals you slightly more. [4:04]
- Less known: the healing trooper also heals your own troopers, so killing it first leaves your troopers with much more HP than the enemy's, a tankier wave that is more likely to win wave priority and let you contest things like the bridge buff. [4:04]
- Players are not going back to base enough; it feels like you cannot ride the zip line home without the boost that comes at 6 minutes, but you can. [4:34]
- If you are too low to ever beat your opponents in a fight, so low that even buying an item like Healing Rite will not let you fight properly, your best bet is almost certainly to go all the way home. [5:04]
- Your ally can pick up the wave solo: two players sharing a wave get 108% total value and one player gets 100%, so you barely lose anything; the main downside is your teammate gets all the souls. [5:04]
- If your teammate is at all low, basing lets them take the solo heal, giving your side much more HP than otherwise. [5:34]
- Example: a Haze on one HP greeded for the survival buff instead of going home and giving Victor the solo heal; despite a big soul advantage, the health gap meant they could not beat him and his Yamato, who easily tower dove the Victor with nothing the Victor could do. [5:34]
- Had the Haze gone home, she would have returned after the Victor likely died, but at full HP and up 1k souls she could probably have hunted down the Yamato or the Mirage to trade the kill one for one. [6:05]
- Freezing is harder in Deadlock than other MOBAs because of the healing trooper: if you kill the enemy healing trooper while freezing, your own wave heals, becomes stronger and naturally pushes out, so a permanent freeze is hard to hold. [6:36]
- That does not mean you should not freeze: a Yamato alone holding the wave with the enemies nowhere in sight did not touch the wave at all instead of clearing theirs fast, letting her own wave die before Victor and Haze were in range to collect, denying two whole troopers' worth of souls. [6:36]
- Denying two troopers at that point of the game is almost the value of a kill. [7:07]
- These techniques are practical almost every game, but they matter much less than the lane phase fundamentals for ranking up. [7:38]
