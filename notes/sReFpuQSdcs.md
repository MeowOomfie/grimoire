# Why Can't I Rank Up In Deadlock? - Explained
channel: Metro Deadlock
date: 2025-09-14
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sReFpuQSdcs

## Covers
How Deadlock ranked works and why new or returning players feel stuck: where to see your rank, the rank distribution, win-loss as the only input, certainty from long 50/50 records, win streaks and hero-based rating.
For new and returning players coming from Overwatch or Marvel Rivals who win games but do not climb.

## Claims
- To see your rank, turn on "show average team rating after the match" in the options menu, then click your profile and your skill rating; the info box lists every rank. [0:00]
- Obscurus means you have not played enough games for the matchmaker to place you. [0:31]
- A majority of new players sit in the lowest rank, Initiate 1 (per Statlocker's distribution); otherwise ranks form a normal distribution with the median around Ritualist, sometimes Emissary or Archon. [1:02]
- Being placed in Initiate as a new player is normal and intended, so you are matched against other new players. [1:33]
- Rank is determined only by your win-loss ratio; nothing else matters. [2:03]
- Unlike Marvel Rivals, playing many games in the low ranks does not climb you by itself: your rank will not change unless you win more games than you lose. [2:03]
- K/D, damage dealt to players and objective damage do not affect rank at all; they used to, and were removed because people abused them (perma-damaging walkers, damaging players without caring about their lives). [2:33]
- The more games you play at around 50% wins, the more certain the game is of your rank and the harder it is to climb out of it; the only way out is to win a lot more than you lose. [3:03]
- If you are completely new and win more than you lose, you climb very easily. [3:03]
- Win streaks exist (exact numbers unknown, from experience): each consecutive win gives more rating, so winning four or five in a row even from Initiate 1 makes the matchmaker adjust your rank faster. [3:35]
- The easiest way to climb is a big win streak; as long as you win more than you lose you will eventually climb. [4:05]
- Play without minding the rank; focus on learning and you will look back weeks later at a higher rank, since it does not take much to leave the bottom ranks. [4:36]
- Players in Arcanist, Ritualist, Emissary and up have usually played hundreds of hours, so do not feel bad about being stuck in Initiate for a while. [5:08]
- How long climbing takes depends on the player; experience with other MOBAs or shooters makes it faster. [5:39]
- With the median around Ritualist or Emissary, simply becoming an average player climbs you multiple ranks. [6:10]
- Hero-based rating: on a hero you have never played, or for your first few games on it, the game treats you as lower rated and puts you against lower ranked players on average. [6:40]
- This is abusable: picking a hero new to your account that you are confident on gets wins against lower ranked players and climbs faster (he does not say you should). [7:10]
- Playing only one to three heroes makes the game very certain of your rank, so it takes more to climb out of it. [7:10]
- All ranks up to Eternus, including the coach, are not playing the game perfectly; all the way up to Phantom and Ascendant people still make very basic mistakes. [7:41]
- The skill gap has moved: playing like his Eternus self from a year ago would now place him around Emissary or Archon. [7:41]
- There is not a very big difference in skill between, for example, Seeker and Arcanist. [8:12]
- On a three-game win streak, lock in and try to win the next game, since it gives a big boost to your rating. [9:13]
- Climbing feels harder at first in Deadlock than in Rivals or Overwatch, but once you get the hang of it, it is just as easy or easier. [9:43]
