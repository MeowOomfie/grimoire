# 5 Advanced Movement Techniques You NEED To Be Using In Deadlock
channel: Deathy
date: 2025-09-10
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6TZBLvNNook

## Covers
Five lesser known movement techniques: air strafing, bunny hopping with slides, heavy melee to extend or redirect momentum, wall stalling, and wall jumping to stay unpredictable, plus a closing push to be creative with movement.
For players who know basic movement and want to escape and dodge better.

## Claims
- Air strafing (a Source Engine mechanic): while in the air, let go of W, hold A to go left or D to go right, and smoothly move your camera the same way; this lets you turn in the air, whereas holding W sends you in a straight line. [0:00]
- Air strafing is not very useful from a normal ground jump, but Deadlock has many things that launch you high and far (such as jump pads), and if your opponent does not know air strafing they cannot catch up to you. [0:31]
- Air strafing also works with Majestic Leap for more control in the air, and on Lash while grappling. [1:01]
- Bunny hopping with slides in between keeps your momentum, so you go further on the same initial velocity, and makes your hitbox as unpredictable as possible. [1:01]
- Almost every player he coaches does not slide enough; sliding extends your magazine and makes your hitbox less predictable. [1:32]
- Many heroes have slide animations that are hard to hit; Wraith gets very low to the ground and leans back. [1:32]
- A decent share of damage in Deadlock comes from guns, so dodging bullets with unpredictable movement matters. [2:02]
- Mixing slides and bunny hops also saves stamina: instead of spending one, two, three stamina in a row to get from point A to point B, you go just as far using less. [2:02]
- Stamina in the bank lets you survive: an extra stamina is an extra air jump, wall jump or air dash to escape a situation you otherwise could not. [2:33]
- Heavy melee extends your momentum in the air, and high level players use it all the time. [3:03]
- Old edge melee cancel tech (for example on Vindicta) has been toned down significantly, but heavy melees are still an integral part of movement. [3:34]
- Use a heavy melee when you are missing just a little distance to reach a surface; once close to the wall, mantle it or wall jump off it. [4:05]
- Treat your heavy melee as an additional air dash. [4:05]
- Heavy melee lets you redirect your momentum: to switch from one direction to another in the air, heavy melee is the only option other than air dashing. [4:05]
- Wall stalling keeps you in the air for long periods: the wall jump has an internal cooldown, so if you wait long enough between wall jumps you get full height again, then spam wall jumps to hold your height on the wall. [4:35]
- Wall stalling is good against Infernus, who only really does damage with Flame Dash when you are on the ground, so staying in the air delays him. [4:35]
- On Lash, wall stall high while waiting for a slam or ultimate when something blocks you (for example a Kelvin dome) before your next grapple; the tech is better on Lash than most heroes but useful on many. [5:05]
- Use wall jumps to keep your movement unpredictable, be harder to hit and keep escape options open, for example to buy time in a 1v2. [5:35]
- Wall jumping can delay a fight while you wait for a cooldown, for example as Lady Geist 5 seconds off your ultimate. [6:05]
- Be creative with movement: smaller channels make rotation videos on air vents, rollouts, wall jumps and corner jumps that are very valuable. [6:05]
- Good movement is what separates good players from amazing players, and it can carry you in Deadlock. [6:36]
- Older quick movement guides on YouTube are still valuable because movement has not changed much. [7:07]
