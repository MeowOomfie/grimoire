# How to Navigate Deadlock's Mid-Game - 2026
channel: Deathy  date: 2026-02-14  url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YwWdz1h97xc

## Covers
Mid game macro: three pillars (efficiency, pressure, objectives), farm priority, wave pushing, skirmishing, power spikes, Urn states, Walkers, Mid Boss, five hero archetypes (carry, support, frontline, pick, nuker), game state reading; heroes named as examples: Wraith, Seven, Paige, Rem, Kelvin, Abrams, Billy, Yamato, Paradox, Holliday, Pocket, Lash, Mina, Infernus, Mirage, Dynamo, Graves, Victor

## Claims
- The mid game is roughly 10 to 25 minutes; the Urn and Mid Boss both spawn at 10 minutes, which marks its start. [0:00]
- Team goals in the mid game are the Urn, Walkers and Mid Boss; the individual goal is gathering enough souls for your role (Wraith wants a lot, Paige not so much). [0:30]
- Below Ascendant roles do not matter much; just get as many souls as you can unless you are on a support hero, because people at those ranks are so inefficient. [0:30]
- Many heroes can fill different roles, so do not obsess over low-soul or high-soul roles; focus on the pillars. [1:00]
- The three pillars of macro are efficiency, pressure and objectives; Deadlock is won by many small decisions adding up. [1:31]
- To learn what gives souls, open the in-game graphs on a rich player and look at souls by source. [2:01]
- Farm priority order: waves first, then Sinner's Sacrifices, then tier 3 camps if your hero can clear them, then boxes and the rest of the jungle about equal. [2:01]
- Waves are almost always most important because they apply pressure and are usually the most souls on the map; clearing two waves a minute alone is about 1,000 souls per minute. [2:31]
- Sinner's Sacrifices are second, both for their souls and for the strong permanent buffs. [2:31]
- If your hero cannot clear tier 3 camps, do not bother with them. [2:31]
- Jungle is worth slightly more than boxes depending on game time, but boxes are much easier; heroes that farm jungle poorly may prefer box routes. [2:31]
- The boxes underneath mid are worth more than Sinner's Sacrifices on average in his tests, and many people do not clear them. [3:02]
- Efficiency includes not going to areas a teammate already handles: if a teammate is clearing a wave, take the next priority (for example the Sinner's Sacrifice) so the team makes more souls overall, unless the teammate needs help. [3:33]
- Read teammates' movement on the minimap (for example someone ziplining to a wave) and farm camps, boxes or Sinner's Sacrifices instead; keep trying to predict teammates even at low ranks where they are unpredictable. [4:03]
- Use jump pads and ziplines as much as possible to move around the map. [4:34]
- Pressure is what separates decent players from great ones; it means making the enemy do things, and pushing waves is the simplest example. [5:04]
- Push waves as far as you can; "always go one more wave": check the minimap for the next wave, whether it is about to drop off the zipline, whether it just spawned, and whether it is risky. [5:34]
- You will die doing aggressive wave pushes, but you learn how players react; care about learning, not short-term results. [6:04]
- Dying while pushing a wave is one of the best deaths: you drop few unsecured souls unless you just farmed jungle, and the enemy likely sent two players, so your team should have a numbers advantage elsewhere. [6:35]
- In a 1v1 side lane, ask whether you can fight the enemy (example: Infernus with 2,000 more souls than a Wraith); test it with a small skirmish or poke and see whether the trade was favorable, or force his teleport so he cannot walk back to the wave. [6:35]
- Check enemy items before fighting; for example a Wraith relying on teleport to survive should not fight someone with Slowing Hex. [7:05]
- Rule of thumb: fight after you hit a power spike, and wait if you are close to one. [7:35]
- A power spike is buying an item of 3,200 souls or more, or maxing an ability with its last (5 AP) upgrade. [7:35]
- When ahead, steal enemy resources (camps, Sinner's Sacrifices, boxes) and occupy space on their side of the map. [7:35]
- When behind, pressure mostly means aggressively pushing waves and creating side lane engagements to pick off enemies who are farming. [8:05]
- The Urn has three states: friendly comeback (green candles), neutral (no candles), enemy comeback (red candles). [8:05]
- A friendly comeback Urn should always be picked up right away, no exceptions, on any hero; it is the single most efficient way back into the game and low ranks often ignore it. [8:36]
- A neutral Urn is usually worth running if your team leads by a few thousand souls; factors are wave states, numbers advantage (how many enemies are dead), ult economy and how much your team wants to fight. [9:06]
- Picking up a neutral Urn is basically forcing a team fight unless the enemy team is dead, so only pick it up if you want, and expect to win, that fight. [9:36]
- Run the Urn toward a deposit lane where a strong teammate already is (example: a fed Infernus on that lane). [9:36]
- At lower ranks you can often run the Urn for free. [10:06]
- Run an enemy comeback Urn only with an overwhelming advantage (lots of souls up, many more ults), since a living enemy team will fight you for it. [10:36]
- Otherwise grab an enemy comeback Urn and hold it to prevent or delay the enemy from getting it. [10:36]
- If the enemy grabs their comeback Urn, either contest it (only if very confident, since the fight is usually near their Walker) or trade it for a Walker on the opposite side of the map from the deposit. [11:06]
- Walkers should not be forced; teams with a 5,000 to 10,000 soul lead often throw by repeatedly feeding into Walkers. [11:36]
- Dying for Walkers is still worth it, with caveats: deaths are worse when ahead because of comeback mechanics (more souls for the enemy's kills) and longer respawn timers when your net worth is above the enemy team's average. [11:36]
- Losing one player for a Walker is usually fine whether ahead, even or behind; losing two is the problem. [12:38]
- Losing two for a Walker when ahead is usually bad; when even it depends on time: at 25 minutes with 45 to 50 second timers the enemy may take Mid Boss, at 15 minutes with short timers and bad waves they may not. [12:38]
- Ask "if we both die, can they get Mid Boss?"; if yes, do not die for the Walker, because trading a Walker for Mid Boss is a very bad play. [13:08]
- Dying for a Walker when behind is usually great because you are not penalized by comeback mechanics or longer respawns; never lose three. [13:08]
- Best times to go for Walkers: after getting kills, and when all three waves are pushed into enemy Walkers, which forces the enemy to split up or lose a Walker. [13:38]
- With all waves pushed you risk little by pressuring a Walker, because the enemy must deal with the waves and cannot easily take Mid Boss. [14:08]
- You do not need to kill a Walker in one push; taking it to half health and backing off makes it easy to finish later in a 10 to 15 second window after one kill. [14:08]
- Mid Boss is nuanced: sometimes you can force it in a 6v6, other times you need three or more kills. [14:38]
- Before Mid Boss, judge how well the enemy contests the pit: AoE like Paige ultimate, Dynamo, Abrams charge, Graves or other pit heroes, whether those abilities are up and where those heroes are on the map. [15:08]
- If a strong contesting team arrives as you finish Mid Boss, the Rejuvenator becomes a coin flip; coin flipping is acceptable when you are behind and need a risky play, but not in most situations. [15:38]
- Do Mid Boss when the enemy cannot get there in time, or when you believe your team is stronger in the pit: more or better ultimates, a reliable Rejuvenator secure like Yamato going invulnerable with unstoppable, or heroes that like fighting there like Victor or Graves. [16:08]
- The first Mid Boss should almost always be done before all three Walkers are killed, usually after one or two Walkers, and sometimes before any Walkers depending on the game. [16:08]
- The pillars apply to every hero; your role is a modifier. Five archetypes: carry, support, frontline, pick, nuker. Some heroes fit none or several. [16:39]
- Carries (Seven, Wraith) scale with items and souls, farm jungle well, and in the mid game should be as efficient as possible and pressure side lanes; a solo Walker can snowball them. [17:40]
- Carries do not show up to many early fights; when a team fight happens elsewhere, their main move is to push a Walker. [17:40]
- Supports (Paige, Rem, Kelvin) need fewer souls, are utility focused, keep the team alive or disable enemies with abilities or items like Cursed Relic, Rescue Beam and Divine Barrier. [18:11]
- Supports should catch waves the rest of the team cannot, and above all play around and help whoever is having a good game on their team. [18:11]
- Frontliners (Abrams, Billy, Yamato) create chaos and space by tanking damage; they should play aggressively and create fights, but not take fights alone too often since most are not great in 1v1s; pairing with a support helps. [18:42]
- Pick heroes (Paradox, Holliday) move fast and catch people out (Paradox swap, Holliday lasso); target the easiest kills, stay hidden so ganks land, and look at side lanes. [19:12]
- Side lane targets like a cocky Wraith, Infernus or Mirage are good picks (Slowing Hex then swap); rich targets have longer respawn timers and losing them hurts the enemy more, and sometimes they drop unsecured souls. [19:42]
- Nukers (Pocket, Lash, Mina) have burst; they want frontline or pick heroes to create engagements they follow up on, being the "plus one" when a teammate initiates (example chain: Paradox swap into Abrams charge into Lash ground strike into Mina ultimate). [20:12]
- Nukers are usually good at clearing tier 2 jungle camps, often one-shotting them, so clear those often. [20:42]
- After a power spike, always be willing to take a decent fight; the spike is usually the strongest you will be for the next 2 minutes, since on average you make under 1,600 souls per minute and often it is 3 or more minutes until the next comparable upgrade. [20:42]
- This applies whether you are ahead or behind: it is your best time to look for an engagement, though you do not have to fight. [21:13]
- Game state: when ahead, steal resources and play on the enemy side if you are an aggressive hero; when behind, secure your own resources and look for a comeback Urn. [21:13]
- Every time you farm jungle, look at the top of the screen at souls and the clock: who is winning, by how much, and when the next key objective (Urn, bridge buffs) is. [21:43]
- Also use that time to see who is strong and weak on both teams and do a quick item check. [21:43]
