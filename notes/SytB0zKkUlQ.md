# The Ultimate Guide To Deadlock Items
channel: Metro Deadlock
date: 2025-09-02
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SytB0zKkUlQ

## Covers
Beginner-to-intermediate guide to the item shop: the three categories, investment bonuses and the 9.6k sweet spot, slot caps and selling, universally good items, counter items, anti-heal, and spirit scaling.
Ends with a step-by-step Seven build and the most common new-player buying mistakes. Heroes used as examples: Haze, Wraith, Pocket, Victor, Seven, Mina, Abrams, Lash.
For newer players who follow builds and want to start judging or making their own.

## Claims
- There are three item categories: weapon (orange) mostly adds gun damage, vitality (green) adds survivability, spirit (purple) adds spirit/ability damage. [0:30]
- There are exceptions: some weapon items like Spirit Rend raise spirit damage for you and your team, and some vitality items add weapon damage. [0:30]
- Not every hero needs all three types: some are weapon-focused, hybrids benefit from everything, and some heroes can be built as a gun, tank or spirit version of the same hero. [1:01]
- Investment bonuses: money spent in a category gives a passive bonus no matter which item it was (any weapon item, e.g. Close Quarters or Restorative Shot, raises your weapon damage bonus). [1:33]
- Investment has diminishing returns: 9,600 souls in weapon items gives exactly 58% extra weapon damage. [2:35]
- Spending another 6,400 in weapon beyond 9,600 only adds about 14% more weapon damage, which is not worth it. [3:05]
- Up to 9.6k is the sweet spot for weapon investment, and vitality and spirit scale the same way. [3:35]
- If your hero wants a damage type, it is worth getting at least 9.6k in that category. [3:35]
- 80%+ of the roster benefits from eventually reaching 9.6k in all three categories. [4:07]
- His Wraith build is designed to end with almost exactly 9.6k in all three categories: she does good gun damage, wants the +32% health from vitality investment because she is squishy, and does a lot of spirit damage with her cards and Full Auto. [4:07]
- A spirit nuker like Pocket wants mostly spirit items; popular late-game Pocket builds have very low weapon investment, some vitality, mostly spirit. [5:09]
- Getting 9.6k in every category is not essential for some heroes: Pocket does little gun damage, so investing extra into gun to reach the threshold is not worth it in most cases. [5:40]
- A more expensive item is not necessarily much better: Extra Health (800, the cheapest tier) gives 175 health, while its upgrade Fortitude gives 375 health plus other effects for four times the cost. [6:11]
- If you only want health, upgrading Extra Health to Fortitude is not necessarily worth it; look for the best bang for your buck. [6:42]
- Spamming cheap items is not viable because you get slot capped and have to start selling. [7:13]
- A good build sells as few items as possible, because selling only returns half of what you paid. [7:13]
- Monster Rounds (800): more damage against NPCs (lane and jungle creeps) and you take less damage from them, so you clear faster and back to base less. [7:43]
- Buy Monster Rounds early on farming heroes like Seven, Wraith and Infernus and sell it later; the farm it speeds up earns more than the 400 you lose when selling. [8:15]
- Not every item has to be kept all game. [8:45]
- When slot capped and you want an item like Ethereal Shift or Counterspell, sell one of the cheaper, lower-impact items in the build. [8:45]
- Counterspell is a great counter to big ultimates like Lash's ult: press parry and avoid it. [9:16]
- In his example he would sell Suppressor rather than Cold Front, Improved Spirit or Spirit Sap, because those three upgrade into stronger items. [9:16]
- Upgrade paths named: Spirit Sap into Silence Wave, Cold Front into Arctic Blast, Improved Spirit into Boundless Spirit (when you have the money); the build editor shows these. [9:47]
- Suppressor's effect is strong but it can never be more than a 1,600 item because it does not upgrade. [10:17]
- If you don't know how to pick a build, use one from a pro player or streamer of the hero. [10:47]
- Watch out for "fake builds": people impersonating someone else, or troll builds that get popular by abusing the system. [10:47]
- Victor's bullets have slow travel time, making lane shots hard to land at range. [11:17]
- High-Velocity Rounds (800) adds bullet velocity; on Victor it makes shots at the same distance far more consistent. [11:47]
- Buying High-Velocity Rounds on Victor is personal preference; you could skip it and spend the 800 on early spirit instead. [11:47]
- There are many solid builds with different ideas; judge whether an item suits your hero with basic logic. [12:17]
- Haze is mostly a gun hero: Fixation stacks add weapon damage the longer you shoot, and her ult also does bullet damage the longer you shoot. [12:17]
- Haze's Sleep Dagger does high-scaling spirit damage, but a sleep-dagger-only build is a meme, not the best way to play her. [12:17]
- To learn how a hero plays, look at the first few popular builds: Haze's are all orange and green with barely any purple. [12:49]
- Fixation: one stack per bullet hit, two on a headshot; each stack raises your damage on that target, with a spirit burst every few stacks. [12:49]
- On Haze, Swift Striker (fire rate) is obviously better than Melee Charge. [13:20]
- 20% fire rate means bullets come out 20% faster and 20% more damage; think of fire rate as damage. [13:50]
- On Haze, fire rate also applies Fixation stacks faster, so it is more effective than straight weapon damage. [13:50]
- Intensifying Magazine (more damage the longer you shoot) sounds good on Haze but almost no one buys it because fire rate is better; it doesn't apply Fixation faster. [14:20]
- Slowing Bullets is not a bad option on Haze: fire rate plus a slow if you have trouble catching or hitting enemies. [14:51]
- Spirit Shredder Bullets would never be worth buying on Haze because it debuffs enemy spirit and she does little spirit damage. [14:51]
- Everything gives more souls as the game goes on (creeps per minute, player kills, boxes), so big money is hard to get early. [15:21]
- In lane buy cheap 800 and 1,600 items to ramp damage early, then progressively more expensive items. [15:21]
- After laning, at about 12k invested overall, the first 6k item is a major power spike. [15:51]
- Find a build you enjoy first, then start innovating. [16:22]
- Items split into ones solid on almost every hero and niche ones (designed for other heroes or for very specific scenarios). [16:22]
- Extra Health is always solid and makes you more survivable. [16:52]
- On inherently tanky heroes buying Extra Health is probably not worth it; it is especially good on low-health heroes like Mina, Wraith and Haze. [17:22]
- To check how tanky a hero is, pick them in the practice range for base HP, then press Tab and click max level to see max-level HP. [17:22]
- Abrams has about 2,200 HP base at max level and more when you level Infernal Resilience; Mina at max is barely half of that and is the squishiest hero. [17:53]
- Battle Vest and Enchanter's Emblem (both 1,600) have been super meta for a long time and will always be solid. [18:23]
- Battle Vest gives 18% bullet resist and Enchanter's Emblem 15% spirit resist; everything else on them is a bonus. [18:23]
- Spirit damage is anything cast or any spirit effect procced from abilities; it is very prevalent, so Enchanter's Emblem is probably the most popular item in the game. [18:54]
- Bullet resist covers anything from auto attacks including melee; melee counts as bullet damage. [18:54]
- Resist items are really solid and bought on almost any hero universally. [19:25]
- Bullet Resilience and Spirit Resilience (the upgrades) double the price for a small resist increase: Spirit Resilience has 25% vs Enchanter's Emblem's 15%. [19:25]
- What makes Spirit Resilience worth it is the passive: extra spirit resist when below 30% health. [19:56]
- The resilience items are often not worth it on squishy heroes like Haze or Mina, because at 30% health any ability will probably one-shot you anyway. [19:56]
- Buy the resilience items on tanks first: a 3k HP hero at a third of HP still has about 1,000-1,100 HP covered by the bonus resist, while Haze has maybe 400-500 HP and is already in kill range. [20:26]
- Healing Rite: activate on yourself or a teammate when low in lane; it heals as long as an enemy player doesn't hit you (creep hits are fine). [21:28]
- Backing to base early in lane is a big, common mistake that loses wave farm; buy Healing Rite instead. [21:58]
- Extra Regen is the option if you don't want an active cast; Melee Lifesteal is great for heroes that melee a lot. [21:58]
- Restorative Shot is more advanced: it heals a lot when you hit an enemy hero, but goes on cooldown even if you miss. [22:29]
- Restorative Shot heals 50 HP every 6 seconds if you hit an enemy hero every 6 seconds; good for confident aimers who manage the cooldown. [22:29]
- Improved Spirit (1,600) is universally good on every hero that scales with spirit; very cheap for what it gives, plus out-of-combat regen. [22:59]
- Bought first at level one, Improved Spirit gives 18 spirit power plus 11 from the investment bonus: 0 to 29 spirit for 1,600, a big early spike for spirit abilities. [23:29]
- A big part of winning lane is killing enemy troopers faster than they kill yours to pressure the tower; Monster Rounds as first item helps you do that. [24:00]
- For an aggressive, trading lane on almost any hero, buy Headshot Booster: one of the best and most slept-on items. [24:00]
- Headshot Booster: every 6-8 seconds a headshot does an extra 50 damage, a lot early; Mina has only about 600 HP at base. [24:31]
- Magazine items like Titanic Magazine feel nice to new players but are bait on most heroes. [25:01]
- Sliding uses no ammo, so once you are decent at movement (and with the many items that give movement speed) you can extend slides almost forever and ammo matters less. [25:34]
- Learn basic movement by being forced to; always having ammo feels good now but hurts you later. [26:06]
- Silence Wave and Slowing Hex are active counter items against specific heroes. [26:38]
- Slowing Hex silences movement abilities: Mina's and Wraith's teleports, Infernus's dash, Kelvin's Ice Path, Abrams's charge, Shiv's dash, Mirage's teleport and movement items. [26:38]
- Slowing Hex is amazing if you initiate and have trouble catching heroes who escape easily; it auto-aims onto an enemy you hover near. [27:08]
- Silence Wave is even stronger: it disables all their abilities for 3.75 seconds base; you have to aim the wave but it is pretty easy to hit. [27:38]
- Buy Silence Wave or Slowing Hex against heroes that get away from you. [27:38]
- Metal Skin is a great option against a very farmed gun hero: you take no damage from them for three and a half seconds, but it slows you. [28:10]
- Against spirit burst there is no great 3,200 option (Spirit Resilience is possible); the 6k items negate it best. [28:41]
- Spellbreaker vs Lash: Lash always initiates with Ground Slam, and Spellbreaker makes the slam do basically no damage. [28:41]
- Reactive Barrier is a great choice if you are poor and dying too much: it auto-deploys a barrier when you get movement locked. [29:11]
- Movement locks that trigger Reactive Barrier include Haze's Sleep Dagger, Bebop's hook and uppercut, Abrams's charge, and Infernus and Wraith ults; it is active against about 80% of the cast. [29:11]
- Spirit Shielding and Weapon Shielding deploy a barrier after a damage threshold; especially great early in lane. [29:41]
- Spirit Shielding is a bit better right now because most early damage is spirit damage. [29:41]
- Spirit Shielding deploys when an enemy spell does at least 150 damage to you; the barrier is bonus HP lasting 8 seconds, usually long enough to decide the fight. [30:11]
- Return Fire is the cheaper version of Metal Skin: it gives bullet resist, and when activated returns the damage you take to the enemy. [30:11]
- On a tanky hero against Haze, pop Return Fire when she shoots and she can kill herself if you have more HP. [30:41]
- Capacitor (an expensive niche weapon item; captions say it upgrades from "Tesla cannon") is a skill shot beam that slows by the maximum possible slow. [31:11]
- Capacitor also removes all non-ultimate active buffs: Seven's Power Surge, Warden's overshield, Viscous's cube (you can capacitor the cube off him), an Abrams aura buff, and Return Fire. [31:41]
- Counters have layers: if the enemy pops Return Fire, you can remove it instantly with Capacitor. [32:12]
- Armor Piercing Rounds: for a gun hero against a tank like Abrams who bought Plated Armor or lots of Bullet Resilience; half your bullets ignore resistances and deal full damage. [32:43]
- Learn anti-heal early in your journey. [33:14]
- Healing sources to counter: Lady Geist's life drain, Victor's very strong healing on his 2, and items like Leech, Infuser and Vampiric Burst. [33:14]
- If the enemy has too much healing you need to buy anti-heal; search "healing reduction" in the shop to list the items. [34:17]
- Blood Tribute reduces your own healing; super hard to use, no one has found a use case; do not buy it. [34:17]
- Healbane is the cheapest anti-heal: dealing spirit damage gives 35% less healing from any source for 8 seconds; easy for most heroes unless you play a full gun hero. [34:48]
- Toxic Bullets is one of the most liked: bullets build up a damage-over-time effect, and its strongest part is the healing reduction, not the damage. [34:48]
- Decay is situational and pretty weak right now: a targeted cast you can't really miss in range, stronger damage over time than Toxic Bullets and 40% healing reduction. [35:19]
- Crippling Headshot and Inhibitor are 6k items with 30% healing reduction plus other good effects; both very solid right now. [36:20]
- Spirit Burn gives 70% healing reduction for an enemy who just won't die from healing: deal enough spirit damage and an aura appears around them, they burn and basically can't heal. [36:20]
- Spirit Burn's aura hits everyone in its radius, so it can anti-heal the whole enemy team; super good on AoE spirit heroes like Pocket or Lash. [37:22]
- Your whole team doesn't need anti-heal, but at least one person in a team fight must have it, or enemy healing is at 100% (e.g. Kelvin healing his whole team in his dome). [37:22]
- An ability with spirit scaling above 1 is probably really solid to build around if you use it a lot. [38:22]
- Victor's Jumpstart has 1.8 spirit scaling (press Tab and hover to see); buying Boundless Spirit takes its healing from 154 to 270, almost double. [38:22]
- His own Victor build sneaks in orange items because he aims well, making him more effective at medium to long range than a melee Victor who does mostly close-range aura damage; a different style, not the best build. [38:54]
- Vegas's Victor build has no orange items at all and is above 9.6k in at least two categories, which you should always have when making builds. [39:56]
- An all-spirit Victor (Aura of Suffering, charges, spirit-damage healing) is deadly and sustains a lot at close range, but does little damage at long range. [40:26]
- Seven build walkthrough: Seven has decent gun damage but is mostly a spirit hero; every ability does a lot of spirit damage, and he gets stronger the more spirit he has. [41:26]
- Seven's Power Surge and ult are AoE: Power Surge jumps between connected, clumped enemies and can kill people you aren't shooting; the ult storm cloud does massive area damage. [41:56]
- Seven's stun has a long cooldown and is used for the stun, not damage; the ball hurts if enemies sit in it but is mostly a zoning tool. [42:26]
- First win the lane: Monster Rounds to shove the wave, or Extra Spirit and Improved Spirit for more ability damage; both viable. [42:58]
- Power Surge is very strong in lane; there is one item to cut its cooldown and one to extend its duration; running both is usually overkill later, so pick one. [43:28]
- The main meta on Seven is Compressed Cooldown on his 3, to shove waves faster. [43:59]
- Seven lane plan: Monster Rounds, Compressed Cooldown on the 3, more spirit damage, then the resist basics. [43:59]
- Battle Vest's secondary stats (weapon damage, fire rate) aren't useful on Seven; Enchanter's Emblem gives spirit power and cooldown while above 65% health, so it is the solid pick. [44:29]
- About 5,000 souls of items is good value for lane. [44:59]
- In the build editor, add an "optional" healing category holding Healing Rite or Extra Regen for when you are low. [44:59]
- If you don't know the items well, look for what you like and what is good against the enemy heroes. [45:29]
- Suppressor (spirit power, health) on Seven: as long as you deal spirit damage the enemy shoots much slower, and all of Seven's abilities do spirit damage. [46:00]
- Buy Suppressor on Seven against gun heroes that shoot a lot, like Haze, Infernus or Wraith. [46:32]
- Surge of Power (not to be confused with Power Surge) imbues an ability with permanent spirit power and, when that ability is used, gives bonus movement speed, full speed while attacking and fire rate; amazing on Seven. [47:02]
- While building, watch the investment tab: on Seven get at least 9.6k spirit and probably 9.6k vitality, since vitality is always good. [47:33]
- He likes Fleetfoot on Seven later in the game for speed and slow resist; with little weapon invested it also gives weapon investment bonuses, a value buy. [48:04]
- If Seven needs more tankiness, add Weapon Shielding or Spirit Shielding. [48:35]
- If Seven needs lifesteal, buy early Spirit Lifesteal (heals on hero and creep hits), which upgrades into Leech and Infuser, two of the most amazing items on Seven. [48:35]

- Some items are situational or don't work on some heroes: melee items (Melee Charge, Point Blank, Melee resist, Lifestrike, Spirit Snatch, Torment Pulse) are close-range. [49:36]

- Rule out the high gun damage 6k orange items on Seven since he does most damage with spirit. [50:38]

- Late-game hard counters: Spellbreaker is the anti-spirit item, great if you can afford it against big spirit burst: 75% damage reduction on the first enemy spell that hits you, every 10 seconds, plus spirit resist and debuff resist. [51:39]
- Plated Armor is the gun version: it stops certain gun effects from proccing and stops some enemy bullets hitting you. [52:10]
- The biggest new-player mistake is reacting too much to the enemy: buying too many defensive items, lots of lifesteal (rushing Leech) or armors, and ending up with no damage. [52:10]

- Going full tank is viable on certain heroes like Abrams or Victor that just exist in the enemy's face. [53:11]

- As a beginner, follow the build of a creator or pro you like and learn what each item does in it; making your own comes over time. [54:42]
