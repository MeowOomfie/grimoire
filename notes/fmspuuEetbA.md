## Covers
Golden Goose Egg: what it does, how buff tiers decide when to sell it, why the coach thinks it is a bad ranked item, and the counter-arguments (losing lanes, split-pushing gun heroes, Rem).
For anyone tempted to buy it in lane, and for players who want to punish an enemy holding it.

## Claims
- Golden Goose Egg reduces all of your damage by 10% (with a few exceptions), gives a little sprint speed and a little regen. [0:13]
- The egg's main draw is 90 souls per minute while you hold it, plus a permanent buff for every 80 souls you gain. [0:43]
- Buffs come in three tiers: tier one at the start, tier two after 10 minutes, tier three after 30 minutes; buffs from Sinner's Sacrifices, statues or the egg take the tier of the current game time. [0:43]
- Popping the egg at 9:59 gives all tier one buffs; popping it at 10:01 gives all tier two buffs. [1:14]
- Don't sell the egg between 15 and 25 minutes, and don't hold it to 35 or 40 minutes; both are common mistakes. [1:14]
- If you buy the egg, sell it between 10 and 12 minutes, just after 10:00 but before the first rift, so you are not handicapped in the first rift. [1:14]
- If you still have the egg after the second rift around 18 minutes, hold it until 30 minutes, but not a second after. [1:45]
- Holding the egg as a split-pushing gun carry still hurts: the net damage penalty makes you unable to invade enemy farm or contest the enemy side laner, they get permanent wave pressure and can steal your farm, and you kill every camp 10% slower. [1:45]
- The egg's penalty doesn't really affect on-hit items like Siphon Bullets, Headhunter or Scourge, but Headhunter and Siphon Bullets also don't apply to camps, and a tempo build with the anti-tempo egg makes no sense. [2:15]
- Most people agree you should sell the egg before the first rift fight and not hold it to 30 minutes. [2:46]
- Buying the egg in lane ties one hand behind your back and your lane partner's. [2:46]
- From about 22 minutes in solo queue, team fights end in mid boss, so losing one fight to the 10% damage loss costs a huge amount of tempo, not worth the few thousand souls gained. [3:16]
- Losing a fight or a kill because of the egg griefs your whole team, not just you. [3:16]
- Never buy the egg into a winning lane; buying it is basically a guaranteed loss of your lane. [3:47]
- Against the "buy it in a lane you'll lose anyway" argument: enemies should steal your boxes and contest your farm, and the -10% damage applies to troopers, so you never get wave priority. [3:47]
- Against an enemy holding the egg, a Calico leaves lane to roam on the free wave pressure, or steals other boxes to suffocate their farm. [4:17]
- Example: a Victor losing to McGinnis who buys the egg gives up wave priority permanently, and a good McGinnis will take the tower around 5 minutes by sieging off that pressure. [4:17]
- Below Eternus (realistically E5 or below), there is no guaranteed won or lost lane: enemies get bored and dive, overextend and throw, and holding the egg stops you from taking those openings. [5:19]
- If you stay healthy under tower and slow play the lane, low rank enemies will often dive you for no reason. [5:19]
- Even at high level in solo queue you don't know if a lane is won or lost until about the third wave, but the egg is only useful if bought on wave one. [5:49]
- Strong laners such as McGinnis are sometimes played passively by players new to the hero, and you only notice by the second or third wave, after the egg has already given up a winnable lane. [6:20]
- As Calico, hit tab at minute one; if anyone holds the egg, plan to gank them as early as possible, keep harassing that lane and bully that side of their jungle, since they can't fight back. [6:50]
- The egg's 800 souls are also an opportunity cost: they could buy a brawly lane item like Mystic Burst first for a spirit hero or Headshot Booster. [7:20]
- If you are getting shoved in a lot, buy Monster Rounds instead to keep your guardian up longer, buying time for teammates to win their lanes and help. [7:50]
- Rem should not buy the egg: his gun is strong in lane, his pillow is good and his helpers give immediate wave pressure, and he can roam early off that pressure. [8:21]
- In theory supports want low farm because comeback souls are overtuned: a high-farm support denies their own carries comeback souls when behind or feeds the enemy carries comeback souls when ahead; this matters less in solo queue, where playing greedy and selfish climbs best. [8:51]
