# The Ultimate Beginners Guide for Deadlock - New Player Tips and Tricks
channel: Metro Deadlock
date: 2025-08-31
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8Wnsw9qlxOY

## Covers
A start-to-finish guide for new players: getting an invite, picking heroes, using the build browser, basic movement, souls and lane pushing, what to do after lane, jungle camps and Sinner's Sacrifice timing, objectives and flex slots, why KD does not matter, Mid Boss and the Rejuvenator, how to end games, plus crosshair and health bar settings.
For players with no Deadlock or MOBA experience, or a few hours in.

## Claims
- To get the game, ask a Steam friend who owns it to send you an invite from the Steam friend list; if you have none, people post invites daily in his Discord. [0:30]
- Play whatever heroes you enjoy; the roster tells you what each hero does and you can check their abilities. [1:02]
- Coming from shooters he picked gun heroes like Haze and Wraith; you can play tanky heroes, and there are many heroes that need little aim if you come from MOBAs. [1:33]
- Deadlock is a mix between a MOBA and a shooter. [1:33]
- Valve is very good at balancing heroes; some are better or easier to get into at times, but play what you enjoy. He plays Wraith, Billy, Drifter, Victor and Haze. [2:03]
- To learn the game fast, stick to a few heroes first and expand later; trying to learn every hero takes a long time, and no player is really good on every hero. [2:34]
- If playing real people is too much pressure at first, play a bot match to learn abilities; he believes you can leave those without penalty. [3:04]
- In hero select you must pick at least three heroes; right-click a selected hero to raise its priority up to two up arrows, which makes getting that hero much more likely. [3:35]
- During the period with six newly released heroes, to queue a new hero you must also select at least three old heroes. [4:06]
- The in-game build browser lets you sort by popular builds and select one, and it tells you what to buy. [4:37]
- Build popularity is decided by likes and plays, so bad builds can be popular, and some impostors copy names or push trash builds; pick builds high on the list made by known pro players or streamers, and hover over the author to check it is really them (the real Zergy is now named Goonman). [5:08]
- Selected builds also give the ability level-up order, shown in game as a blinking "suggested" prompt, so you do not have to think about it. [6:08]
- You can reach Eternus, the highest rank, just following other people's builds; do not worry about build crafting or countering the enemy until you have hundreds of hours. [7:08]
- Read what your build's items do, especially actives; you can have up to four active items. Cold Front creates an ice aura that slows and damages enemies; Suppressor reduces enemy fire rate when you shoot them, so it helps against gun heroes. [8:08]
- Follow builds left to right; it is the simplest way. [8:38]
- Your rank is hidden by default; turn on show average team rating after a match, or check skill rating in your profile. Do not be discouraged starting in the lowest ranks (Initiate, Seeker, Alchemist). [9:39]
- The stamina bar sits around your crosshair: one jump is always free, a double jump costs one stamina, and a dash (Shift by default) also uses stamina. [10:41]
- Dash then jump with the right timing for a jump dash, a chain using two stamina that covers a lot of distance. [11:11]
- After a dash, hold crouch to slide; with enough velocity you always slide. Hold crouch going down stairs to slide and extend it, even from a walk. [11:11]
- Movement is not the most important thing: his movement is terrible compared to the average Eternus 6 player and he is still the highest rank in the game, though movement helps a lot on some heroes. [12:13]
- The map has three lanes; you are randomly assigned to one with a teammate for the laning stage, usually a 2v2. [12:44]
- Your goal in lane is to get money and items, not die, kill the enemies and kill their guardian before they kill yours; the guardian is the first line of defense, and you win the game by killing the enemy Patron. [13:15]
- Killed creeps drop souls: the soul pops as a black aura, and when it turns teal or bright green, shoot it; whoever shoots it first gets the money, so both sides can deny. [14:15]
- Your number one priority is securing your own souls; denying is a more advanced technique for later. [14:45]
- Push the lane by shooting the enemy creeps; the simplest way to win lane is to push faster than the enemy. [15:47]
- As long as you stand behind your creeps, the enemy tower will not aggro you and you can shoot the tower or the enemy players; step in front of your creeps, or have none, and it aggros the nearest target, does ridiculous damage early and melees you at close range. [16:17]
- Killing the tower opens the map for your team, gives you and your lane partner money, and leaves the two enemy laners with no safety. [16:48]
- On a basic level, 99% of the time the best play, good enough to reach Phantom plus, is shoving the wave fast, damaging the tower, and damaging enemy players when you can. [17:19]
- Sticking to a few heroes teaches you what you can get away with in lane; Seven's Power Surge shocks multiple enemies close together, so he shoves waves fast but is otherwise pretty weak in lane. [18:21]
- What to do after lane depends on your hero: some want to sit on a lane and farm, others are early game roamers or gankers; look up a tutorial for your hero. [18:51]
- If you are a farmer and won lane, your lane partner should leave you alone on the lane; you get roughly double the souls and they help other lanes. [19:21]
- A ganker rotates to another lane to look for a kill, or if there is none, pushes that tower to help the team; aggressive players can gank even on carry heroes. [19:51]
- Map icons: one, two and three arrows are tier 1, tier 2 and tier 3 camps; a box with a dot in the middle is a Sinner's Sacrifice. [20:52]
- Tier 1 camps are the easiest, give very little money, spawn early, and are almost never worth going for unless you are passing by. [21:54]
- Tier 2 camps are tankier and often need abilities, but give almost double a tier 1. [21:54]
- Tier 3 camps have at least one big, very tanky creep; they are much harder but give much more, so do not go for them early; the biggest camp has three big creeps. [22:26]
- Small camps spawn at 2 minutes, so you can take them during lane; medium (tier 2) camps spawn at 6 minutes, though often no hero is fast enough to farm them then. [22:57]
- Sinner's Sacrifices spawn at 8 minutes and are the biggest source of souls for the time invested. [23:28]
- Hit a Sinner's Sacrifice with heavy melees; it drains your life but dispenses money as long as you keep punching. [23:28]
- On the fifth and last punch there is a light-up animation; start your heavy melee when the third-to-last light lights up to get the buffs, which are random and last the whole game: health, spirit power, cooldown reduction, fire rate, weapon damage and ammo. [23:58]
- The double sinner room, one small creep and then two sinners, is the best place on the map to farm. [24:30]
- Advanced double sinner tech: heavy melee one sinner once, finish the other with the timed upgrade, then punch the first as fast as possible and it lines up for another buff; the timing is tight and melee timings changed, so it is a bit inconsistent. [25:01]
- Even for farmers, lane creeps are the most efficient farm, especially early; they are easy to kill and give more than any jungle, while at 20 to 30 minutes you clear jungle very fast. [25:34]
- Abrams pins enemies to walls with Shoulder Charge and drains life with an aura (Siphon Life); he is very good at tanking and roaming. [26:04]
- Roaming example: after winning lane as Abrams, rotate to mid while the enemies are busy fighting, come in from the side with Shoulder Charge for a 3v2, get the kills and then kill the tower. [26:36]
- The more towers you kill, the more map control and money you get, and the stronger your gun and abilities become. [27:07]
- With your tower alive you can farm the wave under its safety, take a tier 2 camp in the downtime between waves, and be hard to kill, while enemies who lost their tower must walk back to their walker to be safe. [27:37]
- When the enemy walker dies the map opens: you can farm the enemy jungle (camps past the halfway line of the map), taking money from them. [28:38]
- If the enemy invades your jungle while you have towers, it is easy to hear the shooting, check the map and take the zipline over to fight with your towers backing you. [29:09]
- Objectives give map control, which means more kills and more money from stealing enemy farm; always kill your incoming waves first and prioritize lanes over jungle. [29:40]
- Do not AFK jungle farm; even as a farmer, be efficient: clear the wave, then go to jungle. [29:40]
- You start with eight item slots plus four locked flex slots: the first unlocks when all three enemy guardians are dead, the second when at least one enemy walker dies, the third when all three walkers die, and the fourth when one set of enemy base guardians dies. [30:13]
- Walkers, the second line of defense, are much tankier than guardians, do much more damage and can stun you if you get close, so be careful pushing them. [30:45]
- The base guardians are the last line of defense, with one set on each of the three lanes. [31:15]
- Do not worry about your KD: dying while killing a walker is an amazing play, since it gives money, opens the map, unlocks a flex slot and pulls several enemies to you. [31:47]
- Trading your life for a richer enemy is worth it: the game gives comeback gold, so the poorer player gains more money from a one-for-one trade. [32:48]
- The two stats that matter are damage dealt to enemy players and damage dealt to enemy objectives; finishing a lost game with no objective damage probably means you played it wrong. Some heroes, like Wraith, naturally deal a lot of objective damage. [33:20]
- A Haze with a better KD but less damage than others did not do his job, because Haze is a high damage dealer. [34:23]
- To end a stale game, do not send all six players to the last enemy walker: the enemy only has to defend that, and fighting under their walker is very hard. [34:53]
- Fighting under your own walker gives you extra spirit and bullet resist, while the enemy walker can stun you, laser you and do a lot of damage. [35:23]
- Priority one is clearing lanes and stopping objectives dying; priority two, over jungle, is helping your teammates: unless you are very low, join nearby fights, since a 2v1, 4v2 or even 4v3 is likely a win. [35:53]
- The Mid Boss spawns at 10 minutes and, he believes, respawns about 8 minutes after it is killed; it is very tanky and usually needs your team. [36:24]
- The Mid Boss drops the Rejuvenator; when it touches down, shows a green aura and the music stops, secure it with a heavy melee only; shooting and light melees do not work. [36:55]
- You can punch the Rejuvenator up to three times for the first Mid Boss and up to four times for every Mid Boss after. [37:55]
- Each Rejuvenator charge makes a dead teammate respawn after about 3 seconds at full health, and while your team holds at least one charge you get more fire rate, weapon damage, spirit power and health, for about five minutes. [37:55]
- The Rejuvenator is game defining: one or two Mid Bosses usually win the game, since with four charges the enemy effectively has to win a 6 versus 10. [38:56]
- To contest the Mid Boss, or to protect your own secure, have at least one player parrying to block the other team's heavy melees while your team hits it. [38:56]
- If an enemy hits you while you parry, they are stunned for quite a while and take more damage; heavy melees are risky because their attack animation and loud sound let the enemy react. [39:58]
- To end a game: secure Mid Boss, then push walkers, invade the enemy jungle, find a kill, push a walker, enter the base, kill the shrines and then the Patron. [40:29]
- In-game crosshair options are bad (you can disable custom hero crosshairs); he disables the crosshair entirely and uses Crosshair X from Steam, about five dollars, as an overlay. [41:29]
- For builds, look for pro players and streamers you trust: he recommends Zergy, Deathy and Pazilion, and has builds for Seven, Billy, Wraith and Warden. [42:29]
- The newer health bars are enabled with a console command (F7), captioned as "citadel_unit status use new equals true"; add it to Deadlock's Steam launch options so you do not have to type it every launch. [43:32]
- The new health bars show each enemy's ultimate status on their health bar, so you see instantly whether they have it, which matters against ults like Lady Geist's life swap. [44:03]
