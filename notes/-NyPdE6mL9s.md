# The Only Beginner’s Guide You Need for Deadlock
channel: Metro Deadlock
date: 2026-02-03
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-NyPdE6mL9s

## Covers
Full beginner guide after the big update: picking heroes and roles, bot matches, the build browser, boons, gun vs spirit items, melee and parry, stamina movement (dash jump, slide, wall jump), the laning stage, securing and denying souls, the healing trooper, jungle tiers, Sinner's Sacrifice and gold statue buffs, unsecured souls, the Urn, comeback souls, the new health bar command, which stats matter, Mid Boss and the Rejuvenator, and ending the game through Shrines and the Patron.
For brand new or returning players.

## Claims
- The game was invite only in closed alpha; you can get an invite through the Discord's request invites channel, usually within 24 hours. [0:32]
- Pick the heroes you enjoy most; balance has been good over the last year and does not matter much unless you reach a very high level. [1:04]
- Maining one hero is fine at the start, but later learn at least two or three heroes to a reasonable level, since another player may take your main. [1:04]
- Deadlock is a MOBA and hero shooter mix; MOBA principles apply and heroes fill roles: carries, supports, gankers, initiators and everything in between. [1:34]
- A carry is usually weak early and needs many souls: play passive, do lots of jungle farming, let your team fight early, then become powerful enough to 1v6 late; good carries to start with are Wraith, Seven and Infernus. [2:05]
- Supports have abilities strong at every point of the game that give value regardless of souls, for example Paige and Mo & Krill. [2:36]
- Dynamo is the best example of a support: even as the poorest player, a big Black Hole stuns the enemy team so your team can clean up. [2:36]
- Supports spend a lot of time in team fights and roaming the map looking for kills. [3:07]
- Tank heroes like Abrams, Billy and Shiv have a lot of HP, absorb damage, go in first and play front-line brawler. [3:07]
- New players should play a bot match or two first: you and five real players against bots, with no stakes and no ranking. [4:08]
- You can look at a hero's abilities in the picking screen before the game. [4:38]
- Items come in tiers of 800, 1600, 3200 and 6400 souls across multiple categories. [4:38]
- Use the build browser: pick a hero, open the public page and use the recommended builds, which rise in the list as more people like and play them. [5:08]
- Most top recommended builds are a good start, but some people spam bad builds to bait new players; pick a content creator you like or a build that looks good, and try another if it does not work. [5:38]
- Builds also tell you the order of ability level ups; an icon on the ability shows which to level next, chosen to synergize with the build's items. [6:08]
- Every hero has four main abilities; boons (level ups) unlock as you gain more souls. [6:08]
- Each hero can be built multiple ways: tanky, glass cannon all-out damage, or in between, with more power in abilities or gun. [6:38]
- The two main damage types are gun and spirit; orange gun items increase fire rate, damage per shot, or debuff enemies to take more gun damage. [7:08]
- Spirit (purple) items amplify abilities; abilities showing the purple star scale with spirit, and most abilities scale with spirit, very few with gun. [7:39]
- Many heroes benefit from having both gun and spirit damage. [7:39]
- Melee damage scales directly with your weapon damage. [8:09]
- A light melee is a single press; holding melee does a heavy melee that reaches further and does more than double the damage. [8:09]
- Parry (F by default) gives you a blue aura; parrying an enemy melee in time stuns them, and while stunned they take more gun, spirit and melee damage. [8:39]
- Many new players get punched to death because they do not realize melees have counters. [9:09]
- Practice parrying heavy melees in the practice range before playing; light melees can be parried too but are instant and harder to predict. [9:41]
- Every hero has a stamina bar below the crosshair that powers movement. [10:12]
- Dash is Shift; a single jump costs no stamina, but a double jump costs a stamina. [10:12]
- A dash jump is dash then jump at the right time; jump as soon as the small glow appears after the dash. [10:42]
- Pressing crouch while you have velocity (for example walking down stairs) slides you, which gives movement. [11:12]
- While sliding you do not lose ammo, so good movement lets you shoot for much longer. [11:43]
- Wall jumps (jump onto a wall, then press Space again) let you choose your direction and cost no stamina, for movement combos without using stamina. [11:43]
- The laning stage has three lanes, each 2v2. [12:14]
- The point of the laning stage is to kill the enemy Guardian before they kill yours, which opens the map, gives map control and extra souls. [12:45]
- Lane troopers are tanky early and deal significant damage, so respect them; your first priority in lane should always be shooting the enemy troopers. [13:15]
- A common new player trap is running headfirst at the enemy heroes while enemy troopers shoot you, then losing the fight. [13:46]
- Troopers are your front line: they absorb damage for you, and when pushing a Guardian they take its damage so you can hit it freely. [13:46]
- Start the laning stage by pushing enemy troopers. [14:16]
- Souls are the main income and the main factor in how strong you are; the team with more souls at the end usually wins. [14:16]
- When a trooper dies one soul drops to the floor (picked up automatically in range) and another floats up; shoot the floating soul to secure it for extra souls. [14:47]
- If the enemy shoots the floating soul from your trooper first, they deny it and get part of its value; always secure your own souls and deny the enemy's when you have time. [15:17]
- Your own troopers' floating souls show orange to the enemy, enemy ones green. [15:49]
- Killing enemy heroes is worth a lot of souls, so killing your laners is also a goal. [16:19]
- Shove the lane first so your troopers win against theirs; then trading is easy because the enemy is under fire from you and your troopers. [16:50]
- The healing trooper has a flag above its head; killing it drops a health orb that heals a base amount plus a percentage of your missing health when you are in range. [17:20]
- Beginner mistake: sitting far back and shooting troopers from range, leaving soul and healing orbs out of reach until they despawn; stay in range to pick them up. [17:50]
- The goal of the game is to kill the enemy Patron, and souls are everything for getting there. [18:51]
- Aggressive heroes can gank or rotate to other lanes after laning, whether they won or lost; a numbers advantage like 3v2 always gives you the edge in a fight. [18:51]
- Guardians only give souls; Walkers unlock flex slots: you start able to buy nine items with the last three slots locked, and each Walker kill unlocks one. [19:22]
- Walkers also give map control and souls, but are far stronger than Guardians early: they stun, deal a lot of long range damage and are harder to kill. [19:53]
- Enemies fighting under their Walker get resistances, so show up with a numbers advantage or do other things on the map before going for Walkers. [20:23]
- The jungle is stationary creeps that give souls comparable to lane troopers; lane troopers are usually the most efficient farm. [20:23]
- After laning, first make sure your lanes are pushed and towers defended, then go farm the jungle depending on your hero. [20:54]
- Jungle camps come in tiers one, two and three by how hard they are to kill: tier one at 2 minutes (some can be done between waves in lane), tier two at 6 minutes, tier three at 8 minutes. [21:25]
- Most heroes cannot farm tier three camps efficiently until later because they are very tanky. [21:25]
- The Sinner's Sacrifice is a structure you hit with heavy or light melees for souls; every hero farms it at the same pace, so even a poor jungler farms it as fast as a hard carry. [21:55]
- Every melee on a Sinner's Sacrifice drains a significant amount of your own HP, especially early. [22:26]
- After four heavy melees, lights on top of the Sinner's Sacrifice start filling; punch it at the third to last light so all lights are full to get the bonus. [22:26]
- The bonus is gold statue buffs lasting the whole game, random among health, spirit power, cooldown reduction, weapon damage, fire rate and ammo; a correctly timed hit gives four of them. [22:57]
- Gold statue buffs do not show on screen or in your soul count but are a massive advantage, so farm Sinner's Sacrifices as efficiently as possible. [22:57]
- In the double Sinner's Sacrifice room, heavy melee one once, go finish the other with the bonus, then turn back and keep heavy meleeing the first; it should line up so you get all eight buffs. [23:28]
- When there is no fight and the lanes are pushed, go farm your jungle. [23:59]
- If it is safe, invade the enemy jungle across the middle of the map to steal their resources and give your team more. [23:59]
- Boxes and gold statues around the map give bonus souls and buffs when shot or meleed. [24:29]
- Souls from jungle, kills and boxes are unsecured: if you die you drop them in an orb for the enemy, so the more unsecured souls you carry, the safer you should play. [25:01]
- Jungle is high risk, high reward because of unsecured souls. [25:32]
- Unsecured souls tick down over time if you play safe, but the best way to clear them is to go to the shop and spend as much as possible on your build. [25:32]
- The Urn spawns at 8 minutes in the middle of the map on one side, and either team can pick it up by walking to it for a few seconds. [26:02]
- Carrying the Urn makes you much tankier but you cannot shoot or cast, only melee. [26:33]
- Delivering the Urn to the marked drop off, usually on the other side of the map, gives your whole team a lot of souls and the carrier bonus souls, ability points and boons. [27:04]
- The enemy can see the Urn carrier, so ideally deliver it uncontested; if contested it becomes a team objective where several people go to the drop spot. [27:04]
- A heavy melee or a stun on the carrier makes them drop the Urn, so enemies use abilities or melees to stop the deposit. [27:35]
- If your team is more than 10% behind in souls when you pick up the Urn, the drop off is on your side of the map, an easier deposit and a chance to come back. [28:06]
- If you are far ahead, the drop off is deep in enemy territory, risky to deliver and likely defended by many enemies. [28:37]
- A team significantly behind in souls gets comeback souls: the further ahead the other team is, the more the behind team gets from kills, lane troopers and jungle. [28:37]
- In practice coming back from a big deficit is very hard, because the team ahead has the items to farm faster, win more fights and push further. [29:08]
- Before fighting, check the top of your UI for whether enemies have more or fewer souls than you, and which ultimates are up or down. [29:39]
- The team with many ultimates up against a team without is very likely to win a bigger team fight. [29:39]
- Press F7 to open the console and enter citadel_unit_status_use_new true for a new, cleaner health bar for you and enemies. [30:10]
- The new health bar shows an eye that is open when that hero has their ultimate and closed when they do not, so check it before starting a fight. [30:40]
- To keep it permanently, add +citadel_unit_status_use_new true to Deadlock's Steam launch options (right click, Properties). [30:40]
- KD is not a good indicator of how well you are doing; the stats that matter are your soul count, and after the game hero damage and objective damage; for supports, healing and damage absorbed matter too. [31:10]
- Your life and health bar are a resource; objectives and value across the map matter more than your own life. [31:42]
- Example: dying after killing a side Walker to two or three enemies is a great play: your team gets a flex slot and Walker souls, and three enemies' attention is pulled to your side while the other five play freely. [32:13]
- If you do not take risks and play aggressively, you will lose; the highest ranked players play very aggressively, make choices and let the enemy react. [32:44]
- Action is better than reaction: come in with your own game plan and play proactively instead of always fixing what the enemy does. [33:14]
- If a fight breaks out near you, always try to help unless you are very low; being there hurt or without an ultimate beats letting your team die. [33:14]
- Respawn timers get longer as the game goes on, giving the enemy more time to act after killing you. [33:44]
- Mid Boss is the most powerful objective; it spawns at 10 minutes but is very tanky and risky to take until around 20 minutes. [33:44]
- Before about 20 minutes Mid Boss takes your team a long time, a map-wide warning sound plays when it reaches 66% health so the enemy can contest, and early kills respawn fast enough to come back and contest. [34:15]
- When Mid Boss dies a Rejuvenator floats down; once it lands and the music stops, heavy melee it to secure a buff, up to three times currently. [34:45]
- Only heavy melees secure the Rejuvenator; light melees and shooting do nothing. [34:45]
- Each Rejuvenator buff is an extra life for your team: the next player to die respawns in place with full health 2 seconds later. [35:15]
- Parrying matters in Mid Boss fights: sit on the Rejuvenator and parry enemy heavy melees to stun them, then secure it yourself. [35:15]
- Stuns and displacements contest the Rejuvenator, for example Dynamo's Black Hole, Ivy's stone form stunning everyone around it, or Billy's chain pulling enemies out of range. [35:46]
- After 20 minutes, winning a big team fight with multiple enemies dead is usually a good time to take Mid Boss, since they stay dead long and you outnumber them in the pit. [36:16]
- With all three Rejuvenator buffs you are basically playing 9v6. [36:16]
- If you are behind and lose a fight around 20 minutes or later, expect the enemy to go for Mid Boss and be ready around mid to contest. [36:48]
- Most Walkers are usually dead before you attack the enemy base, which is the hardest thing to crack: lots of cover, enemy high ground, and base Guardians defending. [37:18]
- You must destroy both tanky Shrines before you can damage the Patron. [37:18]
- The Patron shoots back; after you kill it once it respawns with a second phase, and killing that second phase wins the game. [37:48]
- Push the enemy base only when powerful: a numbers advantage or souls advantage, ideally both, and Rejuvenator buffs as a bonus. [37:48]
