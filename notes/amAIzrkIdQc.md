# Mathematically Misunderstood Items
channel: Deathy
date: 2026-08-14
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=amAIzrkIdQc

## Covers
Mechanics and buy conditions for items that sit in the "optional" box of popular builds: Spirit Rend, Heroic Aura, Silencer, Spirit Sap, Mercurial Magnum, Transcendent Cooldown and Weakening Headshot.
For players who wonder whether an item did anything; heavy on hidden mechanics, stacking rules and damage-share data.

## Claims
- Spirit Rend builds from Spirit Shredder Bullets and keeps its effect of reducing the target's spirit resist by 8%, plus 10% spirit lifesteal against the target for you and all your allies, not just you. [0:31]
- Spirit Rend headshots reduce the target's spirit resist by a further 7% per headshot, on a 2 second cooldown, up to four stacks. [0:31]
- Resistances and resist reduction normally stack diminishingly, but Spirit Rend's stacks count as the same effect, so at max stacks it reduces spirit resist by a full 34%. [1:01]
- The 34% does still seem to stack diminishingly with the base 8% from Spirit Shredder Bullets, but it is more offensive value than Spirit Resilience gives defensively. [1:01]
- Spirit Rend takes 6 seconds to reach max stacks; the first stack comes instantly on your first headshot. [1:32]
- If four teammates with Spirit Rend all headshot the same target, it reaches max stacks instantly, though the UI does not show it. [1:32]
- In Eternus games (data from before the rank rework), past 20 minutes almost 70% of all damage dealt is spirit, and he suspects even more in lower ranks. [2:02]
- Spirit Rend gives the highest spirit resist reduction in the game at -34%, with nothing close; buy it more often. [2:02]
- Heroic Aura is both underrated and overrated; its passive aura gives bullet resist to nearby friendly units in a 35 m radius, a bit bigger than the rift area. [4:05]
- Heroic Aura's active gives movement speed and fire rate, and minions get double value; minions include summons like grave skulls, ghouls and McGinnis's turrets, troopers and rift troopers. [4:05]
- Heroic Aura's bullet resist aura is similar in value to Battle Vest for each nearby ally, theoretically up to 9,600 souls of value from that stat alone. [4:36]
- Heroic Aura shines in the first rift fights: buy it around the 12 minute mark and join the rift fight, especially if behind. [4:36]
- You want as many teammates with you as possible for Heroic Aura's value, and the rift is where that happens most. [5:07]
- In the current meta gun damage is highest in the early game, so Heroic Aura's defensive stat matters more early. [5:07]
- First rift troopers are hard to deal with after a buff a few weeks ago; if your team was behind (a comeback rift), using Heroic Aura's active when they reach the walker kills it in a few seconds. [5:07]
- Heroic Aura is overrated as a defensive layer, especially past 20 minutes, because bullet resist is not a great stat later, the same reason Bullet Resilience is not a very good item. [5:38]
- Silencer gives 12% spirit resist as a base stat and has two independent passive effects. [6:08]
- Silencer's 25% spirit damage output reduction applies on the first bullet that hits, from any distance, with no build-up; only the silence needs build-up. [6:38]
- Silencer is one of the most misunderstood items because there is no visual indicator for the spirit damage reduction; the debuff icon only represents the silence. [6:38]
- The only other gun item with spirit resist is Blood Tribute at 8%, vs Silencer's 12%. [7:38]
- Silencer's silence is extremely effective against heroes like Pocket or Mina. [7:38]
- View Silencer as a slightly weaker but much easier to apply Inhibitor against spirit heroes; with the silence it is not really weaker, it just reduces their damage less. [7:38]
- Spirit Sap has one base stat, 50 health; its active reduces the target's spirit resist by 9% and their spirit power by 30 for 12 seconds, on an 18 second cooldown, about 66% uptime if you keep vision. [8:08]
- Spirit power can go negative, so an early Spirit Sap can nullify some heroes' damage in lane; an Infernus sapped at 1,600 souls might do only around 10 damage per second with Afterburn. [8:38]
- Spirit Sap is an incredibly strong laning answer to high spirit damage heroes, and the better their ability's spirit scaling, the more effective it is. [8:38]
- Spirit Sap removes 30 spirit for 1,600 souls, while Improved Spirit costs 1,600 and gives only 18 (plus 11 from the shop investment bonus), so Sap removes more than the target can buy with that item. [9:08]
- Against a laner who does a lot of spirit damage, consider Spirit Sap; it could completely change your lane. [9:08]
- Mercurial Magnum upgrades from Quicksilver Reload: bonus spirit damage on the imbued ability, and after using that ability your bullets deal bonus spirit damage until you reload, with no duration. [9:38]
- Mercurial Magnum is a charge-up item: cooldown reduction does not affect it, and the imbued ability's bonus reaches max after 14 seconds unused. [9:38]
- The charge-up only affects the imbued ability's bonus, not the bullet bonus, so using it every 2 seconds still gives the full bullet bonus. [10:08]
- Mercurial Magnum's bullet bonus is a percentage of base bullet damage that scales with spirit power. [10:08]
- What matters for Mercurial Magnum is base DPS (base bullet damage times base fire rate), not base bullet damage; Paige has the highest base bullet damage but that does not make it better on her than on Vyper. [10:38]
- Hero DPS is on the Deadlock Wiki hero comparison table; at max boons the highest DPS heroes are Vyper, McGinnis, Calico, Venator, Vindicta and so on, all excellent Mercurial Magnum candidates. [10:38]
- Graves has the lowest DPS in the game at max boons and is generally not a good Mercurial Magnum buyer. [11:08]
- Base DPS rises only with boons, so Mercurial Magnum gets better as the game goes later. [11:08]
- Also consider for Mercurial Magnum how much spirit your hero buys, whether it likes fire rate, and whether it can amplify the item's spirit damage. [11:08]
- Celeste is bottom five in gun DPS at max boons but can still use Mercurial Magnum: she builds a lot of spirit, likes fire rate, Radiant Daggers amplify all her spirit damage including Magnum's, she likes the reload and has spirit lifesteal in her kit. [11:38]
- Transcendent Cooldown is the tier four upgrade of Superior Cooldown: for an extra 3,200 souls, 5% more cooldown reduction and, mainly, 25% item cooldown reduction. [12:09]
- Transcendent Cooldown's item cooldown reduction applies to every item except charge-up items: Quicksilver Reload, Tankbuster and Mercurial Magnum. [12:09]
- Transcendent Cooldown's 4 out-of-combat regen might as well be nothing for a 6,400 soul item. [12:39]
- Item cooldown reduction also shortens passive intervals: Siphon Bullets goes from every 1.2 seconds to every 0.9, and it works on Capacitor, Escalating Exposure, Spirit Rend, all the barrier items and so on. [12:39]
- He often buys Transcendent Cooldown with only one active item, frequently for Siphon Bullets on gun Lash; count passive-interval items, not just actives. [13:10]
- Weakening Headshot gives 60 bonus health and a headshot passive reducing the target's bullet resist by 13% for 12 seconds. [13:10]
- Since late May Weakening Headshot also works on NPCs, including jungle neutrals and guardians, but base guardians are not affected since they have no headshot hitbox. [13:40]
- Small and medium neutrals have 50% bullet resist and large neutrals 60%, so bullet resist shred is one of the most valuable farming stats, and Weakening Headshot is the best source for 1,600 souls. [13:40]
- Spirit Shredder Bullets, the spirit equivalent, only reduces resist by 10% instead of Weakening Headshot's 13%. [14:10]
- Consider Weakening Headshot on any hero that farms with actual gun damage, for example Venator. [14:10]
- Weakening Headshot is one of the most effective early tower pushing items; combined with Monster Rounds it is the most mathematically efficient tower pushing set in the laning stage. [14:10]
