## Covers
A long talk-over-gameplay overview for newer and low to mid rank players: how to learn in pieces, basic movement, barriers vs resistances, box routes and the soul hierarchy, wave stacking, deathballing and how to counter it, negative souls, map space, fighting vs farming, target choice, parries.
Then lane phase (one shared health bar and souls counter, healing trooper, zipline camping, vents, game timers, bridge buffs, Sinner's Sacrifice, 4.8k spikes), mid game objectives (Urn, Rift, Walkers, base Guardians, Shrines, Mid Boss and Rejuvenator roles), mental habits and beginner heroes (Seven, Mo & Krill, Lash, Dynamo, Wraith).

## Claims
- Don't try to apply a long guide all at once; cut it into sections (farming, lane, mid game) and learn one part per day or week before moving to the next. [0:30]
- The coach went from Initiate to Ascendant in about a month and a half by studying one topic at a time: lane phase first, then farming, then movement, then mid game. [1:01]
- After every mantle (wall, box, anything you climb) hold slide for a free slide that covers extra distance and gives free ammo. [2:02]
- If you are just short of a ledge, heavy melee before reaching it and hold jump to mantle the ledge while heavy meleeing, reaching new spots or covering extra distance. [2:02]
- In lane, when you are being rushed and lack stamina to round a corner, one heavy melee while running carries momentum around the corner and can be the difference between living on 50 HP and dying. [2:33]
- Always wall bounce while moving anywhere; it is free distance. Edge boosting (jumping right at the edge of a corner) sends you flying. [3:03]
- Jumping and dashing at the same time is an instant air dash: it goes farther and faster than a normal dash and you still get a slide after it. [3:33]
- Most players have poor aim, so adding verticality or quick horizontal dashes in a 1v1 throws off their aim and gives you free ammo from slides. [3:33]
- With Superior Stamina (caption "stamina mastery") you move faster using instant air dashes than manual dashes. [4:05]
- Squishy, low health heroes (Mina, Graves) get more from barriers than from resistances; tanky heroes (Abrams, Billy, Rat King) get more from resistances. [5:06]
- The math: 30% resist on 1,000 HP does little, on 5,000 HP it does a lot; a 500 barrier is 50% more health on 1,000 HP but only 10% on 5,000 HP, which is why barriers are weaker on tanks. [5:37]
- After the map changes there are many more boxes and more farm overall, so you need to run boxes more often. [6:38]
- Box route rule "make a big circle": from left lane walk back toward your Walker (few boxes past it), turn toward mid lane killing every heavy and regular box on the way, steal some mid boxes, then return to your lane via the bridge buff area. [7:08]
- Never just leave lane: tell your lane partner you are running a long box route, that they get the solo wave, should play safe near the Guardian and should tell you if the enemies freeze or push. [8:09]
- Running the box route at 3:00 when boxes spawn, both you and your lane partner should be around 3.6k to 3.8k souls when you return, enough for both ultimates, while enemies are usually around 2.8k. [8:40]
- Souls from anything except the minion wave are unsecured souls that you drop on death, so spend them immediately after the box route; if you are just short of an item, kill some waves first, then spend, then look for fights. [9:10]
- Once you have a soul lead from boxes, don't throw it by over pushing for a kill; if the enemies play scared, let them and keep outscaling, take your Sinner's Sacrifice or steal theirs. [10:12]
- Leaving your partner for a box route mostly only works in lower ranks; in higher ranks the enemies synergize and jump the solo laner. [10:42]
- It depends who you leave: a strong solo laner (caption "Magina") can hold alone, while a Vindicta left alone gets jumped and dies. [10:42]
- When surviving a lane solo, use the zipline, the rooftops near lane, the lane shop and the boxes to run circles and stall until your partner returns. [11:42]
- Farm efficiently: take bounce pads, wall bounce and slide between targets; walking to boxes with full stamina wastes time. [11:42]
- Getting off the zipline with crouch gives a little extra speed and distance; saving 5 to 10 seconds per trip stacks up. [12:12]
- Old soul hierarchy was lane, then Sinner's Sacrifice, then camps, then boxes; the coach now thinks boxes rank above camps (and Sinner's Sacrifice) in the early game because camps take too long. [12:42]
- Later in the game camps give more souls because there are so many of them. [13:13]
- The lane wave is the most important soul source; be there for almost every wave. Waves spawn every 30 seconds. [13:13]
- If you clear a wave about 5 seconds after it arrives, you have about 25 seconds to leave for a gank, boxes, camps or Sinner's Sacrifice and still be back for the next wave. [13:44]
- Great players leave lane and come back with an extra 300 to 600 souls in time for the next wave, over and over; that is how a farmer out-farms a teammate with more kills. [14:15]
- Don't kill one wave at a time: let the enemy wave stack to two and kill both together as they arrive, so your wave pushes into the enemy, you gain wave priority and get far longer to farm. [14:46]
- If nobody answers a pushing stacked wave it takes an objective; if they do answer it costs them around 50 seconds or more. [15:17]
- Trading single waves back and forth ("ping pong") in low ranks is why Walkers suddenly fall; always kill two waves to force the enemy to respond. [15:48]
- Deathballing (playing in a big group constantly fighting for picks) splits kill souls: solo or duo kills are huge soul injections, often 1,000+ souls, and an assist counts toward the split. [16:18]
- A six player kill may give only about 200 souls, roughly one tier three camp creep, so it is nearly useless. [16:50]
- The only real benefit of deathballing is being far above the enemy skill level with everyone on Trophy Collector to soften the split. [16:50]
- A team that side pushes, takes Walkers and objectives and farms camps out-scales a deathball. [17:20]
- Deathballs are usually four players, not six, with carries left on the side lanes and supports or spirit nukers diving and invading your jungle; most solo queue teams can't coordinate an answer. [17:51]
- The coach's example of solo queue: his team killed the Mid Boss and left the Rejuvenator behind, which the enemies took a minute later. [18:51]
- Best solo queue answer to a deathball: get one listening teammate to push out every wave and go with you to the side with a solo enemy, jump him, at least damage the Walker, then go steal their jungle. [19:21]
- Counter stealing their jungle makes it about an even trade, and you also hold wave priority on both side lanes while they only hold mid. [19:51]
- Teams that deathball too much end up with four underfed players while the two side laners on your team stay fed; your goal is to steal the farm they steal, kill them if you can, or take an objective. [20:21]

- Solo Walkers are a massive soul injection; letting a farming Wraith take Walkers alone all game is how games are lost, even after your team takes the Rift. [21:21]
- Many heroes can solo a Walker, especially later in the game, so look for them. [22:21]
