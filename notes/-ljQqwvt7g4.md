# Play The LATEGAME Like an ETERNUS | Comprehensive Lategame Guide
channel: HERESY
date: 2026-06-24
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-ljQqwvt7g4

## Covers
A late game guide in three parts working back from the ending: using Rejuvenator and sieging the base (Urn, jungle, shrines, base Guardians, Patron mechanics), tactics (calling and fighting Mid Boss, winning ahead and behind, base defense, shot calling, with Eternus clips), and strategy (late game itemization, who farms waves vs jungle, ganker vs team fight comps).
For players who reach the late game but fail to close out or come back.

## Claims
- The late game is best defined as the part of the game where securing one or two kills is likely to convert to a Mid Boss take. [0:30]
- Late game starts sooner in high elo than in low elo, because players are more confident and skilled at taking Mid Boss fights and see more opportunities. [0:30]
- The Mid Boss is the designers' intended mechanic for letting the winning team actually win, because sieging the base is extremely defender sided. [1:02]
- Sieging is hard: the last Walker is the tankiest and grants defenders resistances, defenders have high ground on ziplines, balconies and ramps, can shop and heal freely in base, and the Patron and shrines curse and damage attackers. [1:32]
- The Mid Boss sits in the most neutral space but slightly favors the winning team, which has more map presence and pushes waves further. [2:02]
- Later in the game, think first about how to set up for and secure Mid Bosses rather than how to push the enemy base. [2:32]
- Deadlock's late game is when kills and fights center on securing the Mid Boss over anything else. [2:32]
- The Mid Boss respawn timer goes from 7 minutes at first to 6 then 5 after later kills, but the Rejuvenator always lasts 4 minutes. [3:02]
- In most cases delay using your Rejuvenators as long as possible: don't throw yourselves at the nearest Walker or base Guardians right after Mid Boss; become as strong as possible before one single all-in push. [3:34]
- Rejuvenator lasts 4 minutes but grants at most three lives, so you really get one all-in good fight; gather as much strength as possible first while you have the safety of free respawns. [4:04]
- First, if an Urn is on the map, your team almost certainly wants to run it before sieging: it gives souls for the siege and removes the enemy's main comeback tool, which they could run while you respawn after a lost siege. [4:34]
- If the Urn will respawn at the very end of the Rejuvenator you can ignore it and siege first; if it spawns in one to three minutes while you still have Rejuvenator, get it off the map before burning your respawns. [5:05]
- A comeback Urn gives the depositing team 35% bullet, spirit and debuff resistance inside the king of the hill circle, worth thousands of souls, so even with Rejuvenator you are probably more likely to lose an even 6v6 over it unless far ahead in souls. [5:37]
- If the enemies control a comeback Urn, don't fight on the deposit; play for cross map kills and harass. In high elo most teams let the enemy deposit the comeback Urn and then fight and kill them after. [6:07]
- Second, before sieging, take care of the jungle: with free respawns the enemy won't want to fight you there, so invade and eat their camps, fall back to farm your own, and spend the souls on items before a full siege. [6:38]
- Growing a soul advantage first is the most reliable way to make the Mid Boss push succeed; sieging without one can burn away your Mid Boss. [7:08]
- Respawn timers are perhaps the most valuable resource in Deadlock; almost no soul advantage is more important than the enemy team simply being dead. [7:38]
- If you kill three players, regardless of being down souls or not having cleared the jungle, run down their objectives and get something for it. [8:08]
- Knowing how much value to squeeze from enemy respawn timers is perhaps the single most important late game skill and the main reason lower ranked players fail to close games. [8:08]
- When the enemies are dead, push something even if you are low HP or have souls to spend; spend and heal later. [8:38]
- Ideally don't start a fight at enemy objectives, all of which are favored ground: Walkers break your armor, set the ground on fire and grant allies resistance, and in the base defenders have high ground, minion and healing advantage. [9:09]
- Push out the waves so all three lanes push into the enemy base; this creates splits where some enemies go out to catch waves instead of grouping in one six man death ball. [9:40]
- Initiate fights on isolated enemies catching waves; if three enemies are across the map, your team can take a quick 6v3 on the Walker instead. [10:12]
- If many enemies group on the last Walker you want, don't start the fight there; that hands them an even fight around a Walker. Start base sieges by getting gank kills. [10:42]
- You are typically not trying to drop the Patron and end the game with your first Mid Boss, but one Mid Boss played right practically guarantees the win most of the time. [11:13]
- If the enemy has one last Walker, unless your team is severely slot locked well past 30 minutes, prioritize the base Guardians and pushing forward to take shrines. [11:13]
- Shrines are the most important permanent objective in Deadlock. [11:45]
- The first shrine always has half the HP of the second; killing it gives your troopers in that lane and the blue lane +60% damage and +40% health, and makes them worth 15% fewer souls to the enemy. [11:45]
- Shrine wave buffs guarantee constant wave pressure that forces the enemy to respond, which you convert into ganks or cross map 6v4 or 6v5 fights, then the respawning Mid Boss or a traditional end if timers are long enough. [12:16]
- Destroying base Guardians gives your team a permanent zipline speed boost in that lane. [12:47]
- You cannot kill shrines until you have killed one set of base Guardians, which acts as backdoor protection. [13:18]
- As a general rule Walkers are more important than base Guardians, but shrines are more important than anything else. [13:18]
- When defending, trading a Walker to take an enemy shrine is almost always worth it. [13:18]
- Base Guardians have less HP than lane Guardians but can't be headshot and have a built-in 20% bullet resist. [13:50]
- Base Guardians also have 40% bullet resist that decays by 20% per nearby enemy player down to zero; shrines have the same mechanic starting at 60%, so both are much easier to take sieging as a team and harder to backdoor. [13:50]
- Patrons are the only objective that regenerates health over time, currently 120 HP per second. [14:20]
- When the Patron's first phase falls and it goes invulnerable while transforming, a last stand reduces dead defenders' respawn timers: by 20 seconds if over 30 seconds, by 10 if between 10 and 30 seconds, down to a minimum of 10 seconds. [14:20]
- A defender in the Patron's pit for at least two seconds gives the Patron 50% damage resistance, two defenders 100%, so attackers must kill all defenders in the pit. [14:52]
- Those Patron mechanics make ending off one push extremely hard, so the goal with the first Rejuvenators is to amass as much soul advantage and as many structures as possible to be favored for the second Mid Boss and end right after. [15:23]
- If you drop the Patron's first phase or shrines with significant Rejuvenator time left and 5,000 unspent souls each, back, spend, heal and quickly re-engage; this makes most sense after the first Patron phase since the enemies will all be respawning anyway. [15:23]
- Call for Mid Boss when you are convinced your team is much more likely to win a fight: up souls, up kills, or half the enemy team pushing a Walker across the map while yours is ready. [16:25]
- You never have to fully burn down the Mid Boss; damage it to 50% so it roars, bait the enemies in to contest, and beat them in the fight before killing it. [16:56]
- You can force the Mid Boss even with all six enemies alive if you have other reasons to be confident of winning the fight. [17:27]
- Team composition is crucial for Mid Boss fights, especially AoE hard CC such as Ivy's Stone Form, Sinclair's Rabbit Hex, Dynamo's ult and Infernus's ult, which can stop a team from securing the Rejuvenator when it hits the floor. [17:57]
- Teams up four or five players often get their Rejuvenators stolen not from missed parries and punches but because the enemy had superior ultimates for securing them; this calculation is separate from players alive and souls. [18:27]
- Call Mid Boss when you are very likely to win the fight; then if your team is favored to secure the Rejuvenators, kill it instantly, and if the enemy has the tools to steal it, make it roar and kill your opponents first. [18:58]
- Making correct calls confidently works more often than not and raises your win rate even when teammates don't always follow. [19:29]
- In the Mid Boss fight, your role depends on your hero: a backline DPS with little CC like Wraith should not stand in the pit punching but play the high ground, stairs and outskirts to kill anyone who contests or secures the crystals. [19:29]
- If you are the hero with the key AoE stun or CC (Dynamo, Ivy with Stone Form), you are the most important person for the Mid Boss fight: don't waste your skills beforehand and position correctly. [20:00]
- An Ivy who times Stone Form and avoids being CC'd or picked before the Mid Boss fight secures many more Rejuvenators and wins more games. [20:31]
- When the enemy initiates Mid Boss, everything applies in reverse: even a 3v6 can secure some or all Rejuvenators with tools like Stone Form, Dynamo's black hole, or The Doorman carting everyone through a wall. [21:01]
- It is very frequently worth attempting to steal at least one Rejuvenator, because it cripples the enemy push. [21:31]
- If most of your team is alive and the enemy calls Mid Boss, consider how to win the fight: a backline carry like Venator or Wraith can pump AoE damage into the pit to strip Rejuvenators after they are secured. [21:31]
- Even if you die doing it, taking away one or two enemy Rejuvenators to weaken their push is very often the best thing you can do; with high ranged or AoE damage heroes, encourage your team to contest even if you expect to lose the fight. [22:01]
- Use wave priority to predict where enemies must show: with priority right and Rem minions pushing yellow, at least one enemy will catch the yellow wave, so fights mid or green are likely numbers advantaged. [23:08]
- If you're down your most important player while Mid Boss is alive you're not favored even when up souls and should retreat, unless wave priority shows an enemy is off fixing waves, giving a brief 5v5 window. [23:08]
- With waves shoved out and enemies split, act immediately on kills even when down a player; disabling their carry with Curse can almost guarantee the following Mid Boss. [24:10]
- A side laning carry with a soul advantage gets wave priority and rotates first; precise calls like matching an enemy who shoves green can turn an Urn fight into 5v4 or 6v5. [24:40]
- Carries are supposed to farm a lot, but the most fed player must join game ending late game team fights; you are not supposed to farm all game long. [25:11]
- Squeeze all the value from Rejuvenators: with 60 seconds and one life left, don't retreat just because a few are low; free lives are often more than enough to win otherwise even exchanges. Be vocal if your team doesn't know this. [25:11]
- With a free respawn you can take an otherwise suicidal position to bait out key enemy abilities (for example Mo & Krill combo and a Seven stun) for an advantageous fight. [26:14]
- Blue lane wave priority plus knowing one enemy is running the Urn tells you your side likely has equal or more players. [26:44]
- After wiping most of their team with long respawn timers you can push the base even with no Mid Boss alive; it is always possible, just less preferable if Mid Boss is alive. [26:44]
- With long enemy respawn timers you don't need every wave crashed into the base; crash any minion wave in as soon as possible to remove base Guardian backdoor protection and get to the shrines. [27:14]
- When committing a final push with Rejuvenators left, don't send a straggler to fix a lane; commit aggressively. [27:45]
- With 30 seconds and two extra lives left, a confident call to re-engage a 5v6 after losing a player turned a push that took one set of base Guardians into clearing all the shrines. [28:16]
- After cleanly wiping the enemy with 50+ seconds of timers, don't send the whole team to Mid Boss; send one player who can solo it, or two, and everyone else pushes the base as fast as possible. [28:47]
- If enemies respawn faster, call "if you are low, go mid": Mid Boss rarely kills low players late since they can pass it to a healthier teammate, while low players could die to a defender, so healthy teammates push shrines. [29:18]
- Look for kills on an isolated carry in the 60 seconds before Mid Boss spawns; players think about not getting picked while it is alive but much less before it spawns. [29:49]
- When a fight stabilizes, falling back instead of pushing further can be right. [30:19]
- With full HP, high spirit damage for the base and an ultimate that gives a second life, pushing the base solo while your team does Mid Boss can be correct, but only if you know where key enemies are and their team isn't good at stealing; never let Mid Boss get stolen behind you. [30:50]
- A player who is weak at both pushing base and Mid Boss can run the Urn while the team handles everything else. [31:20]
- When winning, you still win through the basics: keep wave priority, take jungle camps when proper, buy items and fight around power spikes. [31:50]
- Being up souls and structures lets you dictate the map state and shove waves at will; use the soul advantage to create numbers advantage fights rather than handshaking 6v6s. [31:50]
- When behind and the enemy has Rejuvenators, they have the ball: farm as much as you can (jungle camps, boxes) while playing safe to avoid being the breakthrough kill. [32:50]
- Pay close attention to the wave state; you can often predict when the enemy will push from the waves they play around. [32:50]
- When behind with Mid Boss spawning in 80 seconds, kills in the next fight overlap with the spawn and favor the winner, so send five players to one lane (yellow) to hunt someone down and ignore the other lanes. [33:20]
- If you see one enemy green and one blue, death balling yellow is likely a numbers advantage; this slows the enemy push and buys time to exploit comeback souls and scale. [33:52]
- When behind with no Mid Boss up but an Urn available, force a 6v5 Urn exchange as your best chance to slingshot back. [33:52]
- Keep taking numbers advantaged fights and your soul deficit can be completely negated. [34:25]
- In the deep late game kills mean about 80 second respawn timers, enough to convert to objectives or the Mid Boss much later; with a massive wave pushing out, wait for someone to show on it before fighting. [34:55]
- Track ultimates: when the enemy is missing all their ults, especially heroes built around them like a fed Victor or Mo & Krill, call a fight in that window. [35:55]
- An enemy like Infernus walking up too far and getting caught solo can be converted immediately into a Mid Boss take. [35:55]
- Comeback Urn resistances can make a fight roughly equal or favored even when down a player, if your team gets into the zone; a side laner with green priority reaching the teleporter first can make it 5v5 before the sixth enemy arrives. [36:25]
- Very often after dunking the comeback Urn the correct call is to retreat, because you lose the resistances; play on only if you know you are winning the fight. [36:57]
- On base defense, play the high ground and remember you can heal in base behind you, which lets you take aggressive trades and build a health advantage even against Rejuvenator. [36:57]
- The second shrine has twice the HP of the first, so you often want to wait to dump AoE ults until attackers stack up on the second shrine, where they take longer and are all in one place; you can also fight after they drop your Patron. [37:28]
- If the enemies don't shove all your waves, consider threatening a backdoor against them, especially if you have dropped their Patron before. [37:58]
- Deadlock has the highest skill ceiling for shot calling of any competitive game the coach has followed; noticing wave states and making tactical calls turns into ganks, Mid Boss kills, Urns and Walkers. [38:29]
- Some late game items are nice to have one per team, because practically no effects stack; duplicates only refresh duration. [39:30]
- Healing Tempo is only worth it if your team has a hero good at applying it. [39:30]
- Heroic Aura is mandatory: in practically every game one player on your team should have it. [40:03]
- Silencer is basically the spirit damage version of Heroic Aura and is best combined with a hero who builds Ricochet. Juggernaut, Inhibitor and Healing Tempo are also good one per team. [40:03]
- Multiple Healbane instances don't stack; only one player's Healbane applies, and duplicates just refresh duration. [40:33]
- Heal reduction from different sources stacks multiplicatively, so it is good to get multiple different anti-heal sources, especially late. [41:04]
- Counter carry items deal with particular carry threats; Cursed Relic is especially good against an enemy with a very high net worth lead over the lobby. [41:04]
- A supportive or utility hero facing a single hard carry (for example a Wraith at 32K while her team is at 22K) should consider an earlier Cursed Relic or Disarming Hex instead of the normal build. [41:34]
- Phantom Strike grounds flyers, and lets a close range or melee hero gap close onto a fed Wraith. [41:34]
- Anti-tank items do some variation of max HP or (Tankbuster) current health damage, which scales as carries gain HP late; Armor Piercing Rounds can sometimes be a gun hero's only way through a tank's stacked resistances. [42:05]
- Shred from different sources stacks multiplicatively, but each item applies only once, so two Hollow Point Rounds don't double; want one player with Hollow Point and spread out shred types. [42:36]
- If a teammate like Seven or Victor already applies Mystic Vulnerability to the whole enemy team, don't buy it yourself just for the 8% spirit resist. [43:08]
- Practically every hero every game, except Rem, should own at least one of the three 3,200 soul defensive items by the deep late game (30+ minutes), to deal with DoTs like Infernus afterburn, roots, silences, Slowing Hex, Shiv's knives, and to counter spell ults like Lash's, Pocket's Affliction and Infernus's. [43:38]
- Warp Stone can also dodge some of those abilities like a counter spell, and works as a repositioning, gap closing or defensive item; skipping these defensive items is sometimes okay but probably not optimal. [44:08]
- Refresher, "Indom" (as captioned) and Unstoppable are tied to Mid Boss fight economy; Dynamo and maybe Kelvin often buy them. [44:08]
- Track the cooldowns of enemies with Refresher and who has Unstoppable or similar CC immunity, since Mid Boss calls depend on whether enemies have ultimates and CC immunity to secure the Rejuvenators. [44:39]
- If you play a hero with a very impactful Mid Boss oriented ultimate, heavily consider buying those items. [45:09]
- Do not hold Golden Goose Egg until 30 minutes; that is throwing the game. [45:09]
- Every hero, especially in matchmaking, wants practically as many souls as possible. [45:40]
- Immobile heroes like Venator and Seven are vulnerable walking up to collect side or mid waves late; getting caught 5v1 and dying probably loses the game regardless of souls. [45:40]
- Hyper mobile, good wave clear heroes like Pocket should take care of the waves since they survive when multiple enemies show; immobile heroes should mainly take the jungle, the safer camps on your side unless teammates are directly supporting them. [46:11]
- Farming is not a win condition by itself: if more farming won't get you another item before an important Mid Boss or Urn fight, or makes you late to the shop and the fight, you are farming too much. [46:43]
- Gankers have ways to lock down, burst or pull out of position single isolated targets, setting up kills that become 6v5 Mid Boss fights, but generally struggle more in 6v6 team fights. [47:14]
- If your team has many gankers, prioritize playing on waves and isolated kills instead of defaulting to 6v6s in blue lane or forcing Mid Boss fights; identify whether your team is gankers or team fighters and plan accordingly. [47:46]
- With a ganker comp, don't let your Holliday and Mo & Krill look for kills alone; play with them in the late game. [48:17]
- Most matchmaking teammates won't follow calls or play ideally, even in Eternus 6 lobbies, but applying these ideas personally and making more assertive comms will increase your win rate over time. [48:48]
