# Why Your Aim Is Inconsistent in Deadlock (And How to Actually Fix It)
channel: Metro Deadlock
date: 2026-05-14
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8NmFosO4_pE

## Covers
Aim fundamentals for players of any rank who feel their aim is inconsistent: where to look, sensitivity and DPI, arm and chair setup, flicking vs tracking heroes, Venator spread, aim training routines, bullet velocity and High Velocity Rounds, mouse and mouse pad, frame rate, and sleep and practice habits.

## Claims
- Average aim is actually quite bad because most players have never practiced it or set up the right settings, so above average aim is reachable with fairly low effort. [0:30]
- Very good players look almost exclusively at the target they are shooting, not at their crosshair; many could track almost as well with the crosshair turned off. [0:30]
- Aim is two parts: 50% is reading the situation with your eyes, 50% is converting it into mechanical aim. [1:00]
- Staring at the target lets you see immediately when an enemy is about to dash, jump or slide (for example a Warden ducking down to start a slide), and you adjust subconsciously. [1:30]
- Start practicing by always looking at the enemy, not the crosshair. [2:32]
- Copying a streamer's sensitivity one to one usually changes nothing and can make aim worse; constantly readjusting sensitivity week to week is a common mistake. [3:03]
- Sensitivity itself is not very important; what matters is sticking to one and practicing with it. A good player can perform near their best on any reasonable sensitivity. [3:33]
- Higher DPI means lower input lag; most high level players use 1600 DPI. [4:03]
- If you are on 800 DPI, double the DPI in your mouse software and halve the in-game sensitivity: it feels identical but is more responsive. [4:03]
- The coach runs 0.42 sensitivity in game. [4:34]
- Starting blueprint at 1600 DPI: wrist aimers around 0.4, arm aimers around 0.25, then adjust up or down. [4:34]
- You are a wrist aimer if most of your adjustments come from the wrist, an arm aimer if you use the whole arm. Arm aimers need lower sensitivity because they make wider movements and would over aim. [5:04]
- A wrist aimer should be able to do a 180 comfortably without moving the arm much; 0.4 is usually the sweet spot. [5:36]
- Try the blueprint settings for a few hours at most, then adjust; neither aim style is better, go with what is comfortable. [6:06]
- Your forearm should sit about halfway on the mouse pad. Keep your elbow off the pad (it drags and slows adjustments); with only the wrist on the pad there is no support and aim feels jittery. [6:37]
- Set chair height so your arm rests comfortably and roughly straight on the mouse pad, with your shoulder neither lifted up nor sliding down. [7:08]
- Arm rests are personal preference; the coach keeps his lowered because anything uncomfortable around the arm is not ideal. [7:39]
- On slow fire rate heroes like Lady Geist or Holliday, flick: hitting every bullet matters, so click manually for each shot instead of holding down mouse one. [8:09]
- Once your fire rate gets high enough later in the game (every hero gets faster with gun items), manual clicking loses damage, so switch to holding mouse one. [8:40]
- Heroes that shoot very fast at base, like Wraith, are tracking heroes: never stop holding mouse one and do not burst fire. [9:10]
- Venator is the only hero with increasing spread: the first shots are 100% accurate, and after around seven to eight bullets the spread kicks in. [9:40]
- On Venator at long range, after about seven bullets use an ability or stop shooting for a second to reset the spread. [10:12]
- For tracking heroes the number one thing is smooth motion; every aggressive readjusting flick is a drop-off point where you miss bullets. [10:12]
- If you miss more than a few bullets on a predictable target, you are either tensing your arm too much or have not got smooth tracking down yet. [11:14]
- KovaaK's or Aim Lab smooth tracking scenarios (search "smooth"), 10 to 20 minutes a day, improve smoothness significantly. [11:14]
- Every hero's fire rate goes up over the game, even without gun items (gold statues raise it too), so most heroes become tracking heroes late and tracking matters a lot in Deadlock. [11:45]
- Cap aim training at 30 minutes a day; there are diminishing returns, like muscle and nervous system fatigue in the gym. Spend the rest of your time playing the game. [12:15]
- In-game warm up: place a few bots in the practice range and go bot to bot making sure every shot hits, to train flick distance. [12:46]
- Once that is consistent, add your own movement (strafing, sliding) while hitting the stationary bots, since in real games you must move and dodge. [13:17]
- Your eyes are a muscle and tire like any other; eyetrainer.gg (said as "itrainer.gg"), free, has you follow a dot with your eyes. Top aim trainer players use it to warm up before touching the mouse, and it helps with eye strain. [14:20]
- Deadlock has bullet velocity that varies by hero, so even high fire rate heroes like Wraith miss more at long range regardless of aim. [15:21]
- Warden's base bullet velocity is quite low; at long range against an enemy who AD strafes you will miss some bullets even with perfect aim. [15:52]
- High level players buy High Velocity Rounds to be more consistent at long range; it removes the guesswork of which way the target moves and is very good on Warden. [16:22]
- Know roughly the bullet velocity of the heroes you play and lead targets at long range; long range starts around 20 m, inside that you can aim directly at them. [16:52]
- A mouse that fits your grip matters: a mouse too small forces you to tense your hand, which tires you out over sessions longer than an hour. [17:24]
- Mouse recommendation: the GPX (Logitech G Pro X) one and two; the budget one is about 60 dollars on sale. It is a universal shape that suits most hand sizes, which is why so many pros use it. [17:54]
- Mouse pads matter almost as much as mice. Test: draw a smooth left to right line at constant speed; if you cannot, your pad is likely worn or uneven. [19:24]
- Mouse pad recommendations: a new budget SteelSeries pad (about 20 dollars on sale), or Artisan pads, the Hien or Hayate Otsu, which are expensive but last a very long time. [19:55]
- More frames make aim smoother and stutters hurt it; Piggies' settings guide covers the Deadlock settings for more frames, and pros run everything on low while keeping decent resolution. [19:55]
- Sleep 7 to 8 hours a day; most learning happens in the sleep after playing, and 7 or more hours on average is needed to regenerate and learn fully. It is the number one life change for better performance. [20:56]
- Good sleep and diet matter more for reaction time than age; a well rested 30 year old will outperform a 16 year old who does not sleep enough, and many Counter-Strike pros are around 30. [21:58]
- High level aim comes from time and effort, not genes; practice matters far more than talent. [22:59]
