# Why Is Eido So Much Better Than You At Deadlock?
channel: Metro Deadlock
date: 2026-04-09
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uXE1ZQN8Y8k

## Covers
Five macro habits that separate top Eternus players from everyone else: take more team fights, stop farming non-sinner jungle, treat dying as a resource, don't mindlessly hit Walkers or base, and don't counter buy when behind.
For players of every rank up to low Eternus who think movement, aim or builds are what hold them back.

## Claims
- The skill gap to the top players is not their movement, aim or builds; it is macro habits. [0:00]
- The number one thing holding most players back is not taking enough team fights. [0:30]
- In high Eternus games people almost always stick around in fights with low HP; lower ranks use their abilities, get damage out, then back out to base, jungle or a side lane. [0:30]
- Staying in the fight with low HP from a safe position nets far more kills; even at one HP, if the enemy is down to one or two players, come in from the side and use your abilities again, accepting you might die, and you win more fights. [1:00]
- Taking more fights makes you better at the game; don't play lane, farm, farm more and only have one to five fights per game without actively looking for engagements. [1:30]
- Treat it as time invested: you will lose fights early from bad micro, missed abilities or bad movement, but the only way to learn to win fights is to take them. [1:30]
- Most heroes benefit far more from fighting than farming. [1:30]
- "Limit testing": every Eternus player has repeatedly tested which fights they can take; you never learn whether you can get away with a fight unless you take it. [2:01]
- Stop farming jungle: apart from Sinner's Sacrifice, all creep camps are currently weak. [2:01]
- Even farmed efficiently, non-sinner jungle camps give less than lane, less than Sinner's Sacrifice, and in most cases less than running boxes. [2:31]
- In high level lobbies most heroes are rarely in the jungle; jungle should be reserved for hard carries that farm it efficiently, currently heroes like Vindicta, Lady Geist, Wraith or Seven. [2:31]
- Jungling on a hero that can't farm it efficiently costs you souls and makes it harder for your team, which should play around the objectives that matter, first and foremost Urn and Walkers. [3:01]
- After the 8 minute mark when Sinner's Sacrifices spawn, if you are still in the laning stage you can farm a sinner or two, especially as a carry, but if there is an Urn fight or an Urn to pick up, do that instead. [3:01]
- Even with no fight and the Urn despawned, better uses of time are rotating into the enemy jungle to steal their sinners or catching an enemy who is farming, for the full unsecured soul value. [3:32]
- Most heroes are designed to fight in the mid game; current macro is push waves first, then look for team fights, ganks and objectives. [3:32]
- Even if you are the only one on your team looking for fights, it nets more wins in the long run. [3:32]
- Encourage your team in voice or text chat to run the Urn, look for fights and invade the enemy jungle; playing actively beats playing reactively. [4:02]
- Sitting in your own jungle won't get you ahead and won't let you catch up when behind. [4:02]
- When behind, look even more actively for single target pick-offs, gank as three or four, or run the Urn for a comeback Urn. [4:32]
- Stop being scared of dying: KD is far less important than other stats in this game. [5:02]
- Dying for an objective is good, for example killing a Walker and dying for it. [5:02]
- Dying to multiple enemies spending solo ultimates on you is good; the coach was solo Black Holed and solo Lash ulted, and his team cleaned up the fights because the enemy spent so much on him. [5:34]
- Dying one for one is always good when you are behind, because comeback gold raises every income source for the team behind; a one for one trade into a target above your net worth is a good death. [6:05]
- Dying on a side lane in the mid game to four or five gankers is fine if you live a while first: respawns are short, the kill is split four ways with low assist gold, and your team can push other lanes, take Walkers, a free Urn or the enemy's Sinner's Sacrifices and jungle meanwhile. [6:35]
- Players from other shooters overvalue KD; a teammate can go 0-10 and still be a carry, though repeatedly running in and feeding for nothing is bad. [7:36]
- Dying is a resource like any other; if dying makes sense, die rather than back out and get nothing for the time invested. [8:06]
- Statlocker data shows top players (Zergi, Eternus 6 players) often have average or below average KD while ranking higher; even top 10 players have below average KDs. [8:06]
- The stats that indicate how well you are doing are objective damage, damage to enemies, and healing if you are a support; check your hero percentiles for them. [8:37]
- The coach's own KD is bottom 30% while he is Eternus 6. [8:37]
- Don't go out of your way just to stay alive while your whole team dies. [9:08]
- Teams that get ahead in low ranks lose by mindlessly attacking Walkers or the base, for example right after Mid Boss with three Rejuvenators. [10:10]
- Walkers and especially the base are very defender sided: Walkers give every nearby defender spirit and bullet resist, so a fight under a Walker means taking Walker shots and stomps while every enemy is buffed. [10:40]
- With a lead, first look for enemy kills; after a kill or two, push the Walkers that are still up for free. [10:40]
- If you can't find kills, take the entire enemy jungle, cutting off everything but lane creeps and forcing them to sit in base. [11:11]
- Push the base once the lead reaches around 20, 30 or 40k souls; it is pretty safe then. [11:11]
- Invading the enemy jungle usually gets someone on the enemy team caught out for a free kill, which then gives you the rest of the objectives for free. [11:11]
- The other good time to push Walkers is when every wave is pushing into the enemy Walkers, even on an even playing field, because the enemy can't split efficiently to defend two or three Walkers at once. [11:41]
- When waves push into several Walkers, either one goes undefended and dies for free, or they defend all and lose two; both are efficient trades even if you lose someone. [12:11]
- Only attack objectives if the waves are pushed and you have map control, or if you are ahead. [12:11]
- Do not counter buy when behind; next to not taking team fights it is the biggest mistake in every rank, especially low rank. [12:11]
- Example: enemies of a farmed Wraith all bought Spirit Resilience, which is technically correct since Wraith does mostly spirit damage, but the real problem was they never focused her; she had zero or one green item and was very squishy. [12:42]
- To counter a fed player when behind, focus them first: save your ultimate for them, go into the fight looking for them, especially as a tank, and tell your team to focus them. [13:44]
- If the whole team counter buys after losing to a fed player, they likely lose the next fight too, having spent souls on defense they needed for damage; the fed player just takes longer to kill them while they can't kill her at all. [13:44]
- Instead buy items that help you kill the fed player: Slowing Hex, Knockdown, anything that catches them off guard, maybe Curse. [14:15]
- If the enemy is ahead they already out-damage you; buying green items makes you lack even more damage, and no amount of HP helps if you can't kill them. [14:46]
- Buy counter items when you are one of the most farmed players or your team is ahead; the most farmed player, already doing enough damage, should invest in staying alive when the enemy focuses them. [14:46]
- Counter buying is better when ahead and worse when behind; this doesn't mean never buying resistances when behind. [15:16]
- When behind, focus on utility items and items that let you kill the enemy, start fights 6v5, and ideally kill the most farmed enemy, which is how you come back. [15:46]
- These five mistakes run through every rank up to low Eternus and only stop at the very highest ranks. [16:16]
- Ilari (caption spelling), called the best movement player in the game, is not even high Eternus, which shows movement alone matters less than people think; the same goes for aim and fighting micro. [16:46]
- Macro is what wins games, and every Eternus player will say so. [17:17]
