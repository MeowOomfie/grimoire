# Deadlock's FIRST Ranked Season is Out! | Patch Breakdown
channel: Deathy
date: 2026-07-30
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xJ8jkZxfqMk

## Covers
Deathy reads the late July 2026 matchmaking update that added the first ranked season (Ranked Beta Season 1): standard vs ranked queues, eligibility rules, calibration, rank points and loss protection, the pregame hero swap, bans, leaver rules and leaderboard requirements. No hero or item balance in this patch. For players about to start ranked who want to know the rules.

## Claims
- The update splits matchmaking into a standard (unranked) mode and a new ranked mode, Ranked Beta Season 1, with new ranks and placement matches. [0:00]
- Standard mode has no restrictions on party size or skill spread, and you can use it to practice heroes because the matchmaker adjusts for hero experience. [0:30]
- Ranked has seasons; ranks now carry two names, a standard name plus the old one (for example Initiate, Seeker, Acolyte alongside Brick, Stone, Iron; Oracle is diamond), with Phantom, Ascendant and Eternus at the top and new art for every rank. Each rank has six sub ranks. [1:00]
- To queue ranked you need at least 60 wins in standard mode; Deathy likes this because it stops brand new players on your team (60 wins is about 100 games for most people) and keeps smurfs from playing ranked right away. [1:31]
- In ranked you can only pick heroes you have won at least 15 standard games with, and you need at least three such heroes unlocked to queue. [2:02]
- Season one started on patch day and ends October 7th; the highest rank you reach is saved to your rank history page. Future seasons will likely be longer, with splits that adjust ranks and recency requirements on heroes played, which suggests the 15 wins have no recency requirement for now. [2:02]
- Everyone starts with an eight game calibration phase after a soft reset that keeps some history of past performance; Deathy says a hard reset was never an option. [2:33]
- You can exit calibration at most at Oracle 6, so nobody starts in Phantom, Ascendant or Eternus, even former Eternus 6 players; Deathy expects it to be rocky at first. [2:33]
- Ranked allows only solo or duo; duo partners must be at most one rank apart, and Eternus players can only duo with another Eternus within three sub ranks. Valve may loosen these limits later. [3:03]
- A rank hub in the hideout shows current and past seasons, global leaderboards and rank history; you get a hideout item for your season's highest rank. [3:33]
- After each match you see your rank points on a visible progress bar; each win or loss is about plus or minus 250 points on average, varying slightly with matchmaker expectations and win streaks. [3:33]
- Each sub rank promotion needs 1,000 points, except the last sub rank before a new rank, which needs 2,000, so a full rank is 7,000 points; at about 250 per game that is a net plus 28 wins per rank, which needs a really high win rate over 100 games, so ranking up will not be quick (win streaks may help). [4:05]
- From Eternus 1 up there is no fixed point threshold; sub rank thresholds update daily on a percentile basis. [4:36]
- Rank loss protection: five games of protection on a full rank boundary (for example Mystic 1 at zero points keeps the badge until five losses before dropping to Sentinel) and two games on a sub rank boundary. Deathy is not sure he likes it and will wait to form an opinion. [4:36]
- There is no draft: you pick your hero before queueing and the matchmaker mixes players from the larger queue to give people their hero and a reasonable team composition, with improvements to how it builds comps. Deathy agrees with the approach. [5:06]
- New pregame phase: after a ranked match forms you see every player's rank, then in a waiting area you may change your hero once to any unselected hero you have unlocked for ranked. It is meant to fix team composition, not to counter pick, so you don't see live enemy swaps, only their initial comp; swapping into a hero the enemy also swapped to gives an error and you pick another. Deathy likes this a lot. [6:08]
- You also set starting lanes in the pregame area and can no longer zipline swap lanes after. [7:08]
- Pauses are disabled until 2 minutes after the game fully starts. [7:08]
- Ranked bans go from two to three heroes per game. [7:08]
- You cannot queue ranked with any active sanction, such as low priority or a communications ban. [7:08]
- If a player abandons before 5 minutes, the game is voided for everyone except the leaver and their party, who take a loss, and the leaver gets penalties; your duo partner can't dodge with you. [7:38]
- If a player abandons after 5 minutes, the leaver gets penalties and their party takes the loss; other players on that team get the game voided and can leave safely, but if they stay and win it counts as a win. [7:38]
- If every player on a team leaves, the game ends as a win for the other team; if the other team then also has an abandon, the same rules apply to them and no remaining player takes a loss, with a win only for a team that finishes and wins. [8:08]
- Leaver penalties start with queue timeouts and escalate to more low priority games. [8:38]
- Global leaderboard needs 75 games in the last 30 days (was 50); hero leaderboards need 30 games in 30 days (was 20) plus 100 lifetime wins on that hero, making it harder to camp leaderboards on multiple accounts. Deathy notes he lacks 100 wins on most heroes despite playing full time. [8:38]
- Valve expects the first week to be rocky: a thin pool hurting queue times and match quality until it stabilizes, players taking time to calibrate, and a couple of days of bugs from the large rewrite. [9:08]
