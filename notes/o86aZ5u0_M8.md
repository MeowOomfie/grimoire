# Gun Builds Are Boring
channel: Deathy
date: 2025-12-25
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o86aZ5u0_M8

## Covers
Why gun builds look the same on every hero: how the vitality, spirit and weapon investment categories interact with items, why fire rate and resist shred beat weapon damage once the weapon investment is up, and a step-by-step recipe for any pure gun build (ammo fix, Cultist, shred, fire rate, Glass Cannon, Lucky Shot). For players who build gun and want to understand the damage math.

## Claims
- The easiest way to play a hero you don't know (he did it on Viscous) is a gun build, which he built off his own knowledge without following a guide. [0:00]
- Wander, known for his Ivy build, builds almost the same gun items left to right on nearly every hero; gun builds allow that more than spirit builds because of the investment categories. [1:01] [1:32]
- The vitality investment now gives flat HP, so no item makes the vitality investment better; Colossus scales off base HP, which is different from the flat investment HP. [2:03] [2:33]
- In spirit, only Boundless Spirit scales with the spirit investment: at the maximum investment (28k) it is the item that makes most sense, since it is the only item giving percent spirit power (15%). Every other spirit item sits outside the system. [3:03]
- The weapon investment gives weapon damage, a stat that is widely available on shop items. [3:33]
- Gun damage scales through three pillars: fire rate, weapon damage and resist shred. Lucky Shot is its own separate multiplier, and damage amp mostly comes from heroes (Shiv, Calico, Pocket), not builds. [4:03] [4:33]
- Investing 9,600 souls in weapon already gives 80% weapon damage. [4:33]
- Gun heroes very often build a lot of fire rate; the John John build's first damage items are Kinetic Dash, Long Range, Headshot Booster, Monster Rounds, then Cultist. [5:04]
- Kinetic Dash is far more effective than items of its tier because its fire rate multiplies on top of the weapon damage multiplier; Fury Trance gives 40% fire rate for the same reason. [5:34]
- Percent weapon damage only multiplies your base bullet damage: with 29 base and 60% extra you deal about 47 per bullet. [5:34] [6:04]
- Fire rate multiplies that full bullet damage, so the more weapon damage you have, the better fire rate gets. [6:04]
- With no items, Long Range (40% conditional damage) beats Swift Striker (20% fire rate) because you lack the weapon damage for fire rate to pay off. [6:35]
- With 4,800 already invested in gun, Swift Striker or another fire rate item is almost certainly better than Long Range, because the investment already supplies weapon damage. [7:05]
- That is why Long Range, Intensifying Magazine and Opening Rounds are not popular in gun builds: fine early, but they don't scale your damage like their alternatives, and the later the game the more fire rate is favored. [7:35] [8:05]
- Kinetic Dash is a no-brainer in many gun builds because it also gives flat ammo; gun Doorman has 8 bullets and Titanic Magazine only takes that to 16, while Kinetic Dash fixes damage and ammo in one item. [8:05]
- Berserker is rarely bought unless the hero can really abuse it (Lady Geist can); otherwise a reliable fire rate item is better at 3,200. [8:36] [9:08]
- Sharpshooter is very rarely bought, maybe on gun Vindicta or gun Grey Talon, and even there more fire rate would be better. [9:08]
- Resist shred multiplies all of your damage, so treat it like damage amp: Weakening Headshot's 13% shred is roughly a 13% amp against a target with 0% resist. [9:38]
- A slow, hard-hitting one-shot build (for example Grey Talon with Opening Rounds, Express Shot, Glass Cannon) works, but buying a little more fire rate instead would do tremendously more damage, which kills gun build diversity. [10:40] [11:10]
- Worked example: from 100 base DPS with no items, buying 40% weapon damage is a 40% DPS increase, but a second 40% weapon damage is worse than 40% fire rate would have been. [11:40] [12:11]
- Once you have one of the three stats the others become more desirable, and since the investment gives weapon damage by default, fire rate and resist shred are by default more desirable than weapon damage items, which are underbought at the top level. [12:11] [12:42]
- Step one of any gun build: solve ammo. The main ammo fixes are Titanic Magazine and Kinetic Dash. [12:42]
- With a very low magazine (Doorman) buy Kinetic Dash, and you can pair it with Titanic Magazine. [12:42] [13:12]
- Kinetic Dash tech: start a reload, then Kinetic Dash before the reload finishes and it reloads your whole max magazine plus the bonus, for example 31 bullets instead of 16 with Titanic Magazine, effectively infinite ammo on any hero. [13:12] [13:43]
- Once ammo is solved you mostly don't have to think about weapon damage; most players buy Cultist, which gives weapon investment and helps you farm. [14:13]
- Cultist pays back about 800 souls almost instantly, so its cost hardly matters; it lets heroes like Doorman farm a tier three camp they otherwise couldn't. [14:13] [14:43]
- After that only resist shred and fire rate remain. The common shred items are Hollow Point and Bullet Resist Shredder, because they also apply against neutrals and objectives (walkers, mid boss, jungle). [14:43]
- Weakening Headshot does not apply against neutrals or buildings, but it builds into Crippling Headshot, so it is still good. [15:14]
- Hunter's Aura is viable but bad against buildings because it only applies one stack (-8). [15:14] [15:44]
- Then get Glass Cannon and Lucky Shot every time: Lucky Shot is roughly a 1.27x multiplier on all your damage, and Glass Cannon gives weapon damage and fire rate once stacked. [15:44]
- The alternative to Glass Cannon is Spellslinger if your hero can build its stacks easily (for example Wraith); this recipe is for pure gun builds, not hybrids like Infernus or Wraith. [16:15]
- In the John John build the green items Fury Trance and Battle Vest are there for fire rate, and the other green it buys also gives fire rate: scale fire rate through greens while getting the vitality investment and tankiness. [16:45]
- He likes the change to flat HP vitality investment, because the old percent HP version heavily favored bruisers with large base HP. [17:15]
