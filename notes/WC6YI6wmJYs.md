# Deadlock Mechanics EVERY PLAYER Should Understand
channel: Natan
date: 2026-02-02
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WC6YI6wmJYs

## Covers
Hidden and unexplained mechanics: trooper soul sharing, the healing trooper's pack, Guardian and Walker resistances, flex slots, base Guardians and Shrines, comeback souls and the comeback Urn, hero base stats and hidden spirit scalings, boons and ability points, investment bonuses (800, 4.8k, 9.6k), and how resistance shred works and stacks.
For new and returning players.

## Claims
- Trooper waves are your main source of soul income for the whole game. [0:30]
- Sharing a wave with one or two teammates is slightly beneficial: you get a combined 108% of the troopers' total soul value. [0:30]
- Sharing a wave with your whole team gives each person only 16% of the value, 96% in total, so grouping up with many people nets less souls, before even counting the waves lost elsewhere. [0:30]
- Make sure almost every trooper wave is cleared by at least one person on your team, and do not group up too much. [1:01]
- The medic trooper's health pack heals a flat amount plus 16% of your missing health, so clear it last when your health is lowest. [1:01]
- If you plan to pop healing when low, get the heal pack first and then use the heal. [1:01]
- The heal pack is divided between the players in its area, so if you are full HP and your team is low, walk out of range to give them the whole heal. [1:01]
- You can parry Guardians to disable them for a few seconds, letting you get more damage on them while pushing or letting you dive enemies. [1:31]
- Guardians start with 75% damage resist, scaling down to -50% over the first 12 minutes. [1:31]
- In the first few minutes it is often pointless to hit the Guardian after a hard push; poke the enemy laners while they clear your troopers instead. [1:31]
- After the laning phase you can often run up to a Guardian without a wave, parry it and take it alone in a few seconds, because Guardians have no backdoor protection unlike other structures. [2:01]
- Killing Walkers gives your team flex slots (extra item slots). [2:01]
- When a Walker dies, the remaining two get extra health, and when a second dies, the last one gets even more. [2:01]
- Take the middle (blue lane) Walker first or second if you can; taking both side Walkers first leaves a very tough mid Walker that is often hard to kill. [2:01]
- Near one of your own Walkers you gain 15% spirit and bullet resistance, an advantage while defending. [2:32]
- Walkers also start at 75% damage resist scaling to -50%, but reach -50% at 20 minutes. [2:32]
- If you won lane, it is generally not worth immediately pushing a Walker; push the wave as far as you safely can, usually into their Walker, then use the time until the next wave to invade their jungle (slot machines, camps, boxes) or help another lane take its Guardian. [2:32]
- Killing base Guardians increases the zipline speed for your team in that lane, which helps keep up pressure. [3:02]
- Base Guardians have bullet resistance that scales down with how many of your players are nearby, so they are easier with more teammates present. [3:02]
- Before you can hit the Patron you must kill the two Shrines; taking them alone has value because your troopers get 30% stronger, making the Patron easier to push later. [3:02]
- Shrines use the same nearby-player bullet resist logic as base Guardians with slightly different numbers. [3:33]
- Games where you are 20k behind at 14 minutes are more winnable than they seem. [3:33]
- A lead that stays the same size gets smaller in percent over time: 10k at 40k vs 50k is about a 25% difference, but at 90k vs 100k it is about 10%. [3:33]
- A team that is behind gets bonus souls on everything (troopers, objectives, camps, players), scaling with how far behind, currently peaking at 30% bonus souls when the net worth difference is 20% or more. [4:04]
- If your team is behind by more than 10% (15% for the first Urn), the Urn drop off is moved to a safer location for you and the Urn is worth more. [4:34]
- The comeback Urn is a great way to close a lead because people do not pay attention to it; watch the net worth difference and the game timer to know when one is about to spawn. [4:34]
- The first Urn at 10 minutes always spawns in the yellow lane, then alternates every 5 minutes between green and yellow. [4:34]
- Always try to secure or deny the souls that come out when the Urn is delivered; they hold 50% of the Urn's total value, so you can salvage most of it even without the turn in. [5:04]
- He considers "if you're behind, make something happen" bad advice for most players because it is often misinterpreted. [5:04]
- Up to the very highest ranks, players do not know how to use leads: they get 10 kills, do not buy enough defensive items, overextend, get caught alone and die, giving comeback souls. [5:35]
- On the losing team, value stability: if you are not constantly dying and losing objectives, you are doing fine, you benefit from comeback souls, and often you just wait for the enemy to throw their lead. [5:35]
- Games last forever in lower ranks because the losing team does not need to take risks, just play a stable game and stay farmed. [5:35]
- Running into a 1v3 for the fifth time at 0-10 is not "trying something"; that is the misreading of the advice. [6:05]
- The advice is correct when the game is truly lost, for example 30k to 40k souls behind with your Patron in its last phase and their fat carry just killed: risking it all on a Mid Boss flip may be the only way to win. [6:05]
- The point where the game is truly lost shifts with rank, and the "something" must be a calculated team play, not a random play. [6:35]
- Every hero has different base stats and scalings, visible by hovering the stats panel at the bottom left or top of the hero screen. [6:35]
- Pocket starts the game with -15% spirit resist. [6:35]
- Mina has faster stamina regeneration than the rest of the cast. [6:35]
- Seven, Rem and Mo (but not Krill) take reduced crit damage because their hats are so big. [7:05]
- Rem has a -5% melee resist scaling, so melee him whenever possible. [7:05]
- Hidden spirit scalings: Seven, Grey Talon, Sinclair and Vyper's movement speed, Wraith's sprint speed, Victor's health regen, Grey Talon's bullet damage, "Fenix" (caption, hero unclear) and Warden's bullets per second and fire rate, Haze and Yamato's ammo, and Paige's melee all scale with spirit. [7:05]
- Gaining souls levels you up and grants boons that increase base stats, which differ a lot between heroes. [7:35]
- Lady Geist gets nearly double the health per boon of Mina, so she benefits more from resist and sustain items, while Mina may buy items like Stamina Mastery to avoid damage by being mobile. [7:35]
- Each boon also gives an ability point; knowing the souls needed for a given number of points shows when your power spikes are. [7:35]
- Pocket example: 6 AP to upgrade Cloak and Dagger (caption name), then another 8 to max Affliction, so at 13.8k souls he has 14 AP and his ult maxed, a big power spike to play for. [8:05]
- Buying items invests in three categories that give extra stats: weapon damage for weapon, flat health for vitality, spirit for spirit. [8:36]
- Mystic Slow puts 1,600 souls into spirit; even though it gives no spirit itself, reaching the 1,600 investment spike gives 11 spirit. [8:36]
- The most important investment spikes are 800, 4.8k and 9.6k. [8:36]
- It is common to spend at least 800 souls in each category in the laning phase, and there is a strong incentive to reach 4.8k in each category; the 4.8k spike is marked with a star. [9:06]
- Even on a spirit build, consider a tier two and a tier three weapon item to reach 4.8k weapon, which improves gun damage by at least 50%. [9:06]
- Pocket example: buying Swift Striker to reach 4.8k weapon takes his weapon DPS from 118 to 173, not counting the weapon damage from his other item. [9:06]
- Beyond 9.6k in a category you get much less value per soul, since the checkpoints are spaced further apart. [9:36]
- For most heroes it is worth hitting the 4.8k spike in every category before about 15 or 20 minutes; after 4.8k spirit, consider vitality items to reach 4.8k vitality before more spirit items. [9:36]
- Do not obsess over the spikes; the most important part of a good build is getting the right items. [10:07]
- Shred example with Lady Geist's Essence Bomb doing exactly 100 damage: Spirit Strike's melee lowers the target's spirit resist by 6%, so the bomb does 106, and the extra 6% applies to damage from any source, including teammates. [10:07]
- Damage is the tooltip value reduced by the target's spirit resist minus your shred: against 30% resist (Spirit Resilience) the bomb does 70, and with Spirit Strike's 6% it does 76. [10:37]
- Shred adds the same flat amount no matter the enemy's resist, so it becomes more valuable the more resist the enemy has (6 on top of 50 or 70 is worth more than 6 on 100). [11:09]
- Bullet shred works the same way: at 100 damage per bullet into 50% bullet resist you deal 50, and Bullet Resist Shredder raises that to 60, a 20% increase rather than the 10% the tooltip implies, very good value for 1,600 souls, which is why it was nerfed last patch. [11:09]
- Troopers, jungle camps and structures also have resists, so shred that works on non-players (Bullet Resist Shredder, Mystic Vulnerability) is often, but not always, valued more than shred that only works on players (Spirit Snatch, Weakening Headshot). [11:41]
- Different shred sources stack diminishingly: Spirit Snatch plus Mystic Vulnerability shreds 16%, not 12% + 8% = 20%. [12:11]
- The same shred source does not stack with itself: if you and a teammate both buy Mystic Vulnerability, it does not stack on one enemy, so diversify shred on your team (for example Weakening Headshot if a teammate already has Bullet Resist Shredder). [12:11]
- Exceptions that do stack with themselves (diminishingly) when you and a teammate proc them on one target: Rusted Barrel and Crippling Headshot. [12:42]
