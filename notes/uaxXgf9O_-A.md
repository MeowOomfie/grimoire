channel: Sensu

## Covers
A mental game guide: what tilt is, blaming teammates vs reviewing your own deaths, the 30-30-40 rule, how to talk to teammates and toxic enemies, and when to stop queueing.
For solo queue players whose frustration costs them games at any rank.

## Claims
- A strong mental is the most underrated skill in Deadlock and one of the biggest factors in being low or high Elo. [0:00]
- Tilt is emotional frustration that hurts your in-game performance; it is usually triggered by things you cannot control and turns many winnable games into losses. [0:00]
- The biggest tilting habit is reacting to your own death or lost lane by looking at what your team is or isn't doing, instead of asking why you got caught. [0:00] [0:30]
- The Dunning-Kruger effect makes players overestimate their skill and refuse to admit mistakes, especially at lower ranks, creating a ceiling on improvement. [0:30]
- Every time you die unnecessarily, reflect on how you could have prevented it (positioning, movement, anything else); look at the death analytically without emotion, and you will blame teammates less and improve much faster. [0:30] [1:00]
- Hyper-focusing on teammates' mistakes is the fastest way to spiral; their performance is out of your control and the enemy team can also have throwers. [1:00] [1:30]
- Don't waste energy being upset or let a teammate's bad performance affect your own. [1:30]
- The 30-30-40 rule: 30% of games are guaranteed losses, 30% guaranteed wins, 40% decided by your performance; if you are better than your rank, you control a larger share. [1:30]
- A decent share of games are unwinnable: 6v6 with no draft means your comp can be bad for Urns and Mid Boss, and you can get an AFK, a thrower, or a rank disadvantage in matchmaking, sometimes many games in a row. [2:01]
- Solo queue odds slightly favour you: if you won't tilt, AFK or throw, your team has five possible throwers against the enemy's six. [2:01] [2:31]
- Beefing with teammates is the fastest way to drop your win rate; many players tie their self-worth to the game and take criticism personally. [2:31]
- If a teammate whines at you, don't talk back; agree with them ("yeah, I should have done that") to validate their feelings. If you can't de-escalate and they give no useful comms, mute them. [3:01]
- Telling a raging teammate to calm down ("it's only a minute in") can invalidate their feelings and get them to run it down your lane; tread lightly in comms. [3:31] [4:02]
- Never single someone out or ask why they made a play; instead of "Dynamo, why did you waste your ult?" say "I think we should use Dynamo's ult for the next Urn". Use inclusive words like "we" and "let's". [4:02]
- If you want to rage, take it out on yourself, your Discord or your table, never on a teammate. [4:32]
- If a toxic enemy is getting in your head even slightly, mute them; it costs nothing. [4:32]
- If enemy trash talk isn't getting to you, their trash talk shows what would tilt them; a question mark, a tea bag or a mid-game commendation after their deaths can rattle them. [4:32] [5:03]
- Never use more than two words at a time when trash talking enemies (for example "why pause?" every time they pause); don't argue in all chat, since it costs your focus. [5:03]
- In general don't trash talk enemies, especially unprovoked, as it can make them lock in harder. [5:03]
- After a tilting game or a loss streak, don't queue straight away; take a short break, or play Street Brawl or an alternate account if you still want to play. [5:03] [5:34]
- If you are mentally wrecked, get off your chair, walk around, do push-ups or splash water on your face before requeueing. [5:34]
- Never play sleep deprived: performance drops and your tilt threshold is much lower. [5:34]
- On breaks, watch a high Elo stream or video and copy how Eternus players use wall jumps, positioning and macro decisions. [6:04]
- Treat each new game as a clean slate and don't bring the last game's negative energy into it. [6:04]
- Set performance goals instead of outcome goals, such as using wall jumps or denying more souls in lane, and keep a learning mindset in every game. [6:04] [6:35]
- Climbing is a numbers game: you can't control teammates, but you have full control of your own gameplay and approach. [6:35]
