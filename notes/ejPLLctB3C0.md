# Why Deadlock is a TURN BASED Game
channel: Natan
date: 2026-06-20
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ejPLLctB3C0

## Covers
Tempo as "a time frame where you have an advantage" (a turn): spending your turn, respecting the enemy's turn, waves as the clock of the game, kills and death timers, cross map plays and playing weak side, with replay examples including top players.
For players who throw leads by overstaying or who want a framework for when to fight and when to reset.

## Claims
- Tempo is a time frame where you have a certain advantage; having tempo means it is your turn, and you want to use that window to take something. [0:30]
- You can be up in tempo (your turn) or down in tempo (the enemy's turn). [0:30]
- To win you find or create advantages and take resources, objectives and kills while you have them; avoid fair fights when possible. [1:00]
- Every hero gets a power spike when they unlock their ultimate; being more efficient in lane (more boxes, small camp off cooldown, a kill) gets you there first and gives you tempo. [1:31]
- Your tempo runs out: if you unlock your ult first and do nothing with it (damage, a kill, or shoving the wave and hitting the Guardian with the ult threat), the enemy unlocks theirs too. [1:31]
- When you are at a temporary disadvantage, minimize losses and respect the enemy's strength until their tempo runs out. [2:01]
- Tempo ebbs and flows: after you use your turn you are often down in tempo and it is the opponent's turn next. [2:01]
- Example: after killing McGinnis and Billy while low, walking up into them as they return from base at full HP gets you punished instantly. [2:31]
- Example from top 10 players: after a Seven died and respawned at full HP while the Abrams and Lady Geist who killed him were low, they stayed too long and gave him an easy double kill. [3:01]
- Many throws in every rank come from overstaying your tempo and disrespecting the opponent's turn. [3:01]
- Even when behind or just after dying, you still get turns; recognize them and use them, since the winning side often will not respect that they are down in tempo. [3:31]
- Turns are invisible unless you learn to spot them; if you do not spot them you lose them. [3:31]
- Waves generally decide the tempo of the game: a pushed wave forces an enemy to respond or lose structure damage and trooper souls. [4:01]
- Since someone must come clear your pushed wave soon, pushing gives you time and information to act elsewhere. [4:31]
- In a neutral tempo state the winning team is stronger, so it pushes waves first and further. [4:31]
- Example: after winning lane and taking the Guardian, push the wave into the enemy Walker, then take short turns stealing their jungle camps, boxes and Sentinels, and return in time to push the next wave. [4:31]
- The tempo from pushing a wave is limited because the next wave arrives roughly 30 seconds later. [5:01]
- When winning you can choose a longer turn, for example ganking another lane after a big power spike; the enemies in your lane then get a turn by pushing into your Guardian, but on your terms, not theirs. [5:01]
- If your team respects the tempo the enemy gets after your play, they usually cannot do much productive with it; that is how you snowball. [5:31]
- When possible always look to go one more wave: push lanes as far as you reasonably safely can so it takes the longest for enemy waves to push back. [6:01]
- Early death timers are short, so early kills do not give tempo and can even leave you down in tempo right after. [6:01]
- In the mid and late game, as death timers grow, kills do give tempo. [6:01]
- Killing someone defending a side Walker creates a vacuum where your team has tempo; the enemy must fill it by rotating to defend, or create a vacuum of their own elsewhere knowing two of your players are on that Walker. [6:31]
- A cross map is when one team makes a play on one side, likely with a numbers advantage, which opens a play for the other team elsewhere. [6:31]
- When the enemy overloads one side, often do not go back to defend it; make a play of your own on the other side with the turn they gave you. [7:02]
- When your team is making a play elsewhere, you are weak side and at risk of a cross map play: play more disciplined until you have enough information to walk up. [7:02]
- Example: farming a side lane while your team delivers the Urn on the other side, be very careful walking up, since an enemy team not contesting the Urn is likely looking for a cross map on you. [7:32]
- Replay: with a 2v1 in his lane after Vindicta left, he and Shiv shoved, then he went for a 3v2 on the other side instead of joining a 3v3. [7:32]
- Generally avoid ganking losing lanes because success is far less likely; play around your winning teammates as much as possible. [8:03]
- He ganked a losing lane anyway because there was nothing else to do, Calico gains little from jungle camps, he had no big item or ability spike coming, and he could at least get someone low, which is still value. [8:03]
- When an ally (Paige) is not in range to help with kills, pushing the wave in is a good choice because it lets the team take the Guardian right after. [8:33]
- After a gank you are often down in tempo: unspent souls, ultimates used, partial HP, and your own lane shoved in by the enemy. [8:33]
- At 6:00 zipline boosts are up, so killed enemies return at full HP with ultimates up. [9:03]
- When down in tempo after a play, do not stick around; reset by spending souls at the secret shop and running boxes, then look for the next play elsewhere. [9:03]
- Calico can run boxes on the increased health regen from Ava while resetting. [9:03]
- Count visible enemies on the map; with six seen on one side, look for another 3v2 on the other lane. [9:34]
- Use your turn for the most aggressive play available, then respect that it is the opponent's turn. [10:04]
- Late game example: when the map is on fire (an ally dead, the comeback Urn lost, a forced base trip), trade by taking a Walker elsewhere rather than hesitating and losing both. [10:35]
- Enemies who push a second, tanky Walker without an advantage in that area overstay their tempo; collapse with the whole team to turn a 4v2 into a 6v4. [11:05]
- At about 20 minutes death timers are long, so punishing an overstay with kills can lead straight into a Mid Boss. [11:35]
- Taking a Walker you did not have the tempo for does not matter if your whole team dies for it. [11:35]
- After winning a fight and taking the Mid Boss you are usually down in tempo: the enemy returns at full HP with souls spent while you must push side waves, spend souls and heal. [11:35]
- A common mistake after Mid Boss is instantly donating your Rejuvenator lives by dying while down in tempo; the opposing team should look to punish this for a comeback. [12:05]
- Many plays that worked were because you had tempo, and many deaths came from not having tempo or not respecting the opponent's. [12:37]
