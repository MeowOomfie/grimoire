# The Art of Meleeing | A Deadlock Guide
channel: Midknight
date: 2026-05-29
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-MLs9gpA0uw

## Covers
Melee mechanics: why heavy melee on heroes is bad and light melee is very strong, who should melee by role and game phase, parry numbers, safer parry reads, Hail Mary parries, never meleeing to secure a kill, and conditioning tricks to bait parries.
For any player who under-uses melee or overuses heavies.

## Claims
- Heavy meleeing heroes is extremely bad: it is very telegraphed, and every heavy that lands is because they didn't parry, which hands control of the fight's outcome to the enemy. [0:00]
- Light meleeing is extremely strong: it can't be reacted to and must be predicted, so just standing in the enemy's face applies constant pressure, and if they guess with a parry you melee them for big damage. [0:30]
- When in the enemy's face, intersperse light melees into your combos; the risk-reward favors the meleer. [0:30]
- Heavy melee does only about 5% more DPS than light melee, so heavy melee objectives, but against heroes the parry risk isn't worth it. [1:00]
- Looking up moves the heavy melee hitbox above you, which is good for hitting people jumping around in the air. [1:00]
- In the laning stage every hero gets a DPS gain from working a couple of light melees into their combos. [1:30]
- In E6 games, players trying to win lane weave in light melees, while players stalling a lane or boxing out melee much less. [1:30]
- Gun carries: after the 8 minute end of lane, gun DPS tends to exceed melee DPS, so you can switch to fully shooting. [2:01]
- Spirit heroes: know whether your abilities alone have kill potential or you need a couple hundred more damage; usually that comes from your gun, but put a light melee at the start or middle of your combo, where it is unlikely to be parried, rather than after it. [2:01]
- Holding your melee until after the full combo is very predictable; meleeing to secure a kill is very bad. [2:01]
- Front liners should light melee the whole game; getting parried doesn't matter much and it adds a lot of DPS to the kit. [2:32]
- Supports should melee very often: once everything is on cooldown (for example Rem with ult, pillow and his 2 used), meleeing makes enemies CC themselves for half a second in the parry animation while your gun carries do damage. [2:32]
- Baiting out a parry puts it on cooldown, opening chances for your front liners and others to punish. [3:03]
- Pick heroes do the same to a lesser degree: after burning lasso or barrels, try to bait enemies into the parry animation. [3:03]
- After a parry you can't parry again for 4.5 seconds, enough time for two heavy melees or four light melees. [3:33]
- A parry lasts 0.75 seconds and a successful parry stuns the attacker for 2.75 seconds. [3:33]
- A successful parry also gives the parrying player a 25% damage boost for the 2.75 seconds, and it is a global damage buff, not only against the stunned player. [3:33]
- Punish missed parries with light melees: you lose little DPS, and players with good movement can out-move a heavy melee's wind-up. [3:33]
- Enemies can even parry your second heavy melee if they put distance between your melees, because the wind-up and dash time widen the gap back into their parry window. [4:03]
- When trying to read a light melee with a parry, input a movement action first so a missed parry isn't punished as badly. [4:03]
- The classic is wall bounce then parry: it gives height and mobility, and if the enemy heavy melees you, double jump to stay in the air out of range. [4:33]
- You can also instant air dash (IAD) into a parry, or now ground dash then parry, though it feels janky and may be patched. [4:33]
- Meleeing to kill is one of the worst things in the game; never do it. [5:04]
- At 1 HP in the open with a healthy enemy nearby, throw a Hail Mary parry: even a 5% chance they light melee you is worth it, and a parry before dying can still be very useful in a team fight. [5:04]
- If you light melee correctly you will get parried a lot; don't stop meleeing after being parried once. [5:35]
- Rough math: each light melee adds about 30% DPS with about a 5% chance of giving the enemy 50% more DPS, an expected value of about a 20% DPS buff. [6:06]
- Use conditioning as in fighting games: on Rem, the first couple of times he pillows someone he light melees them in the air, twice, then stops, so they parry in the air and he punishes. [6:36]
- In general, melee after an ability twice and then stop; if they never parry, keep light meleeing on and off until they try to parry. [7:06]
- Many players react off their experience against your hero in other games, not what you did: Billy players commonly melee off their 2 (Rising Ram), so the first time you use it, whether they parry depends on their last Billy. [7:06]
- After abilities that are commonly followed by a melee (your first Calico slash, your first Yamato grapple), don't melee, to bait the parry. [7:36]
- For those common melee-follow abilities, once you start meleeing, assume one melee is enough to make them ready to parry next time. [8:07]
- Basic light meleeing: stay in their face, vary your light melees, keep them relatively slow, and throw one about every three or four seconds. [8:07]
- Never light melee in the first half second to second after entering melee range; that is the most predictable moment and they will parry it. [8:07]
- Most players won't parry your first light melee; a second after getting in their face, light melee twice and stop, because people very commonly parry right after expecting a third. [8:38]
