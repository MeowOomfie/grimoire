# You'll Never Play Deadlock The Same, Just Try This.
channel: Shotty
date: 2026-08-29
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aWDSUhuAXPA

## Covers
Long aim talk for Deadlock: why raw aim matters more than crosshair placement here, why gun damage is strong against low bullet resist, staggered tracking, flick then micro adjust, desk height and arm angle, crosshair size, wrist versus arm and sensitivity, mouse grips, aim trainers (KovaaK's over Aim Labs) and how to practice tracking and clicking, plus sleep, age and reaction time.
For any player who wants more consistent aim, on tracking heroes (Wraith, Venator, Vyper, Vindicta, Haze) and clicking heroes (Holliday, Drifter, Lady Geist, Mina) alike.

## Claims
- Practicing aim on its own beats only playing the game, because most people default to bad technique and get stuck in one aim archetype without guidance. [0:31]
- Your weakest aim category is what makes your aim feel bad; even a top 5% player with one bottom 10% category will feel held back until they raise it. [1:02]
- Deadlock is aim intensive: with its verticality, jump pads, vent boosts and movement, no one stays at a set height, so passive aim like crosshair placement matters very little and raw aim (see target, move mouse, shoot) matters much more. [2:04]
- Fast heroes like Holliday, Celeste and Mina move so much that even aiming abilities needs aim skill. [3:05]
- Carrying is easier on heroes that deal a lot of gun damage, because few players have over 20% bullet resist in a normal match. [3:35]
- In lower ranks Metal Skin often is not bought at all, maybe by one player out of six, while each team usually has one or two 30% spirit resist items, so inherently gun damage is the stronger damage type. [3:35]
- The gun crit multiplier in Deadlock is very high compared to body shots; with Crippling Headshot on Lady Geist, five headshots on a stunned target deleted him. [4:06]
- Third person view makes the game more accessible: you see more of the screen and get awareness for free. [4:37]
- The game is balanced around average players (about 30% accuracy and 3% crit), not high accuracy players, so gun heroes such as Wraith, Vyper and Vindicta are almost unbalanceable: strong enough for average players makes them too strong at the top, and the reverse. [6:08]
- Because gun heroes may never be balanced for skill, most players should invest in their aim as soon as possible. [6:38]
- Staggered aim is the most common problem: tracking a target strafing left and right at one speed, most players go stop, go, stop, go instead of one smooth line. Test it on a strafing target in the practice range or sandbox. [8:11]
- For tracking heroes, removing staggering is what matters; it also matters for clicking, since you cannot aim without drawing straight lines. [9:12]
- A flick is for getting close to the target fast; a micro adjustment (a short, small movement) is for actually aiming. [10:13]
- Do not flick and pray at a target like a bouncing Holliday; flick close, then micro adjust, in two motions. [10:43]
- Every flick carries a "tax", a human buffer time where you find where your crosshair landed before shooting, so minimize flicks: one flick, then continuous micro adjustments as the target strafes. [11:44]
- Most people cannot even track a big, slow target smoothly, so they cannot track a headshot hitbox; build the technique first. [12:45]
- Desk height: your arm on the desk should be at a right angle; an acute angle forces wrist-only aiming, and an obtuse angle (reaching) gives no stability. [13:15]
- Reaching too far forward rolls the shoulders forward and moves the scapula so you lose stability from your back muscles, which hurts aim; sit close enough that you are not reaching. [13:47]
- Crosshair choice: a big crosshair is easier to keep track of during fast movement and suits newer players or a fast play style; a small crosshair suits precision, since it does not block the headshot hitbox. [15:21]
- Look at the target, not at your crosshair, keeping only a subconscious sense of where the crosshair is. [15:52]
- If you lose track of your crosshair a lot, move toward a bigger crosshair; if you miss shots you swore were on target, move toward a smaller one. [16:52]
- Use both wrist and arm, with one used a bit more depending on sensitivity; slower than 40 cm per 360 needs more arm, faster than about 30 cm per 360 needs more wrist and fingertips. [17:23]
- Using only wrist or only arm always leaves a weakness in turning, speed or precision. [17:54]
- Start at around 30 to 40 cm per 360. [18:24]
- Sensitivity is just a tool: fast sensitivity suits faster pace and frequent 360s, slow sensitivity suits precision. [18:24]
- Shotty scored top 200 on a module with a sensitivity that kept changing during play, so the exact sensitivity matters little. [18:54]
- Sensitivity priorities: first your preference, second what fits the game and your play style, third keep it in a reasonable range (not around 10 or 90 cm per 360). [18:54]
- Mouse grip matters more than sensitivity; the three main grips are palm (hand flat on the mouse), fingertip (only fingertips touch) and claw (fingers arched, back of the palm touching). [19:55]
- Rocket Jump Ninja's website helps find mice that fit your hand and grip. [20:25]
- Look after wrist and eye health: wrist pain makes you build bad habits or stop using your wrist, and too much screen time degrades vision. [20:56]
- Aim trainers are worth it: real games give few, short fights, so 10 minutes of aim training is more direct practice than three or four Deadlock games (about 2 hours). [23:29]
- The two main aim trainers are Aim Labs and KovaaK's; Shotty rates Aim Labs poorly and recommends KovaaK's, which serious aimers use, costs about 10 dollars and is often about 5 on sale. [24:30]
- Aim training fails when practice is not specific: train maps that match your technique and hero, for example tracking maps for Vyper rather than clicking maps. [25:01]
- Popular maps are often too hard; training on maps above your level builds bad habits because you need bad habits to hit the scores. [25:32]
- Aim training also fails when you use perfect technique in the trainer but stop thinking about technique and posture in real games. [25:32]
- The goal of aim training is raising your subconscious baseline: consciously aiming at a top 1% level might make you subconsciously aim at top 5%. [26:03]
- Trainer skill will not translate perfectly (bullet drop, movement, stress), though there are KovaaK's maps designed for bullet drop and movement. [26:34]
- Tracking heroes include Wraith, Venator, Vyper, Vindicta and Haze. [27:04]
- For tracking practice, smoothness is the number one thing; spend the first month or more just removing staggering, and keep moving to harder maps as a progressive overload. [27:34]
- Once you are smoother, apply it on precision maps that need both accuracy and smoothness; do it in that order, or you force scores without fixing technique. [28:04]
- Expect scores to drop at first when you focus on technique; the consistency shows up about a week later. [28:35]
- Minimize wasted movement: aiming past a target (or chasing far behind it) means extra correction, and if they strafe the other way you must correct both your overshoot and their movement. [28:35]
- Clicking heroes include Holliday, Vindicta's ultimate, Drifter, Lady Geist and Mina. [29:35]
- On clicking heroes you still track a little (or predict a little) and click when on target; on Mina you might track rather than tap each shot, depending on target speed and ammo. [29:35]
- Wasting all your ammo on missed shots leaves you to quick melee, then get parried and lose the fight. [30:06]
- For clicking practice, train precision and speed as separate goals: small targets for precision, big targets for speed. [30:36]
- The common clicking mistake is slowing down the flick: make the flick as fast as possible and the micro adjustment as slow as necessary, not lingering on target but not shooting before you are on it. [31:06]
- Studies show the reaction time gap between a 20-year-old and someone decades older is about 50 to 60 ms, while losing sleep (6 hours instead of 8) costs 100 ms or more, more than aging 40 years. [31:37]
- Many of Shotty's biggest improvers are players aged 30 to 50, so age is not a reason your aim cannot improve. [32:07]
- Sleep enough, keep a normal sleep schedule and work out; exercising before playing makes you more alert and play better. [32:39]
