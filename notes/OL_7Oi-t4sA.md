## Covers
One short "real depth" tip per hero, roughly alphabetical: Abrams, Apollo, Bebop, Billy, Calico, Celeste, The Doorman, Striker, Dynamo, Lady Geist, Graves, Grey Talon, Haze, Holliday, Infernus, Ivy, Kelvin, Lash, McGinnis, Mina, Mirage, Mo & Krill, Paige, Paradox, Pocket, Rem, Seven, Shiv, Silver, Sinclair, Venator, Victor, Vindicta, Viscous, Vyper, Warden, Wraith, Yamato.
For players who already know a hero's basics and want the high-level identity and decision making behind it.

## Claims
- Abrams is a front-line tank, but to master him understand his role as an initiator for closing out the late game. [0:00]
- In the late game, when respawn timers turn single kills into 6v5 Mid Boss chances, Abrams should watch wave states and predict where and when isolated enemies will show up so he is there with his team to Shoulder Charge them. [0:00]
- Getting attacked is fine on Abrams because he is tanky; play him like a hard-to-punish assassin in the late game. [0:31]
- Apollo is one of the most aggressive snowballing heroes and has one of the weakest late games, so going even is not enough; push the opponents everywhere, always. [0:31]
- Apollo: use your 1 and 3, which do massive AoE damage, to instantly blow up enemy camps and escape. [0:31]
- Apollo: use the healing from your 3 and the parry ability on your 2 to duel enemies even at low HP. [1:02]
- Apollo: watch for missing enemy jungle camps to track opponents down in their jungle and kill them with your ultimate execute to steal their bag. [1:02]
- If you do not enjoy extreme tempo and aggression, Apollo is probably not for you. [1:02]
- Bebop's main utility is the CC chain from Hook and Uppercut, which makes him a facilitator best played with teammates. [1:02]
- A good Bebop has strong map reading and communication: identify likely picks in advance and tell teammates so they can be there to secure kills. [1:32]
- Bebop: note objective timers and look for hooks just before an objective spawns, when enemies are less likely to think about the threat. [1:32]
- Bebop: always think about how a kill converts into a map objective, ideally before you get the kill. [2:02]
- Billy's Chain Gang is often used only to pull, but its slowing hex effect locks down highly mobile enemy carries like Mina, which is key to securing early mid-game leads or shutting carries down. [2:02]
- Billy: sometimes use Chain Gang as a counter-carry tool on the single most important (often most mobile) enemy so the team can focus them; do not get baited into only looking for three or four person ults. [2:33]
- Billy: in the early game, a shackled enemy is usually best punished by hitting free headshots for several hundred damage, not by dashing away to drag them under your tower. [3:03]
- Calico, like Apollo, is a strong early game snowballer with a weaker late game; Apollo has stronger jungle clear for counter-jungling, Calico has better cross-map mobility. [3:03]
- Calico must read the map to anticipate ganks and third-party fights, rolling in with her ultimate burst and Ava. [3:33]
- Calico wins as a carry by aggressively hunting enemy players, not by farming the jungle for a solo lane; track the relative health and position of every enemy to know who to go for. [3:33]
- Celeste's main skill expression is movement; because she has high mobility but is not directly countered by items like Slowing Hex or Silence Wave, she can aggressively farm dangerous waves. [4:04]
- Celeste can take wave priority alone with no teammates near and stay safe if she conserves stamina and positions well, forcing multiple enemies to deal with her so her team can act on the other side. [4:35]
- Unless she has her ult active and a good team fight opportunity, a good Celeste pushes solo waves further than most heroes can to create pressure and secure solo farm to scale. [4:35]
- No one uses The Doorman to its full potential yet, and he believes every pro agrees. [5:06]
- Doorman door uses to plan: escape doors for a teammate or yourself, pre-placing an escape door on yourself plus one on a teammate, placing both on the fly, pre-planned tech doors, doors on your Walker, your Guardian, two in the Mid Boss pit, between both bridge buffs, for the Urn pickup, before clearing a wave as an escape route, to cut enemies through, or just to give a teammate shields, and telling teammates every time. [5:06]
- Striker: time the ultimate just as everyone enters combat; too early lets opponents escape, too late lets them see your team's positions so they know who to jump or where to retreat even while blind. [6:07]
- Dynamo's ultimate has such a long cooldown that it must convert into objectives; it nearly guarantees kills, but if those kills do not lead to a Mid Boss, Urn or important Walker it may have been used wrong. [6:37]
- Most Dynamos build some combination of Refresher, Unstoppable and Indomitable for Mid Boss fights, giving more ultimates and CC immunity or resistance during them. [7:08]
- Dynamo cannot have all of those items as soon as he wants, so choosing which to buy in what order is highly situational and a hidden skill. [7:40]
- Lady Geist is a power farmer who wants a side lane and to push lanes alone, but she is very immobile, so she must track enemies on the map to predict who might show up to gank her. [7:40]
- Lady Geist is very good 1v1 and, thanks to her swap, can even win a 1v2, depending on who she fights, how fed they are and their items, all of which she must track. [8:10]
- There is no AFK farming on heroes like Lady Geist; even while just clearing camps and pushing waves, watch the map state closely. [8:41]
- Graves is about controlling space, which is hard when roaming into fights without a setup. [8:41]
- Graves example: placing the grave behind the veil makes it harder for enemies to push, partly forcing them back while a wall behind them cuts off their retreat. [8:41]
- Fights are dynamic, so finding the best place for the Graves wall and grave is deceptively hard and worth thinking about. [9:12]
- Grey Talon is very active; both the gun and spirit builds involve perma-stacking. [9:12]
- Spirit Grey Talon stacks his owl (ultimate): it takes skill piloting the owl and map awareness to know which enemies are realistic gank targets. [9:42]
- Most gun Grey Talon builds use Glass Cannon at some point, which also perma-stacks; excellent Grey Talons are always on lanes looking for kills. [9:42]
- Unlike most initiators, Haze can capitalize on her own engagement to find solo kills, and in the late game can single-handedly create 6v5s by assassinating the right people at the right time. [10:13]
- That Haze skill needs close attention to map and wave states to predict where and when people will be isolated and be there first; it is not luck. [10:13]
- Holliday is very early game oriented and must snowball; Lasso picks are a key late game tool but her team fighting falls off drastically. [10:43]
- Good Holliday players are extremely active in the first 15 minutes, seeking kills even in the first 5 minutes between lanes with barrels. [11:15]
- Holliday's barrels (Powder Keg) and Crackshot deal heavy early damage but do not scale well; master movement and map awareness to visit the right lanes at the right time. [11:15]
- Even after nerfs to Flame Dash damage against objectives, Infernus is still an excellent split pusher, his main identity through early and mid game. [12:17]
- Infernus: constantly shove lanes in spots that would be dangerous for other heroes but are safe for you thanks to multiple Flame Dashes; otherwise you are not applying all your pressure. [12:48]
- Ivy is the only hero who can travel across the map quickly while bringing a teammate, unlike Mirage's ultimate. [12:48]
- Ivy can invade the enemy jungle, push Walkers or start fights, then carry a teammate across the map to a more important objective play. [13:19]
- In the deep late game, Ivy shoving a side lane safely with a teammate and then flying them across the map to a team fight creates 6v5 plays that can win games. [13:19]
- Kelvin's kit has built-in healing and high AoE damage, so he can always reinforce fighting teammates or invade the enemy jungle, giving him more options and decisions than most heroes. [13:50]
- Kelvin's self-healing from his grenades and ultimate often means he does not need to return to base, so he can stay active on the map; he is not a lazy support, always look for the most useful thing to do. [13:50]
- Lash's skill is knowing how far you can push his mobility to steal enemy resources, not ganking enemies on his own side or farming his own camps. [14:20]
- Lash has good escapes and buys items that reinforce them, so he should aggressively invade the enemy side even when down souls, watching the map closely so he does not get ganked or drop a big bag. [14:50]
- Even when Lash is low on health he should probably spend most of his time on the enemy side, invading their jungle. [15:21]
- McGinnis's turrets last 47 seconds on only an 18 second cooldown, so she uniquely benefits from setting up before a fight starts. [15:21]
- McGinnis skill is map reading: predict where and when an Urn, Mid Boss or team fight will happen as early as possible so you arrive uncontested and place turrets in tricky spots enemies must fight through. [15:21]
- Mina has very fast jungle clear, very high mobility, high objective damage and built-in healing when fighting camps, making her probably the best hero for skirmishing in the enemy jungle. [15:51]
- Mina can drive-by one-shot jungle camps while healing and escape constantly, so push her limits by invading the enemy jungle whenever possible, even with many enemies near; wasting many enemies' time chasing her is good for her team. [16:22]
- Mirage's depth is his ultimate: out of combat for splitting waves, catching waves shifting to your Walker, or rotating to objectives, and now also mid combat. [16:53]
- Mirage: use the ultimate to bait enemies toward your position then reposition safely, or to save teammates who are caught. [16:53]
- The roughly 20 seconds Mirage saves by teleporting to a wave right after a fight can decide whether you can push an objective. [16:53]
- Mo & Krill's late game ult to create 6v5s is key, but his low need for souls also frees him to do mundane team tasks. [17:24]
- Example: an Eternus 6 Mo & Krill player constantly contests Urns, makes objective calls, and stacks the blue wave into a slow push so it pushes blue and wastes enemy time while his team runs the Urn. [17:24]
- Paige has had a terrible win rate in high elo tournaments for months and is barely played; she is the truest support besides Rem. [18:28]
- Rem can skirmish in the enemy jungle because he can reposition teammates and steal farm with helpers; Paige has few escape tools and riskier positioning, so she pressures opponents less directly. [18:28]
- Paige's depth is knowing how close to the fight you can get without much risk of death: close enough to land her slow shots and hit as many teammates as possible with her ultimate. [18:58]
- Paradox, like other pick heroes, has depth in predicting the right late game assassinations to create 6v5s. [19:29]
- Paradox's ultimate is on a very low cooldown and works as an ult counter: preemptively position to swap an enemy into your Time Wall and silence them before a fight-winning ult. [19:29]
- Paradox is one of the counters to Dynamo as long as the Dynamo does not yet have Indomitable or Unstoppable; a great Paradox predicts key enemy ults and is positioned to counter them instantly. [20:00]
- Pocket is the safest wave clearer in the game thanks to the long cloak plus the satchel's immunity, which can even block some enemy ultimates or effects like Slowing Hex. [20:31]
- Pocket can push waves in spots almost no other hero could, keeping wave priority even when contested, then rotate to side lanes or mid for a numbers advantage while the enemy laner is stuck clearing. [21:02]
- Rem's complexity is item counter-buying: Tag Along grants 35% increased barrier effectiveness, item duration and range, making him a potent utility item user. [21:02]
- Rem's utility items are highly situational, including the order you buy them; build optimally by knowing which enemies to counter-buy against and which items buff your teammates. [21:33]
- Rem can use practically every active item to great effect but cannot buy them all; optimal helper usage is too complex to cover here. [22:05]
- Seven is a powerful late game scaler but very immobile, so he is vulnerable to ganks. [22:05]
- Seven is weak at constant skirmishing, so he scales by farming the jungle and side lanes, which puts him in risky spots; watch the map and possible ganks closely to judge when pushing a Walker is worth it. [22:35]
- Bonus for Seven: know how to use your ultimate for impactful but rare team fight rotations. [23:05]
- Shiv's movement is tied to his gun ammo, not his abilities, so a spirit Shiv can commit all abilities to fighting even in the enemy jungle and still escape. [23:05]
- Shiv's built-in tankiness from Bloodletting makes him very hard to kill and often pulls more enemy resources than he is worth; know where to push in the enemy jungle to create the most chaos, since even a bad outcome buys your team space. [23:36]
- Silver is less mobile than Shiv but very tanky once she procs Lycan form, which has a very low cooldown and procs in combat if you hit shots and abilities. [24:10]
- Silver uses very high native resistances and health to constantly bully enemies and make them spend more resources chasing and killing her than it is worth. [24:10]
- Sinclair steals ultimates and, with Rabbit Hex, is one of the best Mid Boss contesters in the game; that is the easiest way to win on him. [24:42]
- Sinclair is a lane bully who can also roam: pick lanes to gank based on their stealable ultimates and use quick rotations back and forth to win other lanes. [24:42]
- Venator, like Seven, is an immobile but very powerful late game scaler; players used to carries with escapes like Wraith or Lady Geist may struggle because positioning is very punishing. [25:13]
- A good Venator uses traps and positioning so enemies hunting him must walk into bad spots; his traps and shotgun wall stun interact with terrain uniquely, so take positions you can use without leaving them. [25:44]
- After Victor's major update several builds are viable, from pure gun carry to full spirit carry to utility, and none is optimal every game; pick before the game based on team compositions. [26:15]

- Vindicta is a perma-fighter with soft CC and high damage but no fast jungle clear, little AoE and no built-in sustain; she is easy to lock down if you counter her flight, immobile and squishy, and not good at shoving lanes like Pocket, Mina or Viscous. [27:47]
- Vindicta's skill is knowing which waves you can push without dying and picking the right fights on the map. [28:17]
- Viscous is extremely mobile, with Puddle Punch giving some of the fastest lateral movement in the game, and his ultimate gives a strong escape after split pushing deep. [28:48]
- Puddle Punch and Splatter give Viscous high burst for ganks and his cube can rescue teammates, so he wants to farm, fight and threaten enemy Walkers; it takes solid macro to know which lanes to push without overextending. [29:19]
- Vyper is a late game gun carry, but maxing Slither gives a temporary shield whenever she slides. [29:19]

- Warden also scales into a late game gun carry; his cage is situationally very effective for setting up ganks to get ahead of the farm curve. [30:19]
- Warden: watch enemy stamina and ability usage and cage when enemies are least able to escape. [30:49]

- Wraith, another late game carry, wants early kills mainly set up with her ultimate, though it is on a long cooldown and rarely enough alone unless she is up farm in a 1v1. [31:19]
- Wraith: use the ultimate to rotate into mid for brief team fights with teammates, which quickly turns into early kills; read the map to see these ganks in advance. [31:19]
- Yamato has essentially no escapes, unlike other all-in heroes, so her decision making is deceptively complex. [31:49]
- Yamato is great at hunting specific targets, especially with her ultimate resetting cooldowns, and at shutting down enemy carries who cannot run, but she is equally exposed afterwards, so only engage targets worth it. [32:20]
