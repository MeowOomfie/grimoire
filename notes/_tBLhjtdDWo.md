# YOU'RE Playing the Wrong Deadlock Hero.
channel: Sensu
date: 2026-06-26
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_tBLhjtdDWo

## Covers
Splits Deadlock skill into three layers, micro (execution), meso (reading the enemy) and macro (economy and objectives), with signs of strength and weakness in each and which roles each layer suits.
For players stuck in low ranks who want to pick heroes and roles that fit their strengths and diagnose their own mistakes.

## Claims
- A big reason players are hard stuck is playing heroes that do not fit their strengths, not being bad at the game. [0:00]
- Deadlock balances all three layers (micro, meso, macro), which is why it has a very high skill ceiling and feels overwhelming to new players. [0:00]
- Ask yourself "if I could have one hack, what would it be?" to find which skill you most need. [0:30]
- Without a decent baseline in every layer you will never reach the highest level; players with aimbot he has faced usually lost to poor decision making elsewhere. [1:01]
- Micro is execution: securing and denying souls, gun and ability fighting, parrying heavy melees, dash jumps, wall jumps and corner boosts while conserving stamina. [1:31]
- Strong micro shows as winning lane trades and soul denies, dealing damage in team fights while taking as little as possible, winning even 1v1s, escaping bad spots and high player damage. [2:01]
- Players from shooters (Overwatch, Fortnite, CS2, Valorant) usually start with stronger micro: flicking, tracking and positioning in chaotic fights. [2:31]
- Weak micro signs: missed kill opportunities, high deaths, low end-game damage, failing to escape ganks or catch runners. [2:31]
- Constantly reloading means you are not sliding and shooting enough; constantly out of stamina means you are not using wall jumps enough. [3:02]
- Always losing duels and being first to die in team fights is also a sign of a weak layer (he says weak macro, within the micro section). [3:02]
- Strong micro suits gun carry or spirit nuker roles, which rely on mechanical expression and are the most sought-after targets in fights, so one late-game mistake can decide the game. [3:02]
- Meso is the psychological layer: predicting enemies by recognizing patterns, mind games and reads. [3:32]
- Meso examples: anticipating light attacks to pre-parry, predicting rotations to waves, Sinner's Sacrifices or jungle camps, tracking enemy cooldowns to know when to play aggressive or defensive, and baiting out ultimates and item cooldowns before the next fight. [4:03]
- Strong meso shows as catching people out in their jungle or alone in a lane, stealing enemy camps with low risk, predicting lane dives and ganks, and landing melees without getting parried. [4:34]
- Good meso lets you predict enemy items and abilities and buy counters in advance, for example buying Rescue Beam in case a teammate gets hooked. [5:04]
- Meso is mostly learned from game time; players good at poker, Among Us or fighting games tend to start stronger. [5:04]
- Weak meso signs: blindsided by ganks, tower dives and counter items, baited into bad fights, losing Walkers by surprise, killed alone in your jungle, mistimed parries, repeatedly caught by death balls. [5:34]
- In replays with enemies visible on the minimap, look for missed gank or counter-jungling chances you failed to read. [5:34]
- Strong meso suits pick heroes, tanks and supports, which rely on staying two steps ahead of the enemy. [5:34]
- Pick heroes with good reads time ganks and counter-ganks to catch enemies when vulnerable for free kills. [6:05]
- As a tank, engage a numbers-advantage fight when you think the enemy carry is away catching a big wave; tanks use light and heavy melee more than other roles, so mind games matter. [6:05]
- As a support, map out team fight win conditions ahead of time and hold abilities and counter items to protect teammates from ultimates or burst before they happen. [6:05]
- Macro is the strategic layer: wave management (when to freeze or shove), box and jungle routes for soul efficiency, when to do or skip the Urn or Mid Boss, when to take or trade Walkers and Shrines. [6:35]
- Strong macro lets you weigh cost and benefit fast and helps itemizing. [7:05]
- Strong macro shows as being ahead in souls, always watching the timer and minimap, net positive souls or tempo plays, map pressure from rotations, starving enemies of boxes, Sentinels and camps, and fully using enemy death timers. [7:05]
- Good macro makes enemies hesitate to commit too many players to one area because it may cost them a Walker or Shrine. [7:36]
- Players from League of Legends, Dota or StarCraft usually start with stronger macro because they already watch the minimap and manage waves and economy. [7:36]
- Weak macro signs: falling behind in souls in the mid game, nothing to farm, never ready to group for Mid Boss or Urn, chasing pointless kills or farming instead of objectives. [7:36]
- Also weak macro: always separated from your team without trading anything important, calling bad Mid Bosses, underestimating what your team can do on enemy death timers, and low objective damage and souls as a gun carry. [8:07]
- The two macro-dominant roles are gun carries and supports. [8:07]
- Gun carries have the best damage profile to melt objectives alone; a gun carry who spots a map opening can take objectives while enemies cannot stop it. [8:37]
- A gun carry ahead in souls forces the enemy to send more than one player to stop them, creating tempo for the team. [8:37]
- Supports dictate map space and lane states with utility and wave clear, such as Rem's little helpers. [8:37]
- Supports need little farm, so they can spend attention tracking timers and calling when to run the Urn, take enemy Sentinels or start Mid Boss. [8:37]
- Supports can take more risks creating space in the enemy jungle because their healing and utility make them harder to kill. [9:07]
- What frustrates you most usually shows your weak layer: missed kills from aim point to micro, getting caught by death balls points to meso, always being behind in souls points to macro. [9:07]
- On every gray screen or VOD review, ask whether the mistake was micro, meso or macro, then train the weakest one. [9:38]
- If there is a gap between your strengths and your main, consider switching mains. [9:38]
