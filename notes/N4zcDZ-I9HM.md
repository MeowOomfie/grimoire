# I'm The Worst Deadlock Movement Player, So I Called The Best
channel: Metro Deadlock
date: 2026-06-12
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N4zcDZ-I9HM

## Covers
Metro gets a movement lesson from a top movement coach (with an interpreter who has thousands of bunny hop hours). For players who know wall jumps and dashes in theory but cannot execute them.
Topics: strafing as the base of all movement, mouse speed vs hero speed, wall jumps at 45 degrees, jump pad trajectory, dash direction, instant air dash, the plus one / minus one landing mechanic, dashing into walls, edge wall jumps, jump pad wall jumps, practice setup (Momentum Mod, sv_gravity 0, cl_showpos, host_timescale).

## Claims
- About 80% of all movement in the game depends on your strafes; if your strafes are bad, work on them first. [2:32]
- You always gain more speed from strafes when doing wall jumps and dashes; after every dash, instantly put your mouse on the trajectory of your flight and start strafing. [3:04]
- Strafing is hard to learn from Deadlock alone; Celeste is the easiest hero to learn the basics on. [4:04]
- Strafe mouse speed depends on hero speed: at low speed move the mouse faster, at high speed (zipline, jump pad) move it slower. [5:05]
- The more speed you have, the closer the dash diagonal gets to a 90 degree angle; learn the diagonal of your dash after wall jumps, dash jumps and ziplines. [5:36]
- After every dash, follow the movement with the mouse, and dash more sideways the slower you are. [6:38]
- Wall jump: jump onto the wall, turn the mouse 45 degrees away from it, hold the key toward the wall's side (wall on the right, hold D), then strafe toward that side. [7:08]
- Move the mouse 45 degrees away before the wall jump, not after, then instantly start strafing the other way. [7:38]
- Direct your character's motion, not your crosshair, toward where you want to go; watching where the crosshair points is a common mistake. [8:08]
- Bucket one heroes are a little easier to train movement on. [9:08]
- Wall jump drill: get on top of the wall and practise moving the mouse outward without pressing space; place yourself aiming away from where you want to go, then press space and let the character fly. [10:10]
- Don't flick the mouse to a random spot; learn the 45 degree angle. Aiming the mouse slightly toward the ground makes it easier because it needs less mouse movement. [10:41]
- Speed out of a wall jump depends on the entry angle: flying close along the wall gives much more speed; running into the wall at a steep angle sends velocity into the wall and loses speed. [11:43]
- Throughout the whole time you are airborne, keep moving the mouse to the side at a steady speed so you are strafing through the whole jump. [12:14]
- Move the mouse at the same speed without changing it; strafe with your forearm so the wrist stays free, and focus on the horizontal plane only. [13:15]
- Wrist players can still bunny hop well, but strafing is generally better done with the forearm. [13:46]
- A common wall jump error is flicking right too fast after the jump; do it slowly at a constant speed. [14:16]
- Jumping too late, once the hero starts falling, is a mistake; wall jump near the peak of your height. [14:47]
- On a jump pad your hero flies on rails, so holding A alone does nothing; you can only strafe when the mouse moves along your flight trajectory, so instantly move the mouse onto it after touching the pad. [15:50]
- Whenever you move (wall jumps, dashes, jump pads, plain jumps) always keep moving the mouse, look where you want to go and strafe to gain speed. [16:21]
- Your hero only gains speed while you move along the flight trajectory; looking left or right away from it before dashing has a huge negative impact on dash speed. [17:23]
- To reach a spot off to the side of a jump pad (for example the camps), strafe the trajectory toward it first, then dash in that direction with good speed. [17:54]
- After a dash jump, if you need to turn, move the mouse onto the flight trajectory, strafe toward the new direction, dash, then put the mouse back on the trajectory and strafe again. [18:59]
- You can start holding A (the strafe key) before you land on the jump pad, since your hero keeps going forward. [20:30]
- Moving the camera faster than your trajectory turns causes drifting, which loses a lot of speed; don't over-strafe on jump pads. [20:30]
- Feel the trajectory your character will take off the jump pad and memorise the angle your character moves after a dash compared with before it. [21:34]
- Short hopping (one stamina wall hops) is overvalued; it is very good mainly for bucket one heroes and Celeste. [23:35]
- Corner wall jumps use the same principle: strafe into the corner and turn the mouse 45 degrees away before jumping. [24:36]
- Walk around corners manually to feel where the edges are and choose your approach angle instead of running and pressing space at random. [25:06]
- Jumping toward a corner as late and as tight as possible creates resistance and a lot of momentum. [25:36]
- Practise strafing in Momentum Mod (free on Steam, Source engine) on bunny hop maps; surf is useless for learning strafes. Bhop Ambience is a good map. [26:38]
- In Momentum Mod the velocity number is blue when gaining speed, white when holding, red when losing. [27:38]
- Start with one strafe per jump and remove any stopping or pause of the mouse between strafes; the motion should be smooth and non-stop. [28:08]
- Once you can do three or four strafes per jump in Momentum Mod you can stop using it and move to Deadlock. [28:39]
- Keep strafe speed constant and only change it as your speed changes: fast strafes at low speed, slower strafes at high speed. [29:09]
- Drifting is the indicator in Deadlock that your strafe speed is wrong. [30:10]
- To test strafe speed in Deadlock: sv_cheats 1, sv_gravity 0 (800 is the default), cl_showpos 1 to show velocity, then press space to float and strafe with A or D. You can also test on Holliday's jump pad or on Celeste. [30:10] [31:42]
- If you can hold a steady speed (around 700 to 780) even with one strafe, you understand strafing. [31:12]
- Deadlock has drag: if you are not moving your mouse you lose speed. [31:12]
- Keep the arm relaxed when strafing; tensing up doesn't help consistency. [33:16]
- Vertical mouse movement does nothing for speed; only horizontal movement matters. Look slightly down so you see where you are in space. [33:16]
- Drill: do one very wide single strafe (360 or 720 degrees) and watch the velocity meter to find your maximum. [33:46]
- You cannot strafe to the opposite side right away: at the end of a strafe, release the key, move the mouse a little further the same way, then start the opposite strafe at the same speed. This is advanced and not needed at a beginner level. [34:46] [38:20]
- What is required at a beginner level is smooth, straight horizontal mouse movement, not an up and down "banana" curve. [38:51]
- Strafing down is a little easier; keep the mouse moving and focus on terrain, hero position and strafes together. [39:51]
- Keep mouse movement constant through the end of each strafe; don't slow down and stop before the next one. [40:27]
- Instant air dash: jumping high loses dash speed as it turns into vertical speed, so the less time you are airborne before dashing, the less speed you lose. [41:28] [42:58]
- Instant air dash method one: from about the third or fourth stair of a staircase, press jump and dash at the same time to go straight into a slide with more speed. Lower down, press slightly later to gain some height. [42:28] [44:00]
- Using instant air dash on heroes like Seven could ruin your games unless you do it on a staircase. [44:33]
- Instant air dash method two: dash jump onto stairs, bunny hop once on landing, then dash instantly after the hop. You have about 0.3 seconds (until max height) to dash after the hop. [45:04] [45:34]
- Before the dash, strafe slightly the opposite way (left to dash right) to line up the dash diagonal and gain speed. [46:35]
- Practise movement in the sandbox, including while queueing; once the basics are down they cost no energy. [48:07]
- Plus one / minus one: landing from a high fall puts your character closer to the ground (minus one); pressing jump and dash in the same frame on landing then gives huge speed and goes into a slide. Minus is good, plus is bad. [48:38] [49:38] [51:40]
- Dashing out of a slide puts you higher off the ground (plus one), so you cannot instant air dash optimally from a slide. [50:38]
- A dash jump gains height and can set up a minus one landing: on landing, jump and dash at once; the quicker you press after landing, the more speed. [51:40] [52:42]
- Hold only A or D, not W, while dashing in these techniques. [52:42] [64:34]
- There is no too early for the landing jump as long as you get the bunny hop; press space then dash immediately. Space then dash is the default combination on all heroes. [53:12] [53:42]
- Approaching an elevation loses instant air dash speed, while approaching from lower ground gains it; use the terrain. [56:15]
- A non-optimal instant air dash loses speed; if you can't create the conditions for an optimal one, a regular dash with a slide is better. [56:46]
- Wall strafe: dash into a wall at about 45 degrees (aim away from the wall about 45 degrees, keys toward the wall) to gain speed; two plain dashes gave about 1,100 speed, two dashes into the wall more. [57:16] [57:49]
- Wall strafing works on all heroes, including bucket two heroes like Apollo, and on almost any wall. [58:20]
- Holliday drill: from the yellow lane jump pad with a stamina item, jump, turn 45 degrees and do two dashes into the wall; aim for at least 1,450 speed, 1,500 is very good. [59:21] [59:52]
- Edge wall jump: you need a texture under you that ends and a supporting texture on the side; walk along it holding W and A and press space right at the edge to wall jump. [61:55] [62:27] [63:01]
- Escaping The Doorman's ultimate (the hotel): run into the corner edge, wall jump while turning the mouse a little left and strafing right, dash left, bunny hop once and slide. [63:31] [65:04]
- Struggling with that escape usually means bumping into the wall before the edge, which sends speed into the wall; run right into the corner with your character's movement, not your crosshair. [64:34]
- Learn routes with a bucket one hero: pick two points, follow the route while watching your velocity, keep speed in the same range and use as little stamina as possible. This transfers to real lane-to-lane and camp rotations. [66:37] [67:08]
- Jump pad wall jump: you get one frame while touching the jump pad where pressing space gives a wall jump; holding D, W+A or A+S changes the direction you fly, which lets you rotate to the camps. [68:09] [68:39]
- For the jump pad wall jump, look into the jump pad as you approach, since the camera angle changes which way the keys send you. [70:10]
- With a lot of speed from a jump pad, use an air jump, not a dash; a dash changes your speed, while a mid-air jump extends your time in the air. [71:12]
- From the harder side of that jump pad, climb with a heavy melee, then hold W and A at the corner (W alone won't climb two textures) and wall jump; the same trick works at other spots, including a faster route to the other shrine. [72:44] [73:15]
- Every advanced technique, including corner boosts, is based on strafing; learn strafing first. [73:46]
- There are thousands of escape routes like a one-stamina corner boost route on the map, though not all are useful. [75:23]
- Good movement lets you predict where an enemy will land and how far they can go when escaping. [75:23]
- When starting out, practise in slow motion with host_timescale; slower makes many techniques easier. [76:24]
