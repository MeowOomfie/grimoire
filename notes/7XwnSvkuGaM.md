# Ignoring Rifts is Losing you Games | Deadlock Guide
channel: Midknight
date: 2026-08-21

## Covers
Rifts: spawn timers, comeback resistances and capture times, when to fight or give a comeback rift, setting waves before the rift, what rift troopers are for, and side-lane or mid boss alternatives.
Examples from a coached Ghost Academy scrim (about Eternus 2 average) and a lower rank match, so it applies at average and higher ranks.

## Claims
- The first rift spawns at 12 minutes, give or take a minute; every later rift spawns 7 minutes after the last spawn, give or take a minute. [0:31]
- The side of the map a rift spawns on is random; a glowing portal shows 1 minute before it spawns, and at 20 seconds a pop-up notification appears and it shows on the minimap. [0:31]
- Rifts give the losing team scaling comeback resistances up to a maximum of 35% when they are behind by 15% or more; even a 1% deficit gives about 2.5% resist (rough math). [0:31]
- If the rift is a comeback rift for the enemy and you lead by about 10% or more, do not fight the enemy in the rift; if you have the comeback advantage by 10% or more, you must fight the rift. [1:02]
- The most classic throw is contesting a comeback rift that shouldn't be contested; the second is never fighting your own comeback rift, so you never come back into the game. [1:02]
- With the rift about 30 seconds out, light trades are very impactful, because damage on heroes without healing is hard to reset before the fight. [1:32]
- Before a rift, a laner should not wander off; let a wave-clearing teammate such as an Infernus with Flame Dash clear, play safe, then rotate, but first and most importantly shove mid lane. [2:02]
- Before rift fights, get priority on your mid and side waves; a wave crashing into the enemy guardian makes them bleed damage, risk a split push for a free tier one mid walker, or send a player off the rift, giving you a one-player advantage for killing four minions. [2:33]
- A big benefit of keeping your mid guardian up is that it clears waves naturally, so your team doesn't have to respond unless the enemy really pushes or splits. [3:03]
- When the rift is about to spawn, be on the map creating pressure in lanes and getting priority, especially in the pre-20 second window when the next wave decides how your waves are set for the rift fight. [3:34]
- To set a wave: when a wave is pushing into you and your wave is healthy, kill only the enemy healing trooper so your wave slowly pushes out, creating passive pressure; done at your walker, enemies can't walk up to clear it the way they could a hard shoved wave. [3:34]
- Example: a Rem using his helpers on waves put pressure on mid, forcing Lash to clear mid so he arrived very late to the rift. [4:04]
- The resistance zone is a circle of about 35 m, larger than the 25 m rift itself, but it is easiest to remember that anyone in the rift gets resist. [4:35]
- The winning team defending against a comeback rift should not fight inside the rift; take the space aggressively and fight in neutral space before the enemy reaches it, so they must clear through you. [5:05]
- Don't run down a huge open space to reach a rift alone; one player engaging before teammates arrive gets cooldown dumped and picked. [5:37]
- Nobody needs to touch the rift until it is about 90% (really 99%) charged, so you can wait. [5:37]
- A rift normally takes 12 seconds to capture; a full 35% comeback rift takes the winning team 18 seconds and the losing team only 6 seconds (up to 6 seconds slower or faster). [5:37]
- Wait for teammates and for the wave to be shoved before rotating in, and come into the rift from a different angle (up on a building or from the side) instead of the open middle. [6:07]
- Fighting around the rift, for example blocking with the zipline rope, very commonly finds kills before the rift fight. [6:38]
- If the enemy contests a comeback rift, you can give it, let them capture it, and the second it is captured hard fight them: they lose their resistances and haven't spent the rift souls, so you are still much stronger. [7:09]
- Rift resistances are set when the rift spawns and do not change. [7:39]
- A common mistake: the team that just captured a comeback rift plays overly aggressive with its rift troopers while still down about 7K, and gets mowed down, especially pushing toward the walker. [8:10]
- Rift troopers aren't that strong; they help sieging but mostly they are space on the map, guaranteeing about 20 seconds of space in the enemy jungle, which is useful for stealing key farm like Sinner's Sacrifices. [8:40]
- If you are far behind, back up and let the enemy instantly clear the rift troopers; in a neutral game, use the fact they must respond to steal their jungle. [8:40]
- Rift troopers matter more in later rifts; in early rifts, when walkers are very tanky, they don't matter for more than stealing jungle. [9:10]
- For a 22-minute or later full-resist comeback rift, the winning team very commonly trades: give the rift and force mid boss. [9:10]
- At high level the losing team expects the mid boss trade and captures the rift with one person, sits mid or leaves a scout; below about Eternus 3, if your whole team goes mid and trades the full comeback rift, it is a free win because they are all across the map. [9:41]
- Early rifts aren't that valuable: the first is worth about 1,500 souls and the second about 2,200, while walkers are worth a flat 3K. [9:41]
- Leaving your gun carry in a side lane to potentially split the walker while the team fights the rift is great; usually they get mirrored and it becomes a 5v5 rift plus a 1v1 between carries. [10:11]
- During one of the first three rifts, an option is to skip the rift, jump and kill the enemy gun carry on the other side of the map (the most important kill if they farm correctly), then push the walker with your carry or give the solo souls to your carry. [10:41]
- If you kill them early you can use teleporters to reach the rift; teleporters are insanely important for rotations and especially for making it to rifts. [10:41]
