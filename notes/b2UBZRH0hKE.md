# How do you even counter gun heroes?
channel: Deathy  date: 2025-06-27  url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b2UBZRH0hKE

## Covers
Countering late game gun heroes (Infernus, Lady Geist, Mirage) with items: Return Fire, Suppressor, Disarming Hex, Rusted Barrel, Warp Stone, Bullet Resilience, Plated Armor, Phantom Strike; fire rate slow stacking, resist stacking, on-hit prevention vs afterburn and Toxic Bullets; Lash Flog, Abrams and Warden as Phantom Strike buyers

## Claims
- Many games have almost everyone holding a lot of orange items, and gun heroes like Infernus, Lady Geist or Mirage feel like they inevitably win the game at 30 to 35 minutes. [0:00]
- Gun heroes are definitely a little overtuned at the moment, at least on the upper end; past a certain point they feel unstoppable, and these counters mitigate but do not fully eliminate their strength. [0:00]
- Most counters are through itemization: either disable them with some crowd control (stun and the like), for which you do not have many item options, or build items that nullify some of their damage, reduce their attack speed or deal damage back to them. [0:30]
- Return Fire automatically fires a bullet at any attacker who damages you with abilities or weapon; it gives 10% bullet resist passively and returns 60% of bullet damage versus 30% of spirit damage, so it is inherently much better against gun heroes than spirit heroes. [1:30]
- Return Fire returns damage before any reductions, so shooting someone with lots of resistances can hurt you more than them; in his demo against an Infernus with 69% bullet resist, Lady Geist took 921 incoming damage from Return Fire while her outgoing damage was less. [2:00]
- He has not done the math on Return Fire's pre-mitigation return, but is pretty confident it works that way; take it with a grain of salt. [3:03]
- Return Fire is incredibly potent at countering gun heroes; its biggest downside is it only lasts 6 seconds, but it does a ton of damage to any weapon hero shooting you. [3:33]
- Suppressor is one of the most underbought items at lower levels: 6 spirit power, 75 health, and a 35% fire rate slow for 4 seconds when you apply spirit damage, which a lot of heroes can apply fairly easily. [3:33]
- Swift Striker costs the same and gives 20% fire rate, so Suppressor slows fire rate by almost double that value for the same cost, which is very efficient. [4:04]
- Suppressor's biggest problem is that it has no upgrade: it matters a lot early to mid game, but by 30 to 35 minutes against a lot of fire rate items it matters less. [4:34]
- Fire rate slows subtract from fire rate: a Lady Geist with Burst Fire and Swift Striker at 30% fire rate goes to -5% under Suppressor; the lowest any hero can go is -50% fire rate. [4:34]
- Fire rate slows stack diminishingly: Lash's Flog (35% fire rate slow) plus Suppressor (35%) should be 70% but is actually 58%, which he tested. [5:04]
- Stacking fire rate itself does not have diminishing returns, while fire rate slows do, which he thinks makes no sense. [5:35]
- Shred stacking diminishingly makes sense because resistances also stack diminishingly: Weakening Headshot plus Bullet Resist Shredder gives about 21% rather than 24%, and Battle Vest (18%) plus Return Fire gives 26%, not 28%. [6:05]
- Disarming Hex is technically another good option but hard to slot: it builds out of Rusted Barrel, which kind of sucks (800 souls for about Suppressor's value but only 5 seconds, with a 30 second cooldown and little uptime), so you want to go straight into Disarming Hex, which disarms for 4 seconds. [6:37]
- Disarming Hex is good and worth remembering, but not that great an answer; it is a bit of an answer to M1 heroes. [7:07]
- Warp Stone is an often forgotten answer: 40% bullet resist for the money is crazy value, and it makes you extremely mobile. [7:37]
- Spirit heroes generally do burst damage with abilities (many AoE) that are consistent to hit even on moving targets, while gun heroes require aim, so moving erratically (Warp Stone, Stamina Mastery, hopping wall to wall) makes you much harder to hit; against these heroes, mobility is survivability. [7:37]
- Bullet Resilience's extra 30% bullet resist when you are "below 30 health" (as said) is additive with its own base, since it is the same source, so it goes from 30% to 60% below the threshold; it is very strong at countering M1 heroes. [8:38]
- Plated Armor has deflection percent and on-hit prevention percent and is incredibly valuable, but do not buy it before you have some bullet resistance, as it is less efficient mathematically; for example, buy Bullet Resilience first and then go into Plated Armor. [9:10]
- Plated Armor's on-hit prevention is incredibly strong with Infernus extremely meta and feeling almost uncounterable: 50% on-hit prevention makes him much weaker against you because he struggles to apply his afterburn. [9:40]
- In his demo, procing afterburn on an Infernus with no items took roughly eight or nine bullets, and with Plated Armor roughly 50% more. [10:10]
- On-hit prevention also works against Toxic Bullets, an extremely common item right now, so preventing Toxic Bullets and afterburn is very valuable. [10:41]
- Phantom Strike is essentially a better Disarming Hex: it disarms, and it feels good on heroes that want to close the gap, for example Abrams; Hoot has bought it on Warden a lot. [10:41]
- Phantom Strike is a green item rather than purple, so it gives a lot of health and tankiness, and it does damage and reduces move speed; valuable overall, but it helps rather than fully countering gun heroes. [11:11]
- The meta is very favorable towards gun at the moment and has shifted further as people figured out gun items, and certain items are definitely problematic. [11:42]
