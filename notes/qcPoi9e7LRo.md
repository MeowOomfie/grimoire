# Coaching A GOAT of Dota in Deadlock (ft. Quinn)
channel: Deathy
date: 2025-11-17
url: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qcPoi9e7LRo

## Covers
Deathy coaches Quinn, a retired professional Dota mid laner with about 120 hours of Deadlock, on two of his games: one on Wraith and one on Mina.
Topics: how carries snowball (solo walkers, side lanes, Sinner's Sacrifice), lane pressure vs economy, out of combat regen, the Urn and comeback mechanics, unsecured souls, walker resistances, box routes, melee and parry, and a 0-15 / 15-25 minute plan for Wraith and Mina.

## Claims
- The biggest difference from Dota is that in Deadlock you basically want to perma push, especially in the lane stage. [1:32]
- Carries snowball differently than in Dota: hit your timings much earlier, because high level games end around 25 minutes, so you want to spike at around 15 to 20 minutes. [2:34]
- On Wraith you snowball by killing walkers alone; a solo walker kill gives you most of the souls yourself, a huge amount. [3:04]
- Lane pressure is super important, and there is not much kill threat in this game. [3:34]
- At this rank people rarely buy utility; when nobody on the enemy team can stop your teleport you can shove waves aggressively a lot of the time. [3:34]
- Slowing Hex is a cheap item that prevents any movement ability, like Wraith's teleport, for 3.5 seconds; a lot of things are balanced by active slots. [4:06]
- Silence Wave is a 3 second AoE silence. [4:06]
- Wraith is not a good laner; in lane her priority is hitting troopers, taking trades only if you are just better than the enemy, and carefully. [5:07]
- Kelvin, Quinn's lane partner, is called super broken: cannonball shots, the best base stats, base spirit resist, good base damage and health, no real weaknesses. [5:38]
- As Wraith, trades between waves should generally be somewhat disadvantageous for you unless your lane partner is applying pressure. [6:08]
- Outside of pressure, Wraith's lane priority is boxes and the small camp, which spawns at 2 minutes; kill it instantly if you have no pressure on the enemy. [6:08]
- When the enemies are very hurt, keep the pressure and go for the kill instead of going back to do the camp. [6:39]
- General principle, like Dota: if you have pressure, abuse it over economy; it is okay to sacrifice a bit of economy for a kill, because an enemy who dies and misses a wave basically loses the lane. [6:39]
- Whether to shoot the tower or the enemy heroes depends on the hero: Abrams has no pressure under tower so might as well shoot the tower, but Wraith can use her cards on them. [7:09]
- As Wraith you could have curved a card around the wall to cancel Grey Talon's Healing Rite; when enemies are low, focus your cards on them rather than the tower. [7:09]
- Guardians give no extra armor or regen to defenders; only walkers do. [7:39]
- At 10 minutes Wraith can walk up to a guardian without a wave and kill it alone; there is never any backdoor protection on guardians. [7:39]
- If you are having a terrible game and are really behind, sometimes feeding for a guardian can be a play. [7:39]
- Quinn's lane problems were mostly micro: recognising he had pressure, positioning better and buying different items. [8:09]
- His items (stacked regen) all had the game plan of chilling and not fighting, which did not match a lane where he had lots of pressure. [8:09]
- Out of combat regen has two timers: with NPCs it kicks in about 0.5 seconds after you stop shooting or being shot; with heroes it is about 4 seconds (Deathy says he would need to double check). [8:39]
- Monster Rounds plus Sprint Boots is the meta "chilling" build: clear the wave, then run boxes with the sprint speed and out of combat regen. [9:11]
- When you get nuked every time you try to hit the tower, push the wave and farm camps and boxes instead. [9:42]
- Against opponents of similar skill you won't win lane as Wraith most of the time, so the plan is push the wave and get a lead through boxes and camps. [9:42]
- Hit Sinner's Sacrifice basically off cooldown; every hero farms them at the same speed because you are just punching them. [10:12]
- You can let the wave push into your tower a bit to finish a camp; giving up the tower is not ideal but not that bad. [10:44]
- Slide while Wraith's full auto is active, since it only lasts a short time and you need the most value from it. [10:44]
- With the enemy Lady Geist at that health, Wraith could just kill her with her ultimate; look for that opportunity. [11:14]
- Following a guide's item order meant accepting being weak in lane; at this skill level itemise more aggressively for card damage so you win the lane more often. [11:14]
- Quicksilver Reload helps farming since it reloads your gun, and also adds 44 damage to Wraith's card. [11:45]
- He missed the Sinner's Sacrifice timing; he could have farmed two by then. [11:45]
- Deathy coaches according to rank: at this rank you can push the wave and steal the enemy's Sinner's Sacrifice, and as Wraith teleport away, basically for free every game. [11:45]
- Everything scales over time, but boxes scale differently: by 20 to 25 minutes boxes become worth more than camps, and at 40 minutes box routes give far more than camps. [12:16]
- Comeback mechanic: you get a percentage more souls for everything you do depending on how far behind your team is in souls. [13:17]
- It is too early to kill the ancient camp without a farming item; you probably need something like Tesla Bullets. [13:17]
- The moment the laning stage is over, a carry should get out of the mid lane; mid is the last place a carry wants to be. [13:17]
- At this rank everybody fights mid permanently without power spikes, purpose or advantage, so a carry should go to any side lane that is open or has an opportunity, partly to get solo souls. [13:48]
- With no side lane opportunity, max out Sinner's Sacrifice: hit the ones near you and the double Sinner's Sacrifice in your jungle building. [14:18]
- Inefficient soul sources mean you won't spike early enough to carry; to carry you have to be online at about 15 minutes. [14:48]
- Don't join a mid fight at half HP when you are not strong; trade for another tower instead. [14:48]
- Out of combat travel: with enough sprint speed just sprinting is fine; otherwise dash jump, slide and bunny hop to keep momentum. [15:18]
- The most important movement is jump pads and ziplines; use zipline tech, and getting off the zipline with the crouch key keeps more momentum. [15:49]
- The devs nerfed deathballing: soul splitting gets far worse the more players share, so farming souls as five gives almost nothing. [16:19]
- Walkers take less damage based on how many enemy players are near them, so five people shooting a walker run into damage resist. [16:19]
- The game is essentially a mid boss simulator: the buff gives your whole team more health, fire rate and spirit power. [16:50]
- Mid boss used to halve the team's respawn time and give mega creeps; megas were removed and there is currently no way to get them. [16:50]
- One of the game's biggest flaws right now is that in the mid game there is nothing to do but mid boss. [16:50]
- The first Urn is better than one guardian but not better than two; if you can trade the first Urn for two guardians you are happy. [17:20]
- The second Urn is at 10 minutes; a new Urn spawns every 5 minutes with a set amount of souls that scales over time. [17:51]
- If the Urn is ever on your side (a comeback Urn) it is always the number one priority; drop everything and do it. [17:51]
- Whether the Urn favours a side is set by a 10% net worth difference: 100,000 vs 90,000 souls makes it a comeback for the 90,000 team. [17:51]
- In non-organised play the comeback Urn is extremely strong because people are too slow to contest it and give it to you for free. [17:51]
- At that stage the carry wants to be hitting a side walker while the rest fight elsewhere; Wraith can kill the walker alone and go from about 18k to 21k souls. [18:22]
- A big part of being a carry is knowing side lane 1v1 matchups against other carries; Wraith only beats Infernus in a 1v1 if she ults him. [18:53]
- Wraith is quite strong right now and in a really good spot. [18:53]
- Do the Urn when you have an advantage that is not so big that it becomes a comeback Urn for the enemy; Kelvin should have picked it up earlier, and a carry can only communicate it. [19:23]
- Carry is the less frustrating role and you can learn in any role, but it teaches less about map movement since you follow a very simple flowchart. [20:23]
- Pocket suits a former mid laner: no aim needed, a spirit nuker with tempo and huge map presence, like the Puck of Deadlock. [20:23]
- Pocket is one of the hardest heroes to balance: OP most of the time, hard to kill with lots of i-frames and movement, and he runs low rank lobbies where people don't buy silence. [20:54]
- With a 20k lead, all you need is to win a fight and take mid boss, and that is pretty much the game. [21:24]
- Mina is still kind of a carry and a high net worth hero, like Pocket; you can't play low economy Mina, you still need to farm. [21:24]
- Mina is a very strong laner, unlike Wraith. [21:24]
- Mina is strong mostly because of Love Bites; Rake is her lane sustain, making her a squishy glass cannon with a lot of sustain. [21:55]
- Mina's Rake sustain was nerfed; she used to be nearly unkillable in lane unless one-shot, now you have to invest in it. [21:55]
- The moment Paige shields herself, stop trading and run; you will never win a trade into the shield, a 200 damage barrier you must beat. When it is down she is not very strong. [21:55]
- One deny in the first two waves matters, like Dota: it lets you hit your second ability before the enemy, giving a small timing where you have two abilities and they have one. [22:25]
- If you get denied once, expect to be missing your second ability and be careful on the third wave. [22:55]
- Never heavy melee for the most part: heavy melee DPS is not much higher than light melee, it is 100% reactable, and getting parried is a death sentence. [23:26]
- Light melee is basically instant and can't be reacted to, so the enemy has to guess to parry it; throwing in light melees is a bit meta on many heroes, with some RNG and mind games. [23:26]
- You can aim a melee up or down; the model doesn't change but the melee hitbox does. [24:28]
- Players double jump while depositing the Urn to dodge melee, but in Deathy's testing it doesn't work if the attacker looks up with the melee. [24:28]
- Never buy Extra Regen on Mina; Rake is basically her regen. [24:58]
- Think of the bridge buffs as stronger than most tier 2 items at the start, and they scale through the game. [24:58]
- Billy's ultimate prevents Wraith from teleporting and is one of the few ultimates you can't really dodge, so it is a problem matchup. [25:28]
- Don't be afraid to reset to base: with the zipline speed boost (unlocked at about 6:10) you miss nothing, and without it you still miss almost nothing if you reset right after killing the wave. [25:59]
- Fighting at low HP instead of resetting is very risky; you could be at full HP. [25:59]
- Parry the guardian when hitting it; doing so there would have been a good play. [26:31]
- With your tower taken and both side lanes pushed in and all guardians dead, steal camps and invade the jungle. [27:01]
- The most valuable thing to invade is the enemy double Sinner's Sacrifice; at high level, teams with a lead out of lane invade the double. [27:01]
- A team that is behind has three ways back: more souls from everything, comeback souls for killing your guardians (they can suicide for guardians), and securing their own farm. [27:31]
- The losing team here was more than 20% behind, so they got 25% more souls for everything they farmed; if you each do your own farm, you are losing ground. Steal their farm. [28:01]
- Only fight when the enemies are pushing your tower, contesting your Sinner's Sacrifice or are out of position; otherwise steal their Sinner's Sacrifice. [28:31]
- The camp he took was worth less than doing the Sinner's Sacrifice. [28:31]
- Everything farmed from the jungle and boxes is unsecured souls, which slowly turn into regular souls. [29:03]
- When you spend, unsecured souls are spent last but still spent; with 1,600 souls of which 400 unsecured, spending 1,600 leaves no unsecured souls. [29:03]
- When you die you drop your unsecured souls for the enemy to pick up, so dying with an 800 bag can be game losing. [29:33]
- After farming jungle with a big unsecured bag, buy the moment you reach an item threshold (1,600, 2,400, 3,200) to clear the bag, so you can play aggressive without feeding thousands. [29:33]
- Mid game plan: keep stealing camps, extend the lead, know your power spikes, and take advantageous fights around objectives. [30:03]
- People can't teleport to react in this game, so outplay by counting where enemies are on the map, for example three on the right side means a guaranteed 4v3 on the left. [30:33]
- With the Urn done and the enemy Sinner's Sacrifice gone, farming your own double Sinner's Sacrifice is okay; keep pressuring lanes in between instead of wasting time. [31:03]
- Trying to push the walker when everyone is standing next to it is exactly how you throw a 20k lead: defenders get resists and the walker itself fights. [31:33]
- Walkers have two resistances: one based on the number of enemy players near it, and one based on time, falling from some percentage at 10 minutes to 0% at 18 minutes (roughly). [32:04]
- The mid walker is tankier than the other walkers and central, so it is a nightmare to take. [32:04]
- Clear all the boxes in a room when there is nothing better to do, but time is of the essence; at higher rank skip boxes to rotate faster. Boxes are what you do when there is nothing better. [32:34]
- The most efficient box route is the mid boxes, which spawn at 10 minutes and give more than a Sinner's Sacrifice on average, plus golden statues that give buffs. [33:04]
- The mid boxes are about the highest concentration of souls in the game, except maybe tier three camps; at high level everyone does them right at 10 minutes. [33:34]
- The other efficient box route is the tunnel route, mirrored on both sides of the map. [33:34]
- With a big lead you can force mid boss whenever, especially with Kelvin, the king of mid boss thanks to his dome. [34:05]
- Mid boss is much better than Roshan; the game seems designed to end in about 30 minutes. [34:05]
- A 10k lead in Deadlock is about a 5k lead in Dota: everyone earns over 1,000 souls per minute, so halve the numbers to compare. [35:06]
- After mid boss, do the Urn; with a 30k lead the game is basically over. [35:37]
- The only time a big lead is throwable is when mid boss is about to spawn: a bad fight then lets the enemy get picks and mid boss and flip the game even 40k down. [35:37]
- Mid boss is like Roshan's spawn but way more OP, and you can't play around the Rejuvenator. [36:07]
- Wraith plan, 0 to 15 minutes: get rich, prioritise Sinner's Sacrifice and tier three camps as soon as you can. [36:38]
- Wraith plan, 15 to 25 minutes: take walkers and take side lane 1v1s. [36:38]
- Wraith late game: play front to back, stay behind some of your players, focus on shooting, win the fight, go mid. [36:38]
- Most guns have no damage falloff before 20 meters, so there is no downside to standing 20 m away. [37:08]
- Mina plan, 0 to 15 minutes, assuming you win lane: steal camps, steal Sinner's Sacrifice, and show up at your power spikes such as Tankbuster. [37:08]
- Mina plan, 15 to 25 minutes: active heroes win by abusing jump pads, teleporters and ziplines to take numbers advantage fights, and limit testing who you can fight. [37:38]
- Mina late game is a burst assassin: go into the backline, burst someone and get back out; pick who you can burst and target them. [38:09]
- General farm priority: Sinner's Sacrifice and tier three camps if you can do them, then tier two camps, which are roughly equal to boxes depending on the time. [38:09]
- The mid boxes are probably a higher priority than tier three camps on most heroes, because anyone can do them. [38:39]
